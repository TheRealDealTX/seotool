## Mississippi facts you may use (stay inside these; be cautious)
- 82 counties. Capital and largest city: Jackson (Hinds County; city limits extend into Madison and Rankin counties).
- Regulator: Mississippi Insurance Department (MID), headed by an elected Commissioner of Insurance. Consumers can file complaints with MID.
- Wind pool: Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association (MWUA) — a residual-market insurer of wind and hail coverage
  for property in the six southernmost counties: Hancock, Harrison, Jackson, Pearl River, Stone and George. Policies there
  are often split: a homeowners policy that excludes wind, plus an MWUA wind policy. Flood is separate (usually NFIP).
- Percentage hurricane / named-storm / wind deductibles (e.g. 2%–5% of Coverage A) are common on and near the Coast.
- Bad faith (describe only generally): Mississippi recognizes a court-made (common-law) bad-faith claim when an insurer
  denies or delays without an arguable or legitimate basis; punitive damages are governed by statute (Miss. Code Ann. § 11-1-65).
  Appraisal does not decide bad faith. Point people to a Mississippi attorney.
- Limitations (general, cautious): Mississippi’s general limitation period for many contract claims is three years
  (Miss. Code Ann. § 15-1-49), and state law generally restricts contracts from shortening statutory limitation periods
  (§ 15-1-5). Always say "confirm your deadline with a Mississippi attorney".
- Appraisal comes from the policy’s appraisal clause (no special Mississippi appraisal statute to cite). Typical clause:
  written demand; each side names a competent, independent appraiser (often within 20 days); the appraisers pick an umpire
  (often within 15 days, or a court of record can appoint one); agreement of any two sets the amount of loss.
- Storms (dates verified):
  - Hurricane Camille, Aug 17, 1969 — Category 5 landfall near Pass Christian / Bay St. Louis.
  - Hurricane Katrina, Aug 29, 2005 — catastrophic storm surge across Hancock, Harrison and Jackson counties
    (Waveland, Bay St. Louis, Pass Christian, Long Beach, Gulfport, Biloxi, Ocean Springs, Pascagoula), wind damage far
    inland (Hattiesburg, Laurel, Meridian area). It produced years of "wind vs. water" (wind-vs-flood) claim disputes and
    anti-concurrent-causation arguments.
  - Hurricane Isaac, Aug 2012 (rain and surge on the Coast). Hurricane Nate, Oct 2017 (landfall near Biloxi, Category 1).
    Hurricane Zeta, Oct 28, 2020 (landfall in Louisiana, strong wind across the Mississippi Coast). Hurricane Ida, Aug 2021
    (landfall in Louisiana, wind and tree damage in southwest Mississippi).
  - Tupelo tornado, April 5, 1936 (one of the deadliest in US history). Vicksburg tornado, Dec 5, 1953.
  - Yazoo City EF4 tornado, April 24, 2010. Smithville EF5 tornado (Monroe County), April 27, 2011.
  - Hattiesburg EF4 tornado, Feb 10, 2013 (University of Southern Mississippi and Oak Grove area).
  - Louisville EF4 tornado (Winston County), April 28, 2014; Tupelo was hit by an EF3 the same day.
  - Columbus EF3 tornado, Feb 23, 2019.
  - Easter Sunday outbreak, April 12, 2020 — EF4 tornado near Bassfield and Soso (Jefferson Davis, Covington, Jasper counties), wide damage in the Pine Belt.
  - Rolling Fork EF4 tornado, March 24, 2023 (Sharkey County; Silver City in Humphreys County; the same storm system hit Amory in Monroe County).
  - North Mississippi ice storm, Feb 1994 (massive tree and power-line damage). Winter storm, Feb 2021 (statewide freeze, burst pipes). Dec 2022 hard freeze.
  - Pearl River flood in the Jackson metro, Feb 2020. Jackson’s water-system failures in 2022 (pressure loss, boil-water notices).
  - Great Mississippi Flood of 1927 (Delta). Mississippi River flooding in 2011.
- Geography / regions: Gulf Coast (Hancock, Harrison, Jackson counties); Pine Belt (Hattiesburg, Laurel, Petal, Columbia);
  Capital / Metro Jackson (Jackson, Brandon, Madison, Ridgeland, Clinton, Pearl, Flowood); the Delta (Greenville,
  Clarksdale, Cleveland, Greenwood, Yazoo City, Indianola); North Mississippi / Hill Country (Oxford, Tupelo, Southaven,
  Olive Branch, Starkville, Columbus, Corinth); East Central (Meridian, Philadelphia, Louisville); Southwest (Natchez,
  McComb, Brookhaven, Vicksburg). Roughly two-thirds of the state is forest (loblolly pine, oaks, pecans) — tree-fall
  claims are common. Yazoo clay (expansive soil) in the Jackson metro (foundation movement is usually excluded — say so).
- Housing mix: raised/elevated coastal homes rebuilt after Katrina, Creole cottages and shotgun houses, antebellum and
  Victorian homes (Natchez, Columbus, Vicksburg, Holly Springs), brick ranches from the 1960s–80s, newer slab-on-grade
  subdivisions (DeSoto, Madison, Rankin, Lamar counties), manufactured homes, metal farm buildings in the Delta,
  casinos/hotels on the Coast, student rentals in Oxford and Starkville, churches.
- Common local claim issues: wind vs. flood allocation; named-storm deductible math; matching shingles; tree removal limits
  (often $500 per tree / $1,000 per occurrence on many HO forms — say "many policies"); ice-dam and frozen-pipe water losses;
  hail on architectural shingles and metal roofs; code upgrades (ordinance or law) after storms; mold limits; contents.
