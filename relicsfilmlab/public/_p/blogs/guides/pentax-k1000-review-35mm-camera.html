Pentax K1000 review: the student camera that never retired

The Asahi Pentax K1000 was introduced in 1976 and made, almost unchanged, until 1997. It has no autofocus, no auto exposure and no program modes — and that is exactly why generations of photography teachers put it in students' hands.

Pentax K1000 at a glance

Type35mm manual-focus SLR
Lens mountPentax K (bayonet)
ShutterMechanical cloth focal-plane, 1 s – 1/1000 s + B
Flash sync1/60 s
MeteringCentre-weighted match-needle CdS meter
BatteryOne LR44/SR44 — for the meter only
Made1976–1997 (Japan, later Hong Kong and China)

What the K1000 does brilliantly

  • It works without a battery. The shutter is entirely mechanical; the battery only powers the light meter. Run it flat in the mountains and you can keep shooting with the Sunny 16 rule.
  • It teaches exposure. A needle in the viewfinder tells you to open up or stop down. You set aperture and shutter speed yourself, so every frame is a small lesson.
  • K-mount lenses are everywhere. The SMC Pentax-M 50mm f/1.7 and f/2 are cheap and excellent, and decades of third-party K-mount lenses fit.
  • It is built like a tank. A metal body, few electronics, and a simple layout mean there is not much to break.

The quirks

  • No depth-of-field preview, no self-timer and no mirror lock-up.
  • The meter is always on when the lens cap is off — keep the cap on in your bag to save the battery.
  • Later Chinese-made bodies have more plastic parts than the early Asahi versions.

Pentax K1000 photos: what to expect

The K1000 doesn't have a "look" of its own — the lens and film do. With a 50mm f/1.7 and a roll of Kodak Portra 400 you'll get warm, natural skin tones; with Ilford HP5 Plus you'll get classic, punchy black & white. The meter is reliable for average scenes, so watch for backlit subjects and snow.

What to check before buying

  1. Fire the shutter at 1 s and 1/1000 s: the difference should be obvious.
  2. Check that the meter needle moves when you point the camera at light and dark areas (with a fresh LR44).
  3. Look for sticky or crumbling light-seal foam around the back and mirror box.
  4. Inspect the lens for haze or fungus and make sure the aperture blades are dry and snappy.

Best film for the Pentax K1000

Start with a 400-speed film — Kodak UltraMax 400, Portra 400 or Ilford HP5 Plus — for flexibility indoors and out.

Verdict: is the Pentax K1000 still worth it?

Yes — if you want to learn. The K1000 is honest, durable and simple; nothing gets between you and the exposure. If you'd like aperture-priority automation, look at the Pentax ME Super or Minolta X-700; for a similar fully mechanical experience from Nikon, the Nikon FM.
