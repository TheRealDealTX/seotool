The best point & shoot film cameras

A great point and shoot film camera fits in a pocket, focuses for you and still makes pictures you'll print. After years of selling and repairing them, these are the compacts we recommend — grouped by what you want to spend and how you shoot.

The best overall: Olympus Stylus Epic (mju-II)

A fast, sharp 35mm f/2.8 lens, weather sealing, spot metering and a body the size of a bar of soap. It's the benchmark. See the Olympus Stylus Epic →

Best with no batteries: Olympus Trip 35

Solar-powered selenium auto exposure, a superb 40mm Zuiko lens and zone focusing. Ten million sold for good reason. See the Olympus Trip 35 →

Best lens for the money: Nikon L35AF

Nicknamed the Pikaichi in Japan, its 35mm f/2.8 Nikkor is one of the sharpest compact lenses of the 80s, and it runs on AA batteries. See the Nikon L35AF →

Best low light: Canon AF35ML

A 40mm f/1.9 lens — almost unheard of in a compact — makes it a night-time star. See the Canon AF35ML →

Best zoom: Canon Sure Shot Z115 / Olympus Stylus Epic Zoom 80

For travel, a zoom compact covers more situations. The Canon Z115 reaches 115mm; the Stylus Epic Zoom 80 is smaller and weather-sealed.

Best budget: Canon Sure Shot Owl, Olympus Infinity Stylus

The Sure Shot Owl's huge viewfinder makes it a joy to use, and the original Olympus Stylus is a genuine pocket camera.

The luxury pick: Contax T2 and T3

Titanium bodies and Carl Zeiss Sonnar lenses — the Contax T2 and Contax T3 are as good as compacts get, priced accordingly.
