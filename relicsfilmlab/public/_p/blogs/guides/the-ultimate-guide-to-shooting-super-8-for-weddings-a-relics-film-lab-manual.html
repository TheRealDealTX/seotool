Shooting Super 8 at weddings: the Relics manual

Nothing captures a wedding like Super 8: the warm colour, the grain, the soft flicker. This is the manual we gave couples and filmmakers who came to the Relics lab — from choosing a camera to getting the footage scanned.

1. Choose the right camera

Look for a camera with an XL (existing light) lens and shutter, reliable auto exposure and 24 fps. Our favourites are the Canon 514XL, the low-light Canon 310XL with its f/1.0 lens, and the Chinon 20P XL. Test it with a cartridge before the big day.

3. How many cartridges?

A 50ft cartridge runs about 2.5 minutes at 24 fps. For a highlights film plan on 3–4 cartridges for a short ceremony, and 6–8 to cover getting ready, ceremony, portraits and reception.

4. Shooting technique

  • Hold each shot for 3–5 seconds, then stop — Super 8 rewards short, deliberate takes.
  • Move slowly, or keep still and let the couple move through the frame.
  • Keep the sun behind or beside you; backlight is beautiful but check your exposure.
  • Indoors, add a continuous light or use 500T.
  • Label every cartridge as you finish it.

5. Developing and scanning

Vision3 Super 8 is ECN-2 film — send it to a lab that processes and scans Super 8, and ask for a 2K or higher scan with the full frame visible. See our ECN-2 guide.

Super 8 wedding checklist

  1. Camera tested with fresh batteries
  2. Spare batteries for the camera and meter
  3. Film cartridges in a cool bag
  4. A marker for labels
  5. An LED light for the reception
