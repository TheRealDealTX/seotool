Vintage camera repair

Most film camera problems are simpler than they look. Here is the restoration know-how from the Relics repair bench — what you can fix yourself and when to call a pro.

Common film camera faults

SymptomLikely causeFix
Dead — nothing worksBattery or corroded contactsNew battery, clean contacts
Orange or red streaks on photosLight leaks from old foam sealsDIY light-seal replacement
Shutter stuck or slowDried lubricantProfessional CLA
Hazy, low-contrast imagesLens haze or fungusLens cleaning by a technician
Wrong exposuresMeter drift, wrong battery voltageCorrect battery or meter calibration
Sticky aperture bladesOil on the bladesLens service

Start with a diagnostic

Run our five-minute camera repair diagnostic before booking a repair. Then try basic camera cleaning.

Finding a repairer

Look for a technician who specialises in your brand, publishes turnaround times and offers a warranty on work. Ask for a quote first: if a repair costs more than a replacement body, buying another may make more sense.

FAQ

Is it worth repairing a vintage film camera?

Usually, yes — a CLA (clean, lube, adjust) on a quality SLR or rangefinder costs less than replacing it and can add decades of life.

What is a film camera CLA?

A clean, lube and adjust: the technician cleans the shutter and mechanism, lubricates where needed and calibrates shutter speeds and meter.

Can I fix light leaks myself?

Yes. Replacing sticky light-seal foam is an easy DIY job with a seal kit, a toothpick and some isopropyl alcohol.
