Exposure Calculator

Sunny 16 and EV exposure calculator for film: get the right shutter speed and aperture for any light.

Equivalent exposures

How to use the exposure calculator

Pick your film speed, the light you're in and the aperture you want. The calculator works out the shutter speed using exposure value (EV), then lists every equivalent aperture/shutter pair so you can trade depth of field for motion blur.

The Sunny 16 rule

In bright sun, at f/16, use a shutter speed of 1 / your ISO. With ISO 400 film that's f/16 at 1/500 (or 1/400). Every step darker on the light scale opens up one stop: f/11 for hazy sun, f/8 for overcast, f/5.6 for heavy overcast, f/4 for open shade.

Tips

  • Colour negative film forgives over-exposure — when in doubt, give it more light.
  • Slide film and instant film need accurate exposure.
  • Below 1/30 s, use a tripod or brace yourself.

FAQ

What is the Sunny 16 rule?

On a sunny day, set the aperture to f/16 and the shutter speed to the reciprocal of your film speed — e.g. 1/100 s for ISO 100 film.

Do I need a light meter for film?

Many vintage cameras have a built-in meter. If yours doesn't (or the meter is dead), this calculator, the Sunny 16 rule or a phone light-meter app will get you close; a handheld meter is the most accurate.
