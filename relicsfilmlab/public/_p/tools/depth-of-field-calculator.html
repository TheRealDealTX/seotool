Depth of field, explained

Depth of field is the zone in front of and behind your subject that looks acceptably sharp. It gets shallower with a wider aperture (smaller f-number), a longer lens and a closer subject — and deeper with the opposite.

Hyperfocal focusing

Focus at the hyperfocal distance and everything from half that distance to infinity is sharp — the classic street-photography trick for zone-focus cameras like the Olympus XA2 and Trip 35.

FAQ

What aperture gives the most depth of field?

The smallest aperture (largest f-number), such as f/16 or f/22, combined with a wide-angle lens.

Why does medium format have shallower depth of field?

For the same framing you use a longer lens, which gives less depth of field at the same aperture.
