Why we rate the Yashica Mat-124 G

  • Waist-level viewing on a big, bright ground glass slows you down in the best way.
  • A big 120 negative delivers smooth tones and detail no 35mm camera can match.
  • Simple controls — there is very little to go wrong and very little to learn.

It is a great fit for portrait, landscape and fine-art photographers who want the most out of every frame.

About Yashica

Yashica built affordable, well-made cameras for everyone — from the Electro 35 rangefinders with their fast f/1.7 lenses to the Mat-124 G twin-lens reflex.

Buying a used Yashica Mat-124 G: what to check

Most listings on Amazon come from independent sellers, and condition varies. Read the description and seller rating, and when it arrives:

  1. Check light seals and film-back light traps — medium-format backs often need new seals.
  2. Test the leaf shutter at all speeds and check that the lens and body couple correctly.
  3. Look for haze in the taking and viewing lenses (TLRs) and a bright, clean focusing screen.
  4. Confirm which backs, inserts and prisms are included — they are often sold separately.

Film & batteries

It takes 120 roll film, the medium-format standard still made by Kodak, Ilford and Fujifilm. It uses a PX625-type mercury cell (use an MR9 adapter or a modern 1.35V zinc-air substitute).

FAQ

What film does the Yashica Mat-124 G take?

It takes 120 roll film, the medium-format standard still made by Kodak, Ilford and Fujifilm.

Does the Yashica Mat-124 G need batteries?

Yes, it uses a PX625-type mercury cell (use an MR9 adapter or a modern 1.35V zinc-air substitute).

Is the Yashica Mat-124 G good for beginners?

It's a rewarding step up once you understand exposure. Medium format slows you down and rewards careful composition.
