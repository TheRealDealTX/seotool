About Polaroid

Polaroid invented instant photography. Classic cameras fall into two families: SX-70-type cameras such as the OneStep Land Camera and Pronto, and 600-type box cameras such as the Sun 600, Impulse and Spirit. New film for both is made today.

Buying a used Polaroid 252 BV: what to check

Most listings on Amazon come from independent sellers, and condition varies. Read the description and seller rating, and when it arrives:

  1. Check that the rollers are clean and turn freely — dirty rollers cause streaks and spots.
  2. Look for cracks or tears in the bellows on folding cameras.
  3. For 600-type cameras the battery lives in the film pack, so test with a fresh pack.
  4. Make sure the film door latches firmly and the ejection slot is clear.

Film & batteries

It uses Polaroid instant film. 600-type cameras take Polaroid 600 film; SX-70-type cameras take Polaroid SX-70 film (or 600 film with an ND filter). Instax cameras take Fujifilm Instax film. No separate battery is needed: Polaroid 600 film packs contain the battery that powers the camera.

FAQ

What film does the Polaroid 252 BV take?

It uses Polaroid instant film. 600-type cameras take Polaroid 600 film; SX-70-type cameras take Polaroid SX-70 film (or 600 film with an ND filter). Instax cameras take Fujifilm Instax film.

Does the Polaroid 252 BV need batteries?

No — Polaroid 600 film packs contain their own battery.

Is the Polaroid 252 BV good for beginners?

Yes — instant cameras are about as simple as photography gets. Keep fresh film out of the heat and shield prints from light as they develop.

Where can I get my film developed?

Any lab that processes the format will do — see our guide to where to develop film, or learn to develop film at home.
