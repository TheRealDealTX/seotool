Develop Only (Film Developing)

Film developing guide

Develop only means processing your film into negatives without scans or prints. You can send rolls to a lab — or do it yourself at home with a tank, a changing bag and a few bottles of chemistry. Here is how, including 110 and 127 film.

AdviceWritten by film shooters

Develop-only at a lab

Most labs offer a develop-only price for C-41 colour, black & white and E-6 slide film. You get your negatives back to scan yourself or print in a darkroom. It is the cheapest option if you own a scanner.

Develop at home in 7 steps

  1. Load the film onto a reel inside a changing bag.
  2. Pour in developer at the right temperature (20 °C for most B&W developers).
  3. Agitate as the developer's chart says.
  4. Stop bath (or a water rinse).
  5. Fixer — after this step the film is safe in light.
  6. Wash, then a drop of wetting agent.
  7. Hang to dry in a dust-free room.

Colour (C-41) is just as doable with a kit — it simply needs warmer, carefully held temperatures. See our full guide: how to develop film at home.

110, 127 and odd formats

Can you develop 110 film or 127 film? Yes. 127 loads on adjustable reels set to its width, and 110 fits the 16mm setting on many reels. If you would rather a lab handle it, read where to develop 110, 127 and old film.

FAQ

What does develop only mean?

The lab processes your film into negatives but does not scan or print them. You get the negatives back.

Where can you develop 110 film?

Many mail-in labs still process 110 C-41 film; ask whether they can scan it, as 110 needs special holders. You can also develop it yourself on an adjustable reel.

Can I develop 127 film at home?

Yes — 127 film fits adjustable plastic reels and develops exactly like 120 black & white or C-41.

Is developing film at home cheaper?

For black & white, very much so: a starter kit pays for itself in roughly 10 rolls.
