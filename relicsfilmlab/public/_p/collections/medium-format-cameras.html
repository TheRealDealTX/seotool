Medium format film cameras

Medium format film cameras use 120 roll film, with a negative up to four times bigger than 35mm. The result is smoother tones, finer grain and that unmistakable depth. These are the 120 format cameras worth your money, from cheap TLRs to Hasselblad.

Cheap medium format film cameras

The most affordable route into 120 film is a twin-lens reflex such as the Yashica Mat-124 G, or a Soviet SLR like the Kiev 6C. Studio favourites like the Mamiya RB67 and Hasselblad 500C/M cost more but are fully modular.

Negative sizes

  • 6×4.5 — 15–16 frames per roll
  • 6×6 — 12 frames
  • 6×7 — 10 frames

FAQ

What film does a medium format camera use?

120 roll film (some older cameras use 220 or 620).

Is medium format better than 35mm?

The larger negative gives more detail and smoother tones, at the cost of fewer frames per roll and bigger cameras.
