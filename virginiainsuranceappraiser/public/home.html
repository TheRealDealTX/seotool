The short version

What a Virginia Insurance Appraiser does, and doesn’t do

Most short claims aren’t denials. They’re estimates that missed something: two slopes instead of five, plaster priced as drywall, a floor that can’t be matched, a crane left off a tree job. Virginia Insurance Appraiser works on that gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

  1. We value the loss from scratch. Our own inspection and measurements, never an edited copy of the insurer’s estimate.
  2. We build numbers that survive scrutiny. An inflated estimate loses credibility with an umpire. A documented one moves the award.
  3. We tell you when appraisal is the wrong tool. Denials, coverage fights and looming suit deadlines belong with a Virginia attorney.

Honest triage

When to call a Virginia Insurance Appraiser, and when not to

Appraisal usually fits

  • Coverage is accepted, but the amount is disputed
  • Repair-versus-replace disagreements on roofs and siding
  • Matching problems with discontinued materials
  • Missing trades, code items, crane work or overhead and profit

Appraisal usually doesn’t

  • The claim was denied outright
  • The dispute is about what the policy covers
  • A suit deadline is close and needs an attorney
  • The gap is too small to justify the fees

The process

Seven steps from short check to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined procedure. Here is how Virginia Insurance Appraiser moves a file from the first call to a binding number.

  1. 1

    Spot the disagreement

    Coverage is accepted, but the insurer’s figure and the real repair cost are far apart.

  2. 2

    Build the file

    Gather the policy, the full estimate, photos, a contractor’s estimate and the correspondence.

  3. 3

    Make the written demand

    Either side demands appraisal in writing, citing the policy’s appraisal condition.

  4. 4

    Name the appraisers

    Each side names a competent, disinterested appraiser, often within 20 days.

  5. 5

    Select an umpire

    The two appraisers agree on a neutral umpire, or a court can be asked to appoint one.

  6. 6

    Work the differences

    The appraisers compare scopes and prices and resolve as many items as they can.

  7. 7

    Sign the award

    Any two of the three sign. The award sets the amount of loss, and payment follows under the policy.

Virginia ground rules

Four facts every Virginia policyholder should know

A Virginia Insurance Appraiser isn’t a lawyer, but these basics shape every claim we see. They’re general information, not legal advice.

Regulator

Bureau of Insurance

Part of the State Corporation Commission. It licenses insurers, agents and public adjusters and takes consumer complaints.

Market of last resort

VPIA, the FAIR Plan

The Virginia Property Insurance Association offers basic property coverage to owners who can’t find it in the regular market.

Bad-faith remedy

Va. Code § 38.2-209

Lets a court award costs and attorney’s fees in certain cases where an insurer refused to pay in bad faith. That’s a legal question, outside appraisal.

Deadline

Commonly two years

Virginia’s standard fire policy provisions commonly allow two years from the loss to file suit. Your policy controls, and appraisal may not pause it.

FAQ

Questions people ask a Virginia Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All appraisal questions Claim & policy terms
What does a Virginia Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree about what it costs. As your named appraiser, we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and present open items to a neutral umpire if needed.

How is that different from a public adjuster?

Public adjusters are licensed by the Virginia Bureau of Insurance to handle and negotiate whole claims. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When is it worth calling a Virginia Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage has been accepted, you have the insurer’s estimate, and the gap between it and a qualified contractor’s number is meaningful, often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is an appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, yes, once two of the three panel members sign. That’s why we build the file carefully before anyone demands appraisal.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire’s fee. We put our fee in writing before any work begins, and the first review is free.

Which parts of Virginia do you serve?

All of it: 95 counties and 38 independent cities, with regular work in Hampton Roads, Northern Virginia, Richmond, Charlottesville, Roanoke and Lynchburg.
