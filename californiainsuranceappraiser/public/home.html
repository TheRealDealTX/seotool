The short version

What a California Insurance Appraiser is hired to do

Most short claims in California aren’t denied. They’re scoped narrowly. A tile roof priced as a spot repair, a smoke-filled house priced as a wipe-down, a hillside rebuild priced like a flat lot. California Insurance Appraiser exists for the space between “covered” and “paid in full.”

When you hire us, we become your appraiser under the policy’s appraisal clause, the process set out in the standard fire policy in Insurance Code §2071. We build the number from our own inspection, defend it line by line with the carrier’s appraiser, and present what remains to a neutral umpire.

Good fit

  • Coverage accepted, amount disputed
  • Repair-versus-replace disagreements
  • Rebuild cost below your real cost
  • Smoke scopes that stop at cleaning

Not a fit

  • Full coverage denials
  • Bad-faith or legal claims
  • Small gaps that fees would erase
  • Deadlines that need a lawyer first

After a wildfire, the rebuild estimate is the claim

A California Insurance Appraiser can establish the full cost of a comparable rebuild under current code, which decides whether you reach extended replacement cost and code upgrade coverage.

The path

Five stages from disputed estimate to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process. Here is how California Insurance Appraiser runs a file.

  1. 1

    Free file review

    We read the policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos, and tell you whether appraisal makes sense.

  2. 2

    Independent estimate

    We inspect, measure and build our own line-item valuation, priced for your part of California.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and California Insurance Appraiser is named as your appraiser within 20 days.

  4. 4

    Appraiser-to-appraiser

    We work through every disputed item with the carrier’s appraiser. Most gaps narrow here.

  5. 5

    Signed award

    Remaining items go to the umpire. Any two signatures on an itemized award set the amount.

Questions

What owners ask a California Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

Every question Claim terms
What does a California Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on the cost. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster manages and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and don’t give legal advice.

When should I call a California Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s estimate, and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful, generally more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Can appraisal help after a wildfire?

Yes, when the carrier accepts the loss but its rebuild, smoke or contents figure is too low. Appraisal cannot raise policy limits, but it can establish the full cost of the loss.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and shares the umpire’s fee. We put our fee in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my county?

Yes. California Insurance Appraiser works in all 58 counties, from Del Norte to Imperial.
