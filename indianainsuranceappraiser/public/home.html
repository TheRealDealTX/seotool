Anatomy of a short claim

Where an Indiana Insurance Appraiser finds the missing money

Most underpaid claims aren’t denied. They’re short: two roof slopes instead of four, gutters left off, a siding elevation that can’t be matched, no overhead and profit. Indiana Insurance Appraiser rebuilds the scope from your property, not from the carrier’s estimate, so the missing lines show up.

The chart is an illustrative hail claim on a central Indiana two-story. The carrier’s estimate totals $13,100. An independent line-item scope for the same storm comes to $33,830. That $20,730 difference is exactly the kind of gap the appraisal clause exists to resolve.

Five steps

From stuck claim to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here is how an Indiana Insurance Appraiser file moves from the first call to the award.

  1. 1

    Free review

    We read your policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos and tell you plainly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 2

    Independent scope

    Our own inspection, measurements and a line-item estimate priced for your Indiana ZIP code.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and Indiana Insurance Appraiser is named as your appraiser on time.

  4. 4

    Panel negotiation

    We work line by line with the carrier’s appraiser. Most of the gap is settled here.

  5. 5

    Signed award

    Anything left goes to a neutral umpire. Two of three signatures set the amount of loss.

Four claim zones

From the snow belt to the river towns: Indiana’s claim zones

An Indiana Insurance Appraiser has to know how each part of the state’s weather shows up on a roof and in an estimate. Pick a zone to see what we see there.

Lake Michigan snow belt

  • Lake-effect snow load
  • Ice dams
  • Frozen pipes
  • Wind off the lake

Lake County, Porter, LaPorte, St. Joseph and Elkhart counties get Indiana’s heaviest snow. Low-slope additions, porch roofs and pole barns take the load, and ice dams send meltwater into ceilings every winter.

City pages: Hammond, South Bend

Central hail corridor

  • Hail
  • Straight-line wind
  • Tornadoes
  • Basement water

Storms crossing from the west regularly drop hail on Marion and Hamilton counties. Fast-growing suburbs share discontinued shingle and siding lines, so matching disputes follow nearly every big storm.

City pages: Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, Kokomo

Wabash Valley & farm country

  • Tornadoes
  • Derechos
  • Farm buildings
  • River flooding

Open farmland lets storms build fast. Pole barns, grain bins and farmhouses in Tippecanoe, Vigo, Allen and the counties between them see wind and hail losses that need structure-by-structure valuation.

City pages: Lafayette, Terre Haute, Fort Wayne

Southern hills & Ohio River

  • Ice storms
  • Tornadoes
  • Tree damage
  • Karst ground

Wooded hills, limestone country and river towns. Ice storms bring limbs down on roofs, tornadoes like Henryville’s in 2012 cut long paths, and historic brick and stone homes need specialized pricing.

City pages: Evansville, Bloomington, Jeffersonville

Storm almanac

The storms that shaped Indiana claims

Every Indiana Insurance Appraiser file sits against this history. These events changed building codes, floodwalls and how carriers look at wind, water and ice in the state.

  1. 1937

    Ohio River flood

    Record flooding swamped Evansville, Jeffersonville and river towns, leading to today’s floodwalls.

  2. 1965

    Palm Sunday outbreak

    April 11 tornadoes struck northern and central Indiana, the deadliest tornado day in state history.

  3. 1974

    Super Outbreak

    On April 3, tornadoes hit communities across Indiana, including Monticello and Madison.

  4. 2005

    Evansville tornado

    A pre-dawn November 6 tornado tore through Vanderburgh and Warrick counties.

  5. 2008

    Floods and Ike’s winds

    June floods hit central and southern Indiana; in September, Ike’s remnants brought widespread wind damage.

  6. 2009

    Southern ice storm

    January freezing rain downed trees and power lines across southern Indiana.

  7. 2012

    Henryville and the derecho

    A March 2 tornado devastated Henryville; a June 29 derecho swept the state.

  8. 2023

    Sullivan tornado

    A March 31 tornado struck Sullivan in the Wabash Valley.

Straight answers

Is appraisal the right tool for your claim?

An Indiana Insurance Appraiser is the right call for some problems and the wrong one for others. We’ll tell you which on the first call.

Appraisal usually fits

  • Coverage accepted, cost disputed
  • Partial vs. full roof or siding replacement
  • Matching and discontinued materials
  • Missing trades, code items and overhead and profit
  • Snow-load, ice dam and wind scope disagreements

Call someone else

  • The claim was denied outright: see an Indiana attorney
  • Bad-faith or conduct claims: courts decide these
  • A suit deadline is days away: attorney first
  • Gaps too small to cover appraisal fees
  • River flooding under a homeowners policy: usually excluded

FAQ

Questions for an Indiana Insurance Appraiser

How appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All FAQs Glossary
What does an Indiana Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your named appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, negotiate with the carrier’s appraiser and present unresolved items to the umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside your policy’s appraisal clause on one question, the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When should I call an Indiana Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s estimate, and the gap to your contractor’s number is meaningful, roughly more than 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is the award binding?

Yes, on the amount of loss, once any two of the three panel members sign it. That’s why the file should be complete before appraisal is demanded.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you work outside Indianapolis?

Yes. We cover all 92 Indiana counties, from Lake County to Posey County and from Fort Wayne to Jeffersonville.
