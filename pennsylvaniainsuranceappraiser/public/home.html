What we do

What a Pennsylvania Insurance Appraiser does — and doesn’t

Most underpaid claims in Pennsylvania aren’t denied. They’re short. A slate roof priced as asphalt, a rowhome flat roof patched on paper when it needs replacing, plaster walls written up as drywall, a frozen-pipe loss with half the tear-out missing. Pennsylvania Insurance Appraiser exists for the gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

We value the loss from scratch — our own inspection, measurements and pricing — and we build numbers that hold up in front of an umpire. If your problem is really a coverage denial, a bad-faith question or a looming deadline, we’ll tell you on the first call that you need a Pennsylvania attorney instead.

Appraisal usually fits

  • Coverage accepted, cost disputed
  • Repair vs. replace arguments
  • Matching, slate and specialty materials
  • Missing trades, code items and O&P

It usually doesn’t

  • Outright coverage denials
  • Bad-faith or legal claims
  • Flood losses under a homeowner policy
  • Gaps too small to justify fees

Pennsylvania’s claim year

The four seasons a Pennsylvania Insurance Appraiser plans around

Pennsylvania weather sends a different kind of claim every quarter. Knowing what each season does to a roof, a pipe or a basement is half of writing an accurate estimate.

Winter

Dec – Mar
  • Burst and frozen pipes
  • Ice dams and attic leaks
  • Snow-load collapse
  • Ice storms and fallen limbs

The Blizzard of 1993, the January 1996 blizzard, the February 2010 storms and Erie’s Christmas 2017 lake-effect snow all left roof and water claims behind.

Spring

Apr – Jun
  • Hail and severe storms
  • Tornadoes
  • Flash flooding
  • Roof wind damage

Western Pennsylvania’s May 31, 1985 tornado outbreak and Agnes in June 1972 are the reminders every adjuster in the state knows.

Summer

Jul – Sep
  • Tropical storm remnants
  • Lightning fires
  • Downbursts and trees
  • Water through roofs

Remnants of Ivan (2004), Lee (2011), Ida (2021) and Debby (2024) showed how much rain a fading tropical system can drop inland.

Fall

Oct – Nov
  • Nor’easters and wind
  • Clogged gutters and leaks
  • Early freezes
  • Power-outage losses

Superstorm Sandy in October 2012 brought hours of wind, fallen trees and long outages across the eastern half of the state.

How it works

From first call to signed award with Pennsylvania Insurance Appraiser

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here is how a file moves.

  1. 1

    Free review

    We read the policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos, then tell you plainly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 2

    Our own scope

    A Pennsylvania Insurance Appraiser inspects, measures and writes a line-item estimate priced for your area.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and your appraiser is named, with the suit-limitation date in view.

  4. 4

    Panel work

    Both appraisers compare scope and pricing line by line. Most of the gap is settled here.

  5. 5

    Signed award

    An umpire decides anything left. Two of three signatures set the amount of loss.

FAQ

Questions people ask a Pennsylvania Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers on how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All FAQs Claim glossary
What does a Pennsylvania Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, write an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, take unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is appraisal the same as hiring a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster, licensed by the Pennsylvania Insurance Department, handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When should I call a Pennsylvania Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s estimate, and the gap between it and a contractor’s line-item estimate is meaningful — often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Does appraisal stop my one-year suit deadline?

Not automatically. Many Pennsylvania policies limit lawsuits to one year from the date of loss, and appraisal may not pause that clock unless the policy or a written agreement says so. If the date is close, talk to a Pennsylvania attorney.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my county?

Yes. We work in all 67 Pennsylvania counties, with frequent work around Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, the Lehigh Valley, Harrisburg, Lancaster, Scranton and Erie.
