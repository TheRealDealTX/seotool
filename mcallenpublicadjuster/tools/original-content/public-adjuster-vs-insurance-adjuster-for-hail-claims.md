## Understanding the Two Roles

### What Is an Insurance Adjuster?

An insurance adjuster is assigned by your insurance company after you file a claim. Their responsibility is to assess the damage, interpret your policy, and determine how much the insurer should pay. They follow internal guidelines. They use estimating software. They document what they see during a relatively short inspection window.

And yes they are trained professionals. But here’s the key point: they work for the insurance company. Their job is to settle claims efficiently while protecting the company’s financial exposure. That doesn’t mean they’re unfair. It means their priorities are different from yours.

### What Is a Public Adjuster?

A public adjuster is a licensed professional who works exclusively for you, the policyholder.

They handle the entire claims process from your side:

- Inspecting the damage in detail

- Reviewing your insurance policy

- Preparing a comprehensive estimate

- Submitting documentation

- Negotiating with the insurance company

They’re not tied to the insurer. Their goal is simple get you the full amount you’re entitled to under your policy. And because they typically work on a contingency fee, they’re incentivized to maximize your claim.

## How Each Adjuster Handles a Hail Claim

### Insurance Adjuster Process

The insurance adjuster’s process is streamlined for speed:

- Schedule inspection

- Perform visual assessment

- Create estimate using software

- Issue settlement

It’s efficient. But efficiency often comes at the cost of depth.

### Public Adjuster Process

Public adjusters take a more strategic approach:

- Conduct multi-point inspection

- Document every visible and potential issue

- Review policy coverage in detail

- Create an independent estimate

- Identify missed items

- Submit supplements

- Negotiate actively

They don’t just process your claim. They build leverage into it.

## Inspection Depth and Damage Identification

### What Insurance Adjusters Typically Look For

Insurance adjusters often focus on:

- Visible hail strikes

- Obvious structural damage

- Surface-level roofing issues

Because of high claim volume, inspections are often quick. That’s where gaps begin.

### What Public Adjusters Identify

Public adjusters go deeper. Much deeper.

They analyze:

- Granule displacement on shingles

- Soft impact bruising

- Flashing and seal failures

- Ventilation damage

- Underlayment exposure

- Long-term water intrusion risks

- Code compliance issues

Interestingly, the analytical approach they use can feel almost like a structured system of classification similar to how complex systems are organized in fields like [Ontological classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) breaking down every component to ensure nothing is missed. Because when everything is identified, everything can be claimed.

## The Supplement Process Explained

Here’s where most of the missed money lives. A supplement is an additional claim request for damages not included in the original estimate. And it’s more common than most property owners realize. This stage is often referred to as [Hail Damage Claim Supplements: What Carriers Miss in Their Scope](https://mcallenpublicadjuster.com/hail-damage-claim-supplements/), because it focuses on identifying and correcting incomplete initial estimates.

### Commonly Missed Items in Hail Claims

- Flashing replacement

- Ridge vents

- Ice and water barriers

- Drip edges

- Disposal fees

- Code upgrades

Individually small. Collectively massive.

### Insurance Adjuster Approach to Supplements

Reactive. They review supplements if submitted but rarely initiate them.

### Public Adjuster Approach to Supplements

Proactive and strategic.

They:

- Identify missing line items

- Document additional damage

- Submit detailed supplements

- Justify each request with evidence

That’s where claims evolve from basic to fully compensated.
