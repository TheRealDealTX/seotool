Easy DIY Home Decor Ideas on a Budget (That Actually Look Good)

July 16, 20264 min readBy the Oh, Mother Mine DIY team
DifficultyEasy
Time30 minutes–1 day per project
Cost$0–$50

Our homes work hard. They hold muddy shoes, school projects, and a lot of living, so it's no wonder they start to feel tired. The good news is that the best DIY home decor ideas on a budget don't require a renovation. A can of paint, a thrift store trip, and a free afternoon can make a room feel brand new.

Here are the ideas we come back to again and again. Most can be finished in a nap time or a weekend, and many cost next to nothing. If you want a starting point that fits your schedule, our project finder will suggest one.

Start with What You Already Have

Before you spend a dime, shop your own home. Moving things around is free and often makes the biggest difference.

  1. Rearrange the furniture. Pull the sofa off the wall, angle a chair toward the window, or create a cozy reading corner.
  2. Edit your surfaces. Clear everything off a shelf or mantel, then add back only your favorite pieces in small groupings of three.
  3. Swap decor between rooms. A lamp, mirror, or throw from the bedroom can feel new in the living room.
  4. Rehang art at eye level. The center of a frame should sit roughly 57 to 60 inches from the floor, a common gallery guideline.

Paint: The Best of All DIY Home Decor Ideas on a Budget

Nothing transforms a space for less money than paint. You don't have to paint a whole room to see results.

  • Paint an accent wall or the back of a bookshelf in a deeper color.
  • Refresh a thrifted dresser or side table. Our guide on how to paint furniture with chalk paint walks you through it.
  • Update picture frames so mismatched thrift frames look like a set.
  • Paint interior doors a soft black, navy, or sage for an instant custom look.

If you're unsure about colors, read how to choose paint colors before you buy, and use the paint calculator so you only purchase what you need.

Thrift and Flip

Thrift stores, yard sales, and online marketplaces are full of solid pieces that just need a little love. Look past the color and focus on shape and sturdiness.

  • Brass candlesticks, ceramic vases, and wooden bowls that can be cleaned or painted
  • Solid wood frames for gallery walls
  • Baskets for blanket and toy storage
  • Lamps that only need a new shade
Mom tip: Keep a note on your phone with the measurements of the spaces you're decorating, like the wall above the sofa or the width of the entryway. You'll know right away whether that thrift store find will fit.

Bring in Greenery and Natural Texture

Plants make any space feel fresh and lived in. Pothos, snake plants, and spider plants are forgiving for busy households, though check the toxicity of any plant if you have pets or curious toddlers. Group pots of different heights on a shelf or windowsill.

Natural textures like wood, woven baskets, linen, and clay add warmth without adding clutter. Even fallen branches in a tall vase or a bowl of pinecones can look styled and intentional. Need new pots? Our terracotta pot makeover turns plain clay pots into custom planters.

Soft Goods and Lighting

Textiles and light shape how a room feels, and both are easy to change.

  1. Swap pillow covers instead of buying new pillows. Covers are cheaper and easy to store by season.
  2. Layer a throw blanket over a chair or the end of the bed.
  3. Hang curtains high and wide, close to the ceiling and a few inches past the window frame, to make windows look larger.
  4. Use warm light bulbs around 2700K in living spaces for a cozy glow.
  5. Add a lamp to dark corners so the room isn't lit only from overhead.

Budget-Friendly Wall Decor

Empty walls can make a room feel unfinished, but filling them doesn't have to be expensive. A few ideas that cost little or nothing:

  • Frame free printables or pages from an old calendar, botanical book, or children's book in thrifted frames.
  • Hang a textile. A vintage quilt, a pretty tea towel on a wooden dowel, or a woven rug can work as wall art.
  • Lean instead of hang. Layer frames and mirrors on a picture ledge or mantel so you can swap them easily without extra nail holes.
  • Use a large mirror across from a window to bounce light around a dark room.
  • Create a gallery wall by tracing each frame on kraft paper, taping the paper to the wall, and adjusting the layout before you hammer a single nail.

Keep spacing consistent, about 2 to 3 inches between frames, and the arrangement will look polished even if the frames don't match.

Small DIY Projects with Big Impact

Once the basics are in place, a few handmade touches make your home feel personal.

  • Make a seasonal wreath for the front door, like our DIY farmhouse wreath.
  • Pour simple candles in thrifted jars or teacups.
  • Frame your kids' artwork in matching frames for a rotating gallery.
  • Create a simple fabric or paper garland for a mantel or doorway.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to update a room?

Rearranging furniture and editing your decor is free. After that, paint is usually the least expensive way to make a big visual change.

How can I make my home look more expensive on a budget?

Hang curtains high and wide, use warm lighting, group decor in odd numbers, and choose a cohesive color palette throughout the space.

Where can I find cheap home decor?

Thrift stores, yard sales, online marketplaces, and dollar stores are great sources, especially for frames, vases, baskets, and lamps you can update yourself.
