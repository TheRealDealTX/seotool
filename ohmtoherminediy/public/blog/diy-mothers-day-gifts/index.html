Heartfelt DIY Mother's Day Gifts Kids Can Actually Make

June 25, 20264 min readBy the Oh, Mother Mine DIY team
DifficultyEasy
Time30 minutes–2 hours
Cost$5–$25

What you'll need

  • Washable, non-toxic kids' paint
  • Cardstock or a blank canvas
  • Granulated sugar and a light oil (such as coconut or sweet almond)
  • Small glass jars with lids
  • Terracotta pots and potting soil
  • Herb or flower seedlings
  • Ribbon, twine and gift tags
  • Paper, markers and a stapler

Tap items to check them off — we'll remember your list on this device.

Ask almost any mom what she treasures most and it is rarely something from a store. It is the crooked handprint, the card with backward letters and the little jar that someone small wrapped with way too much tape. These DIY Mother's Day gifts are easy enough for kids to make with a little help, affordable for any budget and full of the kind of love that lasts.

Whether you are a dad helping the kids surprise Mom, a grandparent planning a crafty afternoon or a mom making something for your own mother, you will find ideas here for toddlers through teens.

Choosing DIY Mother's Day Gifts by Age

The best gift is one the child can mostly make on their own. That way, it really is from them.

  • Toddlers (1–3): Handprint and footprint art, sticker cards and finger-painted wrapping paper.
  • Preschool and early elementary: Painted flower pots, coupon books and decorated picture frames.
  • Older kids and teens: Sugar scrub, simple sewn pouches and hand-poured candles with adult help.
Mom tip: Start a few days early. Paint and glue need drying time, and nobody wants to be blowing on a wet card at 7 a.m. on Mother's Day.

Handprint Keepsake Art

Handprints capture a moment you can never get back. Frame them, and they become the gift Mom pulls out for years.

  1. Brush washable paint onto your child's palm and fingers with a foam brush. It tickles, which is half the fun.
  2. Press the hand firmly onto cardstock or a small canvas, then lift straight up.
  3. Turn the prints into flowers by adding stems and leaves, or make a bouquet with a print from each sibling.
  4. Write the child's name, age and year in the corner once it dries.
  5. Pop it in an inexpensive frame or roll it up with a ribbon.

Homemade Sugar Scrub in a Jar

Sugar scrub feels like a spa treat, and kids love measuring and stirring. It takes about ten minutes.

  1. Add 1 cup of granulated sugar to a bowl.
  2. Pour in about 1/2 cup of a light oil like coconut, sweet almond or a mild olive oil. Stir until it looks like wet sand. Add a bit more sugar if it seems too oily.
  3. If you like, mix in a little lemon zest or a few drops of skin-safe essential oil. Skip scents for anyone with sensitive skin.
  4. Spoon into a clean, dry jar and close the lid.
  5. Add a tag: "Use a small scoop on damp skin, rinse well. Use within a few weeks."

Two quick notes: keep water out of the jar so it stays fresh, and remind Mom that oil can make the tub or shower floor slippery. Avoid nut oils if anyone in the family has nut allergies.

Painted Planter with Herbs or Flowers

A painted pot with a living plant is a gift that keeps growing. Basil, mint, marigolds and pansies are cheerful and easy to care for.

  1. Wipe a terracotta pot clean and let it dry completely.
  2. Let kids paint stripes, polka dots, handprints or "Happy Mother's Day" with acrylic craft paint.
  3. Once dry, an adult can add a coat of clear sealer for durability.
  4. Fill with potting soil, plant the seedling and water gently.

Coupon Books and Handmade Cards

Coupon books cost almost nothing and might be the most used gift of all. Fold a few sheets of paper in half, staple the spine and let kids fill each page with a promise:

  • "One breakfast in bed"
  • "I will fold the laundry without being asked"
  • "Ten extra hugs"
  • "One quiet hour while I play nicely"

Pair it with a homemade card. Ask younger kids questions like "What is Mom's favorite thing to do?" and write down their answers word for word. Those little quotes become instant family treasures.

Gifts for Older Kids and Teens

Tweens and teens can take on projects with a few more steps:

  • Soy candles: With an adult nearby for the hot wax, teens can follow our guide on how to make soy candles at home.
  • A sewn zipper pouch or tote: Check out our beginner sewing projects for easy first makes.
  • A memory jar: Fill a jar with folded notes of favorite memories. Find more inspiration in our mason jar crafts roundup.
Mom tip: Wrap gifts in kids' artwork or plain kraft paper they have stamped with potato prints. It saves money and makes the outside just as special as the inside.

Memory and Photo Gifts

Some of the most meaningful gifts cost almost nothing but a little time. Try one of these:

  • "All About Mom" interview: Ask kids a list of questions, such as how old Mom is, what she cooks best and what makes her laugh, and write their answers down exactly. The results are usually hilarious and sweet.
  • Decorated photo frame: Glue buttons, painted pasta shapes or pressed flowers around a simple wooden frame and add a favorite family photo.
  • Pressed flower bookmark: Press small flowers between book pages for a week or two, then arrange them on cardstock and cover with clear contact paper.

A handmade gift is even sweeter with a little ceremony. Let the kids set the table with fresh flowers from the yard, make simple toast or pancakes with help and present their gifts together. Frequently asked questions

What can a toddler make for Mother's Day?

Handprint or footprint art, sticker cards and finger-painted wrapping paper are great for toddlers. Use washable, non-toxic paint and supervise closely.

How long does homemade sugar scrub last?

A simple sugar and oil scrub usually keeps well for a few weeks if water stays out of the jar. Make small batches and discard it if it smells off.

What is a good last-minute DIY Mother's Day gift?

A coupon book or a handmade card with your child's own words takes just minutes and uses supplies you already have. They are often the most cherished gifts.
