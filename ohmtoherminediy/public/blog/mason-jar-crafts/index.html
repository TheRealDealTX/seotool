Easy Mason Jar Crafts for Gifts, Decor and Everyday Life

July 9, 20264 min readBy the Oh, Mother Mine DIY team
DifficultyEasy
Time30 minutes–2 hours
Cost$10–$25

What you'll need

  • Clean mason jars in assorted sizes
  • Acrylic or chalk-style paint
  • Foam brushes and fine sandpaper
  • Jute twine, ribbon and fabric scraps
  • Battery-operated tea lights or fairy lights
  • Rubbing alcohol and a lint-free cloth
  • Labels or gift tags
  • Clear matte sealer

If there is one craft supply that has earned a permanent spot in our cabinets, it is the humble glass jar. Mason jar crafts are inexpensive, endlessly flexible and easy enough to make during nap time. With a little paint, some twine and a few minutes, a plain jar becomes a vase, a lantern, a thoughtful gift or a tidy home for cotton swabs.

Best of all, you can often find jars for very little at thrift stores and yard sales, or simply save and wash the ones from your pantry. Here are our favorite ways to use them around the house and for gifting.

Prepping Jars for Mason Jar Crafts

Good prep makes paint and labels stick and helps your finished jar look polished.

  1. Soak jars in warm, soapy water to loosen old labels, then scrub off any residue. A little cooking oil on a cloth helps lift sticky glue.
  2. Dry completely.
  3. Wipe the outside with rubbing alcohol on a lint-free cloth to remove fingerprints and oils before painting.
  4. Check each jar for chips or cracks and set damaged ones aside for non-food uses or recycling.
Mom tip: Keep a box in the pantry for "craft jars." Every time you finish a jar of jam or pasta sauce, wash it and toss it in. You will have a full set before you know it.

Painted and Distressed Jar Vases

Painted jars with a lightly sanded finish give that cozy farmhouse look for just a few dollars.

  1. Brush on a thin coat of chalk-style or acrylic paint with a foam brush. Thin coats dry faster and look smoother.
  2. Let it dry, then add a second coat for full coverage.
  3. Once fully dry, gently rub the raised glass details and rim with fine sandpaper so a bit of glass shows through.
  4. Finish with a clear matte sealer, applied outdoors or in a well-ventilated space.
  5. Tie twine around the neck and add fresh flowers, dried stems or greenery.

Want to mix your own chalk-style paint? Our DIY chalk paint recipe and the chalk paint mixer tool will help you get the ratio right.

Painted jars are best for faux or dried flowers, or you can place a smaller glass inside to hold water. Painted exteriors should be hand-washed only.

Mason Jar Lanterns and Luminaries

Jar lanterns add a warm glow to porches, patios and holiday tables.

  • Fairy light jars: Drop a strand of battery-powered fairy lights into a clear jar. That is it.
  • Frosted luminaries: Paint the inside of the jar with a thin coat of white paint or tissue paper and glue for a soft, frosted look.
  • Hanging lanterns: Wrap wire tightly under the jar rim and form a handle. Hang from a sturdy hook or tree branch.

We strongly recommend battery tea lights, especially around kids, pets, paper or dried flowers. Regular canning jars are not designed to take direct, repeated flame heat, so treat real candles with extra care and never leave them unattended.

Gift-in-a-Jar Ideas

Jars make adorable, budget-friendly gift packaging. A few favorites:

  • Cookie or pancake mix: Layer the dry ingredients from a favorite family recipe in a quart jar. Pack each layer down firmly. Attach a tag listing the wet ingredients to add, plus the baking temperature and time.
  • Hot cocoa kit: Layer cocoa mix, mini marshmallows and crushed peppermint.
  • Spa jar: Fill with a homemade sugar scrub, a washcloth and a little note. See our DIY Mother's Day gifts for an easy scrub recipe.
  • Candle jar: Pour your own candle right into a sturdy jar. Our post on how to make soy candles at home walks through the full process.

Top the lid with a circle of fabric or kraft paper tied with twine for a finished look. Always label food gifts with ingredients so anyone with allergies can check.

Mason Jars for Everyday Storage

Jars are not just pretty. They are useful for corralling the little stuff that clutters a busy family home.

  • Bathroom: Cotton balls, swabs, hair ties and bath salts.
  • Craft room: Buttons, washi tape, beads and paintbrushes.
  • Kitchen: Dry goods like rice, beans and oats, labeled with the contents and date.
  • Desk: Pens, markers and scissors (points down).

For more ways to bring order to chaos, check out our DIY organization ideas for busy moms.

Mom tip: Glass can break, so store jars of small items like beads and buttons on higher shelves, out of reach of toddlers, and use plastic containers in kids' rooms.

Mason Jar Crafts With Kids

Kids love making jar projects, and a few simple adjustments keep things safe and fun. Choose smaller, thicker jars that are easier for little hands to hold, and set them on a towel so they cannot roll off the table. Let kids paint the outside with washable paint, fill jars with layers of colored sand or rice, or turn a jar into a "calm down" jar filled with water, clear glue and glitter. An adult should seal the lid tightly with glue so it cannot be opened.

  • Swap glass for clear plastic jars with toddlers and preschoolers.
  • Handle any cracked or chipped jars yourself and keep them out of the craft bin.
  • Sealers and spray finishes are an adult job, done outdoors.

Seasonal Jar Ideas

Once you start, you will see jar projects for every season. Fill clear jars with mini pumpkins and leaves in fall, paint them white with a stenciled snowflake for winter, or plant small succulents or herbs in spring. A simple palette keeps everything looking cohesive, and our color palette generator can help you choose colors that match your space.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of paint works best on mason jars?

Acrylic craft paint and chalk-style paint both work well on clean glass. Finish with a clear sealer for durability and hand-wash only.

Can you put candles in mason jars?

Battery tea lights are the safest choice. Canning jars are not designed for repeated direct flame heat, so use real candles with extra caution and never leave them unattended.

Are painted mason jars dishwasher safe?

No, painted jars should be hand-washed gently on the outside only. The dishwasher can cause craft paint to peel.
