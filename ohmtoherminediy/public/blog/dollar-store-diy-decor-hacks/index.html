Dollar Store DIY Decor Hacks That Don't Look Cheap

July 30, 20264 min readBy the Oh, Mother Mine DIY team
DifficultyEasy
Time20 minutes–2 hours
Cost$5–$25

What you'll need

  • Glass vases, jars, and candle holders
  • Picture frames
  • Woven or plastic baskets
  • Acrylic or chalk-style craft paint
  • Baking soda (for a textured pottery look)
  • Jute twine or rope
  • Hot glue gun and glue sticks
  • Spray paint in matte or metallic finishes
  • Foam brushes

Tap items to check them off — we'll remember your list on this device.

We love a good dollar store run. Between the glass vases, frames, baskets, and seasonal aisles, there's a surprising amount of potential tucked onto those shelves. With a little paint and creativity, dollar store DIY decor can look like it came from a boutique, and it costs just a few dollars per project.

These are our favorite hacks for busy weeks. Most take under an hour, and several are fun to do with older kids. Prices and stock vary by store and season, so treat this list as inspiration and grab what's available near you.

Faux Pottery Vases from Glass

Plain glass vases are some of the easiest dollar store finds to transform. A little baking soda mixed into paint gives them a textured, stoneware look.

  1. Wash and dry the vase, then wipe it with rubbing alcohol so paint grips better.
  2. Mix about 1 tablespoon of baking soda into 1/4 cup of acrylic or latex paint.
  3. Dab the paint on with a foam brush or sponge rather than brushing, which adds texture.
  4. Let it dry, then add a second coat.
  5. For an aged look, lightly sand the high spots or dry-brush a little white paint over the top.

Painted vases work best for dried or faux stems. The paint isn't waterproof inside, so keep real flowers in a jar or water tube tucked inside.

Mom tip: Paint a set of three vases in coordinating neutrals and different heights. Grouped together on a shelf or table, they look intentional and styled instead of random.

Dollar Store DIY Decor with Picture Frames

Frames are endlessly useful, and a coat of paint makes mismatched ones look like a collection.

  • Gallery wall: spray paint a mix of frame styles in one color, like matte black or warm brass, for an instant cohesive look.
  • Dry-erase board: put pretty scrapbook paper behind the glass for a family menu or chore chart.
  • Pressed flower art: sandwich pressed leaves or flowers between two pieces of glass from double frames.
  • Kids' art display: frame children's drawings and swap them out each month.

Spray paint outdoors on a calm day, in thin, even coats, and let each coat dry according to the can's directions.

Baskets and Storage Upgrades

Plastic bins and simple baskets can look custom with a few natural touches.

  1. Rope-wrapped bins: starting at the bottom, wrap jute twine or rope around a plastic bin, adding small dots of hot glue every few inches.
  2. Label it: add chalkboard labels or clip-on tags so everyone knows where things go.
  3. Line it: use a fabric remnant or tea towel as a liner for a soft, finished look.

Rope bins are especially handy in kids' rooms and entryways. For more ideas, see our DIY organization ideas for busy moms.

Candle Holders and Lanterns

Candle holders are a great way to add warmth to a room. Spray paint glass candlesticks in a metallic or matte finish, or glue a wooden bead garland around a glass hurricane for a farmhouse feel.

  • Fill a lantern with battery-operated candles and faux greenery for a mantel or porch.
  • Stack wooden beads on a dowel to create a rustic taper holder.
  • Use flameless candles in anything painted, glued, or wrapped with twine or ribbon, since those materials can be flammable.

Want to fill your holders with homemade candles? Our tutorial on how to make soy candles at home shows you how, using safe containers made for candles.

Seasonal and Wall Decor

The seasonal aisle is a goldmine, especially when you skip the brightly colored plastic and focus on shapes you can paint.

  • Spray paint faux pumpkins in neutral creams and sage green for fall.
  • Combine a few greenery picks into a fuller stem bundle for a vase.
  • Make a simple wreath on a foam or wire form; our DIY farmhouse wreath tutorial shows the technique.
  • Hang a row of small mirrors from ribbon for a light-reflecting wall display.

Safety and Clean-Up Tips

A little planning keeps craft time fun and stress free, especially with kids around.

  • Spray paint outdoors only, and keep children and pets well away until the items are dry.
  • Use a low-temperature glue gun when older kids are helping, and keep a bowl of cool water nearby for quick finger dips.
  • Protect your table with a drop cloth, old shower curtain, or flattened cardboard box.
  • Check labels. Decorative painted items aren't meant for food or drink, so keep painted glasses and jars for display only.
  • Let pieces cure. Paint may feel dry within an hour, but give it a day or two before handling heavily or styling.

Keep a small bin stocked with your go-to supplies so the next project is ready to start in minutes.

How to Make Dollar Store Decor Look High-End

A few styling rules make all the difference:

  • Stick to a color palette. Neutrals, metallics, and one accent color look cohesive. Our color palette generator can help you choose.
  • Use matte finishes. Shiny plastic looks cheap; matte paint instantly reads as ceramic or stone.
  • Mix in natural materials like wood, real greenery, and linen.
  • Group in odd numbers and vary heights.

For even more low-cost ideas, browse our list of DIY home decor ideas on a budget.

Frequently asked questions

What paint works on dollar store glass?

Acrylic craft paint or chalk-style paint works well after cleaning the glass with rubbing alcohol. Spray paint made for multiple surfaces is another good option.

Can painted vases hold water?

Paint on the outside of a vase usually holds up, but avoid getting it wet. Use a smaller jar or water tube inside for fresh flowers.

How do I make dollar store decor look less cheap?

Choose matte finishes, stick to a cohesive color palette, add natural textures like wood and twine, and group items in odd numbers.
