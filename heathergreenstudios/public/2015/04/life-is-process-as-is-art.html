Life Is a Process, as Is Art

Simplify, purge, transition: a practical framework for artists juggling family, teaching and a studio, and for making room to build a real body of work.

Studio LifeFirst published April 10, 2015Updated October 10, 20265 min read

Most artists wear several hats: parent, teacher, curator, shopkeeper, bookkeeper. Each one is worthwhile, and together they can leave no time for the thing that started it all: making art. Here's a three-part framework for clawing that time back.

1. Organize

Clutter is a tax on attention. Before adding anything new, sort what's already there: supplies, paperwork, half-finished pieces. Group materials by process (all relief printing in one bin, all binding in another) so starting a session takes minutes, not an hour of searching.

2. Purge

Most studios hold tools bought for one project and never touched again. Sell or donate them to students, schools and community art programs. Letting go of the "someday" projects is freeing, and it pays for the materials you'll actually use.

3. Transition

This is the hard one: aligning your time with your goals. If the goal is a cohesive body of work, a graduate program or a solo show, then roles that don't serve it need to shrink, change shape or go. Sometimes that means closing a storefront and moving into a shared studio where the only job is to make work. Sometimes it means taking a business online so it can run in fewer hours.

Closing one door is rarely a defeat. It is usually just a change in the shape of the work.

Small habits that help

  • Protect a recurring block, even two hours a week, and treat it like a class you've paid for.
  • Keep a project going in the sketchbook so short pockets of time still count.
  • Work in series. Variations on a theme, like the paper dresses or a run of collagraph squares, turn scattered sessions into a coherent body of work.
  • Find a community. Shared studio buildings, critique groups and exchanges like mail art calls keep momentum going.
