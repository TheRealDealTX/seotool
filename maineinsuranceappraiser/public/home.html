Why owners call

Accepted is one word. Paid in full is another.

Most disputed Maine claims aren’t denials. They’re short: an attic that was never opened, a slope left off the roof report, ice-barrier membrane priced for one course when the eave needs three, clapboard that can’t be matched. A Maine Insurance Appraiser exists for the distance between the carrier’s number and the real cost to put the building back.

We build our own scope from the inspection, not from an edited copy of the carrier’s estimate, and we price what we can defend in front of an umpire.

The Maine claim year

What each season sends to a Maine Insurance Appraiser

Weather here runs on a calendar. So do claims. Tap or hover a season to open it.

Dec–Mar

Winter

Most Maine claim files open in the cold months, and many of them stay open until the snow comes off the roof.

  • Ice dams behind gutters
  • Snow load on flat and low roofs
  • Frozen pipes at closed camps
  • Chimney and wood-stove fires

Apr–May

Spring

Thaw shows what winter hid. The roof that looked fine in February often shows the damage in April.

  • Snowmelt leaks found late
  • Roof damage revealed by thaw
  • River ice and high water
  • Basement water and sump failures

Jun–Aug

Summer

Short, violent storms track across the interior and drop hardwoods and pines onto houses and camps.

  • Thunderstorm wind and microbursts
  • Hail in the western hills
  • Lightning strikes
  • Trees on roofs and cars

Sep–Nov

Fall

Coastal storms and the remnants of tropical systems test siding, windows and shingles from Kittery to Eastport.

  • Nor’easters and wind-driven rain
  • Post-tropical storms
  • Coastal surge and spray
  • Early wet snow on leafy trees

How it moves

Five stages from a short estimate to a binding number

The appraisal condition turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with an end. Here’s how Maine Insurance Appraiser carries a file through it.

Read the full appraisal path

  1. 01

    Free estimate review

    We read your policy’s appraisal condition, the carrier’s full estimate and your photos, then tell you whether appraisal fits or another path is cheaper.

  2. 02

    Independent scope and pricing

    On-site inspection and measurements, including the attic and the areas winter hides, and a line-item estimate priced for your part of Maine.

  3. 03

    Written demand and appointment

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and Maine Insurance Appraiser is named as your appraiser within the time your policy allows.

  4. 04

    Line-by-line reconciliation

    We work through each disputed line with the carrier’s appraiser. Many disagreements narrow sharply at this stage.

  5. 05

    Umpire and signed award

    Items still in dispute go to a neutral umpire. Once two of the three sign, the amount of loss is set and payment follows under your policy.

    October fires

    Groundhog Day Gale

    February blizzard

    December 18 storm

    Wind and heavy rain on snow sent the Kennebec and Androscoggin over their banks.

    A new Portland tide record of 14.57 feet; wharves, fishing shacks and beach homes damaged.

Fit check

Good fits and poor fits for appraisal

Maine Insurance Appraiser will tell you on the first call if your claim belongs somewhere else.

Usually a good fit

  • Coverage accepted, cost disputed
  • Repair versus replace on a roof or floor
  • Matching siding, shingles or flooring that can’t be found
  • Missing trades, code items, ice barrier or overhead and profit
  • Depreciation that doesn’t match the age of the item

Usually a poor fit

  • A full coverage denial
  • Claims-handling or bad-faith complaints
  • Gaps too small to justify the fees
  • Flood losses under a homeowners policy
  • A suit deadline that’s days away

Answers

Questions owners ask a Maine Insurance Appraiser

Short answers about the process, the cost and when appraisal is the wrong move.

All answers Glossary
What does a Maine Insurance Appraiser do on a claim?

We value the damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your named appraiser we inspect, write an independent line-item estimate, reconcile it with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present the open items to an umpire.

Is appraisal the same as hiring a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal condition on one question, the amount of loss, and doesn’t give legal advice.

When does it make sense to call a Maine Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s itemized estimate, and the gap between it and a contractor’s itemized number is meaningful, often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Can appraisal fix a denied claim?

Usually not. Appraisal sets the amount of a covered loss. Coverage denials and claims-handling complaints belong with a Maine attorney or the Bureau of Insurance.

How are fees handled?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half the umpire. We put our fee in writing before work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you travel to islands and The County?

Yes. We work in all 16 counties, including island properties reached by ferry and farms in Aroostook. Remote access is planned into the inspection, not avoided.
