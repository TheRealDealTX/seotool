Insurance Appraisal FAQ, Grouped by Topic

Before you invoke appraisal

How do I know if my dispute is a good fit for appraisal?

Appraisal tends to work best when the insurer has accepted the claim and the fight is over scope, quantities or pricing. If the carrier has denied the claim outright or says the damage came from an excluded cause, the dispute is more about coverage, and an attorney is usually the better first call.

Should I ask for a supplement before demanding appraisal?

Often, yes. A written supplement request that lists missing items with photos and a contractor bid gives the adjuster a chance to correct the estimate. If that request is ignored or only partly accepted, you will have a cleaner record of the disagreement to bring into appraisal.

Can the insurance company force appraisal on me?

Many policies let either party demand appraisal, so an insurer may invoke it too. Whether you can decline, and what happens if you do, depends on your policy language and West Virginia law, so check your clause and talk to a West Virginia attorney if you are unsure.

Do I need a contractor bid before starting?

It is not always required, but it helps a great deal. A detailed bid from a contractor who works in your area gives your appraiser a local pricing reference and shows where the carrier’s estimate falls short.

What documents should I gather first?

Collect the full policy with endorsements, every estimate and letter from the insurer, photos and video of the damage, repair bids, receipts for emergency work and records of extra living costs. The appraisal readiness scorecard on our site can help you spot gaps.

Costs and fees

Who pays the appraisers and the umpire?

Under most clauses, each side pays its own appraiser, and the umpire’s fee and other shared costs are split evenly. Your policy may word this differently, so read the clause before you commit.

How do appraisers charge?

Arrangements vary. Some charge hourly, some a flat fee and some a percentage of the increase they help produce. Whatever the model, ask for it in writing and make sure you understand what happens if the award does not move much.

Is appraisal cheaper than going to court?

It is frequently faster and less expensive than litigation because there is no formal discovery or trial. That said, it is not free, and for a small gap the fees may eat most of the gain, so run the numbers first.

How large should the gap be before appraisal makes sense?

There is no fixed threshold. Compare the likely increase with what you expect to pay your appraiser and half an umpire’s fee. Our claim gap and fee estimator lets you test a few illustrative scenarios before deciding.

Timing and deadlines

How long does an appraisal usually take?

Many residential appraisals wrap up in a few weeks to a few months. Complex commercial losses, slow responses from either side or a contested umpire selection can stretch that out.

Is there a deadline to demand appraisal?

Some policies set one, others do not, and courts may look at whether a demand was made within a reasonable time. Read your policy closely and ask a West Virginia attorney if timing is tight.

Does appraisal stop the clock on my right to sue?

Not necessarily. Many policies contain a suit limitation provision, and appraisal may not pause it. Talk to a West Virginia attorney about how that deadline applies to your claim before it gets close.

What if the insurer’s appraiser is slow to respond?

Document every contact attempt with dates. Your appraiser can push for a schedule, and the clause may allow either side to ask a court to appoint an umpire if selection stalls. Persistent delay may also be worth raising with an attorney.

Should I start repairs before appraisal is finished?

You generally should take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, such as tarping or drying out. Before larger permanent repairs, make sure the damage is thoroughly photographed and that both appraisers have had a chance to see it.

West Virginia specifics

Can I file a complaint with the state about how my claim was handled?

Yes. The West Virginia Offices of the Insurance Commissioner in Charleston has a consumer services division that accepts complaints about insurers. Filing a complaint is separate from appraisal and does not replace it.

Does appraisal work for policies from the Essential Property Insurance Association?

The Essential Property Insurance Association is West Virginia’s insurer of last resort for owners who cannot find coverage in the regular market. Its policies have their own terms, so read the loss settlement and dispute sections to see whether and how appraisal applies.

Can appraisal help with a mine subsidence claim?

If the insurer accepts that subsidence damage is covered under your mine subsidence endorsement but disputes the repair cost, appraisal may be an option. The endorsement commonly carries its own limit and deductible, so check those numbers, and remember that the cause of the damage is often the harder question.

What about landslide or hillside damage?

Many policies exclude earth movement, and landslides are a frequent problem in West Virginia’s steep terrain. Appraisal cannot create coverage that the policy excludes, so a dispute over whether a slide is covered belongs with your insurer and possibly a West Virginia attorney.

Does appraisal resolve a bad-faith claim?

No. West Virginia has an Unfair Trade Practices Act that addresses claim handling, and courts have recognized certain claims against insurers in some circumstances. Those are legal questions for a West Virginia attorney; appraisal only values the loss.

The award and after

Is the award binding?

On the amount of loss, an award signed by two of the three panel members is generally treated as binding. Courts may set an award aside in limited situations, such as fraud or a panel going beyond its authority, but that is uncommon.

How soon should the insurer pay after the award?

Policies often set a payment window after an award or agreement, so check yours. If payment does not arrive in a reasonable time, keep records and consider contacting the Offices of the Insurance Commissioner or a West Virginia attorney.

Will the award include recoverable depreciation?

A well-written award usually lists replacement cost, depreciation and actual cash value separately. Recoverable depreciation is commonly paid once repairs are completed and documented, so keep invoices and photos of the finished work.

Can I still dispute coverage issues after the award?

Possibly. Because the panel generally decides only the amount of loss, coverage disputes may remain open after the award. A West Virginia attorney can tell you whether anything you signed or accepted affects that.

What if more damage shows up after the award?

Hidden damage sometimes appears once repairs begin, such as rot under a roof deck or wet framing behind a wall. Contact the insurer promptly, document it and ask whether it can be handled as a supplement; your policy and the award’s wording will shape the answer.
