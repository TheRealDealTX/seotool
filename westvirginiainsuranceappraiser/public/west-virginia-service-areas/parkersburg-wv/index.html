Two rivers and a floodwall

Parkersburg sits at the confluence of the Little Kanawha and the Ohio, with Blennerhassett Island just downstream. The 1937 flood drowned large parts of downtown, and the floodwall that followed is now part of the riverfront. Behind it, the historic core around Julia-Ann Square holds some of the finest Victorian homes in the state. Across the Little Kanawha, South Parkersburg spreads along flatter ground, while Vienna, Mineral Wells and Lubeck carry newer growth along I-77 and Corridor D (US-50).

Standard homeowner policies exclude flood, so water claims in Wood County usually start with a cause question: did the water come up from the ground or the river, or did it come down through a storm-damaged roof or in through a burst pipe? The answer determines which policy, if any, pays.

Victorian homes, modern pricing problems

Homes around Julia-Ann Square and the nearby historic blocks feature tall windows, turned porches, decorative trim, slate roofs and plaster walls. When a storm or fire damages one of these houses, an estimate built from standard line items often misses the craftsmanship needed to restore it. Matching wood siding profiles, recreating porch brackets and reglazing old windows take time and specialty labor. In the North End and South Parkersburg, early 1900s frame houses raise similar issues on a smaller scale, often with older wiring and plumbing that may trigger code upgrades.

Storm season in the Mid-Ohio Valley

Thunderstorms tracking up the Ohio Valley bring straight-line winds and, a few times a decade, hail big enough to damage roofs and siding across Vienna and the newer subdivisions. Ice storms load the valley’s mature trees and drop limbs on houses and cars. In winter, crawlspace pipes in older homes freeze and burst. Each of these can lead to arguments about repair versus replacement, the extent of interior water damage and how much depreciation applies.

Commercial and church buildings add another layer. Downtown Parkersburg and the older river towns have brick storefronts, upper-floor apartments and historic churches with tall roofs, stained glass and bell towers. Those claims often involve a commercial policy with coinsurance, business income coverage and separate limits for signs or outdoor property. A careful reading of the declarations before the inspection helps everyone price the same scope rather than arguing after the fact.

Common Wood County disputes

  • Historic trim, windows and porches priced with generic modern materials.
  • Roofs on newer Vienna homes called “repairable” after a hail event.
  • Interior water damage denied as “flood” when the source was a roof opening.
  • Depreciation applied heavily to older but well-maintained roofs and siding.
  • Mold remediation limits cutting short a long-running pipe leak claim.

Parkersburg questions

My Julia-Ann Square home has original woodwork. Will appraisal account for that?

An appraisal estimate should price the repair that restores what was there, including specialty trim and labor, subject to your policy’s terms. Documenting the original features with photos helps.

Water came into my Parkersburg basement during a storm. Is that flood?

It depends on how the water got in. Rising surface water is usually excluded as flood, while water entering through storm damage to the roof or a burst pipe may be covered. Sewer backup often needs a separate endorsement.
