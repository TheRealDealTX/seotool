Building on the Monongahela’s steep banks

Morgantown climbs up from the Monongahela River in a tangle of hills, with neighborhoods like South Park and Sunnyside hanging onto slopes so steep that some streets are stairways. WVU’s campuses spread from downtown to Evansdale, linked by the PRT, and the city has grown outward toward Cheat Lake, Westover and Granville along I-79 and I-68. That terrain shapes nearly every claim: limited access for equipment, retaining walls holding up yards, and runoff that funnels into the lowest house on the street.

Underground, much of Monongalia County was mined for coal. Settlement cracks in a foundation or a sudden sag in a floor can raise the question of mine subsidence, which is handled under a separate endorsement with its own limit and deductible. Our guide to landslide versus mine subsidence claims walks through the difference.

Student rentals and owner-occupied homes

A large share of housing near campus is rented, often older frame houses that have been divided into apartments. Landlords carry dwelling or commercial policies with very different terms from a standard homeowners form, and tenant damage, vacancy clauses and loss-of-rent coverage can all come into play. In Suncrest and South Park, owner-occupied homes from the 1920s through the 1960s mix brick, frame and stone. Out at Cheat Lake, newer houses and townhome associations bring HOA master policies into the picture.

Wind, hail and snow in the Mon Valley

The 2012 derecho is still the benchmark storm for many local owners, but strong thunderstorm lines roll through most summers. Hail is not an every-year event, yet when it hits Morgantown it tends to mark a lot of roofs and siding at once, and repairability fights follow. Winter adds heavy wet snow that can load flat and low-slope roofs on rentals and commercial buildings, plus freeze-ups in vacant units over winter break, which may trigger vacancy or heat-maintenance conditions in the policy.

Pricing is another sore spot. Contractors in the Morgantown area stay busy with university and commercial work, and after a regional storm their schedules fill quickly. An estimate built on statewide average pricing may not reflect what local roofers and remodelers actually charge, especially for steep-slope roofs, stair-only access or small jobs that still require a full crew and a dumpster. Getting at least one written local bid gives you something concrete to compare.

Where Morgantown estimates tend to fall short

  • Labor and access costs on lots reachable only by stairs or a narrow alley.
  • Retaining wall and drainage damage lumped into an earth movement exclusion.
  • Siding and roof matching on older homes where the original product is discontinued.
  • Loss-of-rent periods cut short on multi-unit rentals.
  • Foundation cracking dismissed as settlement without a subsidence review.

We serve Morgantown, Westover, Star City, Granville, Cheat Lake and the rest of Monongalia County, and we regularly work on rental and small commercial properties as well as single-family homes.

Morgantown questions

I own a student rental near WVU. Can I use appraisal on a landlord policy?

Many dwelling and commercial policies have an appraisal clause, but the wording varies. Check your policy for the clause and for how it handles loss of rents, which can be part of the valued loss.

My Morgantown foundation cracked suddenly. Could it be mine subsidence?

Possibly. Much of the county was undermined. If you carry the mine subsidence endorsement, a separate claim and review process may apply. If you don’t, an earth movement exclusion may limit coverage.

Do Cheat Lake townhome owners deal with the HOA policy or their own?

Often both. The association’s master policy may cover exterior elements and common areas, while your individual policy covers interiors and contents. The bylaws and both policies decide the split.
