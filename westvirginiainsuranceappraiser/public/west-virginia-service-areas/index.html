How claims differ around the state

River towns, mountain counties and fast-growing suburbs each produce their own kind of dispute.

Wheeling · Weirton · Moundsville

Northern Panhandle

  • River flooding
  • Thunderstorm wind
  • Hillside slips
  • Fire in older buildings

Claims here often involve brick rowhouses, shared walls and historic masonry that standard estimates price poorly. River and creek flooding is a constant backdrop, so separating covered storm water from excluded flood is a frequent fight.

Also: Wheeling, Weirton, Moundsville, Wellsburg, New Martinsville, Follansbee, Bethlehem

Martinsburg · Charles Town · Shepherdstown

Eastern Panhandle

  • Hail
  • Derechos
  • Heavy snow
  • Ice storms

Fast-growing subdivisions bring siding and shingle matching disputes that older parts of the state see less often. At the same time, 1700s and 1800s stone and brick buildings need specialty repairs that generic line items miss.

Also: Martinsburg, Charles Town, Shepherdstown, Ranson, Inwood, Berkeley Springs

Morgantown · Fairmont · Clarksburg

North-Central (Mon Valley / I-79)

  • Mine subsidence
  • Thunderstorm wind
  • Landslides
  • Heavy snow

Old mine workings under many neighborhoods make foundation claims a regular question of subsidence versus settlement versus landslide. Steep lots and dense rental housing near the universities add access and loss-of-rent issues.

Also: Morgantown, Fairmont, Clarksburg, Bridgeport, Westover, Shinnston, Kingwood

Parkersburg · Vienna · Williamstown

Mid-Ohio Valley

  • River flooding
  • Hail
  • Ice storms
  • Thunderstorm wind

Victorian homes and river-town construction mean many losses involve historic materials and craftsmanship. Water claims often turn on whether damage came from river flooding or from a storm opening in the roof.

Also: Parkersburg, Vienna, Williamstown, Ravenswood, Ripley, St. Marys

Charleston · Huntington · South Charleston

Metro Valley (Charleston–Huntington)

  • Flash flooding
  • Ice storms
  • Falling trees
  • Landslides

Homes split between creek bottoms and steep hillsides, so a single storm can bring wind, runoff and slope damage that carriers try to separate. Older housing in the East End and around Ritter Park raises plaster, slate and code-upgrade disputes.

Also: Charleston, Huntington, South Charleston, St. Albans, Barboursville, Hurricane, Teays Valley

Beckley · Bluefield · Logan

Southern Coalfields

  • Ice storms
  • Mine subsidence
  • Flash flooding
  • Wildfire

Coal-camp houses, manufactured homes and older frame construction make scope and pricing disputes common. Mine subsidence questions and narrow, steep access roads also raise costs that estimates frequently overlook.

Also: Beckley, Bluefield, Princeton, Logan, Welch, Williamson, Oak Hill

Elkins · Lewisburg · Petersburg

Potomac Highlands & Greenbrier Valley

  • Heavy snow
  • Flash flooding
  • Frozen pipes
  • Sinkholes

Snow-load collapses, vacant cabin pipe bursts and remote access costs drive many claims in the mountains. Karst terrain and the memory of the 1985 and 2016 floods make coverage questions about ground movement and water especially sharp.

Also: Elkins, Lewisburg, Petersburg, Moorefield, Davis, White Sulphur Springs, Marlinton
