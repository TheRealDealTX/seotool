Where the clause fits in a Mountain State claim

Most homeowners, farm and commercial property forms sold in West Virginia carry a short appraisal provision. In plain terms, it says that when the policyholder and the carrier cannot agree on the dollar amount of a covered loss, either one may ask in writing for appraisal. Each side then picks an appraiser, the two appraisers try to agree on an umpire, and a written agreement signed by any two of those three generally fixes the amount.

The wording varies from one company to the next. Some forms give a set number of days to name your appraiser, some describe how the umpire is chosen if the appraisers deadlock, and some split costs in a particular way. Before anything else, check your policy and read the demand letter closely. Our appraisal clause guide walks through the common variations.

What appraisal clause representation in West Virginia looks like day to day

  • Policy and file review. We read the declarations, the loss settlement wording, any endorsements and every estimate and letter in the claim file so we understand exactly where the numbers split.
  • Our own inspection. We measure, photograph and document the property ourselves. On a steep lot in a Logan County hollow or a row house in Wheeling’s Centre Market area, access and site conditions can change labor costs, and we note them.
  • A line-item valuation. We price repairs for the local market, whether that is the Eastern Panhandle, where contractors also bid in Maryland and Virginia, or a Southern Coalfields county where a qualified crew may have to travel an hour or more.
  • Working with the other appraiser. Most of the dispute usually gets narrowed at this stage, item by item.
  • Umpire submissions. If items remain open, we present them to the umpire with photos, measurements and pricing support.
  • Award review. We make sure replacement cost, actual cash value and any depreciation holdback are clearly separated before signatures go on the form.
Scope of the processAppraisal is generally limited to the amount of loss. It usually does not decide whether a peril is covered, whether an exclusion applies, or whether an insurer handled the claim unfairly. Questions about bad faith or West Virginia’s unfair claim settlement rules belong with a West Virginia attorney.

Disputes that tend to suit appraisal

The process works best when the carrier has accepted the loss and the argument is about size or price. Typical examples we see across the state:

  • How many roof slopes on a cut-up Victorian in Parkersburg actually need replacement, versus spot repairs.
  • Whether an older metal or slate roof can be repaired with matching material at all.
  • Drywall, insulation and trim quantities after a burst pipe in a 1920s frame house.
  • Whether overhead and profit belong in the estimate when several trades are involved.
  • Code upgrade items, such as ice barrier or drip edge, where the policy includes ordinance or law coverage.

It is a poor fit when the real fight is a denial for wear and tear, late notice, landslide or flood. Those are coverage questions, and appraisal cannot create coverage the policy does not provide.

Before you send or answer a demand

Gather the policy, every estimate, your photos and any contractor bids. Write down dates: the loss, the first report, each inspection and each payment. If you are unsure whether you need an appraiser, an attorney or both, our guide on appraisal versus hiring an attorney may help you sort it out. You can also send us the file through request a review and we will tell you plainly whether appraisal looks worthwhile.

Keeping our numbers credible

An appraiser who inflates figures tends to lose the umpire’s trust, and that trust is what moves the final award. We build estimates that we can defend line by line, and we tell you where we think the carrier’s figure is fair as well as where it falls short.

Questions about this service

Who can I name as my appraiser in West Virginia?

Many policies require the appraiser to be competent and independent or disinterested. Someone with a financial stake in your repair contract may draw an objection, so check your policy wording before naming anyone.

What happens if the two appraisers cannot agree on an umpire?

Many policies say either side may ask a court to appoint one. The exact mechanism depends on your form, so read the clause and talk to a West Virginia attorney if a court request becomes necessary.

Does invoking appraisal stop my insurer from paying the undisputed amount?

Not usually. Carriers often pay what they agree is owed while the disputed portion goes through appraisal, but practices vary. Ask your adjuster in writing how undisputed amounts will be handled.
