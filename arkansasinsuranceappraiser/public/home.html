Covered is not the same as paid

Why owners bring in an Arkansas Insurance Appraiser

Most underpaid claims in Arkansas aren’t denied. They’re short: a roof slope priced as a repair, a hallway of flooring left out, drip edge and ridge vent missing, depreciation taken on items that shouldn’t lose that much value. An Arkansas Insurance Appraiser is there to close the gap between what the carrier approved and what the repair really costs.

  • An independent scopeWe measure and document the damage ourselves before looking at anyone else’s estimate.
  • Numbers built to hold upEach line is supported by photos, measurements and current local pricing an umpire can follow.
  • Straight answersIf your dispute is really about coverage, or the gap is too small, we tell you on the first call.

How appraisal works

From stuck claim to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here is how Arkansas Insurance Appraiser takes a file from the first call to the award.

Read the full process guide

  1. 01

    Pin down the disagreement

    Confirm the carrier has accepted coverage and that the dispute is about the amount of loss, not whether the loss is covered.

  2. 02

    Read the appraisal paragraph

    Find the appraisal condition in your policy and note its deadlines, how the umpire is chosen and how costs are split.

  3. 03

    Send a written demand

    Either side can demand appraisal in writing. The letter identifies the claim and the items in dispute.

  4. 04

    Name the appraisers

    Each side names a competent, impartial appraiser, usually within about 20 days of the demand.

  5. 05

    Select an umpire

    The two appraisers try to agree on an umpire. If they can’t, either side can usually ask a court to appoint one.

  6. 06

    Appraisers compare estimates

    The appraisers inspect, exchange estimates and work through the differences line by line.

Six regions, six kinds of claims

Hail in Bentonville is not ice in Texarkana. An Arkansas Insurance Appraiser has to know how each part of the state’s weather shows up on a roof, and in an estimate.

  • Ozark Plateau & Northwest Arkansas

    Fayetteville · Springdale · Rogers · Bentonville

    The state’s busiest hail corridor and the core of the January 2009 ice storm. Fast growth means many young roofs, complex rooflines and discontinued products.

    • Ice storms

  • Arkansas River Valley

    Fort Smith · Van Buren · Russellville · Conway

    A natural storm track between the Ozarks and the Ouachitas. Tornadoes, straight-line winds and the 2019 river flood all left their mark here.

    • Tornadoes
    • Wind
    • River flooding

  • Central Arkansas

    Little Rock · North Little Rock · Benton · Cabot

    The metro area hit by the March 31, 2023 tornadoes, with historic homes, mid-century ranches and large commercial buildings side by side.

    • Tornadoes
    • Hail
    • Burst pipes

  • Ouachita Mountains

    Hot Springs · Hot Springs Village · Mena

    Wooded slopes, lakes and steep lots. Tree impacts, flash flooding and access costs shape claims in Garland, Montgomery and Polk counties.

    • Tree fall

    • Ice

    Jonesboro · Pine Bluff · West Memphis · Helena

    Flat, open farmland where storm systems intensify. Tornadoes and long-track straight-line winds hit homes, churches, shops and grain facilities.

    • Tornadoes

    • Farm buildings

  • South Arkansas Timberlands

    Texarkana · El Dorado · Camden · Magnolia

    Pine forest country where the December 2000 ice storm and Gulf hurricane remnants brought down thousands of trees onto roofs.

    • Ice storms
    • Tropical remnants
    • Fallen pines

Right tool for the job

Appraisal fits some disputes, not all

Picking the right help first saves time and fees. Arkansas Insurance Appraiser will point you elsewhere when appraisal isn’t the answer.

Good fit for appraisal

  • Coverage accepted, cost disputed
  • Repair vs. replace on roofs or floors
  • Matching and discontinued materials
  • Missing trades, code items or overhead and profit
  • Depreciation that looks too heavy

Usually not a fit

  • The claim was denied outright
  • The question is whether a cause is covered
  • Bad-faith or statutory penalty claims
  • Gaps too small to justify fees
  • Deadlines that need an attorney now

Questions

Questions people ask an Arkansas Insurance Appraiser

Plain answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All FAQs Claim glossary
What does an Arkansas Insurance Appraiser do?

An Arkansas Insurance Appraiser values property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on the cost. As your named appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work through the differences with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question, the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When does it make sense to call an Arkansas Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s estimate, and the gap between it and a qualified contractor’s estimate is meaningful, often more than about 10% or a few thousand dollars.

Is the appraisal award binding?

Generally yes, on the amount of loss for the items submitted, once two of the three panel members sign. That is why documentation matters before appraisal is demanded.

What does appraisal cost?

Each side usually pays its own appraiser and half of the umpire. Fees are agreed in writing before work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my part of the state?

Yes. We work in all 75 Arkansas counties, with frequent work in Northwest Arkansas, the River Valley, central Arkansas, the Delta and south Arkansas.
