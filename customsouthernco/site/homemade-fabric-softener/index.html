Homemade Fabric Softener: An Easy Old-Fashioned Recipe

By The Custom Southern Co. Team · September 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Three pantry-shelf ingredients, one big jar and about fifteen minutes. Here's our simple homemade fabric softener and how to use it.

There's something about towels that come out of the dryer soft and fresh that just feels like home. If you already make your own laundry soap, homemade fabric softener is the natural next step. It takes about fifteen minutes, uses ingredients you can pick up at any grocery store, and makes a big batch that lasts for weeks of wash days. We like to mix ours in a big glass jar and keep it right on the laundry room shelf.

Our recipe uses three simple ingredients: white vinegar, inexpensive hair conditioner and warm water. The vinegar helps rinse away soap residue that can leave fabrics feeling stiff, while the conditioner adds softness and a light scent. Below you'll find the supplies and steps, followed by a vinegar-only version, tips for choosing ingredients and a few safety notes.

How to make it

  1. Warm the water

    Run tap water until it's warm (not boiling) and measure 6 cups into a large bowl or pitcher. Warm water helps the conditioner dissolve smoothly.

  2. Dissolve the conditioner

    Add 2 cups of hair conditioner to the warm water and whisk until no streaks or lumps remain. Take your time here, since undissolved conditioner can leave spots on clothes.

  3. Stir in the vinegar

    Slowly pour in 3 cups of white vinegar while stirring. The mixture may foam a little, so pour gently and keep stirring until it's even.

  4. Add scent if you like

    If you want extra fragrance, stir in a few drops of a laundry-safe fragrance or essential oil. Keep it light, since the conditioner already adds scent.

  5. Bottle and label

    Use a funnel to pour the softener into a clean jar or jug, cap it and label it clearly with the contents and date. Store it out of reach of children and pets.

  6. Shake and use

    Shake well before each use, then add about 1/4 to 1/2 cup to the fabric softener dispenser or the final rinse. Start with less and adjust to your load size and water.

Why Make Homemade Fabric Softener?

Store-bought softeners work, but they can add up over a year, and some folks prefer to keep their laundry routine simple. A homemade version lets you control the scent, skip extra packaging by reusing a jar, and pair it with your own detergent. If you haven't tried making detergent yet, our recipe for DIY laundry detergent with 3 ingredients is a good companion project, and the laundry detergent calculator helps you figure out batch sizes and cost per load.

It's also an old-fashioned idea at heart. Long before fabric softener came in bottles, folks relied on a good rinse, line drying and a snap of the sheets to keep laundry soft and fresh. A splash of vinegar in the rinse water is one of those bits of household know-how that has been passed down for generations.

Homemade softener also makes a nice little gift. Pour some into a vintage-style jar, tie on a tag with the directions and you've got a thoughtful housewarming present.

Choosing Your Ingredients

Vinegar

Use plain distilled white vinegar. It's inexpensive and clear, so it won't stain. Don't worry about the smell: it fades as laundry dries, and the conditioner covers it in the meantime. Skip apple cider vinegar, which can tint light fabrics.

Hair conditioner

Any basic rinse-out conditioner works, and the cheapest bottle on the shelf is perfectly fine. Pick a scent you enjoy, since that's what your laundry will smell like. Avoid leave-in conditioners, heavy oil treatments or anything tinted, which can leave residue or color behind.

Water

Warm tap water is all you need. It helps the conditioner blend in smoothly. Avoid boiling water, which can make the mixture separate and isn't safe to pour into a plastic jug.

A Simpler Vinegar-Only Option

If you'd rather skip the conditioner, plain white vinegar on its own is a classic old-fashioned rinse. Add about 1/4 to 1/2 cup to the fabric softener dispenser or the rinse cycle. It won't add fragrance, but it helps remove leftover detergent, which is often what makes towels feel scratchy. Some folks add a few drops of a laundry-safe fragrance to their vinegar jar for a light scent.

Another option is wool dryer balls. Toss three to six in the dryer with your clothes to help fluff fabrics and shorten drying time a bit. They're reusable for a long time and pair nicely with a vinegar rinse.

Tips for the Best Results

  • Shake before every use. The ingredients can settle over time.
  • Start small. Begin with 1/4 cup per load and increase only if needed. Using too much conditioner can leave fabrics feeling waxy.
  • Use the dispenser or rinse cycle. Adding softener with the detergent at the start can cancel out some of the detergent's work.
  • Go easy on towels. Softeners, homemade or store-bought, can reduce towel absorbency over time. Many folks use a vinegar-only rinse for towels and save the conditioner blend for clothes and sheets.
  • For HE machines, use the fabric softener compartment and stay toward the lower amount. Check your washer's manual for its guidance on additives.

Safety and Care Notes

  • Never mix vinegar with bleach. Combining them can release harmful chlorine gas. Don't use this softener in the same load or dispenser as bleach.
  • Label clearly and store safely. Keep the jar out of reach of children and pets, and don't store it in old food or drink containers without a clear label.
  • Spot test delicates. If you're unsure about a fabric, test on a hidden seam first. Check care labels on items like athletic wear, which some manufacturers recommend washing without softener.
  • Be mindful of your machine. Some appliance makers advise against frequent use of undiluted vinegar because it may affect rubber seals over time. Our recipe dilutes the vinegar, but check your washer's manual if you have concerns.
  • Use essential oils sparingly. If you add any, keep it to a few drops, and be aware some people and pets are sensitive to strong scents.

Storing Your Softener

A big glass jar with a lid is our favorite way to store softener. It's easy to pour from, looks nice on a shelf and is simple to clean between batches. An old canning jar works beautifully for smaller batches. If you're hunting for one at a flea market, our guide to vintage jars to look for will point you in the right direction, and how to clean old jars will get it ready to use. Most batches stay usable for several weeks to a couple of months; if it develops an off smell or separates and won't remix, toss it and make a fresh batch.

Soft towels, fresh sheets and a little money saved: that's a pretty good return on fifteen minutes in the laundry room.

Frequently asked questions

Is homemade fabric softener safe for HE washers?

Many people use it in HE machines by adding a small amount, around 1/4 cup, to the fabric softener compartment. Check your washer's manual for its guidance on additives, and avoid overfilling the dispenser.

Will my clothes smell like vinegar?

No. The vinegar smell fades as laundry dries, and the conditioner adds a light scent of its own. If you're sensitive to the smell, start with the lower amount per load.

Can I use vinegar and bleach in the same load?

No. Never mix vinegar with bleach, since the combination can release harmful chlorine gas. Use bleach and vinegar-based softener in separate loads.

How long does homemade fabric softener last?

A batch typically stays usable for several weeks to a couple of months when stored in a closed jar. Shake it before each use, and if it develops an off smell or won't remix, make a fresh batch.

Does fabric softener reduce towel absorbency?

Softeners of any kind can build up on towels and reduce absorbency over time. Many folks use only a plain vinegar rinse for towels and save the conditioner blend for clothes and sheets.
