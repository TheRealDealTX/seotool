Estate Sale Tips: How to Shop Smart and Find the Good Stuff

By The Custom Southern Co. Team · September 2, 2026 · 6 min read

From numbers at the front door to half-off Sundays, here's how to shop estate sales with confidence and bring home the best finds.

Estate sales are one of the best places to find real vintage pieces at fair prices, often straight from the home where they were used for decades. They can also feel a little overwhelming the first time, with crowds at the door, rooms full of tagged items and unwritten rules nobody explains. These estate sale tips will help you go in prepared, shop efficiently and leave with the things you actually want, while being a courteous guest in someone's home.

An estate sale is a sale of most or all of the contents of a home, usually when a family is downsizing, moving or settling the belongings of someone who has passed away. Unlike a yard sale, the whole house is typically open, and items are priced and tagged throughout the rooms. Many are run by professional estate sale companies, while others are handled by the family directly.

Most sales run two or three days, often Thursday or Friday through Saturday or Sunday. Prices usually start firm and drop as the sale goes on.

How to Find Estate Sales Near You

  • Estate sale listing websites and apps: Many companies post their upcoming sales with addresses, hours and photos. You can usually search by zip code and save favorite companies.
  • Company email lists: Sign up with the estate sale companies in your area. They often send previews before sales go public on listing sites.
  • Local newspapers and community groups: Classifieds and neighborhood social media groups still turn up family-run sales.
  • Signs: Keep an eye out on weekend drives. Some of the best small sales are only advertised by a sign at the corner.

Study the photos before you go. They'll tell you whether a sale is heavy on furniture, tools, kitchenware, linens or collectibles, which helps you decide which sales are worth a trip and which room to head to first.

Our Best Estate Sale Tips for the First Day

Arrive early if you want first pick

For popular sales, serious shoppers and dealers often line up well before opening. Many companies use a sign-in sheet or hand out numbers to control how many people are in the house at once. If there's something specific in the photos you really want, plan to arrive early and get a low number.

Know the house rules

Each company does things a little differently. Some let you carry small items as you shop; others use "sold" tags or slips you take to the checkout. For furniture, there may be a hold system. Ask a staff member at the door if you're not sure, and follow the rules even if they feel fussy. It makes the sale go smoothly for everybody.

Head straight to your priority room

Crowds hit the living room and dining room first. If you're after kitchen collectibles, go to the kitchen. If you want tools or jars, check the garage, basement or shed. Some of our favorite finds turn up in places people skip: back closets, the laundry room, under bathroom sinks and in kitchen drawers.

Tip: When you find something you want, keep it in hand or tagged as yours right away. At a busy sale, an item you set down to think about is often gone when you turn around.

What to Bring to an Estate Sale

  • Cash in small bills: Many companies accept cards now, but cash can speed checkout and occasionally helps when negotiating.
  • Tote bags and newspaper or bubble wrap: Not every sale provides bags, and glassware needs padding for the ride home.
  • A tape measure: Essential for furniture. Measure the piece, your car and the spot it's going in at home.
  • A small flashlight: Basements and closets can be dim, and a light helps you check for cracks, chips and maker's marks.
  • A magnet: Handy for telling solid brass from plated steel, among other things.
  • Your phone: Use it to look up sold prices and maker's marks before you commit to a pricier item.
  • A plan for big items: Know how you'll get a dresser home before you buy it. Many sales require large items to be picked up by a set time.

Inspect Before You Buy

Estate sale purchases are almost always final, so check items carefully:

  • Run a finger around rims of glass and china to feel for chips.
  • Hold glassware up to the light to spot cracks or haze.
  • Open and close drawers and doors on furniture, and look at joints and legs for wobbles or repairs.
  • Check textiles for stains, moth holes and odors.
  • Test lamps only if staff allow it, and plan to have old wiring checked before using vintage lighting at home.
  • Look for signs of a reproduction on anything sold as antique. Our guide on how to spot reproduction antiques covers the red flags.

How to Negotiate (Politely)

On the first morning, prices are usually firm. As the sale goes on, many companies offer discounts, such as a percentage off on day two and deeper discounts on the final day. Some take bids: you leave your name, phone number and offer on a bid sheet, and if the item hasn't sold by the end of the sale, the staff may call you.

If you do want to negotiate on day one, keep it respectful. Bundling several items and asking for a price on the lot works better than haggling over a single small item. Remember that estate sale staff are often working on behalf of a grieving family, so kindness goes a long way.

Before you make an offer, have a number in mind. A quick search of sold prices on your phone tells you whether a tag is already fair. If it is, consider paying it and saving your negotiating for items that seem high. And if a price is firm and you aren't sure, it's perfectly fine to walk away. There will always be another sale next weekend.

If tins catch your eye, our vintage tobacco tin value guide will help you judge what you're holding. For jars, our guide to how to date Ball Mason jars lets you place one in its era right there in the garage.

Last-Day Strategy and Bringing Finds Home

If you're more interested in bargains than first pick, the last day can be a gold mine. Discounts are deepest, and some companies run "fill a bag" sales near the end. Selection will be thinner, but for everyday vintage like linens, kitchenware, books and craft materials for projects, last-day shopping is hard to beat. It's also a great time to pick up pieces with a little wear that are perfect for repurposing.

Once you get your treasures home, give them a little care before they join your decor. Wash glassware by hand in warm, soapy water rather than the dishwasher, which can cloud older glass and fade painted designs. Air out linens and launder them gently. Wipe down furniture and check for loose joints before anyone sits on it. Rusty tools, tins and hardware can usually be brought back with patience; our guide on removing rust from vintage metal walks through the gentle methods.

Finally, keep a simple note on your phone of what you bought, where and what you paid. Over time it becomes a handy record of prices in your area, and it helps you spot a genuine bargain the next time you see a similar piece.

Estate Sale Etiquette

  • Treat the home with respect. You're a guest in someone's house.
  • Don't move or swap price tags, and don't hide items to buy later.
  • Don't open areas marked closed or private.
  • Keep comments about the home or its contents kind. Family members may be nearby.
  • Park considerately and don't block neighbors' driveways.
  • Pick up large items when you said you would.

Estate sales pair nicely with the rest of a weekend of hunting. When you're ready to widen the circle, our antique picking tips cover flea markets, barn sales and back roads, too. Bring your tape measure, wear comfortable shoes, and enjoy the treasure hunt.

Frequently asked questions

What time should I get to an estate sale?

If you want first pick at a popular sale, arrive well before opening, since many companies hand out numbers or use a sign-in sheet. If you're after bargains rather than selection, the last day usually brings the deepest discounts.

Can you negotiate at estate sales?

Often, yes, but timing matters. Prices are usually firm on the first morning and drop on later days. Bundling several items, leaving a bid on a bid sheet or asking politely near the end of the sale all tend to work better than haggling on day one.

Do estate sales take credit cards?

Many professional estate sale companies accept cards, but not all, and some family-run sales are cash only. Bring cash in small bills just in case, and check the listing for payment details.

What should I bring to an estate sale?

Bring cash, tote bags, padding for glassware, a tape measure, a small flashlight, a magnet and your phone for looking up prices. If you're shopping for furniture, have a plan and a vehicle ready to haul it home.

Can I return something I bought at an estate sale?

Estate sale purchases are almost always final, so inspect everything carefully before you pay. Check for chips, cracks, stains, wobbly joints and signs of repair or reproduction.
