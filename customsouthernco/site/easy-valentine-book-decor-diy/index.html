Easy Valentine Book Decor DIY With Old Books and Twine

By The Custom Southern Co. Team · August 13, 2026 · 5 min read

A stack of old books, a little twine and a few paper hearts make a quick valentine vignette for shelves, mantels and tiered trays.

If you have a few old books sitting around, you're most of the way to a sweet little valentine display. Valentine book decor is about the simplest seasonal project we know: stack some pretty books, tie them up with twine and lace, and finish with a layered paper heart. It takes less than an hour, costs next to nothing, and works on a shelf, mantel, coffee table or tiered tray.

We like this project because it doesn't use up anything permanently. The books stay whole, the twine comes right off, and come March you can turn the same stack into a spring or Fourth of July display just by changing the topper. It's a good fit for anyone who loves the look of old books but isn't ready to start cutting them up.

Gather your books and a bit of twine and follow the steps below.

How to make it

  1. Choose and arrange your books

    Pick three to five books with spines in muted tones and stack them largest to smallest. Turn spines so the colors and titles look balanced from the front.

  2. Wrap if you like

    For a uniform look, wrap one or two books in kraft paper or sheet music like a gift, folding the ends neatly and securing them with a dab of glue on the paper only.

  3. Cut paper hearts

    Cut a few hearts in two or three sizes from old book pages and cardstock, then layer and glue them so a thin colored edge shows behind the text.

  4. Tie the stack

    Wrap twine around the stack two or three times in one direction, then crisscross if you want, and finish with a simple bow or knot on the top or front.

  5. Add the lace and topper

    Tuck a strip of lace under the twine and set your layered heart on top, attaching it to the twine (not the book) with a small dot of glue.

  6. Finish the vignette

    Slip in a sprig of dried flowers or a vintage key under the twine and set the stack on a shelf, tray or mantel with a candle or small jar nearby.

Finding the Right Books for Valentine Book Decor

The prettiest stacks come from books that look loved. Thrift stores, library sales, church rummage sales and estate sales all tend to have shelves of old hardbacks for a dollar or two. We look for:

  • Cloth covers in soft colors. Faded red, rose, cream, sage and tan all play nicely with valentine colors.
  • Interesting spines. Gilt lettering, worn corners and old publisher marks add character.
  • Similar sizes. A stack looks tidier when the books step down gradually rather than jumping from huge to tiny.
  • Titles with a theme. Poetry, love stories or anything with "heart" or "rose" in the title makes a fun inside joke for anyone who looks closely.

If a book is musty, set it in a dry, sunny spot for a day or two with the pages fanned open, which often helps. Books with active mold or a strong mildew smell are better left at the store, since they can spread spores to the rest of your shelves.

And a word of caution: an old book isn't automatically valuable, but some are. If a book looks unusually old, is a first edition or carries a signature or family records, leave it as-is and don't glue anything to it.

Tip: Remove brightly colored modern dust jackets and you'll often find a plain cloth cover underneath that looks far more vintage.

Styling Ideas for Shelves, Mantels and Trays

Once your stack is tied, think about what sits around it. A book stack on its own is nice; a book stack in a little vignette is what makes people stop and look. Some combinations we like:

  • Mantel: Two or three stacks of different heights, a vintage valentine banner draped above, and a jar of dried roses.
  • Tiered tray: A short stack of two small books on the bottom tier with a candle, and tiny hearts on the upper tiers. Our DIY tiered tray is made for this kind of seasonal swapping.
  • Kitchen shelf: One stack topped with an old Ball jar holding a few red stems.
  • Coffee table: A wide, low stack with a glass cloche or a small framed photo on top.

Varying heights is the trick. Pair a tall stack with something low and something in between, and keep the color palette tight (reds, creams and naturals) so the arrangement doesn't look busy.

Choosing Ribbon, Twine and Toppers

The tie you choose changes the whole mood of the stack. Rough jute twine feels farmhouse and casual, red-and-white baker's twine feels sweet and a little playful, and a length of cream seam binding or a torn strip of muslin looks soft and antique. If you have a bit of old velvet ribbon or rickrack in the sewing basket, this is a lovely place to use it.

For the topper, layered paper hearts are the easiest, but you can also use a small wooden heart, a vintage skeleton key, an old valentine card or a little bundle of dried lavender. Keep the topper in scale with the stack: a tiny heart gets lost on a tall pile of big books, while an oversized one looks top-heavy on two small volumes. Odd numbers usually look best, so try one large heart with two little ones tucked beside it.

Variations to Try

  • Book page wrap. Wrap each book in pages from a damaged book or in tea-stained sheet music. Our tea stained hymn pages post shows how to get that warm aged color.
  • Hidden heart. Fold the edges of a sacrificial paperback's pages inward to form a heart shape in the open book. Use only a book you're happy to retire.
  • Stamped spines. Wrap books in plain kraft paper and stamp letters on the spines to spell LOVE when stacked.
  • Rolled roses. Roll spirals cut from book pages into little paper roses and tuck a few under the twine.
  • Seasonal swap. Keep the stack and twine all year and change the topper: hearts in February, a small flag in July, a mini pumpkin in fall.

Care and Safety Notes

Because this display uses books you might want to read again, keep the glue on the twine, lace and paper hearts, not on the covers. Hot glue is quick, but it can leave a mark on cloth bindings, so a tacky craft glue or a small loop of twine is gentler if you're worried.

Keep stacks away from direct sun, which fades spines quickly, and away from radiators or humid spots. If you add candles to your vignette, place them on a plate or holder well clear of the books and never leave a flame unattended; paper, lace and dried flowers catch fast. Battery-operated candles give the same glow without the worry.

When the season's done, untie the stack, tuck the hearts and lace into an envelope, and shelve the books until next year. Looking for more ideas for what's already on your shelves? Browse the blog for more vintage-inspired projects.

Frequently asked questions

Will hot glue damage old books?

It can, especially on cloth covers, so it's best to glue only to the twine, lace and paper hearts. If you need to anchor something to the book, use a removable adhesive dot or a small tied loop of twine instead.

Where can I find old books cheaply?

Thrift stores, library book sales, church rummage sales and estate sales often sell old hardbacks for a dollar or two. Late-day estate sales can be even cheaper because sellers want to clear books out.

How do I get rid of a musty smell in old books?

Stand the book with its pages fanned open in a dry, airy spot for a day or two. Some people also set books in a closed bin with a box of baking soda nearby for a few days. Books with visible mold are best not brought home.

Can I keep this decor up after Valentine's Day?

Yes. Just swap the hearts for a seasonal topper, such as a sprig of greenery in spring or a small flag in summer, and the same book stack works all year.
