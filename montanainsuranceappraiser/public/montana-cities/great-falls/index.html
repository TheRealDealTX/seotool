Wind first, hail second

Ask a Great Falls roofer what damages roofs here and you will hear about the wind. Chinook winds pour off the Rocky Mountain Front in winter, sometimes gusting for days, and they work loose tabs, ridge caps and siding panels a little at a time. Then summer storms move east across the plains and drop hail on the same houses. The result is a lot of claim files where the carrier argues damage is old wear, and the owner argues it came from a specific storm.

The city’s homes span a century. The Northside historic district has brick and frame houses from the early 1900s, some with slate or cedar under later layers of asphalt. The Southside and the neighborhoods near Malmstrom are mostly mid-century ranches and split-levels with aluminum or vinyl siding that is often discontinued. Newer construction on the edges of town brings different shingles and different matching problems.

Common sticking points

  • Date of loss. Wind damage builds over a season, which invites “wear and tear” denials of parts of the claim.
  • Shingle seal strips. Whether creased or unsealed shingles can be hand-sealed or need replacement.
  • Siding matching. Replacing one elevation of discontinued siding leaves a house that no longer matches.
  • Freeze damage. Pipes that split when a warm Chinook gives way to an arctic front overnight.

What shapes a fair estimate here

Great Falls pricing sits between the higher costs of the mountain towns and the eastern plains, but after a regional hailstorm local contractors book up and the gap closes fast. Short building seasons push exterior work into a tight window, and steep or multi-layer roofs need more labor than a quick estimate may allow. Weather data matters: documenting wind speeds and storm reports for your address and date helps tie damage to a covered event.

An experienced insurance appraiser in Great Falls looks at the full picture, including tear-off of older layers, decking, ice and water shield where code requires it, and the siding that has to come off to do the job right. Our wind damage appraisal page covers how we document wind claims, and the roof pitch and squares calculator helps you check the carrier’s roof measurements.

Appraisal for Cascade County owners

When coverage is settled but the amount isn’t, either party can typically demand appraisal in writing. Each names an appraiser; the appraisers try to agree and refer remaining differences to an umpire. Two of the three signatures generally make the amount binding. Appraisal does not resolve coverage questions, and a licensed Montana attorney is the right call for those.

As an independent insurance appraiser in Great Falls, we inspect, measure and estimate the loss ourselves, then negotiate the amount with the other appraiser. Owners to the south can read our notes on Helena claims.

Questions from Great Falls property owners

My Great Falls roof lost shingles over several windy weeks. Is there a date of loss?

There usually needs to be one. Pick the date of the strongest wind event you can tie to the damage and check local weather records for that day. Carriers often push back on gradual wind damage, so photos taken soon after each event help. If coverage is accepted but the amount is disputed, appraisal can settle how much repair the damage needs.

Can creased shingles be repaired instead of replaced?

Sometimes, but creased shingles have often lost strength at the fold and may not reseal properly. Whether repair is practical depends on the shingle’s age, how many are creased and whether matching replacements exist. These are the kinds of questions an appraisal panel weighs, using photos, product information and contractor input rather than a blanket rule.

Does the Chinook freeze-thaw cycle affect water claims?

It can. A warm wind melts snow on the roof, then temperatures drop sharply and the melt refreezes at the eaves, which can create ice dams and leaks. Rapid swings can also stress pipes in exterior walls. Whether a given leak is covered depends on your policy; if it is, appraisal can resolve disagreements over the repair cost.
