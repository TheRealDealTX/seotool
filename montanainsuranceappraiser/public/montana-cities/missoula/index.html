Missoula claims start with smoke, water and snow

Missoula does get hail, but most of the disputed claims we see in this valley begin somewhere else. Fire seasons like 2017 can leave fine ash in attics, ductwork and soft furnishings across neighborhoods that never saw a flame. Winter brings ice dams on low-slope additions and frozen supply lines in crawlspaces. Spring can bring river and groundwater problems, which are usually a coverage question before they are a pricing question.

The building stock matters. The University District, the Northside and the Westside are full of homes built in the early 1900s with plaster walls, old-growth trim and fir floors. Matching those materials after a water loss is rarely as simple as an estimate line item suggests. Homes tucked into Grant Creek, the Rattlesnake or Pattee Canyon add tree-fall risk and wildland-urban interface concerns, and some of those owners have moved to excess and surplus lines policies with less familiar wording.

Where Missoula estimates tend to fall short

  • Smoke cleaning scope. Wiping visible surfaces without cleaning HVAC systems, insulation or contents that hold odor.
  • Plaster and trim. Pricing drywall patches where lath-and-plaster walls and original millwork were damaged.
  • Drying and mold prevention. Short drying timelines that don’t fit a cold crawlspace in January.
  • Tree removal and roof framing. Missing crane time, debris haul-off or rafter repair after a big ponderosa comes down.

Local cost factors

Missoula’s building trades are busy most of the year, and specialty work like plaster repair, historic window restoration and smoke remediation can involve a wait. That is a real cost, and it belongs in the conversation about what a repair should pay. Older homes also tend to trigger code work once walls are opened: electrical, insulation and egress updates may fall under ordinance or law coverage, if your policy includes it.

When the carrier and the owner are far apart, a dedicated insurance appraiser in Missoula can rebuild the estimate from the ground up, room by room, rather than arguing over the carrier’s totals. Our fire and smoke claim appraisal page explains how we approach odor and residue claims.

Using the appraisal clause

If coverage isn’t in question but the amount is, either side can usually demand appraisal in writing. Each side picks an appraiser, and an umpire resolves anything they can’t agree on. Two signatures make an award that generally binds both sides on the amount of loss. Coverage disputes are a separate matter for a licensed Montana attorney.

Working as your insurance appraiser in Missoula, we document conditions, price local labor and materials and present the loss to the panel. Before you start, our appraisal readiness quiz shows what to gather, and owners up Highway 93 can read about Polson claims.

Questions from Missoula property owners

My Missoula house had no fire damage, only smoke. Is that worth appraising?

It can be, if the carrier agrees smoke damage is covered and the dispute is about how much cleaning or replacement is needed. Fine ash and odor often settle in ductwork, insulation and fabrics that a quick visual inspection misses. Appraisal can settle the scope and price; whether smoke is covered at all depends on your policy.

Is spring flooding along the Clark Fork covered?

Most homeowners policies exclude surface flooding and rising water, which is why flood insurance through the NFIP or a private flood policy is separate. Some water losses that happen at the same time, such as a sump failure or a burst pipe, may be covered depending on wording. Appraisal only applies once coverage is accepted and the amount is disputed.

Why does my estimate price drywall when my walls are plaster?

Estimating software defaults to modern materials, and many adjusters write drywall because it is quicker to price. In Missoula’s older neighborhoods, lath-and-plaster walls and original trim are common. Whether like kind and quality means plaster, or a skim-coated equivalent, is a fair question to raise, and an appraisal panel can weigh the cost difference.
