Appraisal Readiness Quiz

Answer ten yes-or-no questions. You’ll get a weighted readiness score and a personal to-do list before you demand appraisal.

Appraisal readiness quiz10 questions · weighted score
  1. Has the insurer accepted coverage (in writing) for at least part of the loss?
  2. Do you have the carrier’s complete line-item estimate, not just a payment letter?
  3. Do you have a competing estimate from a contractor or independent estimator?
  4. Have you found and read the appraisal paragraph in your policy?
  5. Do you have dated photos or video of the damage before repairs?
  6. Have you kept receipts for tarps, board-up, drying and temporary housing?
  7. Do you know your deductible type (flat, % wind/hail) and amount?
  8. Is the dispute about price or scope, rather than whether damage happened at all?
  9. Do you know your suit-limitation and depreciation-recovery dates?
  10. Are the repairs still undone, or fully documented if already done?
–0 / 10

Your to-do list

    For planning only. Results depend on the figures you enter and your policy’s terms. They aren’t a quote, an appraisal or legal advice, and all example figures are illustrative.

    Why readiness matters

    Once appraisal is demanded, the process moves on its own schedule. Appraisers inspect, compare estimates and, if needed, take open items to an umpire. A file missing the carrier’s full estimate, a competing estimate or dated photos tends to produce a weaker award, no matter how real the damage is.

    How the score is weighted

    Three questions carry the most weight because appraisal can’t really start without them: accepted coverage, the carrier’s line-item estimate and your own detailed estimate. Policy knowledge, photos, receipts and deductibles come next. Timing and repair status round it out.

    What to do with your score

    • 75 or above: your file is in good shape. The next question is whether the gap justifies the cost; try the claim gap calculator.
    • 45–74: close the gaps on your list first.
    • Under 45: hold off. Start with our guide to building a claim file.

    Questions about this tool

    Is a high score a guarantee?

    No. It means your file has the pieces an appraisal panel expects. The outcome still depends on the evidence, the estimates and the policy.

    What if the insurer denied the whole claim?

    Appraisal usually addresses the amount of a covered loss, not whether there is coverage. A full denial is a question for a Montana attorney.

    Can I retake the quiz?

    Yes. Use ‘Start over’ any time. Nothing is saved between visits.
