Homeowner insurance appraisal, explained

A homeowner insurance appraisal is the appraisal clause applied to a residential claim. When you and your carrier agree the damage is covered but disagree on what it costs to fix, either side can invoke the clause. We serve as your appraiser, re-scope the damage from scratch and work toward a signed award with the carrier’s appraiser or an umpire.

Most owners come to us after getting two or three contractor bids that all land well above the carrier’s number. That pattern alone doesn’t prove the estimate is wrong, but it is a good reason to look closely.

Residential policies have several coverage parts, and disputes can involve any of them: the dwelling, other structures such as a detached garage or shop, personal property, and additional living expense while you are out of the house.

Montana houses are not all built the same

A tract home in the Billings Heights, a 1910 brick house near the Helena capitol, a full-scribe log home outside Kalispell and a seasonal cabin on Flathead Lake all need different scopes. Carrier estimates are often written from a template that fits the first and struggles with the rest. Things we watch for:

  • Log and timber work. Chinking, stain systems and hand-hewn trim are specialty labor that generic line items underprice.
  • Older plaster and lath. Historic homes in Butte and Helena often need plaster repair, not drywall, to match what was there.
  • Matching. When a shingle, siding profile or flooring line is discontinued, a patch can leave the house visibly mismatched. How the policy treats that is a frequent dispute.
  • Remote access. Getting a crew and materials to a cabin up a forest road near Seeley Lake costs more than a job in town.
  • Short seasons. Exterior work at elevation may have to wait for spring, which affects temporary repairs and living expense.

Step by step

  1. You send us the policy, the carrier’s estimate and any contractor bids.
  2. We check whether the dispute is about amount, which appraisal can resolve, or coverage, which it cannot.
  3. If appraisal fits, the demand is made or answered and we are named as your appraiser.
  4. We inspect, document and price every room and exterior elevation in dispute.
  5. We work through the differences with the carrier’s appraiser, then send what remains to the umpire.
  6. Once two of three sign, the award states the amount of loss, often broken out by replacement cost and actual cash value.

Depreciation and holdback

Many Montana homeowners policies pay actual cash value first and release the depreciation holdback once repairs are done. An appraisal award usually lists both numbers, so you can see what is owed now and what comes later. If you want to model that before appraisal, our depreciation and holdback calculator lays it out.

When homeowner insurance appraisal is the wrong move

  • The claim was denied, or part of it was excluded. That is a coverage issue for a licensed Montana attorney, not an appraisal panel.
  • The damage was caused by flood, earth movement or another peril your policy excludes without an endorsement.
  • The difference is small enough that appraisal costs would eat most of it.
  • You have not given the carrier documents it asked for. Fixing that first can resolve more claims than appraisal does.

If your loss came from a specific storm, our pages on hail and roof damage and snow load, ice dam and freeze claims go deeper on those disputes.

Homeowner insurance appraisal works best with a tidy file: photos, receipts, bids and every letter from the carrier. Our guide to building a claim file covers what to gather.

Questions about homeowner claim appraisal

Can appraisal include my contents and living expenses?

It can, if those parts of the claim are covered and the dispute is about amount. Some appraisals cover only the dwelling, while others include personal property or additional living expense. The written demand and the appraisers’ agreement should say exactly which items are in front of the panel, so nobody is surprised by what the award does or does not include.

My log home needs specialty work. Will appraisal account for that?

That is one of the main reasons owners of log homes use appraisal. We price chinking, stain systems, scribed log replacement and specialty trim as their own items rather than forcing them into standard framing or siding lines. The umpire, if one is needed, sees the documentation for each item and decides based on what the house actually requires.

Does appraisal mean I have to stop talking to my adjuster?

No. You can keep communicating with the adjuster, and many carriers continue to pay undisputed amounts while appraisal is underway. What changes is that the disputed amount is now decided by the panel. We keep you informed about what we are submitting so your conversations with the adjuster stay consistent with the appraisal record.
