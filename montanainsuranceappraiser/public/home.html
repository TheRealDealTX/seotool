Why people call us

What a Montana Insurance Appraiser does when a claim comes up short

Most underpaid Montana claims aren’t denied. They’re short: a roof slope missing from the estimate, steel siding priced as a patch, a pole barn left off, ice-and-water membrane nobody counted.

Montana Insurance Appraiser works in the space between “covered” and “paid in full.” When either side invokes the appraisal clause, each names an appraiser, and the two of them, with an umpire if needed, set the amount of loss. As your Montana Insurance Appraiser, we build the number you take to that table and defend it item by item.

How our firm works

  1. Your appraiser, not theirs

    We’re appointed by you and owe the carrier nothing but a fair, supportable number.

  2. Measured on site

    Every valuation starts with our own inspection, photos and measurements.

  3. Line by line

    A full estimate priced for your part of Montana, not a percentage guess.

Claim seasons

Montana’s weather writes the claim calendar

A Montana Insurance Appraiser has to know what each season does to a building. Here’s when the calls come in.

  1. Spring

    April–June
    • Heavy, wet spring snow on roofs
    • Snowmelt and rain-on-snow flooding
    • Hail season opens east of the Divide

  2. Summer

    June–August
    • Peak hail on the plains and Hi-Line
    • Thunderstorm winds and the odd tornado
    • Wildfire season builds by late July

  3. Fall

    September–November
    • Wildfire smoke and ash claims
    • Early snow on unfinished repairs
    • Chinook wind events return

  4. Winter

    December–March
    • Snow load and ice dams
    • Frozen pipes in cabins and vacant homes
    • Downslope windstorms along the Front

The process

From first call to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding answer on the amount of loss.

Read the full appraisal roadmap
  1. 01

    Free first look

    Send the policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos. A Montana Insurance Appraiser reads them and tells you plainly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 02

    Our own scope

    We inspect, measure and photograph, then build a line-item estimate priced for your area and season.

  3. 03

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and we’re named as your appraiser within the policy’s timeline.

  4. 04

    Panel work

    We go line by line with the carrier’s appraiser. Most differences narrow sharply here.

  5. 05

    Signed award

    Anything still open goes to a neutral umpire. Two signatures set the amount of loss, and payment follows.

Straight talk

When appraisal is the right tool, and when it isn’t

A good Montana Insurance Appraiser will tell you when to look elsewhere. Here’s the short version.

Appraisal tends to fit

  • The carrier accepted the claim but the estimate is short
  • The dispute is about price, quantity or repair method
  • You have the carrier’s line-item estimate and your own
  • The gap is large enough to justify the cost

Look elsewhere first

  • The whole claim was denied for coverage reasons
  • A deadline is days away (call a Montana attorney)
  • The gap is small and a supplement could close it
  • You suspect bad faith or unfair claim handling

Questions

Questions people ask a Montana Insurance Appraiser

Plain answers on how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All appraisal questions Claim dictionary
What does a Montana Insurance Appraiser actually do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, write an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present open items to a neutral umpire.

Is a Montana Insurance Appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster manages and negotiates a whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When is appraisal worth it?

Usually when coverage has been accepted, you have the carrier’s full estimate, and the gap between it and a qualified contractor’s estimate is meaningful, often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars. Our claim gap calculator helps you test that.

Is the appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, generally yes, once two of the three panel members sign. The award doesn’t decide coverage questions, and your policy wording controls.

How are fees handled?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of any umpire. We put fee terms in writing before work begins, and the first conversation costs nothing.

Do you work outside the big cities?

Yes. We take claims in all 56 Montana counties, from mountain towns and lake cabins to ranches on the eastern plains.
