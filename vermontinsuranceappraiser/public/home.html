Where the money goes missing

Most short claims are not denied. They are just missing lines.

A slope left off the roof diagram. Plaster priced as drywall. Ice-and-water barrier nobody included. Each gap looks small on its own, but together they decide whether the check covers the repair. Vermont Insurance Appraiser starts from the building, not from the carrier’s estimate, and prices what is actually there.

  • Our own measurementsEvery slope, room and wall measured on site rather than borrowed from the carrier’s sketch.
  • Built for an umpireNumbers supported by photos and pricing, because that is what moves an award.
  • Straight answersIf your claim needs a lawyer or just a supplement request, we say so on the first call.

Four seasons of claims

Every Vermont season leaves its own kind of damage

The appraisal clause works the same in January and July, but the losses do not. Pick a season to see what a Vermont Insurance Appraiser is usually called about.

Winter claims in Vermont

  • Ice dams
  • Burst pipes
  • Roof collapse
  • Chimney fires

December through March brings the claims Vermont is known for. Meltwater backs up behind ice at the eaves and into ceilings, pipes split during cold snaps, heavy wet snow loads barn and porch roofs, and wood stoves work overtime. A Vermont Insurance Appraiser spends much of the winter following water through walls and checking whether heat conditions in the policy were met.

Mud season claims in Vermont

  • Ice-jam flooding
  • Sump failures
  • Roof leaks
  • Basement water

When the snowpack melts, rivers rise and ice jams can push water into low-lying neighborhoods, as Montpelier saw in 1992. Basements take on water, sump pumps fail and old roofs that held all winter start to leak. Sorting covered water from excluded flood matters most in this season.

Summer claims in Vermont

  • Flash flooding
  • Hail
  • Microbursts
  • Lightning

Warm-season storms bring hail and damaging straight-line winds, and some summers bring far worse. The July 2023 floods and the remnants of Beryl in July 2024 caused flash flooding across central Vermont and the Northeast Kingdom. Wind and tree damage often arrive alongside the water.

Fall claims in Vermont

  • Tree impact
  • Wind damage
  • Tropical remnants
  • Power-loss freeze-ups

Autumn gales and the occasional tropical remnant, like Irene in late August 2011, bring trees down onto roofs while the leaves are still on. The Halloween storm of 2019 knocked out power across the state. A fall loss can turn into a winter loss if repairs stall before the first hard freeze.

The appraisal clause

From disagreement to signed award

The appraisal paragraph in your policy turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result on the amount of loss. Here is how Vermont Insurance Appraiser moves a file through it.

Read the full process guide
  1. 1

    Pin down the disagreement

    Appraisal is for one kind of dispute: the carrier accepts that the loss is covered but you disagree on how much it costs.

  2. 2

    Read the appraisal condition

    Find the Appraisal paragraph in the conditions section of your policy.

  3. 3

    Send a written demand

    Either side can usually invoke appraisal in writing.

  4. 4

    Each side names an appraiser

    You choose a competent and impartial appraiser, and so does the carrier.

  5. 5

    The appraisers select an umpire

    The two appraisers try to agree on a neutral umpire, often within 15 days.

  6. 6

    The appraisers work the differences

    The appraisers compare estimates line by line.

Statewide

One small state, four very different claim regions

Lake wind in the northwest, river floods in the center, deep cold in the Kingdom and Irene’s scars in the south. A Vermont Insurance Appraiser has to price each one with local knowledge.

Champlain Valley & the northwest

Vermont’s most populated region mixes dense older neighborhoods, condos, lake camps and dairy farms. Wind off the lake, ice storms and summer hail are the main weather risks.

Burlington, South Burlington, St. Albans, Middlebury

Central Vermont & the Green Mountains

River towns that flooded in 2023 and 2024, plus ski-country homes and condos carrying some of the heaviest snow loads in the state.

Montpelier, Barre, Stowe, Waterbury

The Northeast Kingdom

Long cold winters, remote properties and wood heat. Freeze-ups, snow load and chimney fires lead the claim list, and repair costs reflect the distance.

St. Johnsbury, Lyndon, Newport, Island Pond

Southern Vermont & the eastern river valleys

Irene hit this region hardest in 2011. Historic village homes, hillside lots and river valleys shape both the losses and the repair costs.

Brattleboro, Bennington, Rutland, Hartford

Questions

What people ask a Vermont Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers on how appraisal works, what it costs and when it is the wrong move.

All claim questions Glossary
What does a Vermont Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on the cost. As the appraiser you name, we inspect, write an independent line-item estimate, work the differences with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present open items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles the whole claim for you. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question, the amount of loss. We do not give legal advice.

When does it make sense to call?

When coverage has been accepted, you have the carrier’s estimate, and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful, often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Can appraisal help with a flood claim?

Flood from rising water is usually excluded from homeowners policies. If you also have a flood policy, it has its own appraisal provision and strict deadlines. Covered wind, tree or plumbing damage from the same storm can be appraised under the property policy.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half the umpire. We quote our fee in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you work in my part of Vermont?

Yes. We work in all 14 counties, from Grand Isle to Windham and from Bennington to Essex.
