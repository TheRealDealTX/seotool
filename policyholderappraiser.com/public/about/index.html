An appraisal practice built on independence

Joseph Dittman

Certified insurance appraiser and umpire; licensed Texas public and independent adjuster

Joseph Dittman is a certified insurance appraiser and umpire who has worked property claims from every side of the table: eight years as an independent adjuster for carriers, hands-on years as a construction foreman, and public adjusting on the policyholder side with Rise Public Adjusting LLC. He holds Texas public adjuster license #3341461 and independent adjuster license #2341686, both verifiable on the TDI license lookup, and closed 463 appraisals in 2025 alone.

He works all over Texas, not just Houston. Appraisal and umpire engagements run statewide, including Houston, San Antonio, Dallas, Fort Worth, Austin, El Paso, Arlington, Corpus Christi, Plano, Lubbock, Irving, Laredo, Garland, Frisco, McKinney, Amarillo, Grand Prairie, Brownsville, Killeen, Denton, McAllen and Georgetown, with the same flat, written fee wherever the property sits.

Policyholder Appraiser is the part of that practice devoted to the appraisal clause. In the appraiser's chair Joseph's job changes: no longer an advocate negotiating a settlement, but an independent professional determining the amount of loss and defending that determination to the carrier's appraiser and, where needed, to an umpire.

How we keep the roles separate

  • We do not serve as appraiser on any claim in which Rise Public Adjusting acted as the public adjuster.
  • Appraisal and umpire fees are flat or hourly and quoted in writing before you appoint us.
  • Before accepting an umpire appointment we disclose any prior dealings with either party, either appraiser or counsel.
  • We decline engagements where appraisal cannot help, including coverage denials.

Qualified under the 2027 Texas rules

28 TAC §5.9804 requires appraisers and umpires on residential dwelling losses to be competent by training or experience, independent of the parties, disinterested in the outcome, and either a licensed adjuster or public adjuster with residential estimating experience, an engineer or architect, or a qualified tradesperson. Joseph meets the adjuster standard twice over, with daily residential and commercial estimating experience and a construction background behind it.
