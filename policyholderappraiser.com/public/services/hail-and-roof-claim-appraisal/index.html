Hail and roof claim appraisal

Hail Damage & Roof Claim Appraiser

Roof claims are the most frequently appraised losses in Texas, and for a reason: the carrier's adjuster counts hail hits per test square and writes a repair, while your contractor says the roof is done. Appraisal is the process built to resolve exactly that argument.

The roof arguments that end up in appraisal

  • Repair versus replacement. The carrier approves a slope or a handful of shingles; the policyholder's estimate replaces the roof.
  • Brittleness and repairability. Older three-tab and some laminated shingles cannot be lifted to replace individual tabs without breaking the surrounding shingles. Documenting a brittleness test is a core part of our inspection.
  • Matching. Discontinued shingles or siding, and whether a reasonable match is available.
  • Code upgrades. Drip edge, ice-and-water shield, decking replacement, ventilation and other items required by the local jurisdiction, where the policy includes ordinance-or-law coverage.
  • Collateral damage. Gutters, window screens, HVAC fins, fences, painted surfaces and soft metals that corroborate the hail event and belong in the scope.
  • Interior water damage that followed the roof damage.

How we build a roof appraisal position

We walk every slope and photograph test squares, soft metals and collateral indicators. We document shingle type, age, layers and condition, and we perform and record a brittleness test where repairability is in question. Then we price the scope in industry-standard estimating software so the carrier's appraiser and the umpire can compare our estimate against theirs item by item.

The result is not an inflated number. It is a complete, defensible one. Appraisal awards are decided by the two of three signatures, and umpires sign the estimate that holds up, not the biggest one.

"Wear and tear, not hail" is an appraisable dispute

Carriers routinely deny roof slopes as wear, mechanical damage or a prior storm. In Texas that is a causation question, and causation is appraisable: the Texas Supreme Court held in State Farm Lloyds v. Johnson (2009) that an appraisal panel deciding the amount of loss may decide which damage the covered hail or wind event caused and which did not. You do not need the carrier's agreement that the storm damaged the roof before you invoke appraisal. The panel sorts it out with test squares, shingle samples and brittleness testing, and awards the covered portion.

Timing matters

Under the 2027 Texas residential appraisal rules, each side has 20 days after a written demand to name its appraiser, the appraisers have 120 days from the demand to try to agree, and any umpire award must issue within 240 days. Those deadlines reward policyholders who arrive with their documentation ready. Call us when the carrier's estimate arrives, not when the deadline does.

FAQ

Common questions about appraisal

What is an insurance appraiser for policyholders?

An appraiser is the person you appoint under your policy's appraisal clause to determine the amount of your loss when you and the insurance company disagree on it. The carrier appoints its own appraiser, and the two appraisers (with an umpire if they cannot agree) issue a binding award. Appraisers must be competent, independent and disinterested in the outcome; they are not advocates in the way a lawyer or public adjuster is.

How is an appraiser different from a public adjuster?

A public adjuster represents you in presenting and negotiating the claim and is typically paid a percentage of the settlement. An appraiser is appointed only after a dispute over the amount of loss, participates in a formal process that ends in a binding award, and must be disinterested in the outcome, which is why we charge a flat or hourly fee quoted in writing before you appoint us.

The carrier says my damage is wear and tear, not the storm. Can appraisal still help?

Yes. In Texas, causation is appraisable. The Texas Supreme Court held in State Farm Lloyds v. Johnson (2009) that deciding the amount of loss necessarily includes deciding which damage the covered event caused and which is pre-existing or excluded, so an appraisal panel can sort hail from wear, storm from deterioration, and award the covered portion. What appraisal does not decide are pure policy questions, such as whether a type of loss is excluded by the policy's language; those remain for the carrier and, if necessary, a court.

Is the appraisal award binding?

Yes, as to the amount of loss. An award signed by both appraisers, or by one appraiser and the umpire, binds both you and the carrier. Under the 2027 Texas rules it can be set aside only if it was made without authority, not in substantial compliance with the appraisal clause, or affected by fraud, accident or material mistake.

How much does an appraiser cost?

Each party pays its own appraiser, and the umpire's fee is usually split equally. We quote a flat or hourly fee in writing before you appoint us, based on the size and type of the loss, so you know the cost before the process starts.
