How it works

Appraisal in four steps

Deadlines shown are the Texas residential rules for policies issued or renewed on or after March 1, 2027. Older policies follow the clause as printed.

01
Day 0

You demand appraisal

A written demand to the carrier invokes the policy's appraisal clause and names your appraiser.

02
Within 20 days

Appraisers are named

Each side appoints a competent, independent, disinterested appraiser and shares contact details.

03
Within 120 days

Appraisers inspect and compare

Both appraisers document the loss, exchange estimates and agree on everything they can.

04
By day 240

Umpire resolves the rest

Remaining items go to a jointly chosen umpire. Two signatures make the award binding.

FAQ

Common questions about appraisal

What is an insurance appraiser for policyholders?

An appraiser is the person you appoint under your policy's appraisal clause to determine the amount of your loss when you and the insurance company disagree on it. The carrier appoints its own appraiser, and the two appraisers (with an umpire if they cannot agree) issue a binding award. Appraisers must be competent, independent and disinterested in the outcome; they are not advocates in the way a lawyer or public adjuster is.

How is an appraiser different from a public adjuster?

A public adjuster represents you in presenting and negotiating the claim and is typically paid a percentage of the settlement. An appraiser is appointed only after a dispute over the amount of loss, participates in a formal process that ends in a binding award, and must be disinterested in the outcome, which is why we charge a flat or hourly fee quoted in writing before you appoint us.

Does appraisal decide whether my claim is covered?

No. Appraisal is limited to the amount of loss. If the carrier has denied your claim on coverage grounds, appraisal is generally not the right tool. If the carrier accepted the loss but underpaid it, appraisal is designed for exactly that dispute.

Is the appraisal award binding?

Yes, as to the amount of loss. An award signed by both appraisers, or by one appraiser and the umpire, binds both you and the carrier. Under the 2027 Texas rules it can be set aside only if it was made without authority, not in substantial compliance with the appraisal clause, or affected by fraud, accident or material mistake.

How much does an appraiser cost?

Each party pays its own appraiser, and the umpire's fee is usually split equally. We quote a flat or hourly fee in writing before you appoint us, based on the size and type of the loss, so you know the cost before the process starts.

How long does appraisal take in Texas?

Many appraisals conclude within one to three months. For residential policies issued or renewed on or after March 1, 2027, Texas rules require each side to name an appraiser within 20 days of the demand, the appraisers to attempt agreement within 120 days, and any umpire award to issue within 240 days.

Can I demand appraisal, or only the insurance company?

Either party may demand appraisal under a standard clause, and the 2027 Texas rules require residential and personal auto policies to give both sides the unilateral right to demand it without first reaching an impasse. The demand must be in writing.

Who can be an appraiser in Texas?

Under 28 TAC §5.9804, appraisers on residential dwelling losses must be competent by training or experience, independent of the parties and disinterested in the outcome, and must be a licensed adjuster or public adjuster with residential estimating experience, an engineer or architect, or someone with relevant trade experience.
