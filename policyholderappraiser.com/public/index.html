Is appraisal right for your claim?

Three quick questions

  • The carrier paid less than repairs cost, or blamed the damage on wear instead of the storm
  • You have a contractor's estimate, photos or a report that says otherwise
  • Your policy has an appraisal clause (every Texas home policy will from March 2027)

Three yeses and appraisal is probably the fastest, cheapest way to a binding number. In Texas the panel can decide causation too, so a "wear and tear" letter is not the end. Send us the estimate and we will confirm.

How it works

Appraisal in four steps

Deadlines shown are the Texas residential rules for policies issued or renewed on or after March 1, 2027. Older policies follow the clause as printed.

01
Day 0

Invoke the clause

One signed letter to the carrier starts the process and names your appraiser.

02
Within 20 days

Two appraisers

The carrier names its own. Both must be competent, independent and disinterested.

03
Within 120 days

Inspect, estimate, compare

Line by line, in the same software, until only the real disputes are left.

04
By day 240

Binding award

An umpire decides what remains. Two of three signatures and the number is final.

Why independence matters

An appraiser with a stake in the award is an appraiser the umpire discounts.

Texas now requires appraisers to be competent, independent of the parties and disinterested in the outcome. We built the practice around that standard before it was a rule:

  • Fees are flat or hourly and quoted in writing before you appoint us
  • We decline appointments on any claim where Rise Public Adjusting acted as public adjuster
  • Our estimates are written in industry-standard software, line by line, so they can be compared and defended
  • We tell you when appraisal is the wrong tool, including when the real dispute is coverage
