Brownsville Insurance Appraiser for Policyholders

Why Brownsville claims end up in appraisal

Hurricane Hanna's July 2020 landfall on Padre Island pushed wind and water across Cameron County, and the claims that followed showed the usual coastal pattern: windward slopes approved, leeward slopes denied, wind-driven rain through windows and soffits left out of the interior scope, and fences, carports and outbuildings omitted. Tropical rain events flood the resacas and low-lying neighborhoods, and carriers dispute whether interior water came through storm openings (covered) or rose from the ground (flood). Brownsville's humidity and salt air corrode fasteners, flashing and HVAC coils quickly, and carriers use that to attribute storm damage to deterioration.

TWIA and the Valley

Much of Cameron County's wind and hail coverage is written by the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association. TWIA appraisal follows 28 TAC §5.4201 and following, with its own deadlines, appraiser and umpire qualifications and a Department-maintained umpire roster, and is excluded from the general 2027 residential rules. Standard carriers' policies follow their own appraisal clauses. We handle both and explain which process applies before any demand is sent.

Brownsville housing stock and the scope fights it creates

Downtown and the older neighborhoods near the river carry masonry homes with flat and low-slope roofs where membrane replacement versus patching and parapet repair are the disputes. Rancho Viejo and the newer subdivisions on the north side carry tile and composition roofs on stucco homes where wind uplift of tile and ridge caps and stucco matching dominate. South Padre Island and Port Isabel carry elevated coastal construction with metal roofs, impact glazing and exposed decks where carriers dispute whether specialty components were storm-damaged. The Valley's large stock of colonias and manufactured housing raises valuation and coverage questions that must be sorted before appraisal.

Umpire appointment in Cameron County

For non-TWIA policies, a Cameron County district court or county court at law can appoint an umpire when the appraisers cannot agree, after ten days' written notice. TWIA umpire selection runs through the Department's roster. We handle both and serve as neutral umpire on Valley losses where we have no connection to either party.

After Dolly and Hanna, Brownsville carriers paid windward slopes and denied the rest, left wind-driven rain out of the interior and blamed salt air for storm-torn flashing. A Brownsville insurance appraiser who has worked Valley hurricanes documents the storm's full path across the structure, photographs corrosion separately from uplift and impact damage, and moisture-maps the interior so the covered wind damage is fully priced. A Brownsville insurance appraiser also confirms which process applies before anything is sent. TWIA policies follow TWIA's appraisal rules; standard carriers follow their policy clause; a storm that caused both wind and non-wind damage may need two appraisals run in parallel.

Questions for a Brownsville insurance appraiser

My Brownsville wind policy is TWIA and my homeowners policy is a standard carrier. Which one do I appraise?

The one whose covered peril is in dispute. Wind and hail damage is appraised under the TWIA policy and its process; water, fire and other perils under the standard policy's clause. If a storm produced both wind and non-wind damage, two appraisals may run in parallel, and we coordinate them.

The carrier says my roof damage is from salt-air deterioration, not Hurricane Hanna. Can appraisal decide?

Yes. Storm versus deterioration is a causation question, and Texas appraisal panels decide causation as part of the amount of loss. Whether the carrier accepted part of the storm or denied all of it, the panel can determine how much of the damage Hanna caused and award that portion, on the evidence of uplift patterns, impact marks and the roof's documented condition before the storm.
