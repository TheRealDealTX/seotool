McAllen Insurance Appraiser for Policyholders

The Rio Grande Valley's claim history is written in a few dates: the March 2012 hailstorm that shredded roofs and windows across McAllen, the June 2018 floods that stalled over Hidalgo County, and Hurricane Hanna in July 2020. Each produced a wave of accepted claims that carriers scoped quickly and paid short, and each is the reason McAllen policyholders ask about appraisal.

Why McAllen claims end up in appraisal

McAllen's March 29, 2012 hailstorm dropped baseball-sized stones across the north side of the city and remains the benchmark for what hail can do in the Valley. Hurricane Hanna's 2020 wind and the stalled tropical rains of June 2018 added wind and interior water claims across Hidalgo County. The dispute pattern is consistent: windward slopes and obvious breakage paid, leeward slopes and collateral damage denied, interior water intrusion attributed to flood or to pre-existing roof condition, and the stucco, tile and metal common on Valley homes repaired rather than replaced. Hidalgo County's humidity and heat also age roofs quickly, and carriers rely on that condition to minimize storm scopes.

McAllen housing stock and the scope fights it creates

North McAllen, Sharyland Plantation and the newer subdivisions toward Edinburg and Mission carry tile and composition roofs on stucco homes with large wall areas, where tile matching, ridge-cap uplift and stucco color matching dominate the disputes. Central and south McAllen carry older masonry homes with flat and low-slope roofs where membrane replacement versus patching is the argument. The Valley also has a large stock of manufactured housing and winter-Texan park models where valuation basis must be sorted before appraisal, and a dense inventory of strip retail and medical office along Expressway 83 and Nolana with low-slope roofs and rooftop units.

Hidalgo County's wind coverage is a mix of standard carriers and TWIA in the coastal-adjacent areas. TWIA policies follow TWIA's own appraisal process under 28 TAC §5.4201 and following; we handle both and confirm which applies before any demand.

Umpire appointment in Hidalgo County

For non-TWIA policies, a Hidalgo County district court or county court at law in Edinburg can appoint an umpire when the appraisers cannot agree, after ten days' written notice. We prepare the notice and the request and propose neutral candidates. We also serve as umpire on Valley losses where we have no connection to either party.

Why Valley claims need a McAllen insurance appraiser

The March 2012 hail, the 2018 floods and Hurricane Hanna each left McAllen policyholders with the same problem: an accepted claim and an estimate that repaired tile roofs a tile at a time, patched stucco in a different color and attributed interior water to flood. A McAllen insurance appraiser documents cracked and broken tile and researches tile availability, scopes stucco to a reasonable color break, and moisture-maps the interior to show where the water entered. A McAllen insurance appraiser also prices the Valley's manufactured housing and park models on the right valuation basis once the policy is read. A McAllen insurance appraiser who has worked the Valley since the 2012 hail knows which tile profiles are gone and which can still be matched.

TWIA policies in Hidalgo County follow TWIA's own appraisal process. A McAllen insurance appraiser confirms which process applies before the demand is written.

How your McAllen insurance appraiser works a Hidalgo County claim

We serve the demand, inspect every slope, elevation and affected room, and write a line-item estimate with Valley labor rates. Your McAllen insurance appraiser agrees what can be agreed at the appraiser conference and prepares the umpire submission for the rest; Hidalgo County court appointments in Edinburg follow the Texas notice rules.

The McAllen insurance appraiser fee includes travel, is flat or hourly and is quoted in writing before you appoint us. The 2012 hail, Dolly, the 2018 floods, Hanna and the 2021 freeze are covered in our McAllen storm history.

Questions for a McAllen insurance appraiser

The carrier replaced the broken tiles on my McAllen roof but refused to address the cracked ones. Is that an appraisal issue?

If the hail or wind event is accepted, whether cracked tiles are storm-damaged and whether the roof can be repaired tile-by-tile without breaking adjacent tiles are amount-of-loss questions. We document cracks, breakage patterns and tile availability so the panel can decide replacement versus repair on evidence.

Do you serve the whole Rio Grande Valley from McAllen?

Yes. Hidalgo, Cameron, Starr and Willacy counties are covered on the same engagement terms, and we schedule Valley inspections in clusters so travel is efficient and the fee stays flat.
