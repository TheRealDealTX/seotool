Georgetown Insurance Appraiser for Policyholders

Georgetown has been one of the fastest-growing cities in the country for a decade, which means tens of thousands of new roofs installed in the same few years, a large retirement community in Sun City, and a historic square of limestone Victorian buildings. Williamson County hail reaches all three, and the carrier estimates that follow fit none of them.

Why Georgetown claims end up in appraisal

Williamson County takes hail from storms that build over Burnet and Llano counties and track east across Liberty Hill, Georgetown and Hutto. The city's housing growth concentrates the damage: a single storm can hit thousands of roofs installed by the same builders with the same shingle lines, now discontinued. Carriers approve partial scopes that leave two-tone roofs the homeowners associations will not accept. The February 2021 freeze burst lines across Georgetown's new construction, where PEX run through unconditioned attics failed, and across Sun City's single-story homes, where slab-level supply lines froze. Carriers paid those as single-room losses on homes where water ran the length of the house.

Georgetown housing stock and the scope fights it creates

Sun City's roughly ten thousand homes were built in waves from the mid-1990s onward with a limited set of roof products, so a hail event there is a matching problem at community scale; the association's architectural standards are part of the record we build. The historic district around the square carries 1880s through 1920s limestone and frame buildings with standing-seam metal and slate roofs, where preservation requirements and specialty-material matching drive the amount of loss. The newer communities, Wolf Ranch, Teravista, Berry Creek and the subdivisions along Ronald Reagan Boulevard, carry laminated shingles on complex roofs where discontinued colors, steep-slope labor and code corrections on builder-grade installations dominate.

Umpire appointment in Williamson County

Williamson County's district courts and county courts at law sit in Georgetown and appoint umpires on motion when the appraisers cannot agree, after ten days' written notice to the other party. We prepare the notice and the request. We also serve as neutral umpire on Williamson County losses where we have no relationship with either party or appraiser.

A Georgetown insurance appraiser builds the record for it: manufacturer discontinuation documentation, the HOA's architectural standards, and a scope that corrects the builder-grade shortcuts the Georgetown code requires fixed on replacement. In Sun City, a Georgetown insurance appraiser does that work against the association's standards; on the historic square, a Georgetown insurance appraiser prices preservation-compliant repair of limestone, slate and standing-seam metal.

On Georgetown's freeze claims, a Georgetown insurance appraiser moisture-maps the whole house, because water from a failed attic PEX line ran the length of it even if the carrier paid for one room.

Questions for a Georgetown insurance appraiser

I live in Sun City and the carrier approved two slopes. Will the association accept that?

Usually not, and that matters. Where the policy covers matching or a reasonable match is unavailable, and the association's standards require uniform appearance, the appraisers consider full replacement in determining the amount of loss. We include the association's written standards in the submission.

My Georgetown home is new construction and the carrier says the roof damage is installation defects. Can appraisal help?

Yes. Storm damage versus installation defect is a causation question, and in Texas the appraisal panel decides it as part of the amount of loss, whether the carrier carved out a few items or denied the storm entirely. We document the hail and wind damage separately from any workmanship issues so the panel can award the storm-caused portion.
