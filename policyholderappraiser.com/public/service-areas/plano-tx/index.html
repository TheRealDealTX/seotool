Plano Insurance Appraiser for Policyholders

Plano is a city of roofs built in two waves, the 1970s and 1980s subdivisions of west and central Plano and the 1990s and 2000s builds east of Central Expressway, and Collin County hail finds both. The disputes here are about matching, HOA standards and roofs that carriers insist can be repaired a shingle at a time.

Why Plano claims end up in appraisal

Collin County is a national hail hot spot, and Plano's storm history includes the March 2016 event that produced baseball-sized hail across Wylie and east Plano and repeated spring events since. Carriers know the frequency and respond by depreciating roofs steeply and approving partial scopes. Plano's homeowners associations complicate the partial-scope approach: many require uniform roofing appearance and specific shingle classes, which makes a two-slope replacement unacceptable and pushes the dispute to appraisal. The February 2021 freeze added burst-pipe claims across the city's two-story housing where upstairs failures damaged every floor below.

Plano housing stock and the scope fights it creates

West Plano's 1970s and 1980s homes near Willow Bend and Prestonwood carry roofs on their second or third covering, often with original decking and skylights, and brittleness on older three-tab and early laminate shingles makes repairs impractical. East Plano's 1990s subdivisions carry laminated shingles in colors long since discontinued; matching is the argument. Plano's newer Legacy-area townhomes and the condo communities along the Tollway raise unit-owner versus association responsibility questions that appraisal handles once the covered party is clear.

Plano's commercial inventory, the Legacy corporate campuses, retail along Preston and Park, and the large church properties, carries low-slope roofs and rooftop HVAC that hail damages in ways only a roof-level inspection reveals.

Umpire appointment in Collin County

If the appraisers cannot agree on an umpire, either party may apply to a Collin County district court or county court at law in McKinney after ten days' written notice. We handle the notice and the filing and propose neutral, qualified candidates. We also accept umpire appointments on Plano losses where we have no relationship with either side.

What a Plano insurance appraiser does on a Collin County hail claim

Plano's hail disputes are rarely about whether hail fell. They are about matching, HOA standards and repairability on roofs the carrier wants to patch. A Plano insurance appraiser builds the record those arguments need: manufacturer documentation that the shingle line is discontinued, the HOA's written architectural standards, brittleness testing on older three-tab and early laminate roofs, and a scope that includes the skylights, decking and ventilation items the carrier's estimate skipped. A Plano insurance appraiser who has handled Collin County HOA disputes knows which association documents an umpire wants to see.

On Plano's two-story freeze claims, a Plano insurance appraiser moisture-maps every floor the water reached, so the award covers the ceilings, walls and flooring below the failure rather than the single upstairs room the carrier paid. A Plano insurance appraiser working a freeze file maps the water, not the carrier's photographs.

Appointing a Plano insurance appraiser: what happens next

You sign the demand we prepare, the carrier names its appraiser, and your Plano insurance appraiser inspects within days, exchanges estimates and agrees every item that can be agreed. Disputed items go to a jointly selected umpire with a written submission from us; Collin County court appointments in McKinney follow the Texas notice rules.

A Plano insurance appraiser must be independent of both parties, so our fee is flat or hourly and set in writing before you appoint us. The March 2016 hail, the 2021 freeze and the seasons since are described in our Plano storm history.

Questions for a Plano insurance appraiser

My Plano HOA requires a uniform roof. Does that help my appraisal?

It can. Where the policy covers matching or the HOA's architectural rules make a partial replacement impractical, the appraisers consider that in determining the amount of loss. We obtain the HOA's written standards and include them in the appraisal submission.

Can appraisal recover the depreciation the carrier withheld on my Plano roof?

The award states the amount of loss, usually at both replacement cost and actual cash value. Recoverable depreciation is then released under the policy's replacement-cost terms once repairs are complete. An appraisal award that corrects an unreasonably low replacement cost also corrects the depreciation calculated from it.
