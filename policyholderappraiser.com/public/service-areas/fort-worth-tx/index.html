Why Fort Worth claims end up in appraisal

Hail and straight-line wind are the Fort Worth staples. Storms that build over Parker County reach Benbrook, Westover Hills, Arlington Heights and the Cultural District first, then track across downtown toward Haltom City and Keller. After a large event, carriers assign out-of-state catastrophe adjusters working a dozen roofs a day, and the result is a partial scope: a slope or two approved, no decking, no drip edge, no starter course, and gutters and screens left off. The February 2021 freeze brought burst supply lines across the city's older pier-and-beam housing. Both produce accepted claims that are underpaid on scope, which is exactly what appraisal fixes.

Fort Worth housing stock and the scope fights it creates

Fairmount, Ryan Place, Mistletoe Heights and the Near Southside carry early twentieth-century homes with plank decking, multiple roof layers and plaster interiors. Replacement scopes on these homes are large, and the arguments are about decking, layer tear-off and the cost of matching plaster and trim after water intrusion. Westover Hills, Rivercrest and Tanglewood carry slate, tile and standing-seam roofs where repairability and matching dominate. The post-2005 subdivisions in far north Fort Worth, Alliance, Heritage and Presidio, carry laminated shingles on complex roof geometries where the fight is matching on partial approvals and the price of steep-slope and high-roof labor.

Fort Worth's commercial base includes the Stockyards, Alliance-area distribution warehouses with vast low-slope roofs, and church campuses across the county. A hailed TPO roof on a 200,000-square-foot warehouse is a seven-figure scope question that belongs in front of qualified appraisers.

Umpire appointment in Tarrant County

Tarrant County district courts and county courts at law appoint umpires when the appraisers cannot agree. We provide the ten days' written notice the Texas rules require, file the request with a copy to the carrier, and propose qualified neutral candidates to keep the appointment quick. We also accept umpire appointments on Fort Worth losses where we have no connection to the parties or appraisers.

What a Fort Worth insurance appraiser does differently

After a Tarrant County hail event, the carrier's catastrophe adjuster may spend thirty minutes on your roof. A Fort Worth insurance appraiser spends the better part of a day: every slope, every elevation, the attic for decking and layer count, the gutters and screens and painted metal that corroborate the hail, and the interior if water followed the roof damage. The estimate that comes out of that inspection is complete in a way the carrier's estimate is not, and completeness is what wins at the appraiser conference.

A Fort Worth insurance appraiser also knows Fort Worth's housing. The plank decking and multiple layers in Fairmount and Ryan Place, the slate and tile in Westover Hills, and the steep, cut-up rooflines in Alliance and Heritage each change the scope and the labor rates, and a Fort Worth insurance appraiser who has estimated them before prices them correctly the first time. Ask any Fort Worth insurance appraiser how many Tarrant County roofs they have estimated; the answer tells you how the conference will go.

Appointing a Fort Worth insurance appraiser: the process

You sign the appraisal demand we prepare, the carrier names its appraiser, and as your Fort Worth insurance appraiser we schedule the inspection, exchange estimates with the carrier's appraiser and agree every item that can be agreed. Items that remain in dispute go to a jointly chosen umpire with a written submission from us. Tarrant County court appointments, if the appraisers cannot agree on an umpire, are handled with the required notice.

Because a Fort Worth insurance appraiser must be disinterested in the outcome, our fee is flat or hourly and set in writing before the engagement starts. Questions for a Fort Worth insurance appraiser

Does appraisal cover the cost of steep-slope and two-story labor on my north Fort Worth roof?

Yes. Industry-standard estimating software prices steep and high-roof charges as separate line items, and an appraisal award should include them where the roof geometry justifies it. Carrier estimates often omit them or apply them to only part of the roof.

My Fairmount house has two layers of shingles. Does the second layer tear-off belong in the award?

If the roof is being replaced, every layer must come off and the tear-off and disposal of each layer is part of the amount of loss. We document layer count at the eave and in the attic so there is no argument about it at the umpire stage.
