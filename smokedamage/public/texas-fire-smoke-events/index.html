How events are selected

We check official sources each week — the Texas A&M Forest Service, InciWeb, the Texas State Fire Marshal's Office, TCEQ, county emergency management offices, city fire departments, and the National Weather Service — with reputable local news used only for additional confirmation.

An event is added when it is verified by at least one authoritative source (two independent sources for material details where possible) and involves things like property damage, evacuations, multiple structures, large acreage, major commercial impact, or an official incident declaration. We do not add an event because smoke was smelled or air quality dipped, and we never estimate acreage, damage counts, causes or insurance eligibility.

Property insurance considerations after a smoke or fire event

Being near a fire does not by itself mean a property has a covered insurance claim. If your property was affected, the practical first steps are to follow official safety instructions, notify your insurer, and document conditions with photos, video and notes before items are discarded. Coverage depends on the individual policy and the facts of the loss. See smoke damage claims and the Smoke Damage Scope Calculator for help organizing documentation.
