Fort Worth is where the Dallas–Fort Worth metro starts to meet open country. Established neighborhoods near downtown, the Near Southside and the Cultural District sit a short drive from new subdivisions going up on former ranch and farm land to the north, west and south. That edge matters for smoke claims, because it puts a lot of new homes close to grass and brush.

As a Smoke Damage Public Adjuster in Fort Worth, Texas, Rise Public Adjusting LLC represents policyholders, not insurance companies. We document smoke and fire damage, prepare the claim, and present and negotiate it with the carrier. We are licensed statewide by the Texas Department of Insurance (License #3356839), work with Fort Worth clients remotely and on-site, and do not have a local office. We are also not a restoration company; we do not clean or repair property.

Where the city meets the grassland

The Texas A&M Forest Service identifies the Cross Timbers, the post oak and grassland belt that runs through and west of the Fort Worth area, as a region that can face elevated wildfire potential when grass loads are heavy. The agency describes the dormant fire season in winter and spring, when freeze-cured grasses burn and dry cold fronts bring strong winds. It also notes that post oak communities drop deep, loose leaf litter that can carry surface fire.

For property owners in western and northern Tarrant County, and in neighboring Parker and Wise counties, this means grass fire smoke is a realistic concern during dry, windy periods. A home does not need to be touched by flames to be affected. Smoke can enter through soffit and attic vents, open garages, and HVAC intakes, leaving residue and odor inside.

Situations that come up in Tarrant County claims

Smoke from a grass fire next to a new subdivision. Homes on the edge of development can be exposed to drifting smoke even when firefighters keep the fire away from structures. These claims rely on good documentation of what was found inside the home and when.

A garage or kitchen fire in a two-story home. Smoke rises, so second-floor bedrooms and closets are often affected even when the fire stayed downstairs. Contents in those rooms, including clothing and bedding, may need to be inventoried. See smoke-damaged contents.

Rental property owners. Landlords with single-family rentals or small multifamily buildings may be dealing with both property damage and loss of rent, depending on the policy.

Claims that are taking too long. If the carrier has gone quiet or keeps asking for more information, review our page on delayed smoke damage claims.

How we approach a Fort Worth claim

A Smoke Damage Public Adjuster brings structure to what can feel like an unmanageable process.

  1. 01

    Policy review

    We read your policy, declarations and endorsements to understand the coverage limits and conditions that apply to smoke and fire damage.

  2. 02

    Inspection and documentation

    We inspect the property, photograph damage throughout the building, and note affected areas away from the fire, such as attics, closets and duct runs.

  3. 03

    Estimate and inventory

    We prepare a line-item building estimate and help organize a room-by-room contents inventory.

  4. 04

    Presentation and negotiation

    We present the claim to the insurer, compare their estimate with the documented scope, and respond to questions until the claim is resolved.

The insurer decides coverage based on the policy and the facts, and we cannot guarantee any result. You can read more about fire damage claims and the overall claim process.

Do you have a Fort Worth office?

No. We work throughout Texas without local offices, handling most communication by phone and email and scheduling on-site inspections as the claim requires.

My house was not burned, but a nearby grass fire filled it with smoke. What should I do?

Take dated photos of any residue and note the odor and where you notice it. Keep track of any costs you incur. Whether the damage is covered depends on your policy language, so reviewing the policy is an early step.

Does it matter that my home is new construction?

It can affect the claim in practical ways, such as available matching materials and the layout of the HVAC system, but coverage still turns on the policy and the facts of the loss.

Can you help with an industrial or warehouse fire claim?

Yes. We work with commercial policyholders, including owners and tenants of industrial and logistics property. Those claims often involve building, equipment, stock and business income components.
