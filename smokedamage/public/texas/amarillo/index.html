Amarillo & the Panhandle

Smoke Damage Public Adjuster in Amarillo, Texas

From grass fire smoke drifting into town to fires inside homes and commercial buildings, we help Amarillo-area policyholders document and present smoke claims.

County: Potter County and Randall CountyRegion: Texas Panhandle

Panhandle residents know what a windy, dry day in late winter can mean. When grass fires run across open country, smoke can reach Amarillo neighborhoods and small towns far from the flames. At the same time, most smoke claims still begin indoors, with a kitchen fire, an electrical fault or a fire in a neighboring unit. Either way, the claim that follows depends on how well the damage is documented.

Where Amarillo's buildings come from

Amarillo straddles Potter and Randall counties and serves as the commercial hub of the Panhandle. According to the Handbook of Texas, the city grew as a cattle-marketing center along the railroad, and its economy came to include meatpacking, grain processing, feed manufacturing, oil refining and petrochemicals. Interstate 40 and Interstate 27 shaped later growth to the south and west.

That history gives the area a wide range of property:

Established neighborhoods

Older brick and frame homes closer to the city center, with older duct runs, multiple layers of paint and finishes, and attics that collect fine residue.

Southwest and Randall County growth

Newer subdivisions toward Canyon and the southwest side, typically slab homes with forced-air systems that can move smoke throughout the house.

Ranches and rural homes

Homes, barns, shops and equipment on acreage around Bushland, Panhandle and beyond, often on farm and ranch policies.

Commercial and agricultural industry

Highway-oriented retail and hotels, feed and grain operations, processing plants and warehouses with large volumes and specialized equipment.

Each of these raises its own claim questions. A ranch property may have separate coverage for the dwelling, outbuildings and farm personal property. A hotel or restaurant may carry business income coverage. A newer home may have most of its smoke problem hidden inside the ductwork.

Panhandle wildfire and smoke

In February 2024, the Smokehouse Creek Fire started in Hutchinson County, northeast of Amarillo. The Texas A&M Forest Service called it the largest wildfire in Texas history, reporting more than one million acres burned across Texas and Oklahoma. It is a reminder of how far and fast grass fires can move in this region.

The Forest Service's seasonal outlooks explain the pattern. The dormant season in winter and spring brings fire activity in freeze-cured grasses, with strong winds from passing dry cold fronts. The High Plains and Rolling Plains carry heavy grass loads that can support large, hard-to-control fires under dry, windy conditions.

For insurance purposes, Panhandle smoke losses tend to fall into three groups:

  1. Structures reached by fire, where there is direct burn damage along with smoke and soot throughout what remains.
  2. Structures near the fire line, with heavy smoke, ash and heat exposure but little or no flame contact.
  3. Structures miles away, with fine residue and odor from drifting smoke.

The second and third groups are where documentation matters most, because the damage is less visible. Our smoke damage claims and smoke odor claims pages explain how that kind of damage is usually recorded.

Things to keep in mind on an Amarillo claim

  • Outbuildings and equipment. Barns, shops, fencing, hay and equipment may fall under different parts of a policy, or under a separate farm policy. List them separately.
  • Additional living expense. If smoke makes a home unlivable while it is cleaned or repaired, many homeowners policies include coverage for extra living costs. Keep receipts and understand the policy's terms.
  • Timing of cleanup. Wiping down surfaces or discarding smoke-damaged items before they are documented can make the claim harder to support. Take photos first and keep damaged items when you safely can.
  • Duct systems. Forced-air systems can spread smoke into rooms far from the fire. See HVAC smoke damage.
  • Contents. Clothing, bedding, furniture and electronics are often the largest part of a residential smoke claim. Our smoke-damaged contents page explains how to build an inventory.

Coverage depends on the policy language and the facts of the loss, so none of these points means a particular item will be paid.

Our role as your Smoke Damage Public Adjuster

We do not clean, remediate or rebuild. We represent you in the claim: reviewing the policy, documenting smoke, soot and odor throughout the property, preparing a detailed scope and contents list, comparing the carrier's estimate with the documented damage, and presenting and negotiating the claim.

If you are already partway through the claim and something seems off, see underpaid, delayed or denied smoke damage claims. For a fire that damaged the structure itself, start with fire damage claims.

Panhandle communities we serve

We work with policyholders in Amarillo, Canyon, Bushland, Panhandle, Hereford, Dumas, Borger, Pampa, Fritch and across the Panhandle, as well as further south in Lubbock and the South Plains.

Were you involved with the Smokehouse Creek Fire?

We mention that fire only as regional context. We do not publish claim histories, and every claim we handle is evaluated on its own policy and facts.

Smoke from a grass fire reached my house in Amarillo, but nothing burned. Do I have a claim?

Possibly. Many property policies can respond to smoke damage from an outside fire, but it depends on the policy language and the damage that can be shown. Document residue and odor before cleaning.

My ranch has a farm policy. Can you help with that?

Yes. We review farm and ranch policies the same way we review homeowners and commercial policies, and organize the claim around each coverage part that applies.

Do you have staff in Amarillo?

No. We do not have a local office or local staff. We serve the Panhandle remotely and arrange on-site inspections when a property visit is needed.

How soon should I reach out after a fire?

Earlier is usually better, because the property can be documented before cleanup changes it. You can contact us at any stage before the claim is resolved.
