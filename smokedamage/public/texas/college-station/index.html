College Station, Texas

Smoke Damage Public Adjuster in College Station, Texas

In a city built around Texas A&M, smoke claims often involve student rentals, shared housing and investor-owned property. We represent policyholders in documenting and presenting them.

County: Brazos CountyRegion: Brazos Valley

College Station exists because of Texas A&M University, which opened here in 1876. The main campus enrolled more than 71,000 students in fall 2023, and only a fraction of them live on campus; the university says nearly 40,000 students live off campus. That large off-campus population makes rental housing a major part of the city and shapes how smoke claims work here.

Smoke Damage Public Adjuster is the smoke and fire claim practice of Rise Public Adjusting LLC (TDI License #3356839). We represent policyholders, not insurers. We have no office in College Station; we work with property owners throughout Texas.

Student housing and smoke claims

Off-campus student housing in College Station takes several forms: large purpose-built complexes leased by the bedroom, duplexes and fourplexes, and single-family homes rented to groups of students. Many are owned by parents, out-of-town investors or small landlords.

That creates claim situations that are less common elsewhere:

  • Parent-owned properties. A family buys a home for their student and rents rooms to roommates. The policy type matters. A standard homeowners policy may not fit a property the owner does not live in, and a landlord or dwelling policy has its own terms.
  • Several tenants, several policies. Each roommate's belongings are typically their own responsibility and fall under their own renters policy, if one exists.
  • Duplexes and fourplexes. Smoke from a fire in one side often moves through the shared attic into the other unit.
  • Turnover timing. A fire just before move-in or during finals creates pressure to decide quickly. Documenting the damage before anything is removed still matters.

For owners, our residential smoke damage claims page covers how building damage is documented. For belongings, see smoke-damaged contents.

Family neighborhoods and newer growth

Beyond the campus area, College Station includes established family neighborhoods and newer subdivisions in the south of the city toward Wellborn. Newer homes tend to have open layouts where smoke travels freely, and HVAC systems that can move residue into every room. We treat HVAC and ductwork as part of the documented scope.

Businesses and game-day economy

Restaurants, bars, retail and hotels along University Drive, Northgate, Texas Avenue and Harvey Road serve both residents and visitors. A kitchen fire in a restaurant can put smoke into neighboring tenants in the same strip center. Commercial policies often include separate building, business personal property and business income coverage, each documented differently. See commercial smoke damage claims.

Regional considerations

Brazos County's rural land is a mix of pasture, cropland and post oak woodland. Grass and brush fires during dry periods can send smoke toward the edges of the city. As with any outside smoke event, coverage depends on policy language and the evidence of damage.

How we help

We are not a restoration company. We do not clean, remove or rebuild. Our work on a College Station claim follows the smoke damage claim process:

  1. Review the policy and identify which coverages apply
  2. Document smoke, soot and odor in every affected room and unit
  3. Prepare the building estimate and, if applicable, a contents inventory
  4. Present the claim with organized supporting information
  5. Compare the carrier's estimate to the documented scope and request further review where items appear to be missing

No result is guaranteed.

We bought a house for our student and rent rooms to roommates. What policy applies?

It depends on how the property is insured and used. The policy type and its occupancy terms matter. We review the actual policy before giving our view.

Are the roommates' belongings covered by our policy?

Usually not. Tenants' belongings are typically covered, if at all, by their own renters policies.

Do you have an office near campus?

No. We serve policyholders throughout Texas and do not maintain local offices.
