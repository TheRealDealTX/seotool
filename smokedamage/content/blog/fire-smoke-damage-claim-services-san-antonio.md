If a fire has damaged your home or business in San Antonio, you'll probably hear from several kinds of companies within days: the insurance company's adjuster, restoration and cleaning contractors, and possibly public adjusters. They all describe what they offer as "fire and smoke damage services," but they do very different jobs, and only some of them work on the insurance claim itself.

This guide explains what fire and smoke damage claim services in San Antonio actually cover, how they differ from cleanup and repair work, what a claim service does from the first week to the final payment, and how to check out any provider before you sign anything.

If you already know you want a licensed public adjuster to look at a San Antonio claim, our [San Antonio smoke damage public adjuster](/texas/san-antonio/) page covers the area we serve. This post focuses on how the claim side works.

## Three kinds of "fire and smoke damage services"

After a fire, the phrase can mean three different things. Knowing which one you're talking to answers most of the questions about who does what.

| Provider | Who they work for | What they do | How they're paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insurance company adjuster (staff or independent) | The insurance company | Investigates the loss, writes the insurer's estimate, and recommends what the policy pays | By the insurer; no charge to you |
| Restoration, mitigation, or cleaning contractor | You, as their customer | Board-up, water extraction, soot and odor cleaning, contents cleaning, rebuilding | For the work performed, often from insurance proceeds |
| Public adjuster | The policyholder only | Reviews the policy, documents the loss, prepares the claim, and presents and negotiates it with the insurer | By the policyholder under a written contract; in Texas the total fee can't exceed 10% of the settlement |

The first two are part of almost every fire claim. A public adjuster is optional. Many people handle their own claims, and some claims are simple enough that they should. The rest of this guide describes the claim work itself, whoever ends up doing it.

:::note Claim services and repair services are separate on purpose
Texas law restricts a public adjuster from also acting as the contractor, or having a financial interest in the repair work, on a claim they adjust. Smoke Damage Public Adjuster doesn't perform cleaning, restoration or repairs. That separation keeps the person documenting the loss independent of the person billing for the work.
:::

## The first week after a fire in San Antonio

Our [fire damage insurance claim tips](/blog/fire-damage-insurance-claim-tips/) post covers the general steps in detail. Locally, a few things are worth knowing.

### Get the fire department's information

Before you leave the scene, if you can, write down the date and time, the address, and any incident number the San Antonio Fire Department gives you. Insurers often ask for the fire report, and having the incident details makes it much easier to request it later. Fire reports are generally requested as public records through the city. Ask the fire department which process applies.

### Tell the insurer and start a claim log

Report the loss promptly and write down the claim number, the adjuster's name, and every call and email from then on. A dated log is one of the most useful tools you'll have later if timing or communications are ever in question.

### Protect the property, but document first

Policies generally expect you to take reasonable steps to keep the damage from getting worse, such as boarding windows, tarping a roof opening, or removing standing water. Before anything is cleaned or thrown out, take wide photos of every room, close-ups of residue on walls, ceilings, vents and contents, and short videos walking through the property. Keep every receipt and invoice for emergency work.

### Read before you sign a work authorization

Restoration companies often arrive quickly after a fire, and many do excellent work. Before signing, read the paperwork carefully. Look at what work is authorized, how it's priced, whether the form lets the contractor deal with your insurer or receive payment directly, and how you can cancel. If anything is unclear, ask for it in writing or take time to have someone review it.

### Track living expenses separately

If the home isn't livable, many homeowners policies include loss of use coverage, often called additional living expense. In a metro area as large as San Antonio, temporary housing can mean a hotel, a short-term rental, or staying with family across town. Keep those receipts in their own folder, along with extra mileage and meal costs, so they don't get mixed in with repair invoices.

## What a fire and smoke claim service actually does

When people talk about professional claim help, this is the work involved. You can do any of it yourself. A public adjuster does it for you, as your representative.

### Policy review

The claim starts with the declarations page, the coverage parts, the exclusions, and every endorsement. That review shows which coverages apply: dwelling, other structures, personal property, loss of use, and for businesses, business income. It also flags limits and conditions that could affect the claim. Our post on [whether smoke damage is covered by insurance](/blog/is-smoke-damage-covered-by-insurance/) explains how policy structure affects smoke claims.

### Documenting the full scope

Fire damage is easy to see. Smoke damage often isn't. A thorough inspection follows the smoke as well as the flames: rooms far from the fire, closets, cabinet interiors, the attic, and the HVAC system. In San Antonio, many homes have air handlers and ductwork in the attic, so smoke that reached the return side of the system can show up in rooms nowhere near the fire. See [HVAC smoke damage](/hvac-smoke-damage/) for the details.

### Building estimate and contents inventory

The documented scope becomes a room-by-room building estimate and a contents inventory, listing each item with a description, quantity, age, condition, and replacement price. Contents are often the most time-consuming part of a fire claim. Our guide on [how to inventory fire-damaged contents](/blog/how-to-inventory-fire-damaged-contents/) walks through the fields to record.

### Comparing estimates and negotiating

Once the insurer issues its estimate, it gets compared line by line with the documented scope. The differences are usually specific: rooms left out, cleaning where replacement may be needed, missing contents categories, depreciation questions, or no line items for the HVAC system. Those differences are presented to the insurer with supporting photos and documents, and negotiated. Our page on [underpaid smoke damage claims](/underpaid-smoke-damage-claims/) shows how missing scope typically appears.

### Keeping the claim moving

Claims stall when requests go unanswered on either side. Part of claim work is answering the insurer's requests for information promptly, following up in writing, and tracking dates. See [delayed smoke damage claims](/delayed-smoke-damage-claims/) if a claim has gone quiet.

## San Antonio property details that shape a claim

Every claim depends on the specific property, but a few situations come up often in San Antonio and Bexar County.

- **Older and historic homes.** Plaster, original wood trim, beadboard and pressed-tin ceilings in neighborhoods like King William, Monte Vista and Tobin Hill raise questions about cleaning versus replacement and about matching original materials. Pre-loss photos of those finishes are especially valuable.
- **Attic HVAC in newer subdivisions.** Homes along the Loop 1604 corridor and in other recent subdivisions often have attic air handlers and long duct runs. Ask whether the insurer's inspection included the system, not just the rooms.
- **Apartments, duplexes and townhomes.** Smoke from a fire in one unit often reaches neighboring units. Owners usually look to their own dwelling or commercial coverage, while tenants look to their renters policies for belongings and living expenses.
- **Rentals near Joint Base San Antonio.** Landlords should check for fair rental value or loss of rents coverage, and keep tenant communications in the claim file.
- **Restaurants and commercial kitchens.** Kitchen fires and grease-laden smoke can involve the building, equipment, inventory, and business income all at once. Our [commercial smoke damage claims](/commercial-smoke-damage-claims/) page covers those parts.
- **Brush and grass fire smoke.** During dry, windy periods, smoke from fires on the edges of the metro can leave odor and residue inside buildings that never burned. Document it soon after the event.

## Texas claim timelines insurers follow

Texas has a prompt payment law, in Chapter 542 of the Insurance Code, that sets deadlines for many property claims. In general terms, after you give notice of a claim, the insurer has a set number of days to acknowledge it and start its investigation. After it receives the information it asked for, it has a set time to accept or reject the claim in writing. Once a claim is accepted, payment is due within a short period. Extensions are allowed in some situations, including weather-related catastrophes.

The specific deadlines and how they apply to your claim are explained in the statute and in the [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/) consumer resources. Questions about legal rights or deadlines for filing suit belong with a Texas attorney. On the practical side, a dated claim log makes it much easier to see when each step actually happened.

## How to choose a fire and smoke claim service in San Antonio

If you decide to get help with the claim, a few checks go a long way:

- **Verify the license.** Public adjusters in Texas must be licensed by the Texas Department of Insurance. Ask for the license number and confirm it with TDI.
- **Get the contract in writing and read it.** A Texas public adjuster contract must state "We represent the insured only," and you can cancel within 72 hours of signing.
- **Understand the fee.** In Texas, a public adjuster's total fee can't exceed 10 percent of the insurance settlement on the claim, including expenses. Ask how the fee is calculated and on what amount.
- **Keep claim help and repairs separate.** Be cautious of anyone offering to both adjust the claim and do the repair work.
- **Ask who will do the work.** Find out who will inspect the property, who will talk to the insurer, and how often you'll get updates.
- **Expect no guarantees.** No one can promise a specific settlement amount. A claim service's job is a complete, well-supported claim.

Our posts on [public adjusters vs. insurance adjusters](/blog/public-adjuster-vs-insurance-adjuster/) and [Texas public adjuster rules](/texas-public-adjuster-rules/) cover these rules in more depth.

## When a different professional fits

A public adjuster isn't the right fit for every situation. If what you need is cleaning, deodorization, or rebuilding, that's a restoration contractor's job. If the claim involves alleged fraud, a legal dispute over what the policy means, bad faith questions, or deadlines to file a lawsuit, talk with a Texas attorney. Public adjusters aren't attorneys and can't give legal advice.

## Frequently asked questions

:::faq

### What do fire and smoke damage claim services include?

Claim services focus on the insurance claim rather than the cleanup. They typically include reviewing the policy, documenting fire and smoke damage throughout the property, preparing a building estimate and contents inventory, comparing that with the insurer's estimate, and presenting and negotiating the claim. A public adjuster provides these services as the policyholder's representative.

### Is a public adjuster the same as a fire restoration company?

No. A restoration company cleans, deodorizes and repairs the property. A public adjuster represents the policyholder on the insurance claim. Texas law bars a public adjuster from taking part in the repair or restoration of property on a claim they adjust.

### How much does a public adjuster cost in San Antonio?

Texas law caps a public adjuster's total fee at 10 percent of the insurance settlement on the claim, including expenses. The fee and how it's calculated must be in a written contract, and you can cancel the contract within 72 hours of signing.

### How do I get a fire report in San Antonio?

Write down the incident details from the San Antonio Fire Department at the scene if you can. Fire reports are generally requested as public records through the city, and the fire department can tell you which process applies. Your insurer may ask for a copy.

### Do I need a local office to get help with a San Antonio fire claim?

Not necessarily. Much of the claim work happens by phone and email, with on-site inspections scheduled when the claim calls for one. What matters most is that whoever helps is licensed by the Texas Department of Insurance and explains the contract and fee clearly.
