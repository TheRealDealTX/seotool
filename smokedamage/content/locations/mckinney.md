McKinney is the longtime county seat of Collin County and has some of the oldest buildings in the region. Its historic downtown is packed with independent shops and restaurants, and a few blocks south, Chestnut Square Historic Village preserves restored buildings dating as far back as the 1850s. West of downtown, master-planned communities like Stonebridge Ranch and Craig Ranch make up much of the city's newer housing.

Smoke Damage Public Adjuster is the smoke and fire claim practice of Rise Public Adjusting LLC (TDI License #3356839). We represent policyholders across Texas, including McKinney. We do not have a McKinney office or local staff. We work with McKinney owners on their claims.

## Downtown McKinney: old buildings, shared walls

The historic square and surrounding blocks are mostly older brick commercial buildings, many with shops or restaurants at street level and offices or residences above. Nearby neighborhoods include older frame homes.

In a smoke claim, older buildings raise questions that newer ones do not:

- **Shared walls and attics.** A fire in one storefront can push smoke into the building next door, and into upper floors.
- **Original materials.** Features such as pressed-tin ceilings, old plaster, exposed brick and old-growth wood react to soot and odor differently from drywall and newer finishes. Claim discussions often turn on whether a surface can be cleaned or must be replaced, and what an appropriate replacement is.
- **Multiple parties.** Building owners, business tenants and residents upstairs may each have separate policies.

Restaurant kitchen fires are a common source of commercial smoke claims. See [commercial smoke damage claims](/commercial-smoke-damage-claims/).

## Stonebridge Ranch, Craig Ranch and newer neighborhoods

McKinney's west side is dominated by large master-planned communities with HOAs, amenity centers and a mix of home sizes. Newer subdivisions keep extending north and east toward Melissa and Princeton.

In these homes, smoke often spreads through open floor plans, two-story entryways and HVAC systems. A fire that starts in the kitchen or garage can leave soot and odor upstairs, in closets and in the attic. For more, see [residential smoke damage claims](/residential-smoke-damage-claims/) and [HVAC smoke damage](/hvac-smoke-damage/).

## Growth at the edge of the Metroplex

McKinney still has farmland and open land on its northern and eastern edges, where new construction meets pasture. Grass fires in that transition zone can send smoke into nearby homes. Most claims, though, still come from fires inside a structure. Whether a smoke loss is covered depends on the specific policy and what the evidence shows.
