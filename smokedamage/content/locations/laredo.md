Laredo moves an enormous volume of freight. Texas Public Radio, citing U.S. Census Bureau data, reported that Port Laredo was the top international trade port in the United States in 2023, with trade surpassing $300 billion for the first time. Much of that trade passes through the warehouses, cross-dock facilities, customs brokerages and truck yards that line the city's northern corridors.

That makes Laredo different from most Texas cities when it comes to smoke claims. A fire in a warehouse can affect goods owned by several companies at once, and a fire in a truck yard can send smoke into neighboring buildings. At the same time, Laredo is a city of families and neighborhoods where kitchen, electrical and appliance fires are the more common cause of smoke damage.

## Laredo's buildings, from San Agustín Plaza to the trade corridors

The Handbook of Texas dates Laredo's founding to 1755 and notes that San Agustín Plaza, laid out in 1767, anchors the historic district. Railroads arrived in 1881, oil and gas development followed in the early 1900s, and by the late twentieth century Laredo had become one of the state's most active centers for trade with Mexico. Each era left its mark on the building stock:

| Area / property type | What it means for a smoke claim |
|---|---|
| Historic downtown buildings | Masonry, plaster and older wood elements that hold odor; matching historic materials |
| Established neighborhoods | Older homes with renovations over time and attic or duct systems that spread smoke |
| Newer subdivisions | Slab homes with central air; smoke moves through returns into every room |
| Warehouses and cross-docks | Large open volumes, stored goods owned by others, packaging and pallets |
| Truck yards and repair shops | Oily soot from vehicles, tires and fuels; equipment and parts |
| Retail and hospitality | Inventory, fixtures, business income and neighboring-tenant smoke |

## Fire and smoke risks around Laredo

### Commercial and logistics fires

Warehouses and truck yards store pallets, packaging, tires, fuels and a wide variety of goods. Fires involving these materials can produce heavy smoke that travels across a large building or into adjacent properties. Even goods far from the fire can be affected through packaging and air movement.

### Residential fires

In neighborhoods across Laredo and in communities such as Rio Bravo and El Cenizo, most smoke claims start inside the home. Long cooling seasons put heavy loads on air-conditioning and electrical systems, and a fire that starts while the system is running can carry smoke throughout the house. See [HVAC smoke damage](/hvac-smoke-damage/).

### Brush and grass fires

Webb County has large areas of ranchland and brush country. The Texas A&M Forest Service describes South Texas as a lower-threat area during the dormant season until grasses freeze-cure, but brush and grass fires still happen on the edges of the city and along highways and rail lines. Smoke from these fires can reach homes and businesses nearby.

## Claim questions that come up in Laredo

**Who owns the goods, and who insures them?** In a warehouse or cross-dock fire, the building owner, the warehouse operator, the shipper and the buyer may each have different interests. Contracts, shipping documents and the terms of each policy help sort out whose claim covers what. We represent the policyholder who hires us and organize documentation around that policyholder's coverage.

**Smoke-damaged packaging and products.** Products that look undamaged may have been exposed to smoke through cartons and shrink wrap. Food, cosmetics, textiles and electronics raise particular concerns. A careful inventory by SKU, lot and location helps the carrier evaluate the loss.

**Business income and extra expense.** A logistics or retail business that cannot operate normally may have income and extra expense coverage. Revenue records, contracts and schedules help show the interruption. See [commercial smoke damage claims](/commercial-smoke-damage-claims/).

**Historic finishes.** Older downtown buildings may have materials that are difficult to match. How the estimate prices those materials is worth a close look.

**Partial payments.** If a first payment arrives and does not seem to reflect the documented damage, our [underpaid smoke damage claims](/underpaid-smoke-damage-claims/) page explains what a closer review might involve.

:::faq
### Our warehouse was damaged by smoke from a fire in the building next door. Where do we start?
Start with your own policy and document your building and contents before cleanup. Each property owner and tenant usually looks to its own coverage first. Questions about the other party's responsibility are legal matters for an attorney.

### Can smoke damage products that were still sealed in boxes?
It can, depending on the product and the packaging. Whether specific goods are considered damaged depends on inspection, the type of product and the policy. Documenting where each item was stored relative to the smoke is a good start.
