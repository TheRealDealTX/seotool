Edinburg is the county seat of Hidalgo County and home to the main campus of the University of Texas Rio Grande Valley. The city crossed 100,000 residents in the 2020 census and has kept growing, with new subdivisions spreading north and east of the older town center. It is closely tied to McAllen, Pharr and Mission, and many residents live in one city and work in another.

Smoke Damage Public Adjuster is the smoke and fire claim practice of Rise Public Adjusting LLC, licensed by the Texas Department of Insurance (#3356839). We represent policyholders, not insurance companies. We have no office in Edinburg; we work with property owners throughout Texas, including the Rio Grande Valley.

## Edinburg's property mix

**Student and faculty rentals.** UTRGV's main campus draws a steady rental market of apartments, duplexes and houses rented by the room. Kitchen fires and electrical fires in shared housing can put smoke into every bedroom, and each occupant's belongings fall under different policies, or no policy at all. The owner's claim focuses on the building; tenants look to their own renters coverage.

**New subdivisions.** Much of Edinburg's recent housing is newer single-family construction. Newer homes often have open floor plans, which let smoke move through the whole living area quickly, and tight building envelopes that hold odor in.

**Older neighborhoods and colonias.** Closer to downtown, and in unincorporated areas around the city, housing ranges from older block and frame homes to manufactured housing. Documentation for these properties often needs extra care because materials, additions and past repairs vary.

**Government and commercial.** As county seat, Edinburg has county offices, courts and supporting professional offices, plus retail and restaurants along the I-69C (US 281) corridor and University Drive.

## Why documentation matters in a Valley smoke claim

Smoke damage is easy to underestimate because it does not always look like much. In Edinburg's heat, air conditioning runs most of the year, and when a fire starts, the HVAC system can pull smoke through ductwork into rooms far from the origin. Soot can settle on ceilings, inside cabinets and closets, and on clothing and bedding.

A carrier's first estimate may focus on the room where the fire started. Where smoke spread further, the claim needs photos, notes and an inventory that show it. We look at [HVAC and ductwork](/hvac-smoke-damage/), [contents](/smoke-damaged-contents/), and [odor](/smoke-odor-claims/) as part of the scope, not afterthoughts.

## Regional considerations

Hidalgo County's rural land is largely brush and farmland. Agricultural burning, brush fires and dry, windy periods can send smoke into neighborhoods at the edge of development. Policy forms and endorsements also differ from one carrier to the next, even between homes on the same street. Coverage always depends on the specific policy wording and the facts of the loss, so the first step is reading the policy you actually have.

:::faq
### I rent rooms to UTRGV students. Whose policy covers their belongings?
Generally the tenants' own renters policies, if they have them. A landlord policy is usually written for the building and the owner's property. The exact answer depends on the policies involved.

### Why does my whole house smell when the fire was only in the kitchen?
Smoke moves through open floor plans, attics and HVAC systems. That is why we document every affected area, not only the room where the fire started.
