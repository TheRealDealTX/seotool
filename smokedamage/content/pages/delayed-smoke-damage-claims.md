Waiting on a smoke damage claim is hard. You may be out of your home, working around a closed business, or living with odor while the file sits. Calls go to voicemail. Adjusters change. Weeks pass.

A delayed smoke damage claim does not always mean something is wrong. But it does mean it is time to find out what the claim is waiting on and to create a clear written record.

## Why smoke damage claims stall

Most delays fall into a few general categories.

### Missing information

The carrier may be waiting on something it asked for, or thinks it asked for. Common examples include a signed proof of loss, a contents inventory, receipts for additional living expenses, a recorded statement, or photos. Sometimes the request went to an old email or was buried in a longer letter.

### Pending inspections

Smoke claims can involve more than one inspection: the field adjuster, a building consultant, an HVAC specialist, or a contents vendor. Scheduling and report turnaround can add time, especially after a large event when many claims are open at once.

### Consultant or lab reports

If the carrier ordered testing or an engineering or industrial hygiene review, the claim may be on hold until that report is complete. Ask whether a report was ordered, when it is expected, and whether you can receive a copy.

### Disputes over scope or coverage

When the carrier and policyholder disagree about what is damaged, how it should be addressed, or whether something is covered, the claim can slow down while each side gathers information. See our pages on [denied](/denied-smoke-damage-claims/) and [underpaid smoke damage claims](/underpaid-smoke-damage-claims/).

### Adjuster changes and workload

Claims are sometimes reassigned. A new adjuster may need time to review the file. Catastrophe events can increase workloads. None of that changes your obligations or the carrier's, but it helps explain why a file goes quiet.

## Keep a communication log

A simple log is one of the most useful tools you have. It shows what was asked, what was provided, and when.

:::checklist What to record in your claim log
- Date and time of every call, email, letter, and inspection
- Name and role of the person you spoke with
- What was discussed or requested
- What you sent, how, and when
- Any promised follow-up dates
- Claim number on every entry
:::

After a phone call, send a short email confirming what was discussed: "Thanks for speaking with me today. As I understand it, you are waiting on the HVAC report and will contact me by Friday." That creates a written record without being confrontational.

## Make your requests in writing

When a claim stalls, written requests tend to be more effective than repeated calls. Keep them short, specific, and polite.

A useful written request might ask:

- What is the current status of the claim?
- What information, if any, is still needed from me?
- Has an inspection or report been ordered, and when is it expected?
- Who is the assigned adjuster, and how can I reach them?
- When can I expect a coverage decision or payment on the undisputed portion?

Send requests by email when possible so you have a timestamp. If you send documents, list them in the email so there is no question about what was provided.

## Keep your side of the file complete

Some delays come from the policyholder's side of the file, often without anyone realizing it. Before escalating, it helps to confirm that everything the carrier requested has actually been provided.

Go back through every letter and email from the carrier and list each request. Next to each one, note whether you sent it, when, and how. If something is still outstanding, send it, or explain in writing why it isn't available yet and when you expect to have it.

Keep documenting while you wait. Take fresh photos if conditions change, keep receipts for additional living expenses or business costs, and don't discard damaged items before they are documented and the carrier has had a chance to inspect them.

## Ask about the undisputed portion

On some claims, the carrier and policyholder agree on part of the loss while other parts are still under review. If that is the case, it is reasonable to ask in writing whether the carrier will address the portion that is not in dispute while the rest is reviewed. Whether and how that happens depends on the policy and the carrier's review.

## What Texas law and your policy say about timing

Texas law and your policy set timelines for various parts of the claim process. We don't summarize specific deadlines on this page because they depend on the type of claim, the policy, and the facts. Check your policy, review [TDI's consumer resources](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/), and ask an attorney if you have questions about legal deadlines or rights.

## The TDI complaint process

The Texas Department of Insurance accepts consumer complaints about insurance companies and agents. If you have tried to resolve a delay directly with the carrier and are not getting responses, filing a complaint is an option. You can find details on [TDI's complaint page](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/complfrm.html).

A complaint is not a substitute for a well-documented claim. It works best when you can show what you submitted, what you asked for, and when. Your communication log is useful here.

## Frequently asked questions

:::faq
### Why is my smoke damage claim taking so long?
Common reasons include missing information, pending inspections, consultant or lab reports, disputes over scope or coverage, and adjuster reassignments. Ask the carrier in writing what the claim is waiting on.

### What should I put in writing to my insurer?
Ask for the claim status, any information still needed, whether reports have been ordered and when they are expected, and who your assigned adjuster is. Keep it short and specific.

### Can I file a complaint with the Texas Department of Insurance?
Yes. TDI accepts consumer complaints about insurance companies. It helps to have a record of your communications and what you submitted.

### Are there deadlines for insurers to respond in Texas?
Texas law and your policy set timelines, but they vary by situation. Check your policy and TDI resources, and ask an attorney if you have questions about specific legal deadlines.

### Should I keep documenting damage while I wait?
Yes. Keep photos, receipts, and notes current, and don't discard damaged items before they are documented and the carrier has had a chance to inspect them.

### Can a public adjuster speed up my claim?
We can't control the carrier's timeline. We can identify what the file is missing, organize documentation, and handle written follow-up so the claim is ready to move.
:::
