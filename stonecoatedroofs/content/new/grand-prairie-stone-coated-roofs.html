Weather in Grand Prairie: what your roof is up against

Dallas–Fort Worth is among the most hail-prone metro areas in the United States. In a typical year, the most active stretch runs from late March through early June, with a second, smaller window in the fall. Supercells that develop west of the Metroplex often mature by the time they reach the Arlington and Grand Prairie area, and the line of storms that follows can bring damaging straight-line winds across the whole city.

Outside of storm season, the stress continues in a different form:

  • Summer heat. Long runs of triple-digit days bake shingles, dry out their asphalt and loosen granules.
  • UV exposure. Strong sun fades colors and breaks down organic materials over time.
  • Temperature swings. Spring and fall can bring 30- to 40-degree swings in a day, which expands and contracts roofing materials and fasteners.
  • Winter ice. Occasional ice storms and hard freezes test flashing, valleys and any spot where water can sit.

Asphalt shingles in Texas typically last 12 to 18 years under those conditions, often less if hail strikes early. Stone coated steel is expected to last 40 to 70 years. Our guide to the best roofing material for Texas hailstorms compares how each common material holds up.

Stone coated metal roofing Grand Prairie TX: the installation process

A stone coated metal roofing Grand Prairie TX project is more involved than a shingle swap, and the details are what make it last. Here is what a typical job includes:

  1. Inspection and measurement. We look at the existing roof, attic ventilation and signs of decking damage, then measure every plane, hip and valley.
  2. Written proposal. You get the brand, profile, color, installation method, underlayment and a decking allowance in writing.
  3. Permit. We file with the City of Grand Prairie and schedule inspections.
  4. Tear-off and deck repair. Old layers come off, and soft or damaged decking is replaced so the new roof sits on a sound surface.
  5. Underlayment and battens or direct-to-deck. Batten installs leave an air gap above the deck that can help release attic heat; direct-to-deck keeps a lower profile on some designs.
  6. Panels, flashing and trim. Panels interlock and are fastened to resist uplift, with custom flashing at walls, chimneys, vents and skylights.
  7. Final walk-through. We clean up, review the roof with you and register the warranty.

What it costs, and how to think about it

For planning, stone coated steel installed in Texas runs about $10 to $18 per square foot. Treat that as a planning range, not a quote. A single-story Grand Prairie ranch with one layer of shingles and a straightforward roofline usually prices toward the lower end. Steep pitches, complex rooflines, tile or slate profiles, multiple tear-off layers and decking replacement move the number up.

The fair comparison is not one roof against one roof. It is one stone coated steel roof against the two, three or four asphalt roofs you might otherwise buy over the same span, plus the deductibles that come with hail claims. Many North Texas policies now use percentage-based wind and hail deductibles, which can make a covered claim costly out of pocket. Common concerns we hear

"Will it look like a metal building?"

No. The stone granule surface and formed profiles look like shingle, shake, tile or slate from the street. Most neighbors will not know it is steel.

"Is it loud?"

The granule coating, underlayment and solid deck absorb most of the sound. Rain on a stone coated roof sounds much like rain on shingles.

"What happens if it gets damaged?"

Stone coated steel is impact-resistant, not indestructible. Very large hail can cosmetically dent panels in some cases, and individual panels can be replaced. Our honest look at stone coated steel roof problems covers the issues worth knowing about.

Keeping the roof in shape

One of the main reasons Grand Prairie owners switch is to stop spending time and money on the roof. Stone coated steel does not need the frequent repairs that aging shingles do, but a little attention keeps it performing for decades. Clear leaves and debris from valleys and gutters, especially under mature pecans and live oaks. Look the roof over from the ground each spring and after any major storm. Every few years, have the flashing around chimneys, skylights and walls checked, since sealants there age faster than the panels.
