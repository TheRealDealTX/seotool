Denton Weather and Your Roof

Denton sits at the north end of the Dallas-Fort Worth Metroplex, where I-35E and I-35W come together. Storms that build over North Texas in spring regularly cross Denton County, and hail season generally runs from March into June. Severe thunderstorms in summer and fall add straight-line winds, and summer heat routinely pushes toward 100 degrees.

That combination is hard on asphalt shingles:

  • Hail knocks granules loose and bruises the shingle mat, even when damage is not obvious from the street.
  • Wind breaks the adhesive seal strips and lifts tabs, leaving the roof exposed to the next storm.
  • UV and heat dry out asphalt, so shingles that have lost granules crack and curl faster.

Asphalt roofs in Texas typically last 12 to 18 years, and many Denton homes see that cut short by storm damage. Stone coated steel is generally expected to last 40 to 70 years, with 50-year to lifetime manufacturer warranties. Stone Coated Metal Roofing Denton TX: What You Are Getting

Stone coated metal roofing in Denton, TX starts with a steel panel protected by a metallic coating. A layer of stone granules is bonded to the surface with an acrylic base coat, giving the roof its color and texture. The panels interlock and are mechanically fastened, which is why they resist wind uplift so well.

We use two main installation methods in Denton:

  • Batten installation, where panels fasten to wood battens over the deck. The air space reduces heat transfer into the attic and suits shake and tile profiles.
  • Direct-to-deck installation, where panels fasten directly to the deck over underlayment, common for shingle profiles.

Questions to Ask Any Denton Roofer

Whether or not you work with Stone Coated Roofs, a few questions will help you compare proposals on equal terms:

  1. Which brand and exact profile are you quoting, and is the impact rating UL 2218 Class 4?
  2. Is the installation batten or direct-to-deck, and why is that the right choice for this roof?
  3. What underlayment is included, and how will valleys, chimneys, skylights and wall transitions be flashed?
  4. How will soft or damaged decking be handled and priced if it is found during tear-off?
  5. What manufacturer warranty applies, and what workmanship coverage comes with it?

A clear written answer to each of these is a good sign. Vague answers, or pressure to sign the same day a storm passes, are not.
