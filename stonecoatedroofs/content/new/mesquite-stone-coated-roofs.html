Stone coated steel roofing Mesquite TX homeowners are switching to

A lot of people who start looking into stone coated steel roofing Mesquite TX contractors offer have the same story: the roof was replaced after a hailstorm, and a few years later it needed attention again. Mesquite sits on the east side of Dallas County, with a portion of the city reaching into Kaufman County, and it gets the full force of the spring storms that make North Texas one of the most hail-prone regions in the country. Standard asphalt shingles were never designed for that kind of repeated punishment.

Stone coated steel was. It starts with a steel panel, adds a corrosion-resistant metallic coating, then bonds a layer of natural stone granules to the surface and seals it with a clear overglaze. The result carries a Class 4 impact rating under UL 2218, a Class A fire rating, and in many systems a wind rating up to 120 mph. Pressed into shingle, shake, tile or slate profiles, it looks like a traditional roof from the street.

Eastern Dallas County weather

Mesquite's spring storm season usually starts in March and runs into June, with April and May the busiest months. Supercells moving east across the Metroplex often reach Mesquite after crossing Dallas, and the storm lines that follow them can push damaging straight-line winds across the whole east side. The region has seen what severe weather can do: the December 26, 2015 tornado outbreak caused major destruction in neighboring Garland and Rowlett, a reminder that eastern Dallas County is not sheltered from violent storms.

Most roof damage here, though, comes from the routine events: hail from pea to golf-ball size several times a season, gusty outflow winds that peel shingle tabs, and summers with long stretches of 100-degree heat that dry asphalt and loosen granules. Add the occasional ice storm or hard freeze and it is easy to see why asphalt roofs in Texas typically last 12 to 18 years. Mesquite's homes and what fits them

Much of Mesquite was built between the 1960s and the 1980s, in neighborhoods of single-story brick homes that spread out from the older core near downtown and along the US 80 and I-30 corridors. Newer subdivisions have filled in toward the east and south, near the Kaufman County line and along I-20. That housing mix shapes the right roofing choice.

Mid-century and 1970s–1980s ranches

These homes usually have moderate pitches, simple hip or gable rooflines and original plank or plywood decking. A shingle-profile stone coated steel roof is a natural fit: it looks like a heavy architectural shingle and keeps the home's familiar appearance. A shake profile is a good option if you want to add texture and depth to a plain brick front. See our stone coated wood shake roof page for examples.

Newer two-story homes

For newer construction, shingle profiles match the neighborhood look, while tile and slate profiles can give a more upscale appearance. Because stone coated steel weighs about 2.4 pounds per square foot, it does not require the structural upgrades that concrete tile (9 to 10 pounds) or clay (8 to 12) often do.

Budgeting for a stone coated steel roof in Mesquite

For planning, stone coated steel installed in Texas runs about $10 to $18 per square foot. That is a planning range, not a quote. Single-story Mesquite homes with simple rooflines often sit toward the lower half. Steeper or more complex roofs, tile or slate profiles, multiple layers to remove and decking replacement push the cost up.

The higher upfront price is easier to judge when you look at the full ownership period. If you plan to stay in your home for twenty years or longer, a stone coated steel roof may replace one or two asphalt roofs, along with the hail deductibles that typically come with them. Many North Texas policies now carry percentage-based wind and hail deductibles, which makes each claim more expensive for the homeowner. Insurance and Class 4 credits

Many Texas insurance carriers offer premium credits for Class 4 impact-resistant roofs. Across the state, those credits commonly range from 5% to 25%, depending on the carrier and policy. We cannot predict what your insurer will offer, so call your agent and ask whether a Class 4 credit applies to your policy and what documentation they need. Color, heat and warranty details

Color does more than set the look of the house. On a long Mesquite summer, lighter and mid-tone finishes generally absorb less heat than very dark ones, and a batten installation adds an air space above the deck that can help keep attic temperatures down. If your home has an older HVAC system or a lightly insulated attic, it is worth factoring that into the color choice. We bring physical samples to the estimate so you can see each finish against your brick in daylight rather than choosing from a screen.

Warranties are the other detail to read closely. Stone coated steel manufacturers typically offer coverage from 50 years to lifetime, but the terms differ: some cover granule loss, some are transferable to a future buyer, and most require installation to the manufacturer's specifications. How installation works

  1. Inspection. We check the roof, the attic and ventilation, and measure every roof plane.
  2. Proposal. A written scope lists brand, profile, color, install method and a decking allowance.
  3. Permit. We pull the City of Mesquite permit and schedule inspections.
  4. Tear-off. Old roofing comes off and damaged decking is replaced.
  5. Underlayment and panels. Panels go on either over battens, which creates an air gap that can help with attic heat, or direct to the deck.
  6. Flashing and trim. Custom flashing at walls, chimneys, vents and skylights, then ridge and hip trim.
  7. Walk-through. Cleanup, a final review with you and warranty registration.
