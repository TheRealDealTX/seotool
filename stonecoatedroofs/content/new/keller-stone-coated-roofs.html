Stone coated steel roofing Keller TX: why local roofs take a beating

If you are researching stone coated steel roofing Keller TX homeowners can live with for decades, you probably already know the problem: North Texas storms chew through ordinary shingles. Keller sits in northeast Tarrant County, squarely inside the stretch of the Dallas–Fort Worth area that sees large hail almost every spring. Add 100-degree summer afternoons, sudden winter freezes, and straight-line winds that roll in ahead of spring squall lines, and a standard asphalt roof has a hard life here.

Most Keller neighborhoods were built from the 1990s onward, which means a lot of homes are now on their second or third roof. Plenty of owners along Keller Parkway, Rufe Snow Drive and the subdivisions off Keller-Smithfield Road have filed more than one hail claim on the same house. Each new shingle roof resets the clock on a material that, in Texas, typically lasts only 12 to 18 years even without a direct hit.

Stone coated steel changes that equation. It is a steel panel, coated against corrosion, finished with bonded stone granules and an overglaze, and formed to look like tile, shake, slate or shingle. You get the curb appeal Keller buyers expect with a roof that is rated for the weather the city actually gets.

The North Texas hail climate, in plain terms

Hail season in Tarrant County usually builds in March, peaks through April and May, and can run into June. A smaller second round sometimes shows up in the fall. Storms that develop along the dryline west of the Metroplex often mature as they cross Tarrant County, which is why the northern suburbs, Keller included, see so many hail reports on the same afternoons as Fort Worth.

The region has a long memory for this. The May 5, 1995 Mayfest storm dropped very large hail across Fort Worth and caused one of the costliest hail disasters in Texas history up to that time. Storms of that scale are rare, but quarter- to golf-ball-sized hail is a near-annual event somewhere in the area. For a Keller homeowner, the question is less whether hail will hit your neighborhood and more how many times it will during the years you own the house.

What stone coated metal roofing Keller TX homes get from the system

Stone coated metal roofing Keller TX homeowners install is not the same product as a ribbed barn panel or standing seam. Each panel is a layered system:

  • Steel core that provides the strength to resist punctures and denting.
  • Metallic coating (aluminum-zinc on most brands) that protects the steel from rust.
  • Acrylic base coat and stone granules that give the roof its texture and color and soften the sound of rain.
  • Clear overglaze that locks the granules in place and helps the color hold up under UV exposure.

Choosing a profile that fits Keller neighborhoods

Keller has a mix of styles: brick traditionals in older subdivisions near Old Town Keller and Bear Creek Park, larger stone-and-stucco homes on bigger lots toward the north and west sides of town, and newer builds near the Fort Worth line in the Alliance corridor. Stone coated steel comes in profiles that suit each of them.

  • Shingle profiles look like a dimensional architectural shingle and are the easiest match for HOA guidelines written around asphalt. See our stone coated shingle roofs page.
  • Shake profiles give a rustic, deep-shadow look that works well with stone exteriors, without the fire risk and upkeep of cedar.
  • Tile profiles suit Mediterranean and Spanish-influenced homes and avoid the weight of concrete or clay.
  • Slate profiles give a flat, tailored appearance for transitional and modern designs.

We install every leading brand, including Decra, Roser, TEK, Tilcor, Boral (Westlake Royal), Gerard, Metro, Allmet and Unified Steel, so the profile and color drive the decision rather than a single manufacturer's catalog. Most Keller roofs go on with either a batten system, which creates an air gap that helps with attic heat, or a direct-to-deck method that keeps a lower profile.

What a stone coated steel roof costs in Keller

For planning, stone coated steel installed in Texas runs about $10 to $18 per square foot. That is a range for budgeting, not a quote. The things that move a Keller project within that range:

  • Total roof area and the number of hips, valleys and dormers.
  • Pitch, since steep roofs need more safety setup and slower work.
  • Profile and brand; tile and slate profiles often cost more than shingle.
  • How many existing layers come off and whether decking needs replacement.
  • Skylights, chimneys and other penetrations that need custom flashing.

The better comparison is cost over time. A Keller homeowner who replaces asphalt every 12 to 18 years, often after a hail claim with a deductible attached, can buy three or four roofs over the life of one stone coated steel system. Run your own numbers with the lifetime roof cost calculator.

Insurance, deductibles and Class 4 credits

Many Texas insurers offer premium credits for Class 4 impact-resistant roofs. Across the state, those discounts commonly fall between 5% and 25%, depending on the carrier and the policy. We cannot tell you what your specific carrier will offer, so call your agent before you commit and ask what paperwork they need. We provide the product certification and installation documentation after the job.

It is also worth reading your policy's hail and wind deductible. Many North Texas policies now use a percentage deductible, which can make even a covered claim expensive out of pocket. A roof that does not need replacing after a storm avoids that cost entirely. Our Class 4 insurance discount guide covers what to ask.

If your Keller roof was just hit by hail

After a storm, walk the property before you climb anything. Look for dented gutters and downspouts, dings on AC condenser fins, granules collecting at the bottom of downspouts, and bruised or cracked shingles visible from the ground. Our hail damage roof checklist walks you through it step by step.

If you are replacing a damaged roof through insurance anyway, that is the most cost-effective time to upgrade. Your claim covers like-kind replacement, and you pay the difference to move up to stone coated steel. Ask your adjuster how upgrades are handled on your policy before work begins.
