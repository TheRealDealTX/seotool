Why Roofs Take a Beating in Colleyville

Colleyville sits in northeast Tarrant County, just west of DFW Airport, in the middle of one of the most active hail corridors in the country. Tarrant County has a long hail history. The Mayfest storm that struck Fort Worth on May 5, 1995 is still one of the most cited hail events in Texas, and spring storms continue to roll across the county most years, typically between March and June.

Three things make large custom roofs particularly vulnerable:

  • Surface area. A big, complex roof simply catches more hail, and every valley, hip and ridge is a place where damage concentrates.
  • Steep pitches. Steep slopes on the front of many Colleyville homes look great but make repairs harder and more expensive, so each repair cycle costs more.
  • Heavy materials. Concrete and clay tile are common on Colleyville's Mediterranean and Tuscan homes. Tile can crack under hail and is heavy, so replacing it like-for-like keeps a lot of weight on the framing.

Replacing Tile With Stone Coated Steel

One of the most common Colleyville projects is converting a concrete or clay tile roof to a stone coated steel tile profile. The look stays close to the original. The weight drops dramatically.

Replacing cedar shake

Some older Colleyville homes, especially on larger wooded lots, were built with cedar shake. Wood shake weathers quickly in North Texas heat, splits under hail and raises fire concerns. A stone coated shake profile keeps the rustic texture while adding a Class A fire rating and Class 4 impact resistance.

Stone Coated Metal Roofing Colleyville TX: Installation Details

Stone coated metal roofing in Colleyville, TX is usually installed one of two ways:

  • Batten systems fasten panels to wood battens over the deck. The air space reduces heat transfer into the attic and is typical for tile and shake profiles. On a large home with a big attic, that can make a noticeable difference in summer.
  • Direct-to-deck systems fasten panels to the deck over underlayment. These are common for shingle and some slate profiles where a lower, cleaner profile is wanted.

Complex roofs need careful detailing. Turrets, curved sections, dormers, chimneys and skylights all require custom flashing and trim. Before we start, we walk the roof, document every penetration and transition, and put the plan in writing so you know exactly how each area will be handled.

Many Colleyville neighborhoods have deed restrictions or HOAs with architectural review. We prepare the spec sheets, color samples and impact and fire ratings your committee will ask for, and we wait for written approval before scheduling.

Pool Houses, Guest Quarters and Outbuildings

Many Colleyville properties have more than one roof. Pool cabanas, detached garages, guest houses, covered outdoor kitchens and barns on the larger acreage lots all take the same hail as the main house, and they often end up mismatched after years of separate repairs. Replacing them at the same time as the main roof keeps the color and profile consistent across the property and puts every structure on the same long-life, Class 4 system.

Low-slope sections, such as porch roofs or connectors between buildings, may need a different material where the pitch is too shallow for stone coated panels. We identify those areas during the inspection and detail the transitions so they look intentional and shed water correctly.

Living With a Stone Coated Steel Roof

Once it is installed, a stone coated steel roof asks very little of you. There are no tiles to crack, no shakes to split and no shingle tabs to reseal. Walking on tile roofs to service HVAC equipment, satellite dishes or holiday lighting is a common source of broken tiles in Colleyville; properly installed steel panels handle foot traffic far better when walked correctly. A yearly look at gutters, valleys, flashing and sealant around penetrations, plus a check after any major storm, covers most of it. Our roof maintenance checklist and log makes it easy to track.

When Hail Hits Colleyville

After a major storm, storm-chasing crews flood northeast Tarrant County. On a high-value home, a rushed or poorly detailed roof is an expensive mistake. Document the damage, contact your insurer and get a thorough inspection. If your claim covers replacing the roof, ask about paying the difference to upgrade to stone coated steel so the next storm is far less likely to start the process over.
