Stone Coated Steel Roofing Frisco TX Homes Can Rely On

Looking into stone coated steel roofing, Frisco, TX? You are probably tired of replacing shingles every time a spring storm rolls up the Dallas North Tollway. Stone coated steel gives you a roof made of interlocking steel panels with a bonded stone-granule finish, so it looks like a traditional shingle, shake, tile or slate roof while standing up to North Texas hail and wind far better than asphalt. Stone Coated Roofs installs these systems across Frisco, from older streets near Frisco Square to newer sections north of Panther Creek Parkway.

Frisco grew fast. Most of the city's homes were built in the last twenty-five years, which means a very large share of neighborhoods are on their first or second asphalt roof right now. Those builder-grade shingles were never meant to take decades of Collin and Denton County hail. When it is time to replace, many homeowners are asking whether they should put on another round of shingles or move to something that can last as long as they own the house.

Why Frisco Roofs Wear Out Early

Frisco sits squarely in one of the most hail-prone metro areas in the country. The spring storm season, roughly March through June, brings supercells that form west of the Metroplex and track east across Denton and Collin Counties. Hail does not need to punch a hole to shorten a shingle roof's life. Repeated strikes knock granules loose, bruise the mat underneath and expose asphalt to UV, which speeds up cracking and curling.

  • Hail: Frequent spring hail events, sometimes several in one season, leave bruising that is hard to see from the ground.
  • Wind: Straight-line winds from thunderstorm outflow lift shingle tabs and break the seal strips that hold them down.
  • Heat: Long Frisco summers with temperatures near or above 100 degrees bake asphalt and dry out the oils that keep it flexible.
  • Rapid temperature swings: A warm afternoon followed by a blue norther can stress fasteners and flashing on any roof.

Asphalt shingle roofs in Texas typically last 12 to 18 years, and in a hail-heavy pocket like Frisco that number can drop if the roof takes several hits. Stone coated steel is generally expected to last 40 to 70 years, which is why it shows up more often on homes along Legacy Drive, Main Street and the newer communities off US 380. Our guide to how long stone coated steel roofs last explains what drives that range.

Frisco Stone Coated Steel Roofing and Hail Ratings

The most common reason Frisco homeowners call us is hail. Frisco stone coated steel roofing systems from the major manufacturers carry a UL 2218 Class 4 impact rating, the highest rating in that standard. The test drops a two-inch steel ball on the panel and checks for cracking or failure. Steel dents before it fails, and the textured stone surface helps hide minor dents that do happen.

A Class 4 rating also matters for insurance. Many Texas carriers offer premium discounts for Class 4 roofs, commonly in the 5% to 25% range depending on the carrier. That is a planning figure, not a promise, so check with your agent. Our insurance discount estimator can help you see how a discount might add up over the years you plan to stay in the home, and our page on Class 4 stone coated roofing covers the testing in more detail.

Wind and fire performance

Stone coated steel panels interlock and fasten mechanically, so wind has much less edge to grab than it does with a shingle tab. Many systems are rated for winds up to 120 mph. The systems also carry a Class A fire rating, the highest available, which is worth knowing for homes that back up to open space or greenbelts.

Working With Frisco HOAs

A large share of Frisco homes sit inside master-planned communities with architectural review committees. Starwood, Phillips Creek Ranch, Newman Village, Hollyhock, Richwoods and the Fields development all have design standards, and most require an application before any roof replacement that changes material, color or profile.

Frisco stone coated metal roofing works well in these communities because it can match the neighborhood's existing look. A weathered-wood shingle profile can sit next to an asphalt roof and read the same from the street. Here is how we typically handle it:

  1. We review your HOA's published roofing guidelines and note any color or profile restrictions.
  2. We pull manufacturer spec sheets, color samples and impact and fire ratings for the products that meet those rules.
  3. You submit the architectural review request with that package, or we help you prepare it.
  4. Installation is scheduled after written approval, so you are not caught with a roof the committee did not sign off on.

If you manage a community and are looking at a multi-home or amenity-center project, our HOA roofing replacement page covers how we approach larger scopes.

Brands and Install Methods We Use in Frisco

Stone Coated Roofs installs every leading brand: Decra, Roser, TEK, Tilcor, Boral (Westlake Royal), Gerard, Metro, Allmet and Unified Steel. That matters because brands differ in profile options, color palettes and warranty terms, and an HOA may have already approved one manufacturer's colors. We are not locked into a single product line, so we can recommend what fits your home and your association.

For most Frisco homes we use one of two methods:

  • Batten installation: Panels are fastened to wood battens over the deck. The air gap under the panels helps with heat transfer, which matters during Frisco's long summers, and battens are typical for shake and tile profiles.
  • Direct-to-deck installation: Panels fasten straight to the roof deck over underlayment. This is common for shingle profiles and lower roof heights where the HOA wants a close match to the existing roofline.

Foam-set installation is mostly reserved for high-wind coastal work, so it rarely comes up in Frisco.

What Stone Coated Steel Costs in Frisco

As a planning range, stone coated steel installed in Texas runs about $10 to $18 per square foot of roof area. Frisco homes tend to have multiple gables, hips and valleys, and many two-story homes have steep sections over the front elevation, all of which push a project toward the middle or upper part of that range. Simpler single-story roofs land lower.

The upfront cost is higher than asphalt. The comparison that matters is total cost over time. If a shingle roof lasts 12 to 18 years in North Texas, a homeowner who stays put could replace it two or three times in the life of one steel roof, and each replacement brings tear-off, disposal, deductibles and disruption. Our stone coated roof cost calculator gives you a rough budget for your roof size, and you can compare that figure against two or three rounds of shingle replacement over the same period.

Frisco Roof Replacement After Hail

When a storm moves through Frisco, roofing crews from all over the state follow it. Before you sign anything, document the damage, get an inspection and understand what your policy covers. Our hail damage roof checklist, found in our free tools, walks you through what to photograph and what to ask your adjuster.

If your insurance claim covers replacement of an asphalt roof, you may be able to pay the difference to upgrade to stone coated steel. Your insurer pays what your policy owes for the damaged roof, and you cover the upgrade cost. With a Class 4 roof in place, the next hailstorm is far less likely to lead to another claim. Our guide to roof replacement after a hail storm covers the process step by step.
