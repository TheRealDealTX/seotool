01 · What it is

What is stone coated steel roofing?

Stone coated steel roofing is a steel panel sealed in an aluminum-zinc alloy and finished with real stone granules. It looks like slate, shake, tile or shingle — and performs like steel.

  1. Steel coreStructural strength that flexes under hail instead of cracking.
  2. Aluminum-zinc coatingCorrosion protection on both faces of the steel.
  3. Stone granulesColor, texture and sound damping — bonded in acrylic.
  4. OverglazeLocks the stone in and keeps the finish from fading.

03 · Performance

Storm resistant roofs for every Texas threat.

Hail in DFW, hurricanes on the Gulf, heat everywhere. Here's how stone coated steel roofing compares where it counts.

Best roof material for hail

Hail is the number-one reason Texans replace roofs. Stone coated steel carries the top UL 2218 Class 4 rating — a 2-inch steel ball dropped from 20 feet without cracking. The steel flexes; the granules hide minor marks. That's why Stone Coated Roofs recommends it as the best roof for Texas hail storms.

Read the guide →

Hurricane resistant roofing

Interlocking panels mechanically lock to each other and the deck, so there's no seal strip for wind to peel. Standard systems are rated to 120 mph; foam-set and enhanced fastening schedules are used for hurricane-proof roofing on the Texas coast.

Built for Texas sun

Asphalt bakes, curls and sheds granules in Texas heat. Steel doesn't soften, and a batten install leaves a vented air space that reduces attic heat gain. Ceramic-coated stone holds its color for decades.

Class A fire rating

A non-combustible steel substrate earns Class A — the highest fire rating — which matters for insurers and HOAs, especially where cedar shake is common.

04 · Honest answers

Stone coated steel roofing problems — and how we prevent them.

Every roof has weak points. Most stone coated complaints come from installers who don't specialize. Here's what goes wrong and how Stone Coated Roofs avoids it. Full problems guide →

Problem 01

Leaks at flashings

Almost every stone coated leak traces to a penetration — chimney, skylight, pipe boot — flashed like a shingle roof. We use the manufacturer's own trim and flashing details.

Problem 02

Granule loss

A little loose stone after install is normal. Ongoing shedding signals a cheap product or foot traffic damage. We install only tier-one lines and walk panels correctly.

Problem 03

Dents & scuffs

Hail rarely dents stone coated steel, but careless installers do. Specialist crews step on the panel's strong points, not the middle.

Problem 04

Noise myths

Installed over solid decking and underlayment, it's no louder than shingles in rain.

Problem 05

Higher upfront cost

It costs more on day one — and less over 40 years. Run the lifetime calculator to see your break-even.

Problem 06

Finding a real installer

Most roofers install stone coated a few times a year. Stone Coated Roofs installs nothing else.

13 · Install methods

How it gets on the roof.

Three install systems, each with trade-offs in cost, performance and deck compatibility. We pick the right one for your building.

M.01

Batten

Horizontal battens fastened across the deck; panels hook onto them. A ventilated air space beneath gives the best thermal performance — the most common residential system.

M.02

Direct-to-Deck

Panels fastened straight through the deck with no batten layer. Lower install cost and simpler detailing — popular on lower pitches.

M.03

Foam-Set

Adhesive foam bonds panels to the underlayment without penetrating the deck. Exceptional wind-uplift performance — the choice for high-wind coastal homes.

FAQ

Stone coated steel roofing FAQ

What is stone coated steel roofing?

Stone coated steel roofing is a steel panel protected by an aluminum-zinc coating, then finished with acrylic-bonded stone granules. Pressed into shingle, shake, tile or slate profiles, it looks like a traditional roof but weighs about 2.4 lb per sq ft and carries Class 4 hail and Class A fire ratings. Read the full guide.

How much does stone coated steel roofing cost in Texas?

Plan on roughly $10–$18 per square foot installed, depending on profile, pitch, tear-off and brand. Try the stone coated roof cost calculator for a range on your home, or request a free estimate from Stone Coated Roofs.

What are the problems with stone coated steel roofing?

The real problems are installation-driven: leaks at poorly flashed penetrations, granule shedding on cheap products, and scuffing from careless foot traffic. Higher upfront cost is the main trade-off. A specialist installer avoids most of them — see stone coated steel roofing problems.

Is stone coated steel the best roof material for hail?

For most Texas homes, yes. It carries the top UL 2218 Class 4 impact rating, and because the steel flexes rather than fractures, large hail usually leaves no functional damage. Compare options in our best roof material for hail guide.

Are stone coated roofs hurricane resistant?

Interlocking stone coated panels are wind-rated to 120 mph and higher, and foam-set or high-wind fastening schedules push uplift resistance further for the Gulf Coast. See hurricane resistant roofing systems.

Will a stone coated roof lower my insurance?

Many Texas carriers discount Class 4 roofs — commonly 5% to 25% — and some restore coverage on homes they'd otherwise non-renew. Estimate yours with the insurance discount estimator.

Is a stone coated steel roof loud in the rain?

No. The stone granules and the air gap of a batten install break up the sound, so rain sounds much like it does on shingles.
