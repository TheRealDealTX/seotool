What we do

What a Washington Insurance Appraiser does when a claim comes up short

Most underpaid claims in Washington aren’t denied. They’re trimmed. A roof gets two slopes instead of four. A soaked hallway is left out of the flooring line. A Douglas fir that cracked the rafters is treated as a shingle repair. Washington Insurance Appraiser exists for the gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

We are appraisers, not adjusters or attorneys. Our job is one question: what does it really cost to put this property back the way it was? We answer it with our own inspection, our own measurements and pricing for your county.

  1. From scratch. We scope the loss ourselves. We never mark up the insurer’s estimate.
  2. Defensible. Every line is something we can support in front of an umpire.
  3. Honest triage. If you need an attorney or the OIC instead, we say so on the first call.

The process

How Washington Insurance Appraiser moves a stuck claim to a signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result.

  1. 1

    Put the disagreement on paper

    Appraisal starts with a claim the insurer has accepted at least in part.

  2. 2

    Send the written demand

    If the gap remains, either side may demand appraisal in writing.

  3. 3

    Each side names an appraiser

    Each party picks a competent, independent appraiser, often within 20 days of the demand.

  4. 4

    The appraisers choose an umpire

    The two appraisers agree on a neutral umpire, ideally early so one is ready if needed.

  5. 5

    The appraisers work the numbers

    Most of the work happens here.

  6. 6

    The umpire decides what’s left

    Items the appraisers can’t settle go to the umpire.

  7. 7

    The award is signed and paid

    An award signed by any two of the three sets the amount of loss.

Choosing a path

Appraisal, an OIC complaint or an attorney?

Each solves a different problem. Picking the right one first saves time and money, and Washington Insurance Appraiser will point you elsewhere when appraisal isn’t the right tool.

What we do

Appraisal

For when the insurer accepts coverage but you disagree on the amount. Two appraisers and an umpire set a binding number.

  • Scope and price disputes
  • Binding award on value
  • Each side pays its appraiser
Regulator

OIC complaint

For when the handling is the problem: delays, silence, unexplained decisions. The Office of the Insurance Commissioner asks the insurer to respond.

  • Free to file
  • Insurer must answer the regulator
  • Not a lawsuit

Attorney & IFCA

For denials or conduct you believe was unreasonable. Washington’s Insurance Fair Conduct Act may provide remedies. Only a lawyer can advise you.

  • Coverage denials
  • Bad-faith concerns
  • Notice rules apply

FAQ

Questions people ask Washington Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All FAQs Claim glossary
What does a Washington Insurance Appraiser do?

A Washington Insurance Appraiser values property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree about the cost. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

How is an appraiser different from a public adjuster?

A public adjuster manages and negotiates the whole claim for you. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When should I call a Washington Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the insurer’s estimate, and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful, roughly more than 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is an appraisal award binding?

Yes, on the amount of loss, once any two of the three panel members sign it. That’s why documentation matters so much before you demand appraisal.

What does appraisal cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my part of Washington?

Yes. We work in all 39 counties, with regular work around Seattle, Spokane, Tacoma, Vancouver, Bellevue, Everett, Olympia, Bellingham, Yakima, the Tri-Cities, Wenatchee and Bremerton.
