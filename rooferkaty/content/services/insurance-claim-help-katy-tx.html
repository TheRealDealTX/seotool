Texas law: Contractors may not waive, pay or rebate your insurance deductible (Texas Business and Commerce Code Section 27.02). Anyone who offers to is breaking the law and putting your claim at risk.

Step by Step: How a Roof Claim Usually Works

  1. Get an inspection. Start with a free roof inspection so you know whether damage exists before you file.
  2. Review your policy. Note your deductible, any separate wind and hail deductible, and whether your roof is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value.
  3. Report the claim. Contact your insurer or agent. Many Texas policies require prompt reporting, so do not wait.
  4. Protect the home. Your policy likely expects you to prevent further damage. Tarping counts and is often reimbursable, so keep receipts.
  5. Adjuster inspection. The insurer sends an adjuster. We can be there to walk the roof together.
  6. Receive the scope and payment. The insurer issues an estimate and initial payment, minus your deductible and any depreciation held back.
  7. Complete the work. After the roof is done, depreciation held back on RCV policies is often released once you submit the final invoice.

Understanding the Terms on Your Estimate

TermWhat it means
RCV (replacement cost value)What it costs to replace the roof with similar materials today.
ACV (actual cash value)Replacement cost minus depreciation for age and wear.
Recoverable depreciationMoney held back on many RCV policies until work is completed.
Wind/hail deductibleA separate deductible, often a percentage of dwelling coverage, that applies to these storms on many Texas policies.
Code upgrade coverageOptional coverage for items required by current building code.

Why Documentation Matters

Adjusters handle large volumes of claims after major storms like the 2024 derecho and Hurricane Beryl. A clear, organized set of photos helps make sure nothing gets overlooked. Our documentation includes overview shots of each slope, close-ups of hail hits and wind creases with a reference for scale, and photos of damaged vents, flashing, gutters and downspouts. It also notes items that often get missed on estimates, such as drip edge, starter shingles, ridge caps and pipe boots. You keep a copy, so you have your own record no matter what you decide.

If Your Claim Is Underpaid or Denied

Disagreements happen. Sometimes an adjuster misses a slope, attributes hail bruises to wear, or leaves out items required to do the job correctly. If that happens, we can sit down with you, compare our photos to the insurer's scope and explain where they differ. You can then decide whether to request a re-inspection or supplement through your insurer. For larger disputes, you may want to talk with a licensed public adjuster or an attorney.
