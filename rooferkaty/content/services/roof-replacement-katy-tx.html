Roof replacement in Katy, TX is a different job than it is in milder climates. Our roofs bake through long summers, soak up Gulf humidity, take spring hail and have to hold through tropical storm season. A replacement done right accounts for all of that, from the decking and underlayment to the ventilation and the last ridge cap. Done wrong, it can fail years early. This page explains how we approach a full re-roof, what it typically costs, and how to decide whether replacement or repair makes more sense for your home.

When a Roof Replacement in Katy TX Makes Sense

Not every worn or damaged roof needs to come off. Plenty of problems can be repaired. But replacement usually becomes the smarter investment when you see a few of these signs together:

  • Age: Many of the original three-tab and early architectural roofs on 1990s and 2000s Katy homes are at or past their useful life in our heat.
  • Widespread granule loss: Bald spots and heavy granules in the gutters mean the asphalt is exposed to UV and breaking down.
  • Hail or wind damage across multiple slopes: Scattered bruises, creases and missing tabs on several sides of the house rarely make sense to patch.
  • Repeated leaks: If you have repaired the same area more than once, the problem may be the system, not the spot.
  • Soft or sagging decking: This points to long-term moisture and should be addressed before it spreads.

What Our Roof Replacement Includes

A roof is a system, and every layer matters in Katy's climate. A complete replacement from us covers:

  1. Protection and tear-off. We protect landscaping, AC units and pool areas, then remove all existing layers down to the deck.
  2. Deck inspection and repair. Damaged or rotted plywood or OSB is replaced so the new roof has a solid nailing surface.
  3. Underlayment and leak barriers. Synthetic underlayment across the field, with ice-and-water style membrane in valleys and around penetrations where water concentrates during heavy rain.
  4. Flashing and drip edge. New metal at walls, chimneys, valleys and eaves rather than reusing tired, rusted pieces.
  5. Ventilation. Balanced intake and exhaust so the attic can shed heat and moisture.
  6. Shingles or metal panels. Installed to manufacturer instructions and local code, with high-wind nailing patterns.
  7. Cleanup. Debris hauled off and a magnetic sweep of the driveway, yard and beds for nails.

Choosing Materials for Katy Heat, Wind and Hail

Architectural asphalt shingles

The most common choice in Katy subdivisions. They look good, meet nearly every HOA standard and offer solid wind ratings when installed correctly. Lighter colors and reflective options can help with attic temperatures.

Class 4 impact-resistant shingles

Given how often hail moves through the west Houston area, many homeowners upgrade to Class 4 impact-rated shingles. Some insurers offer premium discounts for them, so it is worth asking your agent. Our guide to Class 4 impact-resistant shingles in Texas covers the details.

Standing-seam metal

Metal costs more up front but handles heat, wind and heavy rain extremely well and lasts a long time. See our metal roofing page if you are considering it.

Roof Replacement Cost in Katy: Planning Ranges

Every roof is different, so treat these as planning numbers, not quotes. In the Houston area, architectural asphalt shingle replacement generally runs roughly $4.50 to $7.50 per square foot of roof area installed. Standing-seam metal runs roughly $10 to $16 per square foot. A typical Katy home has about 2,400 to 3,200 square feet of roof area, which is more than the living space because of overhangs, pitch and garages.

HOA Rules and Permits in Katy Neighborhoods

From Cinco Ranch to Cane Island, many Katy communities have HOAs that control roof color and material. Some require an architectural review application before work starts, even if you are matching the existing roof. We provide product names, color samples and spec sheets so your application is complete the first time. Permit requirements vary between the City of Katy, unincorporated Harris, Fort Bend and Waller counties and nearby cities, and we confirm what applies to your address.

Tip: Submit your HOA application as soon as you pick a color. Review boards often meet on a fixed schedule, and approval is usually the longest wait in the process.
