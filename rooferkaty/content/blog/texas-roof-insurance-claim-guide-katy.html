A roof insurance claim Texas homeowners file after a storm does not have to be a headache, but it does reward preparation. Insurers want to see that damage is real, recent and caused by a covered event like wind or hail. The homeowners who have the smoothest experience are usually the ones who documented their roof early, understood their deductible and had a reliable roofer on site when the adjuster came out.

This guide covers the process the way it typically plays out in Katy and the rest of the Houston area. Policies vary a lot, so treat this as a general roadmap and always check your own policy or talk to your agent for specifics.

Step 1: Confirm there is real storm damage

Before you call your insurer, find out whether the roof was actually damaged. Not every storm causes enough damage to justify a claim, and some insurers note claims inquiries on your record even if you never get paid.

Signs worth investigating include dented gutters and vents, missing or creased shingles, granules piled at downspouts, torn screens and new ceiling stains. Our article on how to spot hail damage on your Katy roof covers the details.

Step 2: Understand your policy and deductible

Pull out your declarations page and look for three things:

  • Wind and hail deductible. Many Texas policies use a separate deductible for wind and hail, often a percentage of your dwelling coverage. On a home insured for $400,000, a 2% deductible is $8,000.
  • Replacement cost or actual cash value. Replacement cost policies generally pay to replace the roof, often in two payments. Actual cash value policies subtract depreciation, which can be significant on an older roof.
  • Roof-specific endorsements. Some policies limit coverage for older roofs or for cosmetic damage, such as dents to metal roofing that do not affect function.
About deductibles: You are responsible for your deductible. Under Texas Business and Commerce Code §27.02, a contractor cannot legally waive, rebate or absorb it. An offer to "cover" your deductible is a red flag.

Step 3: File the roof insurance claim in Texas promptly

Report damage as soon as you reasonably can. Most policies require prompt notice, and many set a firm deadline measured from the date of the storm. You can usually file by phone, online or through your insurer's app. Have this ready:

  • Your policy number
  • The date of the storm and a short description
  • Photos of damage, inside and outside
  • Receipts for any emergency repairs or tarping

Texas has prompt-payment rules that generally require insurers to acknowledge a claim, request needed information and make a decision within set timeframes once they have what they need. Write down your claim number, the adjuster's name and every date you speak with the insurer.

Prevent further damage

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to stop additional damage, like tarping a leaking roof or moving belongings. Keep receipts. Do not start permanent repairs or a full replacement before the adjuster has seen the roof.

Step 4: The adjuster inspection

The insurance company will send an adjuster, either a staff adjuster or an independent adjuster hired for storm volume. After big Katy-area events, like the 2024 derecho and Hurricane Beryl, adjusters are stretched thin, so it can take time to get on the calendar.

The adjuster will typically:

  1. Walk the exterior and inspect gutters, siding, windows and soft metals.
  2. Get on the roof, inspect each slope and look for hail hits or wind creasing.
  3. Check interior damage, if any.
  4. Take photos and measurements, then prepare an estimate, often in a program called Xactimate.

Having your roofer present is useful. The roofer can point out damage the adjuster might miss, explain conditions like hidden flashing problems, and make sure measurements and line items are accurate. Step 5: Review the estimate and payment

After the inspection, you will receive an estimate and, if approved, a payment. Read the estimate line by line. Common things to check:

  • Is the roof area measured correctly, including waste factor?
  • Are drip edge, starter shingles, ridge cap, pipe boots and flashing included?
  • Is steep-slope or high-roof labor accounted for where it applies?
  • Are code-required items addressed, and does your policy include ordinance or law coverage?
  • Is gutter, fence or window damage included if it was found?

With replacement cost policies, the first check is often the actual cash value, and the held-back depreciation is released after the work is completed and invoiced. Your mortgage company may also be named on the check, which means it has to endorse it before funds are released.

If something is missing

If the estimate leaves out legitimate damage, you can ask for a supplement or reinspection. Provide additional photos and a contractor's itemized estimate. Many policies also include an appraisal clause for disputes over the amount of loss. If you are unsure of your rights, the Texas Department of Insurance has consumer resources, and an attorney or licensed public adjuster can advise on complicated disputes.

When a roof insurance claim Texas insurers would pay is not worth filing

Sometimes the honest answer is to skip the claim. If the damage is limited to a few missing shingles or a cracked pipe boot, the repair may cost less than your wind and hail deductible, and filing gains you nothing. A small, simple repair is usually the better route in that case.

It can also make sense to hold off when the roof is near the end of its life anyway and your policy pays actual cash value. Depreciation on a 20-year-old roof can shrink the payout to the point where you are mostly paying for a new roof yourself. In that situation, it may be worth pricing a planned replacement, and possibly an upgrade to Class 4 impact-resistant shingles, which some insurers reward with a premium discount.

When damage clearly exceeds the deductible, though, filing is what your coverage is for. Do not talk yourself out of a legitimate claim.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Signing with a door-to-door contractor on the spot. Take time to check that a company is local and has a real track record.
  • Letting a contractor "handle everything" with your insurer. Stay involved; it is your policy.
  • Throwing away damaged items or making permanent repairs before the adjuster sees them.
  • Waiting too long. Damage that goes unreported can be harder to tie to a specific storm.
  • Accepting an offer to waive your deductible. It is illegal in Texas and can jeopardize your claim.
