The roof repair vs replacement question usually shows up at a bad moment: a brown stain spreads across the ceiling after a July thunderstorm, or a neighbor's contractor knocks on your door after a hail event and says your whole roof is "totaled." Both reactions, panic and suspicion, are understandable. The better approach is a simple framework that looks at the roof's age, the type and spread of the damage, what insurance may cover, and the real cost of keeping an aging roof alive. This guide walks through that framework with Katy homes in mind.

Why the Roof Repair vs Replacement Decision Is Different in Katy

Katy roofs work harder than roofs in milder climates. Long summers bake asphalt shingles until the oils that keep them flexible dry out. Gulf Coast humidity encourages algae streaking and keeps decking damp when ventilation is poor. Spring brings hail and straight-line winds, and hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30. The May 2024 Houston derecho and Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 were reminders that a single event can age a roof by years overnight.

Housing stock matters too. Large parts of Cinco Ranch, Kelliwood and Nottingham Country were built in the 1990s and early 2000s, so many of those homes are on their second roof or due for one. Newer communities like Elyson, Jordan Ranch and Cane Island have younger roofs where a targeted repair is often the right call. Knowing roughly when your roof was installed is the first step.

Start With the Age of the Roof

Age is not the whole story, but it frames everything else. Use these rough ranges for architectural asphalt shingles, the most common roof on Katy homes:

  • Under 10 years: Repair is almost always the answer unless there is widespread storm damage. Shingles still have flexibility and granules, and patches blend well.
  • 10 to 17 years: The gray zone. Repairs make sense for isolated issues, but look closely at granule loss, curling and brittleness before investing heavily.
  • 18 years and older: Replacement becomes the stronger option. Old shingles crack when a roofer lifts them to make a repair, and new patches will not match.

Three-tab shingles, still found on some older homes near Old Town Katy and along the I-10 corridor, tend to wear out sooner. Metal and tile follow very different timelines.

Look at the Damage Pattern, Not Just the Leak

A single leak tells you water got in somewhere. It does not tell you why. When we inspect a roof, we are trying to answer whether the problem is localized or systemic.

Signs a repair will do the job

  • A few shingles lifted or torn off by wind on one slope
  • A cracked or dried-out rubber pipe boot (one of the most common leak sources we see)
  • Failed flashing at a chimney, wall or skylight
  • Minor damage from a fallen limb that did not break the decking
  • Exposed nail heads or a small area of lifted ridge cap

Signs replacement deserves a serious look

  • Hail bruising or granule loss spread across multiple slopes
  • Widespread curling, cupping or cracking of shingles
  • Soft or sagging decking in more than one area
  • Repeated leaks in different spots over the past couple of years
  • Large bare patches where granules have washed into the gutters

If you have had a storm recently, our guide on how to spot hail damage on your Katy roof shows what to look for from the ground without climbing a ladder.

Safety first: Do not walk on a wet, steep or storm-damaged roof. Most of what you need to know can be seen from the ground with binoculars, from the attic with a flashlight, or from photos taken during a professional inspection.

Run the Cost Math Honestly

Repairs feel cheaper because the invoice is smaller. Over a few years, that is not always true. A fair way to compare is to think in terms of cost per remaining year of roof life.

Say you have a 19-year-old roof and you are quoted a moderate repair. If that roof realistically has two or three years left, you are paying that amount for a short extension, and you may need another repair next spring. Compare that to replacement. As a planning range only, architectural asphalt shingle replacement in the Houston area runs roughly $4.50 to $7.50 per square foot of roof area installed. A typical Katy home has about 2,400 to 3,200 square feet of roof area, so the total depends heavily on size, pitch, layers to tear off and decking condition. How Insurance Changes the Roof Repair vs Replacement Math

When hail or wind damage is the cause, insurance can shift the decision. If an adjuster finds covered storm damage across enough of the roof, many Texas policies will pay toward a full replacement minus your deductible. If damage is limited, the claim may cover only a repair, or fall below the deductible altogether.

A few points worth knowing:

  • Policies differ on whether they pay replacement cost or actual cash value, and many Texas policies now apply a percentage deductible for wind and hail. Check your declarations page.
  • Older roofs are sometimes covered on a depreciated basis, which can change the math considerably.
  • Your deductible is your responsibility. Any contractor who offers to "cover" or waive it is breaking Texas law (Business & Commerce Code §27.02), and that is a red flag.

Other Factors That Tip the Scale

HOA rules

Many Katy communities, including Cinco Ranch, Cross Creek Ranch and Grand Lakes, have architectural guidelines on shingle color and style. If your exact shingle has been discontinued, a visible patch may not pass HOA review, which can push you toward a full slope or full roof replacement.

Plans for the house

Selling within a year or two? Buyers' inspectors flag aging roofs, and a new roof can remove a major negotiating point. Staying for another decade? Replacement lets you upgrade to impact-resistant shingles, better ventilation or metal, which may lower energy bills or insurance premiums depending on your carrier.

Decking and ventilation

Repeated leaks often trace back to poor attic ventilation that cooks shingles from below and lets moisture condense on the decking. A repair does not address that. A replacement is the cleanest time to fix intake and exhaust venting properly.

A Simple Decision Checklist

  1. Find out the roof's installation year from closing documents, permits or the previous owner.
  2. Note any recent storm dates. Weather events matter for insurance timelines.
  3. Check the attic for stains, daylight or damp insulation.
  4. Look for granules in gutters and at downspout outlets.
  5. Get a professional inspection with photos of every slope.
  6. Compare a written repair scope with a replacement estimate, using cost per remaining year.
