Picking a roofer in Katy should be simple. In practice, it is one of the harder hiring decisions a homeowner makes. The job is expensive, the work happens over your head where you cannot easily check it, and after every major storm the area fills with door-knockers, yard signs and trucks with out-of-state plates. This guide covers what actually separates a dependable contractor from a risky one, starting with a fact that surprises many people: Texas does not license roofers at all.

Texas Has No State Roofing License

Plumbers and electricians in Texas need state licenses. Residential roofers do not. There is no state exam, no state license number to look up, and no state board that will revoke a roofer's ability to work. Anyone with a ladder and a truck can start a roofing company tomorrow.

Some contractors join voluntary programs through industry associations, and some cities require permits or contractor registration, but none of that replaces your own homework. Because the state does not screen roofers for you, the burden falls on you to check:

  • Insurance: Ask for a certificate of general liability insurance, and ask about workers' compensation or how injured workers are covered. Call the insurer listed to confirm it is active.
  • A real local presence: A verifiable phone number that is answered, and a history of work in the area you can confirm.
  • References and reviews: Recent jobs in Katy neighborhoods, ideally ones you can drive past.
  • A written contract: Scope, materials, price, payment schedule, cleanup and how warranty claims are handled.

Why Storm Chasers Target Katy

Katy sits in a busy weather corridor. Spring hail, summer thunderstorms and hurricane season all hit the same roofs, and big events like the May 2024 derecho and Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 brought waves of outside contractors into the Houston area. These crews follow the storm, sign as many contracts as they can, and move on to the next town when the work dries up.

Not every out-of-town crew does bad work. The problem is what happens afterward. If a flashing detail fails eighteen months later, or a leak appears during the next tropical storm, you need someone who will answer the phone and come back. A storm chaser's phone number may be disconnected by then.

Common storm chaser red flags

  • Unsolicited door-knocking within a day or two of a storm, often with pressure to sign "today"
  • Offers to waive, rebate or "cover" your insurance deductible, which is illegal in Texas (Business & Commerce Code §27.02)
  • Asking you to sign a contract or "authorization" before an inspection or before your insurer has reviewed the claim
  • No local phone number, or a phone number that goes straight to a generic voicemail
  • Vague contracts with no material names, no scope and no payment schedule
  • Demands for a large cash deposit before any materials are delivered
  • Claims to be able to "guarantee" your claim will be approved
Watch the fine print: Some door-to-door "inspection agreements" actually commit you to that contractor if your claim is approved. Read every document before signing, and never sign anything on the spot after a storm.

Questions to Ask Any Roofer in Katy

A good contractor will be glad you asked these. A weak one will get vague or defensive.

  1. Where can I see your recent work nearby? Addresses in Cinco Ranch, Cross Creek Ranch, Firethorne or wherever you live let you see the result in person.
  2. Who will actually be on my roof? Ask whether the company uses its own crews or subcontractors, and who supervises the job.
  3. Will you tear off the old roof or overlay? Tear-off is the standard for a quality job, because it exposes the decking for inspection.
  4. How do you handle rotted decking? The contract should state a per-sheet price for decking replacement so there are no surprises.
  5. What about ventilation, flashing and drip edge? These details determine how long the roof lasts in Katy heat. Ask whether pipe boots, flashing and vents are replaced or reused.
  6. How do you protect my property? Landscaping, pool, driveway and the daily magnetic sweep for nails all matter.
  7. What warranty covers workmanship, and what does the manufacturer cover? Get both in writing.
  8. Do you handle HOA approval? Many Katy HOAs require submission of shingle color and style before work begins.

Insurance Claims: What a Roofer Should and Should Not Do

When storm damage is involved, a contractor can be a real help. A good roofer documents the damage with photos, explains what they found, and meets the adjuster on site to point out damage that is easy to miss. That is what we do for homeowners through our insurance claim help.

Local Knowledge Matters More Than You Think

Experienced roofers in Katy know things a crew from out of state may not. They know which HOAs in Cinco Ranch, Grand Lakes or Seven Meadows require architectural review. They know the area spans Harris, Fort Bend and Waller counties, with different permitting realities. They know which shingle colors fade fastest in the Gulf Coast sun, and why attic ventilation is so important when summer attic temperatures soar. They also know traffic and access, which matters when a crew has to haul a dumpster down a tight cul-de-sac off Mason Rd or FM 1463.

Local also means accountable. A roofer in Katy who lives and works here has a reputation to protect in the same neighborhoods where you shop and your kids go to Katy ISD schools.
