Most Katy homeowners have lived through enough Gulf storms to know the drill: stock water, fill the gas tank, bring in the patio furniture. The roof tends to get skipped. That is a problem, because your roof is the one part of the house that takes the full force of wind and sideways rain for hours at a time. Good hurricane roof preparation Katy TX homeowners can do is mostly simple, inexpensive and best done well before a storm has a name.

This checklist is built around what we actually see on Katy-area roofs after tropical systems: lifted shingle tabs, failed pipe boots, overflowing gutters, and debris from trees that were planted too close to the house twenty years ago. The derecho in May 2024 and Hurricane Beryl that July were reminders that our area does not need a direct Category 3 hit to see real roof damage.

Why Katy roofs are vulnerable during hurricane season

Katy sits far enough inland that storm surge is not the concern. Wind and rain are. Tropical systems often bring hours of sustained wind with stronger gusts, and the rain does not always fall straight down. Wind-driven rain gets pushed under shingle edges, through tired flashing and up past worn sealant around vents.

A few local factors make it worse:

  • Roof age. Large parts of Cinco Ranch, Kelliwood and Nottingham Country were built in the 1990s and 2000s. Many of those homes are on their second roof, and some are still on their first. Older three-tab shingles lose their seal strip adhesion over time and lift more easily.
  • Complex rooflines. Many newer homes in Cane Island, Elyson and Cross Creek Ranch have multiple gables, valleys and dormers. Every valley and wall intersection is a spot where water concentrates.
  • Heat cycling. Long Katy summers bake shingles and dry out rubber pipe boots. By September, cracked boots are one of the most common leak sources we find.
  • Mature trees. Live oaks and pines that shade older neighborhoods also drop limbs onto roofs in high wind.

Step 1: Inspect your roof from the ground

You do not need to climb a ladder to learn a lot. Walk around the house on a clear morning with a pair of binoculars or your phone's zoom and look for:

  • Shingles that are missing, curled, cracked or sitting slightly raised at the edges
  • Bare spots where the granules have worn away, often on the south and west slopes
  • Flashing around chimneys, walls and skylights that looks bent, rusted or pulled away
  • Pipe boots and vents that look cracked, warped or loose
  • Sagging sections of the roofline or ridge

Then go into the attic with a flashlight. Look for water stains on the decking, damp insulation, daylight around penetrations, or rusty nail tips. Any of those is worth getting looked at before storm season peaks.

Safety first: Stay off the roof. Katy roofs are often 6/12 pitch or steeper, and dew or heat makes shingles slick or soft. A ground check plus a professional inspection is the safe route.

Step 2: Fix small problems before they become storm leaks

The repairs that matter most before a hurricane are usually small. A single cracked pipe boot can let in gallons of water during a long rain band. A few unsealed shingles near the ridge can peel back and start a chain reaction across a slope.

Common pre-storm repairs

  • Replacing cracked or split pipe boots
  • Re-securing and resealing loose or lifted shingles
  • Resealing or replacing worn step and counter flashing
  • Fixing exposed nail heads and failed sealant at ridge vents
  • Replacing damaged drip edge so wind cannot get under the first course

These are typically quick jobs for a roof repair crew. Waiting until a storm is in the Gulf is the hard way to do it, because every roofer in Katy gets booked up the moment the forecast cone points at the Texas coast.

Step 3: Clear gutters and check drainage

When a tropical system stalls, Katy can get several inches of rain in a short period. Gutters packed with leaves, pine needles and shingle granules overflow, and water backs up under the roof edge or pours down against the foundation.

Before peak season:

  1. Clean gutters and downspouts completely and flush them with a hose.
  2. Check that gutters are firmly attached. Loose sections can tear off in high wind and take fascia with them.
  3. Make sure downspouts discharge well away from the slab, ideally with extensions or underground drains that are not clogged.
  4. Look for sagging runs where water pools instead of draining.

Step 4: Trim trees and secure loose items

Wind damage to a roof is often caused by something other than wind. Limbs, patio umbrellas, trampolines and loose fence boards become projectiles. Before a storm:

  • Have any limbs that overhang the roof trimmed back at least several feet. Use a licensed arborist for big trees.
  • Remove dead branches, especially on older oaks and pines.
  • Store or tie down outdoor furniture, grills, planters and kids' play equipment.
  • Check that satellite dishes, solar mounts and attic fans are firmly attached.

Many Katy HOAs have rules about tree removal, so check with yours before taking out anything large. Trimming is usually fine.

Step 5: Document your roof's condition before the storm

This is the step most people skip, and it is the one that helps most if you end up filing a claim. Insurers generally want to know that damage was caused by the storm and not by age or wear. Clear, dated photos of your roof before hurricane season give you a baseline.

Take photos of every slope, the gutters, the attic decking and any existing issues you already know about. Store them somewhere off your phone, such as a cloud folder or an email to yourself. Keep a copy of your homeowners policy there too, along with your agent's contact info, and note your wind and hail deductible, which on many Texas policies is a percentage of the dwelling coverage rather than a flat amount.

What to do after the storm passes

Once it is safe to go outside, walk the property and look for missing shingles, debris on the roof, bent gutters and new ceiling stains. Take photos right away, before anything is cleaned up.

  • If water is coming in, move belongings, catch drips, and call a roofer for an emergency tarp. Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.
  • Do not climb onto a wet or damaged roof. Leave that to a crew with fall protection.
  • Be wary of door-knockers. After big storms, out-of-town crews flood Katy neighborhoods. Anyone who offers to "cover" or waive your deductible is proposing something that is illegal in Texas.
