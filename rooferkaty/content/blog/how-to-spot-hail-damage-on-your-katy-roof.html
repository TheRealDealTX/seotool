The tricky thing about a hail damage roof Katy homeowners deal with is that it usually looks fine from the driveway. The shingles are still there. Nothing is dripping. Then a year later, a stain shows up on the bedroom ceiling and the storm that caused it is long forgotten.

This guide walks through what hail actually does to a roof, the clues you can safely check yourself, and when it makes sense to have a professional get up there with a camera.

What hail does to a roof

Asphalt shingles are layered: a fiberglass mat, asphalt, and a top coat of ceramic granules that shields the asphalt from UV. When a hailstone hits hard enough, it can:

  • Knock granules loose, exposing black asphalt that then breaks down quickly in the Texas sun.
  • Bruise the mat, leaving a soft spot that feels like a bruise on an apple when pressed.
  • Fracture the mat, which may not be visible from the top but lets water in over time.
  • Break the seal strip between shingles, making them more likely to lift in the next windstorm.

Hail hits are typically random, not in neat lines. Damage concentrated along one edge or in a straight pattern often points to foot traffic, manufacturing issues or blistering instead. Insurance adjusters know the difference, which is why accurate documentation matters.

Hail damage roof Katy signs you can check from the ground

Start below the roof. Soft metals and outdoor surfaces tell you a lot about how big the hail was and which direction it came from.

Check the soft metals

  • Gutters and downspouts: look for small round dents or dings along the top edge and face.
  • Window screens and frames: torn or dimpled screens on one side of the house suggest wind-driven hail.
  • AC condenser fins: flattened fins on the top or side of the outdoor unit.
  • Mailboxes, light fixtures and fence caps: fresh dents or chipped paint.

Check the ground and driveway

  • Piles of granules at the bottom of downspouts, which look like coarse dark sand
  • Shredded leaves and small twigs scattered across the yard
  • Spatter marks on painted surfaces, decks or the driveway where hail knocked off dirt and oxidation

If several of these are present, it is a good sign the hail was large enough to affect the roof too.

Note the date: Write down the date and approximate time of the storm and save any photos of hailstones next to a coin or ruler. That detail helps later if you file a claim.

What to look for on the roof itself

We do not recommend climbing onto your roof. Katy homes are often two stories with steep pitches, and storm-damaged shingles can be slippery. From the ground or a window, binoculars or a phone zoom can reveal:

  • Dark spots on shingles where granules are missing, often roughly circular
  • Dents on roof vents, turbine vents, ridge vents and the metal caps on plumbing stacks
  • Cracked or chipped shingle edges
  • Dented or split skylight frames
  • Shingles that look lifted or out of line after the storm

On the roof, a trained inspector will typically mark a test square on each slope and count hits, press on suspected bruises to check for soft spots, and photograph everything with chalk circles for scale. That is the kind of evidence adjusters work with.

Check the attic after a hailstorm

Go into the attic on a bright day with the lights off. Pinpoints of daylight through the decking are a red flag. Also look for:

  • Damp or darkened spots on the underside of the decking
  • Wet or compressed insulation
  • Water trails along rafters or around pipe penetrations

Leaks from hail may not appear until the next heavy rain, so check again after the following storm.

Hail damage vs. normal wear

Not every worn spot on a 15-year-old roof is hail. Insurers generally cover sudden storm damage, not age-related wear. Here is how the two usually differ:

SignLikely hailLikely wear or other cause
Granule lossRandom circular spots, fresh exposed asphaltEven thinning across a slope, especially south and west faces
CracksRadiating from an impact pointLong thermal cracks, curling or cupping edges
Metal dentsFresh, round dents with clean paint edgesOld dents with oxidation or dirt inside
PatternScattered across slopes facing the stormConcentrated in walkways, valleys or one area

Blistering, a common issue on older shingles in Katy's heat, can look like hail hits to an untrained eye. A good inspection separates the two honestly.

What to do if you suspect hail damage

  1. Take photos of everything you found on the ground, the gutters and in the attic.
  2. Schedule a professional inspection soon after the storm. Our free roof inspection includes photo documentation and a straight answer on whether the damage is worth pursuing.
  3. Review your policy. Note your wind and hail deductible. On many Texas policies it is a percentage of your dwelling coverage, which can be several thousand dollars.
  4. Prevent further damage. If water is coming in, get a temporary repair or tarp in place and keep the receipts.
  5. File the claim if the damage clearly exceeds your deductible. Our Texas roof insurance claim guide walks through the process step by step.

Be careful with storm chasers. After a big Katy hailstorm, door-knockers appear within days. Any contractor who offers to pay or waive your deductible is proposing something illegal under Texas law, and it can put your claim at risk.
