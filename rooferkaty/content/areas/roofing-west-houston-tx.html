West Houston and the Energy Corridor stretch along I-10 from about Beltway 8 out toward Barker and Addicks reservoirs, and they are home to some of the oldest housing we serve. A roofer in West Houston works on 1960s and 1970s ranch homes, two-story houses from the 1980s and newer infill builds, often under a heavy canopy of mature trees. The roofs here have weathered decades of Gulf Coast storms, and many have hidden problems that only show up when the old shingles come off.

Established Neighborhoods, Older Roof Systems

Neighborhoods such as Nottingham Forest, Thornwood and Fleetwood near Kirkwood and Memorial Dr, along with Briar Forest farther south, were mostly built from the 1960s through the early 1980s. Bear Creek and Lakes on Eldridge, west of Highway 6 and closer to the reservoirs, followed later. Many homes have been re-roofed two or three times since construction. When we tear off, we commonly find:

  • Plank or older plywood decking with soft spots from past leaks
  • Minimal attic ventilation and insulation that blocks soffit intake
  • Old flashing at chimneys and walls that was reused rather than replaced
  • Additions with lower-pitch roof sections that drain poorly

These details matter. A new roof over bad decking or poor ventilation will not last the way it should. Our roof replacement process includes deck repair and ventilation corrections.

Trees, Shade and Storm Damage

The big pines and oaks are a big part of West Houston's appeal, and also its biggest roofing challenge. The May 2024 derecho hit this part of Houston hard, and Hurricane Beryl followed that July, toppling trees and dropping limbs onto roofs across the area. Even without major storms, trees cause problems: needles and leaves clog valleys and gutters, shaded slopes grow algae, and branches wear away shingles.

Tip: Keep limbs trimmed back from the roof and clean gutters at least twice a year, more often under pine trees.

Deed Restrictions and City Permits

Unlike the master-planned communities west of town, many West Houston neighborhoods are governed by civic associations and deed restrictions rather than large HOAs. Some still regulate roof materials and colors, so check before choosing something unusual. Much of the area is inside Houston city limits, which has its own permitting process. We confirm what applies to your address.
