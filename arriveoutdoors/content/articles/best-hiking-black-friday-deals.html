The best hiking Black Friday deals go to shoppers who arrive with a short list, know their sizes, and check price history before clicking buy. In 2026, Black Friday is November 27 and Cyber Monday is November 30, but outdoor retailers usually start discounting earlier in November. This guide covers what typically goes on sale, where to look, how to spot a fake markdown, and how to buy a pack or boots that actually fit.

What usually goes on sale (and what doesn't)

Commonly discounted

  • Last season's colors and models. Brands update colorways and minor features every year; the outgoing version is often identical in function and is where the steepest real discounts tend to be.
  • Insulated jackets, fleece and base layers. Seasonal demand is high, so apparel is heavily promoted.
  • Tents, sleeping bags and pads from major brands, often outside their peak season.
  • Backpacks and daypacks, especially previous-year versions.
  • GPS watches, satellite messengers and headlamps, which often see holiday promotions from electronics retailers and brand sites.
  • Socks and accessories, often as bundle or multi-buy offers.

Less commonly discounted

  • Small cottage-industry brands (many ultralight packs, quilts and shelters) make gear in small batches and may run limited sales or none at all.
  • Brand-new releases launched that fall.
  • Fuel, water filters and other consumables, where savings are small in dollar terms.
Tip: Black Friday isn't the only big sale in outdoor retail. End-of-season clearance (late summer for summer gear, late winter for winter gear) and recurring sales at major outdoor retailers in spring and around Labor Day can match or beat November prices. If something isn't on your list for this winter, it may be cheaper to wait.

A good strategy: research and try things on at a store with fitting expertise before November, then buy at a fair sale price wherever returns are easiest. If a local shop fitted you for boots, consider buying from them; that service is worth something.

How to check if a deal is real

A big "% off" badge only means something if the "original" price is a price the item actually sold at. Before buying anything expensive:

  1. Check price history. For Amazon listings, price-tracker sites and browser extensions (CamelCamelCamel and Keepa are widely used) chart an item's price over months. Google Shopping also shows price trends and lets you set alerts. If the sale price matches what the item cost in September, it isn't really a deal.
  2. Set alerts in early November on the specific models on your list, then compare.
  3. Compare the exact model and year. Gear names carry over between versions with different weights and features. Match model numbers, not just names.
  4. Price the whole purchase. Shipping, return shipping and final-sale terms can erase a small discount.
  5. Look at cost per use. A tent you'll use 20 nights a year at a modest discount is a better buy than a gadget at a huge discount that you'll use once.

Fit before you buy: packs and boots

Discounts don't change anatomy. A pack or boot that doesn't fit costs you the full price in pain or return hassle.

Backpack-buying checklist

  • Measure your torso length (C7 vertebra to the top of your hip bones). Our step-by-step guide on how to measure torso length for a backpack takes five minutes with a friend and a soft tape.
  • Know your hip size and check the hip belt range for the size you choose; some packs have swappable belts.
  • Pick capacity by trip length, not by discount. Common ranges: day hikes 15–30 liters, overnights 30–50 liters, multi-day trips 50–70 liters, longer or winter trips 65 liters and up. Lighter, more compact gear shifts you toward the smaller end.
  • Check the load range. Frameless and ultralight packs carry comfortably only up to a modest weight; heavy loads need a real frame and padded hip belt.
  • Women's and men's fits differ in shoulder-strap shape, harness width and hip belt angle. Try both if you can.
  • Test it loaded at home. Put 15–25 pounds (7–11 kg) inside, adjust it fully, and walk around the house and up and down stairs for 30 minutes. Do this before removing tags, and keep it clean so it stays returnable.

If you're shopping for a backpacking pack, models commonly compared include the Osprey Atmos AG 65 (known for its ventilated suspended-mesh back panel) and the Gregory Baltoro 65 (built for heavier loads). For day hikes, the Osprey Talon 22 is a long-running daypack. Check torso sizes and current versions before buying.

Boots and trail shoes

  • Only buy footwear online at a discount if it's a model and size you've worn before, or the retailer offers free returns.
  • Try shoes on in the afternoon (feet swell) with your hiking socks. You want a thumb's width in front of your longest toe and no heel lift.
  • Brands update models frequently and the fit can change between versions even when the name stays the same. A deal on last year's version of a shoe that fits you is often the best footwear buy of the year. The Merrell Moab 3 is a common example of a long-running model that's widely stocked.

A simple Black Friday game plan

  1. October to early November: list the gear you'll actually use in the next 12 months, ranked by priority. Measure torso length and foot size; try on packs and boots in a store.
  2. Early November: pick one or two specific models per item, set price alerts, and note each one's typical price.
  3. Set a budget and stick to it. Sign up for retailer emails or memberships only if their perks (member pricing, longer returns) are worth it to you.
  4. Thanksgiving week: buy items that hit your target price. Popular sizes sell out first, so don't wait for a deeper discount on a common size.
  5. When it arrives: inspect it, test fit at home, and check the return deadline. Holiday return windows vary by retailer; write the date down.
  6. Cyber Monday: revisit anything still on your list. Some retailers drop prices further on remaining stock; others end promotions.

Black Friday vs Cyber Monday

Black Friday has the widest selection and the best odds of finding common sizes. Cyber Monday is mostly online and sometimes brings new markdowns on leftover inventory, but sizes thin out. If you need a specific size in a popular model, buy when you see a fair price.

After you buy

  • Register warranties where brands offer it, and keep receipts with the gear's model number.
  • Set up the tent and inflate the pad at home to check for defects while returns are still open.
  • Break in boots on short walks before a long trip.
  • Sell or donate the gear you're replacing so it gets used.
