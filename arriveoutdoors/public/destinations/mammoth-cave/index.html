Best time to visit

April through May and September through October are the best times to visit Mammoth Cave: tours are easier to book than in summer, and the surface trails are at their best for wildflowers or fall color.

Mammoth Cave National Park in south-central Kentucky protects the longest known cave system in the world, with more than 400 miles surveyed so far. Above ground, the park's rolling hardwood forest is cut by the Green and Nolin rivers and laced with hiking, biking and horse trails.

Because the cave keeps a steady, cool temperature year-round, cave tours work in any season. The surface is what changes: spring wildflowers, humid summers, fall color and quiet winters. Most visits center on a guided cave tour, and popular tours sell out.

Season by season

Spring

Spring is one of the best seasons. Wildflowers bloom on the forest floor, redbud and dogwood flower, and tours are easier to book before summer. Rain is frequent, and the rivers can run high and muddy.

The Green River Ferry, which links the north and south sides of the park, can close during high water; check its status before planning a route.

Summer

Summer is the busiest season. Tours sell out, especially on weekends, so reserve ahead. The cave is a cool refuge from humid surface weather; bring a light jacket for the tour.

Afternoon thunderstorms are common. Surface trails are green and shady, but ticks and chiggers are active.

Fall

Fall brings comfortable hiking weather, mid to late October color and thinner crowds after Labor Day. Paddling the Green River is especially pleasant.

Tour schedules may shrink after the summer season, so check what is offered for your dates.

Winter

Winter is the quietest time. Fewer tours run, but those that do are rarely crowded, and the cave feels the same as it does in summer. Bare trees open up views from bluff trails.

Occasional ice and snow can make surface roads and trails slick. Some facilities reduce hours in winter.

Camping at Mammoth Cave

Mammoth Cave Campground, near the visitor center, is the main campground and is seasonal. Houchin Ferry and Maple Springs offer quieter or group and horse sites. Reservations are made through Recreation.gov; check nps.gov/maca for seasons and availability.

Backcountry campsites on the north side of the park require a permit, and camping is also allowed on some Green River islands and banks under park rules. The Lodge at Mammoth Cave offers rooms and cottages.

Know before you go

  • Most cave tours require tickets, and popular tours sell out; reserve through Recreation.gov well ahead in summer and on holidays.
  • The cave stays cool year-round; bring a light jacket and sturdy shoes.
  • To protect bats from white-nose syndrome, do not wear clothing or gear that has been in other caves.
  • Tours vary from easy to very strenuous with many stairs; read tour descriptions before booking.
  • The Green River Ferry can close in high water.
  • Ticks and chiggers are common on surface trails in warm months.

Official information, alerts and current conditions: www.nps.gov/maca.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need reservations for Mammoth Cave tours?

Reservations are strongly recommended and are made through Recreation.gov. Popular tours often sell out in summer and on holiday weekends.

What should I wear on a Mammoth Cave tour?

Wear sturdy closed-toe shoes and bring a light jacket, since the cave stays cool all year. Do not wear clothing or gear used in other caves.

What is the best Mammoth Cave tour for first-time visitors?

The Historic Tour shows the cave's big passages and history, while Domes and Dripstones or Frozen Niagara focus on formations. Choose based on how many stairs you are comfortable with.

Is Mammoth Cave worth visiting in winter?

Yes. The cave feels the same year-round, crowds are small and tours are easier to book, though fewer tours run in winter.
