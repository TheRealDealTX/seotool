Why owners call

A Maryland Insurance Appraiser closes the gap between “covered” and “paid in full”

Most short claims aren’t denials. A rowhouse roof priced as a patch, plaster walls estimated as drywall, a matching problem nobody mentioned, a code upgrade Baltimore County will require anyway. A Maryland Insurance Appraiser exists for those differences.

  1. We value the loss from scratch. Our own inspection and measurements — never an edited copy of the insurer’s estimate.
  2. We price what we can defend. Numbers built to persuade an umpire, because inflated numbers don’t move awards.
  3. We tell you when appraisal is the wrong tool. Denials, coverage fights and bad-faith questions belong with a Maryland attorney.

Fit check

When Maryland Insurance Appraiser is — and isn’t — the right call

Appraisal answers one question: how much the covered damage costs. It is powerful for that question and useless for others.

Appraisal fits

  • Coverage accepted, price disputed
  • Repair-versus-replace disagreements on roofs and floors
  • Matching and discontinued materials
  • Missing trades, code items, overhead & profit
  • Contents or ALE totals that don’t match receipts

Look elsewhere

  • A full denial or a coverage exclusion fight
  • Bad-faith or unfair-practice questions (MIA or attorney)
  • A gap too small to justify fees
  • A suit deadline that is close — call an attorney first
  • Flood losses under a separate flood policy with its own process

The process

How Maryland Insurance Appraiser moves a stuck claim

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a short, defined procedure with a binding result.

  1. 1

    Review

    We read the policy, the insurer’s estimate and your photos, then say plainly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 2

    Inspect

    An independent inspection and measured, line-item estimate priced for your Maryland ZIP code.

  3. 3

    Demand

    Appraisal is invoked in writing and your appraiser is named inside the policy’s time limit.

  4. 4

    Negotiate

    Your appraiser works item by item with the insurer’s appraiser. Most gaps narrow here.

  5. 5

    Award

    Anything unresolved goes to a neutral umpire. Two signatures set the amount of loss.

Questions

Asked of every Maryland Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers on process, cost and when appraisal is the wrong move.

All common questionsGlossary
What does a Maryland Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work through the differences with the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is appraisal the same as filing a complaint with the Maryland Insurance Administration?

No. An MIA complaint asks the regulator to look at how the insurer handled your claim. Appraisal is a process in your policy that sets the amount of loss. They are separate tracks, and an attorney can tell you how they interact in your situation.

When should I call a Maryland Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage has been accepted, you have the insurer’s estimate, and the gap between it and a qualified contractor’s estimate is meaningful — often more than 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is an appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, generally yes, once two of the three panel members sign. That’s why the documentation behind your number matters so much before anyone invokes appraisal.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire’s fee. We put our fee in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you work outside Baltimore and the suburbs?

Yes. We take claims in all 23 counties and Baltimore City — Western Maryland, the Capital Region, Southern Maryland and the Eastern Shore included.
