The short version

Covered is not the same as paid in full

Most underpaid island claims aren’t denials. They’re estimates with a missing roof plane, a condo ceiling below that nobody opened, freight priced like the mainland or general excise tax left off the bottom line. A Hawaii Insurance Appraiser exists for the gap between “we’ll cover it” and a check that actually rebuilds.

When either side invokes the appraisal clause, you name an appraiser, the insurer names one, and the two choose an umpire. Hawaii Insurance Appraiser can be the appraiser you name — or review the numbers first so you know whether a demand is worth making.

  • We scope from scratch. Our own inspection and measurements, never an edited copy of the carrier’s file.
  • We price for the island you’re on. Barge freight, crew travel and county GET belong in a real estimate.
  • We say no when appraisal won’t help. Denials, bad-faith questions and tight suit deadlines belong with an attorney.

Island by island

Each island files a different kind of claim

A Hawaii Insurance Appraiser has to know how vog shows up on a Kona metal roof, how a stack-pipe leak travels through a Waikiki tower and what it costs to rebuild on Molokai.

Oahu

  • Condo water losses
  • Kona-storm wind
  • Flash flooding
  • Brush fires leeward

Most of the state’s people — and most of its high-rise condos — live on Oahu. Water losses that travel between units, roof damage from Kona-storm winds and flash flooding on the windward side make up a large share of disputed files, alongside leeward brush fires near Waianae and Makakilo.

Communities: Honolulu, Kailua, Kaneohe, Pearl City, Aiea, Kapolei, Ewa Beach, Mililani, Wahiawa

Hawaii Island

  • Lava & vog
  • Extreme rainfall
  • Earthquake questions
  • Catchment & roofs

The Big Island packs rain forest, ranchland and active volcanoes into one county. Hurricane Lane’s rainfall in 2018 hit the Hilo side hard the same year Kilauea’s eruption took homes in lower Puna, and vog keeps metal roofs and fixtures on the Kona side under constant attack.

Communities: Hilo, Kailua-Kona, Pahoa, Waimea, Volcano, Honokaa, Kau

Maui

  • Wildfire
  • Downslope winds
  • Smoke & ash
  • Rebuild costs

Since August 2023, wildfire has defined Maui claims: total losses in Lahaina, partial losses and smoke in Upcountry, and long arguments about rebuilding costs as labor and materials stretch thin. Kona storms and trade-wind gusts add roof and water claims across Central and South Maui.

Communities: Kahului, Wailuku, Kihei, Wailea, Lahaina, Napili, Kula, Pukalani, Makawao

Kauai

  • Hurricane history
  • Record flooding
  • Wind-driven rain
  • Older housing

Kauai carries the memory of Iwa in 1982 and Iniki in 1992, and the 2018 north shore floods showed how fast rainfall can overwhelm roads and homes. Hurricane deductibles, wind-driven rain and the cost of shipping materials to the Garden Isle shape many disputes.

Communities: Lihue, Kapaa, Wailua, Koloa, Poipu, Princeville, Hanalei, Waimea

Molokai & Lanai

  • Travel logistics
  • Limited contractors
  • Wind & salt air
  • Older homes

Small communities mean few local contractors and long lead times. Estimates for Molokai and Lanai need realistic freight, barge schedules and travel for crews — items carrier estimates sometimes price as if the home were in town on Oahu.

Communities: Kaunakakai, Maunaloa, Kalaupapa, Lanai City

Honest triage

When appraisal fits — and when to call someone else

Appraisal is a precise tool. Hawaii Insurance Appraiser will point you elsewhere when another route serves you better.

Appraisal usually fits

  • Coverage accepted, cost disputed
  • Repair-versus-replace arguments on roofs and finishes
  • Matching metal panels, tile or flooring that can’t be sourced
  • Freight, GET, code upgrades or overhead and profit left out
  • Condo building losses where the master-policy number drives assessments

Look elsewhere first

  • A full denial of coverage — talk to a Hawaii attorney
  • Bad-faith or claim-handling complaints — attorney or the Insurance Division
  • A suit-limitation date that is close
  • A gap too small to justify appraisal costs
  • Wanting someone to manage the whole claim — consider a licensed public adjuster

Questions

Questions island owners ask first

Straight answers from Hawaii Insurance Appraiser about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

Questions by topic Claim glossary
What does a Hawaii Insurance Appraiser do?

When you and your insurer agree a loss is covered but disagree on the cost, an appraiser values the damage independently. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build a line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is appraisal the same as hiring a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question — the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

Do you work on neighbor islands?

Yes. We take claims on Oahu, Hawaii Island, Maui, Kauai, Molokai and Lanai, and we’re upfront about any travel costs before work begins.

Can appraisal help with a condo or AOAO claim?

Often, yes. Association master policies usually contain an appraisal clause, and the amount of a building loss affects every owner’s deductible assessment. Unit owners may also have a separate HO-6 dispute.

What if my claim was denied?

Appraisal sets the amount of a covered loss; it doesn’t decide coverage. For a denial, talk to a Hawaii attorney first. We’ll tell you on the first call if your situation needs one.

How much does appraisal cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. Our fee is put in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.
