Your arches are held up by bones, ligaments, and a surprising number of small muscles. When those muscles are weak, the arch can sag more under load, and your feet, ankles, and even knees may feel it. The good news is that a handful of simple flat feet exercises can strengthen the muscles that support your arch, improve balance, and make long days on your feet more comfortable. You need little more than a towel, a few marbles, and a step.

Key takeaways

  • Exercises strengthen the muscles that support your arch; they rarely change foot structure.
  • Start with the short foot exercise, which teaches you to lift your arch on purpose.
  • Three to five short sessions a week is plenty for most people.
  • Pair exercises with supportive shoes for the best results.
  • See a podiatrist or physical therapist if you have ongoing pain.

Why Flat Feet Exercises Help

Flat feet, also called fallen arches, are common. Some people are born with them, while others develop them over time through aging, weight gain, injury, or tendon problems. Many people have flat feet without any pain at all.

When flat feet do cause trouble, it often shows up as tired arches, heel pain, shin pain, or ankles that roll inward. Strengthening the intrinsic foot muscles (the small muscles inside your foot) and the muscles of your lower leg gives your arch more active support. Think of it as training the foot's built-in shock absorbers.

Heads up: If one foot has recently become flatter than the other, or you have swelling on the inside of your ankle, see a doctor before starting an exercise routine. These can be signs of a tendon problem that needs care.

Before You Start

  • Do the exercises barefoot on a firm surface so your feet do the work.
  • Move slowly and with control. Quality beats quantity.
  • Expect some muscle fatigue or mild cramping at first. That is normal.
  • Stop if you feel sharp or worsening pain.

8 Flat Feet Exercises to Strengthen Your Arches

1. Short Foot Exercise

Targets: intrinsic foot muscles, arch3 x 10 holds of 5 seconds

Sit with your feet flat on the floor. Without curling your toes, gently draw the ball of your foot toward your heel so your arch lifts and your foot gets slightly shorter. Keep your toes relaxed and long. Hold for five seconds, then relax. Once it feels easy seated, progress to standing, then to standing on one leg.

2. Towel Curls

Targets: toe flexors, arch3 x 10-15

Sit in a chair with a small towel flat on the floor under your foot. Keep your heel down and use your toes to scrunch the towel toward you. Spread it back out and repeat. To make it harder, place a light object like a book on the far end of the towel.

3. Heel Raises

Targets: calves, posterior tibialis3 x 12-15

Stand with your feet hip-width apart, holding a wall or counter for balance. Rise up onto the balls of your feet, focusing on keeping your ankles from rolling outward, then lower slowly over about three seconds. For extra arch work, squeeze a small ball between your heels as you rise. Progress to single-leg raises when two-leg raises feel easy.

4. Arch Lifts

Targets: arch muscles, ankle control3 x 10

Stand with your feet hip-width apart. Keep your toes and heel on the floor and roll your weight toward the outer edges of your feet so your arches lift. Hold for two to three seconds, then slowly return. Keep the movement small and controlled; this is about feeling the arch muscles switch on.

5. Calf Stretch

Targets: calves, Achilles tendon3 x 30 seconds per side

Face a wall with your hands on it. Step one foot back, keep that heel down and the knee straight, and lean forward until you feel a stretch in your calf. Turn the back foot slightly inward to avoid collapsing the arch. Then bend the back knee slightly to stretch the lower calf. Tight calves can pull the foot into a flatter position, so this one matters.

6. Marble Pickups

Targets: toe flexors, foot coordination2 x 15-20 marbles per foot

Scatter 15 to 20 marbles on the floor next to a small bowl. Sitting in a chair, pick up one marble at a time with your toes and drop it into the bowl. This builds strength and dexterity in the muscles that help hold up your arch.

7. Single-Leg Balance

Targets: ankle stability, foot muscles3 x 30 seconds per side

Stand on one foot near a wall or chair for safety. Keep a slight bend in your knee and try to hold a gentle arch lift (like the short foot exercise) the whole time. To progress, close your eyes, stand on a pillow, or reach your free leg in different directions.

8. Toe Yoga

Targets: toe control, intrinsic muscles2 x 10 each direction

Sit or stand with your feet flat. Lift just your big toe while keeping the other four toes on the floor. Then switch: press the big toe down and lift the other four. It is harder than it sounds. Go slowly, and use your fingers to help at first if needed.

A Simple Weekly Routine

You do not need to do all eight exercises every day. Here is an easy plan that takes about 10 to 15 minutes:

DayExercisesTime
MondayShort foot, towel curls, heel raises, calf stretch12 minutes
TuesdayRest or calf stretch only3 minutes
WednesdayShort foot, arch lifts, marble pickups, single-leg balance12 minutes
ThursdayRest or toe yoga only3 minutes
FridayShort foot, heel raises, single-leg balance, toe yoga, calf stretch15 minutes
WeekendOptional: any two favorites5-10 minutes
Tip: Stack these onto habits you already have. Do toe yoga while brushing your teeth or marble pickups while watching TV.

Other Ways to Support Your Arches

  • Manage your weight. Extra body weight increases the load on your arches with every step.
  • Build up activity gradually. Big jumps in walking or running volume can overload tired feet.
  • Use arch supports if helpful. Over-the-counter inserts help many people; a podiatrist can fit custom orthotics.
  • Avoid long periods in unsupportive shoes. Flip-flops and completely flat shoes offer little arch support.

When to See a Doctor or Physical Therapist

Flat feet exercises are safe for most people, but they are not a substitute for medical care. See a podiatrist, doctor, or physical therapist if you have:

  • Foot, ankle, or heel pain that lasts more than a few weeks
  • Pain that worsens with exercise
  • Swelling along the inside of the ankle
  • A foot that is becoming flatter over time
  • Stiff feet that do not flex normally

A professional can find the cause of your symptoms and build a plan that fits you. The Mayo Clinic offers a helpful overview of flatfeet symptoms and causes.

The Bottom Line

A few minutes of targeted work a few times a week can make your arches stronger and your feet more comfortable. Start with the short foot exercise, add heel raises and balance work, and stay consistent for at least six to eight weeks. Combined with supportive shoes and sensible training, these flat feet exercises are an easy, low-cost way to take care of the foundation you stand on every day.
