Beets have a reputation as a "glow food," and you have probably seen bright magenta juices promising clearer, brighter skin. So what does beet juice for skin actually do? The honest answer: it is a nutrient-dense drink that can support your skin as part of a healthy diet, and it makes a fun, gentle DIY mask ingredient. It is not a miracle cure, and it will not replace sunscreen or a solid routine.

This guide walks you through the nutrients in beets, the difference between drinking and applying beet juice, a simple mask and juice recipe, and the side effects worth knowing before you start.

Key takeaways

  • Beets contain vitamin C, folate, potassium, nitrates, and betalain pigments that act as antioxidants.
  • Drinking beet juice supports overall nutrition; topical use is mild and mostly cosmetic.
  • Always patch test a DIY mask, and expect a temporary pink tint.
  • Pink urine (beeturia) is usually harmless, but people prone to kidney stones should be cautious about oxalates.
  • Daily sunscreen and consistent skincare matter far more than any single food.

What's Inside Beets That Matters for Skin

Beets are humble root vegetables, but they pack a useful mix of nutrients. None of these work like a skincare product on their own, yet they all play a role in how your body builds and protects skin.

Vitamin C

Your body needs vitamin C to make collagen, the protein that keeps skin firm. Beets offer a modest amount, so think of them as one contributor alongside citrus, berries, and peppers.

Betalains

Betalains are the pigments that give beets their deep red color. They act as antioxidants, which means they help neutralize free radicals, the unstable molecules produced by sun exposure, pollution, and normal metabolism.

Folate and potassium

Folate supports healthy cell division and repair, and potassium helps your body manage fluid balance. Both are part of the general nutrition your skin relies on.

Dietary nitrates

Beets are rich in nitrates, which your body converts to nitric oxide, a compound that helps relax blood vessels. This is why beet juice is popular with athletes. Whether it noticeably changes your complexion is not well established, so treat any "glow" claims with healthy skepticism.

Drinking vs. Applying Beet Juice for Skin

It helps to separate the two ways people use beets, because the expectations are very different.

MethodWhat it can doWhat it can't do
DrinkingAdds antioxidants, vitamins, and fluids to your dietErase wrinkles, cure acne, or replace sunscreen
Topical maskFeels refreshing, gives a temporary rosy tint, mild hydration when mixed with yogurt or honeyDeliver lasting brightening or treat hyperpigmentation

Drinking is where beets earn their keep. A varied diet with plenty of colorful produce, enough protein, and adequate water gives your skin the raw materials it needs. Beet juice can be one enjoyable part of that, especially if it helps you eat more vegetables.

Applying beet juice is more of a pampering ritual. It is gentle for most people, but the benefits are short-lived. If you have specific concerns like melasma, dark spots, or acne, proven ingredients such as sunscreen, retinoids, and azelaic acid will do far more.

Good to know: Hydration matters for how your skin looks and feels. Juice counts toward your fluid intake, but water should still do most of the work. Use the calculator below to estimate your daily needs.

DIY Beet Juice Face Mask

This simple mask pairs beet juice with plain yogurt and honey for a soothing, hydrating treat. It is best for normal to dry skin.

What you'll need

  • 1 teaspoon fresh beet juice
  • 1 tablespoon plain, unsweetened yogurt
  • 1/2 teaspoon raw honey
  • A clean bowl and spoon

How to make and apply it

  1. Patch test first. Dab a small amount on your inner forearm or behind your ear. Wait 24 hours and check for redness, itching, or burning.
  2. Mix. Stir the beet juice, yogurt, and honey until smooth.
  3. Cleanse. Wash your face with a gentle cleanser and pat dry.
  4. Apply. Spread a thin layer over your face, avoiding your eyes and lips.
  5. Wait 10 minutes. Keep it short to limit staining.
  6. Rinse and moisturize. Rinse with lukewarm water, then follow with your usual moisturizer. In the morning, finish with sunscreen.
Heads up: Skip this mask if you are allergic to dairy or honey, or if your skin is broken, irritated, or freshly treated with exfoliants or retinoids. Stop immediately if it stings. Very fair skin may stay pink for a few hours, so try it in the evening.

Once or twice a week is plenty. Pair it with a consistent daily skincare routine for glowing skin rather than relying on the mask alone.

Simple Beet Juice Recipe

Straight beet juice is earthy and intense. Blending it with fruit and greens makes it far more drinkable.

Ingredients (makes about 2 small glasses)

  • 1 medium beet, scrubbed and chopped
  • 1 apple, cored
  • 1 carrot
  • 1/2 lemon, peeled
  • A small knob of fresh ginger
  • A handful of spinach (optional)

Directions

Run everything through a juicer, or blend with a splash of water and strain through a fine mesh sieve. Drink it fresh or refrigerate for up to a day. If you blend without straining, you keep the fiber, which makes it more filling and gentler on blood sugar.

Side Effects and Who Should Be Careful

Beets are safe for most people in normal food amounts, but a few things are worth knowing.

Beeturia

Pink or red urine or stools after eating beets is called beeturia. It is usually harmless and simply means the pigment passed through. If you notice red urine without having eaten beets, call your doctor.

Oxalates and kidney stones

Beets, and especially beet greens, are high in oxalates. If you have had calcium oxalate kidney stones, talk with your doctor before drinking beet juice regularly.

Blood pressure

Because nitrates can lower blood pressure, people who already have low blood pressure or take blood pressure medications should check with their doctor first.

Blood sugar

Juicing removes fiber and concentrates natural sugars. If you manage diabetes, keep portions small, pair juice with protein, or blend instead of juicing.

Skin sensitivity

Topical beet is mild, but any food applied to the face can cause irritation or an allergic reaction. That is why the patch test matters.

Bigger Habits That Make Skin Glow

Beet juice is a nice addition, but the habits below have a much larger impact on how your skin looks over time:

  • Wear a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher every day, as the American Academy of Dermatology recommends.
  • Cleanse gently and moisturize daily.
  • Eat a variety of colorful fruits and vegetables, not just one "superfood."
  • Drink enough water throughout the day.
  • Get consistent sleep and manage stress.
  • Avoid smoking, which accelerates skin aging.

When to See a Dermatologist

Food and DIY masks are great for self-care, but they are not treatments for skin conditions. Book an appointment with a board-certified dermatologist if you have acne that leaves scars, dark patches that are spreading, a rash that will not go away, or a mole that changes in size, shape, or color. A professional can identify what is going on and recommend options that actually work.

Enjoy beet juice for skin as a colorful, nutritious part of your routine, keep your expectations realistic, and let sunscreen and consistent care do the heavy lifting.
