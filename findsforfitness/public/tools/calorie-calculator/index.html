How the calorie calculator works

We estimate your basal metabolic rate (BMR) with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the formula most dietitians use today, then multiply it by an activity factor to get your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE), the calories you burn on an average day.

  1. BMR. Men: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5. Women: the same, minus 161 instead of plus 5.
  2. TDEE. BMR × activity factor (1.2 to 1.9).
  3. Goal. Fat loss uses a moderate deficit of about 20% (never below a sensible floor), lean gain adds about 10%.

Using your numbers

Treat the result as a starting point. Track your weight for two to three weeks: if the trend isn't moving the way you want, adjust by 100 to 200 calories. Keep protein high while losing weight so more of what you lose is fat, not muscle. Our protein calculator gives you a daily target.

Heads up: If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, under 18, or managing a medical condition, get calorie targets from your doctor or a registered dietitian.

Frequently asked questions

What is TDEE?

Total daily energy expenditure: the calories you burn in a day from your resting metabolism, digestion, daily movement and exercise.

How big should a calorie deficit be?

For most people a deficit of roughly 300 to 500 calories a day, or about 20% below maintenance, is sustainable and protects muscle.

Why isn't my weight changing at the calculated calories?

Calculators are estimates. Water shifts hide fat loss for a week or two, so watch the multi-week trend and adjust by 100 to 200 calories if needed.
