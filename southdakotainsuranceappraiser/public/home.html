Before you pick up the phone

Three questions a South Dakota Insurance Appraiser will ask you first

  1. 1

    Did the carrier accept the claim?

    Appraisal values covered losses. If the claim was denied outright, the first call is usually a South Dakota attorney, not South Dakota Insurance Appraiser.

  2. 2

    Is the gap big enough?

    Compare the carrier’s estimate with your contractor’s. A few hundred dollars can usually be closed with a supplement. Several thousand often justifies appraisal.

  3. 3

    What does the clock say?

    Policies carry deadlines for proof of loss, depreciation recovery and lawsuits. Know them before anything else happens.

Covered isn’t the same as paid

Why owners bring in South Dakota Insurance Appraiser

Most short claims aren’t denials. They’re estimates with missing slopes, unmatched siding, a basement scoped as one wall, or a pole barn priced like a garden shed. South Dakota Insurance Appraiser exists for that gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

We value the loss from scratch, with our own inspection and measurements, never an edited copy of the carrier’s estimate. And we price it for where the property actually is: Sioux Falls labor, Black Hills access and a ranch two hours from the nearest supplier are not the same.

  • Defensible, not inflated. Numbers built to hold up in front of an umpire.
  • Plain-English reporting. You’ll understand every difference between our number and theirs.
  • Honest triage. If your claim needs an attorney or a supplement instead, we’ll say so on the first call.

The process

How South Dakota Insurance Appraiser moves a stuck claim

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here’s the path from first call to signed award.

  1. 1

    Free review

    We read the policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos, then tell you honestly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 2

    Our own valuation

    We inspect, measure and build a line-item estimate priced for your part of South Dakota.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and South Dakota Insurance Appraiser is named as your appraiser.

  4. 4

    Line-by-line work

    We work through the estimate with the carrier’s appraiser. Most of the gap usually closes here.

  5. 5

    Signed award

    Anything left goes to a neutral umpire. Two signatures set the amount of loss.

Weather on the record

A short South Dakota storm history

These events still shape how carriers and appraisers look at claims here. Each one taught South Dakota Insurance Appraiser something about how damage shows up, and where estimates fall short.

  1. 1972

    Rapid City flood

    Overnight flooding on Rapid Creek reshaped the city and led to the greenway that runs through it today.

  2. 1998

    Spencer tornado

    A violent tornado destroyed much of Spencer in McCook County, one of the deadliest in state history.

  3. 2000

    Jasper Fire

    More than 80,000 acres burned in the southern Black Hills, a benchmark for wildfire risk in the region.

  4. 2010

    Vivian hailstone

    The largest hailstone recorded in the United States fell near Vivian in Lyman County.

  5. 2011

    Missouri River flood

    Record releases flooded low ground from Pierre and Fort Pierre down through southeast South Dakota.

  6. 2013

    October blizzard

    An early storm buried western South Dakota and the northern Hills in heavy, wet snow.

  7. 2019

    Sioux Falls tornadoes

    Three tornadoes touched down inside the state’s largest city in a single night.

  8. 2022

    May derecho

    Hurricane-force straight-line winds crossed eastern South Dakota, and a tornado struck Castlewood.

  9. 2024

    Southeast flooding

    Extreme June rain pushed the Big Sioux River to historic levels in the state’s southeast corner.

Choosing help

Who should be in your corner?

Each professional solves a different problem. South Dakota Insurance Appraiser will point you elsewhere when appraisal isn’t the right tool.

An appraiser

Choose this when coverage is accepted and the number is the fight.

  • Values the amount of loss
  • Result: binding award, 2 of 3 signatures
  • Doesn’t decide coverage

A public adjuster

Choose this when you want help managing the whole claim from the start.

  • Prepares and negotiates the claim
  • Result: negotiated settlement
  • Licensed by the state

An attorney

Choose this for denials, bad-faith concerns or a deadline that’s close.

  • Gives legal advice, files suit
  • Result: settlement or judgment
  • Argues coverage in court

FAQ

Questions people ask South Dakota Insurance Appraiser

What does a South Dakota Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser, we inspect, build our own line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present open items to a neutral umpire.

Is appraisal the same as hiring a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster manages and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When should I call a South Dakota Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s estimate, and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful, usually more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is an appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, generally yes, once two of the three panel members sign. That is why documentation matters before you demand appraisal.

What does appraisal cost?

Each side usually pays its own appraiser and half of the umpire. We put our fee in writing before work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you work in my part of South Dakota?

Yes. We work in all 66 counties, from Sioux Falls and Aberdeen to Pierre, Rapid City and the ranches in between.
