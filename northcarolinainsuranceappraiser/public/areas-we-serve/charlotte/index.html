Housing: Charlotte pairs 1910s–1930s bungalows and colonial-revival homes in Dilworth, Myers Park and Elizabeth with mill houses in NoDa, mid-rise condos in Uptown and South End, and large suburban subdivisions toward Ballantyne and the county edges.

Where we work: Myers Park, Dilworth, Plaza Midwood, NoDa, Elizabeth, South End, Eastover, Ballantyne, University City

A city built under a tree canopy

Charlotte’s older neighborhoods were planted with willow oaks a century ago, and those trees now tower over Myers Park, Dilworth and Eastover. Residents who remember Hurricane Hugo in 1989 know what happens when an inland storm meets that canopy. The 2002 ice storm repeated the lesson in a different form. Today, a thunderstorm with strong straight-line winds can still drop a trunk across a roof, and those claims involve structure, interiors, landscaping and often a crane.

The metro also sees hail. Storms that build over the Piedmont in spring can lay down a swath of damage across one part of the county and miss another entirely. That creates a familiar argument: whether marks on a roof are new hail damage, older wear or something an adjuster considers cosmetic.

From bungalows to towers: the property types we value

Charlotte claims rarely fit a single pattern because the housing stock does not. In Dilworth and Elizabeth, craftsman bungalows and colonial-revival homes come with plaster, original millwork and older roofs that may not survive a partial repair. In NoDa and Plaza Midwood, former mill houses have been renovated many times, so the materials behind one wall can differ from the next.

Uptown and South End have added thousands of condominium units. In those buildings, a water loss that starts in one unit can travel through several floors. Sorting out what the association’s master policy covers and what falls to the unit owner’s HO-6 policy has to happen before the amount of loss can be settled. In the newer subdivisions toward Ballantyne, Steele Creek and University City, the questions are more likely to involve matching siding, roofing and flooring across large, uniform surfaces.

Where Charlotte estimates tend to diverge

  • Hail damage to shingles, gutters and metal trim characterized as wear or cosmetic.
  • Partial roof or siding repairs that leave a visible mismatch.
  • Condo water losses split between master and unit-owner policies.
  • Plaster and custom trim in older homes priced as standard materials.
  • Tree removal, debris haul-off and crane costs on mature lots.
  • Business interruption or contents values on commercial claims.

How appraisal fits

Appraisal addresses the amount of a loss, not whether it is covered. When a policy includes an appraisal provision and it is invoked, each side typically names an appraiser, the two choose an umpire, and an award signed by any two of the three sets the value. For a homeowner who has an accepted claim but an estimate that does not reflect the actual repair, it can be a faster and more focused path than a long back-and-forth. We serve as a party appraiser, as an umpire, or as an independent reviewer who tells you plainly whether the gap is worth pursuing.

Questions from Charlotte property owners

My South End condo was damaged by a leak from the unit above. Who handles the claim?

Usually two policies are involved: the association’s master policy and your HO-6 unit-owner policy. Which one pays for drywall, cabinets, flooring and upgrades depends on the declarations and bylaws. Once responsibility is sorted out, appraisal may be available under the policy that covers your portion if you disagree on the amount.

My Charlotte roof had hail damage but the insurer said it is only cosmetic. Is that an appraisal issue?

It can be part of one. Whether a cosmetic-damage exclusion applies is a coverage question, which appraisal does not decide. But when an insurer acknowledges some hail damage and the dispute is over how much of the roof needs repair or replacement, appraisal may be the right tool for valuing the loss.

Do you handle appraisals for commercial properties in Mecklenburg County?

Yes. Many commercial property policies contain an appraisal clause similar to homeowners policies. We review building estimates for retail, office and multifamily properties and can serve as a party appraiser or umpire. Business income disputes are more complex and depend heavily on the policy wording and the records available.
