Hurricanes that arrive inland

Fayetteville is roughly a two-hour drive from the coast, far enough that hurricanes usually lose some of their wind before they arrive but close enough that their rain does not. Matthew in 2016 and Florence in 2018 both brought extraordinary rainfall to the Sandhills. Water rose along the Cape Fear River, Cross Creek, Little River and smaller tributaries, and wind brought down tall pines onto roofs and vehicles throughout Cumberland County.

That combination makes cause the first question in many Fayetteville claims. Wind damage and wind-driven rain entering through a storm-created opening are typically handled under a homeowners policy. Rising surface water is generally excluded from standard homeowners coverage and handled by a separate flood policy, usually through the NFIP or a private insurer. When both happen to the same home, separating the damage is essential before anyone can agree on a number.

Housing patterns in Cumberland County

Haymount, one of the city’s oldest neighborhoods, has older homes with plaster, hardwood floors and established trees. Much of the rest of Fayetteville was built in the second half of the twentieth century, with brick and vinyl ranches and split-levels, many of them rental properties. Because of Fort Bragg, Fayetteville has a large share of homes owned by military families who rent them out after a reassignment, and owners are often managing a claim from another state or overseas.

Manufactured homes are common in outlying parts of the county and in nearby towns, and they have their own construction and pricing issues. Newer subdivisions toward Hope Mills and Gillis Hill raise familiar questions about matching roofing and siding.

Disputes we see after Sandhills storms

  • Wind versus flood allocation on homes with both types of damage.
  • Roof replacement versus repair after tall pines fall across a ridge.
  • Loss-of-rent calculations for landlord policies.
  • Manufactured home components priced like site-built construction.
  • Mold and drying scope after prolonged water exposure.
  • Contractor pricing after a regional storm compared with pre-storm estimates.

When owners are far away

A service member who has been reassigned, or a landlord who lives elsewhere, may not be able to walk the property with an adjuster. Appraisal can be useful in those situations because it puts the valuation in the hands of appraisers who inspect and document the loss. When a policy includes an appraisal clause and it is invoked, each side typically selects an appraiser, the two appraisers choose an umpire, and an agreement of two sets the amount. It is limited to the value of the loss and does not resolve coverage issues.

We act as party appraisers, as umpires and as estimate reviewers. An honest review sometimes shows the insurer’s estimate is close, and we will say so.

Questions from Fayetteville property owners

I’m stationed away from Fayetteville and my rental was damaged. Can appraisal still work?

Yes. Appraisal does not require you to be at the property. Appraisers inspect the home, review estimates and document the loss, and you can authorize access through a property manager or tenant. If your landlord policy includes an appraisal clause and the claim is accepted, the amount can be resolved without you traveling back.

My Hope Mills home had wind and flood damage from the same storm. How is that handled?

Wind damage typically falls under your homeowners policy, while rising water is generally excluded and handled by a separate flood policy if you have one. The two claims are evaluated separately. Appraisal applies to amount-of-loss disputes under the policy covering the damage, so separating wind and water damage carefully is the first step.

Does appraisal work for a manufactured home in Cumberland County?

It can. Many manufactured home policies include an appraisal provision. These homes have specific components, such as roofing systems, wall panels and underbelly materials, that are priced differently from site-built homes. We review the policy and estimate to see whether those components were priced correctly before advising on appraisal.
