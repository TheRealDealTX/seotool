What we do

What a North Carolina Insurance Appraiser actually does for you

Most underpaid claims in this state aren’t denied. They’re short: a slope left off the roof, a hardwood floor that stops at the doorway, an ice-storm limb strike priced as a patch, code work nobody included. North Carolina Insurance Appraiser exists for the gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

When either side invokes the appraisal clause, you name an appraiser, the carrier names one, and the two of them (with an umpire if needed) set the amount of loss. As your North Carolina Insurance Appraiser, we build the number you take to that table and we defend it item by item.

  • Independent inspection and measurements
  • Line-item estimates priced for your area
  • Panel negotiation and umpire presentation
  • Written fee terms before work begins

Storm record

The storms that shaped how North Carolina claims are valued

A North Carolina Insurance Appraiser has to understand what each kind of event does to a building. These are the storms property owners here still measure against.

  1. 1954

    Hurricane Hazel

    Came ashore near the state line as a major hurricane; its surge flattened much of the southern Brunswick County beachfront.

  2. 1989

    Hurricane Hugo

    Stayed strong far inland and brought down trees across Charlotte and the western Piedmont.

  3. 1996

    Hurricane Fran

    Landed near Cape Fear and carried damaging wind and falling trees deep into the Triangle.

  4. 1999

    Hurricane Floyd

    Produced record river flooding across eastern North Carolina, including the Tar and Neuse basins.

  5. 2002

    December ice storm

    Freezing rain coated the Piedmont from Charlotte to Raleigh and left well over a million customers in the dark.

  6. 2011

    April 16 tornadoes

    An outbreak of tornadoes crossed central and eastern counties, striking Sanford and parts of Raleigh.

  7. 2016

    Hurricane Matthew

    Drove severe river flooding along the Lumber, Neuse and Cape Fear basins.

  8. 2018

    Hurricane Florence

    Made landfall near Wrightsville Beach, stalled, and pushed surge up the Neuse into New Bern.

  9. 2024

    Hurricane Helene

    Caused catastrophic flooding, landslides and wind damage across the western mountains.

The process

From first call to a signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding answer on the amount of loss.

  1. 1

    Free review

    Send the policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos. A North Carolina Insurance Appraiser reads them and tells you plainly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 2

    Independent scope

    We inspect, measure and photograph the property, then build a line-item estimate priced for your area.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and we’re named as your appraiser within the policy’s deadline.

  4. 4

    Panel work

    We work line by line with the carrier’s appraiser. Most disputes narrow sharply here.

  5. 5

    Signed award

    Open items go to a neutral umpire; two signatures set the amount of loss and payment follows.

Questions

Questions people ask a North Carolina Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers on how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All questions Insurance terms
What does a North Carolina Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, write an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present open items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question, the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When is it worth calling a North Carolina Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage has been accepted, you have the carrier’s estimate, and the gap between it and a qualified contractor’s estimate is meaningful, often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is an appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, generally yes, once two of the three panel members sign. Coverage questions are not decided by the award.

How are appraisal fees handled?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee terms in writing before any work begins, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my county?

Yes. We work in all 100 North Carolina counties, from the mountains to the Outer Banks, with frequent work around Charlotte, the Triad, the Triangle, Fayetteville, Wilmington and the coast.
