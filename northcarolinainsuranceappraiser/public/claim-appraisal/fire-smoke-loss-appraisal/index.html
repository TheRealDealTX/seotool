Fire and Smoke Damage Appraisal for North Carolina Properties

Fire claims are the most complex property losses there are. Charred framing is obvious; smoke migration, HVAC contamination, code upgrades and contents are where estimates usually fall apart.

Fire damage goes far beyond the burn

A kitchen fire that never left one room can still send soot through the attic, into the ductwork and across every closet in the house. Heat can warp windows and crack drywall rooms away. Water from firefighting soaks floors and walls. And when a significant part of a structure burns, local building departments may require upgrades to wiring, framing or energy code that the original house never had.

What fire and smoke damage appraisal covers

  • Structural repair or full reconstruction, including demolition and debris removal.
  • Smoke and soot cleaning, sealing and odor control throughout the building, not just near the fire.
  • HVAC cleaning or replacement when ducts carried smoke.
  • Ordinance-or-law costs where repairs trigger current code requirements.
  • Contents: inventory, cleaning versus replacement, and actual cash value versus replacement cost.
  • Additional living expense tied to a realistic repair timeline.

Repair or rebuild?

When damage is heavy, the most expensive question is whether the structure can be saved. We look at char depth, framing condition and what the building department will require, then price the approach that actually returns the property to its pre-loss condition.

Contents claims

A household inventory can run to thousands of items. Carriers often apply steep depreciation or push cleaning for items that can’t realistically be restored. We review the inventory, pricing and depreciation applied, and present contents disputes as clearly as the structure.

Preserve the sceneDon’t throw away damaged items until they’re documented or the carrier agrees. Photograph everything, keep receipts for emergency purchases and ask before demolition begins.

Questions about fire & smoke loss appraisal

The carrier wants to clean my belongings, but they still smell. What now?

If cleaning can’t restore an item, replacement may be owed. We document odor and residue and price replacement where cleaning isn’t reasonable.

Does appraisal cover code upgrades after a fire?

If your policy includes ordinance-or-law coverage, the cost of required upgrades can be part of the valued loss, subject to that coverage’s limit.

How long does a fire appraisal take?

Larger fire claims often take two to four months through appraisal, depending on contents, the umpire’s schedule and how quickly both sides exchange estimates.
