How to Get Rid of Wrinkles Under Eyes: A Comprehensive Guide

Why fine lines form under your eyes, which ingredients are worth using, the habits that help, and a quick look at professional treatments.

The skin around your eyes is some of the thinnest on your body, so it is often the first place fine lines appear. If you are wondering how to soften wrinkles under eyes, the good news is that a few proven ingredients and habits can make a real difference. The realistic news is that no cream erases wrinkles overnight, and genetics and time always play a role.

This guide covers why under-eye lines form, which ingredients are worth your money, the daily habits that help, and an overview of in-office treatments if you want to go further.

Key takeaways

  • Sun exposure, collagen loss, repeated expressions, and dryness are the main causes of under-eye lines.
  • Daily sunscreen and sunglasses are your best prevention tools.
  • Retinol and peptides help over months; caffeine and hyaluronic acid give quicker, temporary smoothing.
  • Start slowly, patch test, and keep products out of your eyes.
  • A dermatologist can offer stronger options like prescription retinoids, lasers, or injectables.

What Causes Wrinkles Under Eyes

Under-eye wrinkles usually come from a combination of factors rather than just one.

Sun exposure

UV rays break down collagen and elastin, the proteins that keep skin firm and springy. Over the years, this is one of the biggest drivers of fine lines, which is why squinting in bright sun is a double hit.

Natural aging

Starting in early adulthood, your body gradually makes less collagen, and the skin around your eyes becomes thinner and less elastic.

Repeated expressions

Smiling, laughing, and squinting create "crow's feet" and fine lines under the eyes. These are a normal sign of an expressive face.

Dryness

The eye area has fewer oil glands, so it dries out easily. Dehydrated skin makes fine lines look more pronounced.

Smoking and lifestyle

Smoking reduces blood flow to the skin and speeds up collagen breakdown. Poor sleep and frequent eye rubbing can also make the area look more tired and creased.

Retinol

Retinol is one of the best-studied ingredients for fine lines. Because the under-eye area is delicate, choose a product designed for it or a low-strength formula, and apply a rice-grain-sized amount two or three nights a week. Increase only if your skin stays calm. Retinol is not recommended during pregnancy, so talk to your doctor if that applies to you.

Peptides

Peptides are short chains of amino acids that help support the skin's structure. They are a good choice if your skin does not tolerate retinol well.

Caffeine

Caffeine can temporarily constrict blood vessels and reduce puffiness, which makes the area look smoother and brighter for a few hours.

Hyaluronic acid

This humectant holds onto water, giving an instant plumping effect. Seal it in with a moisturizer so it does not leave skin feeling tight.

Vitamin C

Vitamin C is an antioxidant that helps defend against environmental damage and supports collagen. Some forms can sting, so look for gentler derivatives if your skin is sensitive.

Heads up: Patch test new eye products on your jawline or inner arm for a few days before using them near your eyes. Apply products along the orbital bone, not on your eyelids or lash line, and stop if you notice stinging, redness, or swelling.

A Simple Under-Eye Routine

Consistency matters more than having a lot of products. Here is a straightforward routine to start with.

Morning

  1. Cleanse gently. Use lukewarm water and a mild cleanser. Do not scrub the eye area.
  2. Apply a hydrating or caffeine eye product. Tap it on with your ring finger, which applies the least pressure.
  3. Moisturize. Use your regular moisturizer on the rest of your face.
  4. Protect. Apply a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher, including around your eyes. Mineral formulas with zinc oxide are often less likely to sting.

Evening

  1. Remove makeup carefully. Press a soaked cotton pad on your lids for a few seconds instead of rubbing.
  2. Apply your treatment. Use a retinol or peptide eye cream a few nights a week.
  3. Moisturize. Finish with a rich, fragrance-free cream to support your skin barrier.

Lifestyle Habits That Make a Difference

Products work best when your daily habits support them.

  • Wear sunglasses. Wraparound UV-blocking sunglasses protect the delicate skin and reduce squinting.
  • Sleep well. Aim for seven or more hours a night. Sleeping on your back may reduce creasing from your pillow.
  • Stay hydrated. Water will not erase wrinkles, but it supports overall skin health.
  • Don't smoke. Quitting is one of the best things you can do for your skin and your health.
  • Stop rubbing. If allergies make your eyes itchy, talk to your doctor about treatment instead of rubbing.
  • Eat a balanced diet. Colorful produce, healthy fats, and enough protein give skin the nutrients it needs.

In-Office Treatments: An Overview

If topical products are not giving you the results you want, a board-certified dermatologist can talk you through professional options. Each has its own risks, recovery time, and cost, so a consultation is essential.

  • Prescription retinoids: Stronger than over-the-counter retinol, with closer monitoring for irritation.
  • Chemical peels: Remove the outer layer of skin to improve texture. Strength varies widely.
  • Laser resurfacing: Uses light energy to stimulate collagen and smooth fine lines.
  • Microneedling: Creates tiny controlled injuries that encourage collagen production.
  • Neuromodulator injections: Relax the muscles that cause crow's feet and expression lines.
  • Fillers: Can address hollowness under the eyes, which sometimes makes lines look deeper. This area requires an experienced injector.
Good to know: Choose a board-certified dermatologist or plastic surgeon for any procedure near the eyes. The American Academy of Dermatology offers helpful background on wrinkle treatments.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using strong face retinoids or acids right up to your lash line
  • Skipping sunscreen around your eyes because it stings; try a mineral stick instead
  • Expecting overnight results and switching products every week
  • Pulling or tugging the skin when applying makeup or removing it
  • Using heavily fragranced products on this sensitive area

The Bottom Line

You cannot stop time, but you can make wrinkles under eyes less noticeable with daily sunscreen, gentle care, and a proven ingredient or two used consistently for a few months. Pair that with good sleep, hydration, and sunglasses, and consider a dermatologist visit if you want stronger options. Frequently asked questions

Can I use regular retinol under my eyes?

Some people can, but the under-eye skin is thin and easily irritated. A lower-strength retinol or an eye cream formulated for the area, used a few nights a week, is a gentler place to start.

How long does it take to see results from eye cream?

Ingredients like retinol and peptides generally need at least 8 to 12 weeks of consistent use before you notice softer lines. Hydrating ingredients can make skin look smoother within days, but that effect is temporary.

Do eye creams really work better than face moisturizer?

Not always. What matters most is the ingredient list and how well your skin tolerates it. Eye creams are often fragrance-free and gentler, which can be helpful for this delicate area.

Will drinking more water get rid of under-eye wrinkles?

Staying hydrated supports overall skin health, but water alone will not erase wrinkles. Sun protection and proven topical ingredients make a bigger difference.

When should I see a dermatologist about under-eye wrinkles?

See a dermatologist if over-the-counter products are not helping after a few months, if you want to explore in-office treatments, or if you notice swelling, discoloration, or a rash around your eyes.
