Every kitchen remodel before and after on the internet ends on the same day: the day the last cabinet door goes on and the countertop is still shiny. Nobody comes back twelve years later to check whether the budget cabinets sagged, whether the laminate counter survived a decade of hot pans, or whether the layout that looked so clever in 2014 still works when two adults are cooking at once. This house gives us the chance to do exactly that. The kitchen in this split-level was remodeled in early 2014 by the previous owners, who documented it on this blog. We live with it now. This is the before, the after, and the much more useful "twelve years after."

A note on who is talking. The [original IKEA Akurum cabinet install](/2014/02/07/akurum-cabinets/) was written by the people who did the work. We are the current owners, and the numbers below mix their recorded costs with what we have spent since. Where we are guessing, we say so.

## The before: avocado green and a wall in the wrong place

The 1970s kitchen that came with the house had the full period set: avocado green appliances, dark oak cabinets with the scalloped valance over the sink, a fluorescent box light, and a peninsula that cut the room in half so that the refrigerator door hit the oven door when both were open. The floor was sheet vinyl with a brick pattern. The counters were laminate with a metal edge strip that had collected forty years of crumbs.

Split-levels add a specific problem: the kitchen sits on the upper level with the dining room, and the ceiling is usually low, around 7 feet 8 inches here. Upper cabinets that reach the ceiling look cramped, and anything hanging over the peninsula makes the room feel like a hallway. The previous owners made the single best decision of the whole project before buying a thing: they removed the peninsula and kept the footprint otherwise unchanged, so the plumbing and the range stayed where they were.

## The after, as it was in 2014

What went in, from the original posts and receipts:

- IKEA Akurum base and wall cabinets in white, installed by the owners over two weekends.
- Laminate countertops, a matte charcoal, from a big-box store, fabricated and installed by the store.
- A mid-range stainless package: slide-in range, over-the-range microwave, dishwasher, and a counter-depth refrigerator.
- Luxury vinyl plank flooring over the old sheet vinyl, also DIY.
- A subway tile backsplash, DIY, with a mocha grout chosen to hide sins.
- Three recessed lights replacing the fluorescent box, done by an electrician along with two new circuits.

The recorded total was just under 14,000 dollars, with appliances the largest line at about 4,800, cabinets around 3,400, counters 1,900, electrical 1,500, flooring and tile under 1,000, and the rest in hardware, paint and the dumpster. The install labor they avoided was quoted at about 4,000, which is the number that makes a DIY kitchen a DIY kitchen.

## Twelve years on: what held up

### The cabinet boxes

The Akurum line was discontinued in 2015, which is worth knowing if you are planning the same route, but the boxes themselves have been fine. Particleboard with a melamine skin is not solid wood, and it does not pretend to be. The boxes are square, the doors still close, and the soft-close hinges still soft-close. Two drawer fronts have small chips at the bottom corner from a decade of being kicked shut. We have not had to adjust a single hinge.

The real test of a budget cabinet is water. The sink base survived a dishwasher supply line failure in 2021 because the previous owners had lined the bottom with a rubber tray, which is a 15-dollar habit we recommend to everyone. The one box that did swell was the base next to the dishwasher, where steam escaped at the end of every cycle for years. That side panel is bubbled along the bottom 2 inches. We replaced the dishwasher's door gasket and stopped the steam; the panel stays.

### The laminate counters

This is the surprise. The charcoal laminate looks nearly as it did on day one. It has two heat marks from pans set down without a trivet, one small chip at the sink cutout, and no stains. Laminate does not love standing water at the seams, and the seam behind the faucet is where it will fail eventually. Twelve years for 1,900 dollars is a better return than most of the quartz we have priced for the eventual replacement.

### The floor

Luxury vinyl plank over the old sheet vinyl has held up to dogs, kids and a flooded dishwasher. The only problem is the one every floating floor has in a split-level: the transition to the stairs. The plank at the top step has lifted twice and been glued twice. Plan a proper nosing there, not a transition strip.

### The tile and grout

The subway tile is fine. The mocha grout has darkened unevenly behind the range, which is the point of dark grout; it hides the darkening. If you want a light grout, seal it twice a year or accept that it will not stay light. The same grout choice worked in the [bathroom reveal](/2015/02/22/main-bathroom-reveal/), and the cleaning answer there is the same here: a soft brush and oxygen bleach, and no acid near the tile edge.

## What failed

- The over-the-range microwave died in 2023. It was the cheapest appliance in the package, and it had the hardest life. Replaced with a similar unit for 380 dollars.
- The slide-in range's control board failed in 2024, a 300-dollar repair we chose over a 1,400-dollar replacement. Modern ranges are computers with burners attached.
- The under-cabinet LED tape the previous owners added peeled off within a year of us moving in. The adhesive gave up in the heat over the range. Re-stuck with mounting clips, which is what it should have had from the start.
- The faucet's pull-down hose split at eight years. Twenty-dollar part, ten-minute fix, if you can find the part number.
- The ceiling. Three recessed lights in a low ceiling leave the corners dark. We added two more in 2025, which means the single best upgrade to a split-level kitchen is lighting, and it is cheap.

## The layout, two cooks later

Removing the peninsula was right. The room reads as one space with the dining area, the refrigerator and oven doors no longer fight, and the work triangle is about 14 feet total, which is close to the textbook figure. What we would change: the previous owners put the dishwasher to the left of the sink and the trash pull-out to the right. Everyone in this house is right-handed and scrapes plates toward the dishwasher, which means walking around the open dishwasher door with the trash. Swap them if you are starting from scratch.

The other change we would make is an island instead of nothing. The peninsula was wrong because it blocked the path. A narrow island, 24 inches deep and 48 long, on casters, gives back the prep space without the wall.

Roughly 15,000 dollars over twelve years for a kitchen that still works and still photographs well is the whole argument for the budget route. For context, the [room-by-room renovation cost](/2026/11/10/home-renovation-cost/) post puts a mid-range kitchen in the same footprint at 30,000 to 50,000 this year.

## What we would do differently

1. Spend more on the dishwasher and the range, less on the refrigerator. The appliances that failed were the ones that get used hardest and were bought cheapest.
2. Put five recessed lights in, not three, on the first day.
3. Plan the stair transition before the floor goes down.
4. Choose a matte laminate again, and keep a trivet by the range.
5. Line every sink and dishwasher base with a tray on day one.

The National Kitchen and Bath Association publishes [planning guidelines](https://nkba.org/) for clearances, work triangles and landing space that are worth a read before any layout decision. Most of what went right in this kitchen matches them, and the one thing that went wrong, the trash and dishwasher swap, is covered there too.

If you are planning the same project in the same kind of house, the [whole-house renovation budget](/2026/10/26/home-renovation-budget/) post explains where a kitchen like this fits in the bigger spend. The short version of this before and after: the budget kitchen aged better than the budget appliances, the layout decision mattered more than any finish, and twelve years is a fair test.
