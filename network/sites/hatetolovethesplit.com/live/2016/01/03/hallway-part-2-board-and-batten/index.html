Hallway Part 2: Board and Batten

By the Split · January 3, 2016 · 2 min read

  1. Home
  2. Hallway
  3. Hallway Part 2: Board and Batten

New doors are in. Now the hallway needed something to make it feel less like a tunnel and more like part of the house. The answer: board and batten.

Why board and batten walls?

Board and batten adds texture and shadow lines without the cost or fuss of real wainscoting. In a narrow hallway it also protects the lower wall from scuffs, bags and the vacuum. And it's very beginner friendly: if you can use a miter saw and a nail gun, you can do this.

Materials

  • 1x4 primed MDF for the vertical battens and the top rail
  • 1x2 for a small cap ledge on top of the rail
  • Construction adhesive and 2" brad nails
  • Paintable caulk and wood filler
  • Semi-gloss paint (we used the same white as our trim)

How we did it

  1. Pick a height. About 2/3 of the wall height looked best in our hallway, roughly 60".
  2. Install the top rail level all the way around, nailed into studs.
  3. Space the battens evenly. We aimed for 16" to 18" between battens and adjusted so each wall ended with a full space.
  4. Glue and nail each batten, checking plumb with a level.
  5. Caulk every seam and fill nail holes. This is the step that makes it look built-in.
  6. Paint the battens and the wall between them the same color for a seamless look.
✦
Outlets and switches

Plan batten spacing so battens don't land on an outlet. If you can't avoid it, add a box extender so the cover plate sits flush on top of the board.

The hallway went from our most hated space to our most complimented. Every guest touches the walls. We're choosing to take that as a compliment.
