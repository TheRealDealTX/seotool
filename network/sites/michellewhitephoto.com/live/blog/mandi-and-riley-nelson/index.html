Mandi & Riley Nelson: a mountain lake wedding

October 20265 min readMichelle White Photo

Some wedding stories are remembered for one big moment. Mandi and Riley Nelson’s is remembered for its light — the soft, low autumn sun on still water, and the way a mountain lake turns an ordinary afternoon into something cinematic. It remains one of the most-visited stories in this journal, so we’ve refreshed it with the lessons it taught us about planning a lakeside wedding in Utah.

Why lakes photograph so well

Water is a giant reflector. On a calm evening a lake doubles the sky, bounces warm light back onto faces and gives every portrait a natural, glowing fill. Add a ring of pines or aspens and a ridge line behind, and you have a frame that needs very little help.

The trade-off is that lakes are exposed. Wind picks up in the afternoon, the light on the water can be harsh at midday, and mountain weather changes quickly. A great lakeside wedding is planned around those realities rather than despite them.

Plan the day around the light

  • Ceremony timing. Aim for a ceremony that ends 60–90 minutes before sunset. Guests aren’t squinting, and you walk straight into golden hour.
  • Portraits in two parts. Do family photos right after the ceremony, then slip away for 20 minutes of couple portraits as the sun drops.
  • Morning calm. If the venue allows, a first look by the water in the early morning gives glassy reflections before the wind arrives.

A palette that suits the setting

Mountain lakes are full of deep greens, blues and slate greys. Warm, earthy accents — rust, terracotta, honey, cream — sit beautifully against them, and they glow in late light. Loose, textural florals with seasonal stems feel more at home here than tight, formal arrangements.

Choose details that belong to the landscape, and the landscape will do half the work for you.

Details guests remember

Blankets on the ceremony benches for when the temperature drops, a hot-cider station, lanterns along the path back from the shore — small touches like these are practical in the mountains, and they make lovely, atmospheric photographs too.

Planning your own lakeside wedding

If you are dreaming of a wedding by the water in the Wasatch or the Uintas, start with the light and work backwards: sunset time on your date, how long it takes to walk from the ceremony site to the shore, and a covered back-up spot if a storm rolls in. Get those three things right and the rest falls into place. See more of our wedding work or check your date.
