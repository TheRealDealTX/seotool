The Harrison Ford Earring: Getting Pierced Later in Life

The Harrison Ford earring story: he pierced his ear in his 50s, reportedly after a lunch with Jimmy Buffett and Ed Bradley. Plus how to wear a classic stud.

Celebrity4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • Harrison Ford is widely reported to have pierced his ear in the late 1990s, in his mid-50s.
  • He has said the idea came from a lunch with Jimmy Buffett and Ed Bradley, who both wore earrings.
  • His look is a small, simple earring in one ear — nothing flashy.
  • It's proof it's never too late: a lobe piercing heals the same way at 55 as it does at 15.

The Harrison Ford earring surprises people who mostly know him as Han Solo or Indiana Jones. Ford got his ear pierced in his 50s — reports usually date it to 1997, when he was about 55 — and has since been photographed wearing a small earring at premieres and events. It's one of the better-known examples of a celebrity getting a first piercing later in life.

Here's the story as Ford has told it, what the earring actually looks like, and what to know if you're thinking about doing the same.

How Harrison Ford got his ear pierced

Ford has told the story in interviews more than once. The short version: he had lunch with musician Jimmy Buffett and journalist Ed Bradley, both of whom wore earrings, and after a few drinks he decided to get pierced too. He told it to Maxim around 2008, and he retold it at a 2024 tribute concert for Buffett, where he joked about the spontaneous decision.

We're paraphrasing rather than quoting here, since accounts differ slightly in the details. But the core of the story — a relaxed lunch with two friends, followed by a spur-of-the-moment piercing — has been consistent.

What the Harrison Ford earring looks like

Ford's style is the opposite of flashy. In photos he's typically seen with a small stud or tiny hoop in one ear, often hard to spot unless you're looking for it. There's no stacked ear and no dangling charm — just a single, quiet piece of metal.

That understated approach is a big reason the look works. A small earring adds a little personality to a classic, rugged style without looking like it's trying too hard.

Getting pierced later in life

Plenty of people get a first piercing in their 40s, 50s or later. The process is the same as at any age, though a few things are worth keeping in mind:

  1. Go to a professional. Choose a reputable studio — ideally an APP (Association of Professional Piercers) member — that pierces with a sterile needle rather than a piercing gun.
  2. Start with the right metal. Implant-grade titanium or 14k-plus solid gold is the safest starter jewelry.
  3. Mention your health. If you take blood thinners, have diabetes or heal slowly, tell your piercer and check with your doctor first.
  4. Be patient. Lobes typically feel healed in about 6–8 weeks but can take a few months to fully mature.

Tip: skip the impulse decision at a mall kiosk. A spontaneous idea is fine — just sleep on it and book a proper piercer the next day.

Choosing a stud that ages well

A single small earring is something you might wear for decades, so it's worth buying one good piece rather than several cheap ones. Solid white or yellow gold, platinum and titanium all hold their color and won't irritate most skin. A screw-back or threadless back is more secure than a standard butterfly back for a piece you never take off.

Plain metal is the most timeless choice. A small diamond or moissanite adds a little sparkle without changing the low-key feel.

One ear or both?

Ford's look is a single earring, and that's a great model for anyone getting pierced later in life. One small earring feels like a personal choice rather than a fashion statement, and it's easy to dress up or down. You can always add the second ear later if you find you love it.

If you're more comfortable with symmetry, pierce both lobes and wear small matching studs. Either way, start small. A 3–4 mm stud is noticeable up close but never loud.

Aftercare basics

Clean the piercing with sterile saline once or twice a day, avoid twisting the jewelry and keep hair products and phone screens off it as much as you can. Don't swap jewelry until your piercer says you're healed.

See a doctor if you notice spreading redness, heat, increasing pain, pus or fever — those can be signs of infection. If your earring area ever develops an odor, our guide on why earrings smell explains what causes it and how to stop it.

Frequently asked questions

When did Harrison Ford get his ear pierced?

It's widely reported that he got pierced around 1997, when he was in his mid-50s.

Why did Harrison Ford get an earring?

He has said the idea came from a lunch with Jimmy Buffett and Ed Bradley, who both wore earrings, and that it was a spontaneous decision.

Which ear is Harrison Ford's earring in?

He wears a single small earring in one ear. It's subtle, so it's easy to miss in photos.

Is it too late to get my ears pierced in my 50s?

No. Lobe piercings heal well at any age; just use a professional piercer and quality starter jewelry, and check with your doctor if you have health conditions that affect healing.
