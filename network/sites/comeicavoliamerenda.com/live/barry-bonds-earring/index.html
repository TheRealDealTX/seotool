The Barry Bonds Earring: How the Dangling Cross Became Iconic

The Barry Bonds earring — a diamond dangling cross — became a 2000s baseball style icon. Here's the story behind it and how to wear a cross earring today.

Celebrity4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • Barry Bonds was well known for wearing a diamond dangling cross earring during his San Francisco Giants years.
  • The look was a single earring, which made it read as a personal signature rather than a matched pair.
  • To get a similar style, choose a small cross charm on a hoop or huggie, about 10–20 mm long.
  • Keep it to one piece and let the cross be the only sparkle.

The Barry Bonds earring is one of the most recognizable pieces of jewelry in baseball history. Through much of his time with the San Francisco Giants, Bonds was frequently photographed wearing a sparkling cross that dangled from one ear — at the plate, in the dugout and in post-game interviews. For a lot of fans who grew up in that era, it's the image that comes to mind when they think of a man's cross earring.

Here's what made the look stand out, how it fit the wider cross earring trend, and how to wear something similar without it looking like a costume.

What was the Barry Bonds earring?

The piece most people remember is a diamond or diamond-look dangling cross, worn as a single earring. Sports coverage from the 2000s mentions Bonds and his diamond cross often enough that it became part of his public image, and in some seasons blogs even noted when he went back to wearing it.

Specifics like the exact maker, metal or carat weight aren't well documented, so we won't guess. What is clear from photos is the overall style: a small cross, set with stones, hanging just below the lobe from a short hoop or bail.

Why it stood out

Baseball has never been short on superstitions or personal style, but jewelry at the plate was still unusual for a player at Bonds' level of fame. The earring stood out for a few reasons:

  • Movement: a dangling cross catches light every time the head turns, which reads well on TV.
  • One ear only: a single earring feels like a signature rather than an accessory set.
  • Contrast: sparkle against a uniform and batting helmet is hard to miss.

Cross earrings in general had been popular in pop and hip-hop style for years before that — many people credit George Michael with popularizing the single dangling cross in the 1980s. Bonds helped carry that style into sports.

How to get the Barry Bonds earring look

The modern version of this look is easy to find. Search for "dangling cross earrings" and you'll see everything from tiny plain crosses to pavé-set statement pieces. Our full guide to dangling cross earrings for men goes deeper, but here's the quick version.

Tip: a dangling earring moves and catches on things. If you play sports, swap it for a flat stud before the game — most leagues and coaches ask players to remove jewelry anyway.

Picking a cross earring that lasts

The biggest difference between a cheap cross earring and a good one is the stones and the plating. Lab-grown diamonds and moissanite give real sparkle at a lower price than mined diamonds. Cubic zirconia looks great at first but can cloud over time.

For the metal, solid 14k gold, sterling silver and stainless steel are all reliable. Avoid unknown plated alloys if your ears are sensitive. If you've never checked how earring backs and closures differ, our types of earring backs guide helps you pick one that won't fall out mid-swing.

Caring for a stone-set cross

Pavé and small set stones collect lotion, sweat and dust, which dulls the sparkle fast. Soak the earring in warm water with a drop of mild dish soap, brush gently with a soft toothbrush and dry it well. Check the prongs every few months so a stone doesn't go missing.

Single earring or a pair?

Part of what made the Barry Bonds earring memorable was that it was a single piece. A lone dangling cross reads like a personal signature; a matching pair reads more like a jewelry set. Neither is wrong, but if you're going for that era's feel, one ear is the move.

If you want balance, put a tiny flat stud in the other lobe. It keeps your look from feeling lopsided while letting the cross stay the focal point.

Wearing it today

The cross earring has gone through several style cycles and is firmly back in rotation, partly thanks to 2000s nostalgia. The easiest way to make it look current is to keep everything else minimal: one cross, one ear, no competing jewelry on the same side.

If the cross has religious meaning for you, it's a natural way to wear your faith. If it's purely a style choice, that's common too — but it's worth knowing that some people will read it as a religious symbol either way. Frequently asked questions

What earring did Barry Bonds wear?

He was well known for a single dangling cross earring set with diamonds or diamond-look stones, worn during his years with the San Francisco Giants.

Which ear did Barry Bonds wear his earring in?

Photos vary, and the details aren't consistently documented. What's clear is that he usually wore one earring, not a matched pair.

How long should a dangling cross earring be?

For an everyday look, 8–12 mm is subtle. For something closer to the Bonds style, 15–20 mm with stones is more noticeable.

Who started the cross earring trend?

Many people credit George Michael with popularizing the single dangling cross in the 1980s. Athletes like Bonds later brought it into sports.
