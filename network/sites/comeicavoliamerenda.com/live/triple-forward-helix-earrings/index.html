Triple Forward Helix Piercing: The Complete Guide to Placement, Pain and Jewelry

Thinking about a triple forward helix piercing? Learn placement, pain, the 6–12 month healing timeline, the best flat-back jewelry and how to style it.

Piercings4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • A triple forward helix piercing is three small holes stacked in the cartilage where the top of your ear meets your face.
  • Expect a sharp, quick pinch for each hole; most people rate it around 5–6 out of 10.
  • Healing typically takes 6–12 months, sometimes longer for three holes close together.
  • Start with implant-grade titanium or 14k+ gold flat-back labrets with tiny gems or balls.

A triple forward helix piercing is three little points of sparkle tucked into the front rim of your ear, right where it curves toward your temple. It is subtle from straight on and striking in profile, which is exactly why it has become a favorite for people building a curated ear. Here is what to know about placement, pain, healing and jewelry before you book.

Where a triple forward helix piercing sits

The forward helix is the small fold of cartilage at the front of your upper ear, just above the tragus and next to your face. A triple places three holes in a short line along that fold, usually descending in a gentle diagonal that follows the natural curve.

Not every ear has room for three. Your piercer will look at how much flat, sturdy cartilage you have and how far apart each hole needs to be so the jewelry tops do not crowd or press on each other. If your forward helix is narrow, a double may look cleaner, and a good piercer will tell you so.

The forward helix is different from the regular helix on the outer rim. If you are still deciding between the two, our guide to helix earrings breaks down every upper-ear option side by side.

How much does it hurt?

Most people describe each needle pass as a sharp, hot pinch that is over in a second or two. The forward helix has relatively thin cartilage, so many find it more tolerable than a rook or conch.

The catch with a triple is repetition. You feel three pinches in a row, and the area gets tender and warm after the first one. Some people do all three in one session; others split them across two visits so the ear is not as swollen. Either is fine, so ask your piercer what they recommend for your anatomy.

Choose a professional piercer (an APP member is a great starting point) who uses a hollow needle, never a piercing gun. Guns are not designed for cartilage and can shatter tissue, which slows healing.

Healing timeline and aftercare

Cartilage heals slowly. A forward helix piercing typically takes 6–12 months to heal fully, and three holes close together can sit at the longer end of that range. It may feel fine after a few weeks while the inside is still healing.

  • Weeks 1–4: Tenderness, redness and some swelling. Your piercer will usually start you on slightly longer posts to allow for this.
  • Weeks 4–12: Swelling settles. Go back to have the posts downsized so the jewelry does not snag or shift.
  • Months 3–12: Gradual strengthening of the channel. Avoid changing jewelry yourself until your piercer confirms you are healed.

Rinse with sterile saline once or twice a day, keep hair products and phone screens away, and do not twist the jewelry. Some irritation bumps are common in cartilage; persistent heat, spreading redness, thick discolored discharge or fever are signs of infection, so see a doctor promptly.

Best jewelry for a triple forward helix

For a fresh piercing, the standard is a flat-back labret: a straight post with a flat disc on the back and a small threadless or internally threaded top on the front. The flat back sits comfortably against the side of your head, which matters in such a tight spot.

How to style it: looks to try

Since you cannot see photos here, picture these combinations, which are some of the most popular ways people wear a triple forward helix.

  • The descending trio: three gems that step down in size, such as 3mm, 2.5mm and 2mm, for a shooting-star effect.
  • All-gold minimal: three polished gold balls that catch the light without adding color.
  • Mixed shapes: a tiny star, a moon and a dot, which is a playful, mismatched take that suits our whole philosophy.
  • Opal or birthstone line: three matching colored stones for a pop of color right next to your face.

Pairing with a tragus

The forward helix sits just above the tragus, so the two are natural partners. A small gem in the tragus anchors the look and makes the triple feel intentional, almost like a column of sparkle along the front of your ear. See our guide to tragus earrings for jewelry that complements it. Add a lobe stack and a small hoop, and you have a fully curated ear.

Is a triple forward helix right for you?

It is a great fit if you love detail, sleep on your back or can switch sides, and are patient with a long healing window. It may not be ideal if you wear headphones that press the front of the ear or play contact sports often. If three feels like a lot, start with one or two and add more later once the first holes are settled.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a triple forward helix piercing take to heal?

Typically 6–12 months, and sometimes longer since three holes sit close together. Leave jewelry changes to your piercer until they confirm you are healed.

Can I get all three forward helix holes at once?

Many people do, but some piercers prefer to split them across two sessions to limit swelling. Your piercer will recommend what suits your anatomy.

What gauge is forward helix jewelry?

Most forward helix piercings are done at 16g (1.2mm), with some at 18g (1.0mm). Flat-back labrets are the usual starter jewelry.

Can I wear hoops in a forward helix?

Only once fully healed, and only very small ones. Hoops move more than labrets and can irritate a healing cartilage piercing.
