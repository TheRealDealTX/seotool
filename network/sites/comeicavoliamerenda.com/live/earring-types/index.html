Types of Earrings: A Friendly Guide to Every Style

Types of earrings explained: studs, hoops, huggies, drops, dangles, chandeliers, jhumkas, threaders, ear cuffs, climbers, jackets and clip-ons, with tips.

Guides4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • The basics: studs, hoops and drops cover most everyday looks.
  • Statement styles include chandeliers, jhumkas and long dangles.
  • Ear cuffs, climbers and jackets add drama to a curated ear.
  • No piercing? Clip-ons and ear cuffs are your friends.

Knowing the main types of earrings makes it easier to shop, describe what you want, and build an ear that feels like yours. Some styles are made for one hole; others shine when mixed together. Here's a tour of every common style, from tiny studs to sweeping chandeliers.

Everyday essentials

Studs

The workhorse of any collection: a stone, pearl, metal shape or tiny charm on a post. Studs come in every size; our 5mm earrings actual size guide shows how they compare.

Hoops

From whisper-thin wires to chunky twisted gold, hoops are endlessly versatile. Diameter and thickness change the mood completely. A small thin hoop is quiet; a large tube hoop is a whole personality.

Huggies

Mini hoops designed to sit close to the lobe. They're the go-to for stacking and many people find them comfortable enough to sleep in.

Drops, dangles and statement styles

Drops and dangles

A drop hangs just below the lobe and stays fairly still. A dangle is longer and swings with movement. Both lengthen the neckline and catch light beautifully.

Chandeliers

Ornate, layered designs with multiple tiers of stones, beads or metalwork. They're the earring equivalent of a ball gown, so let them be the star and keep the rest of your jewelry calm.

Jhumkas

Bell or dome-shaped earrings from South Asian jewelry traditions, often with fringes of tiny beads or pearls. Learn more in our jhumka earrings guide.

Threaders

Thin chains or wires that pass through the piercing and hang on both sides. They're light, modern, and fun to style long in front and short behind.

Ear-shaping styles for curated ears

Ear cuffs

Cuffs hug the outer edge of the ear and usually don't need a piercing. They're a low-commitment way to try the cartilage look.

Climbers or crawlers

These go into a lobe piercing and trail up along the ear edge. One piece mimics the effect of several studs in a row.

Ear jackets

A stud sits on the front of the lobe while a larger decorative piece hangs behind or below it, peeking out. Many jackets can be worn with or without the back piece, so you get two looks in one.

Options for unpierced ears

Clip-ons pinch the lobe with a hinged clip or padded paddle, and modern ones are far more comfortable than the vintage versions. Magnetic earrings use two magnets, and ear cuffs need no hole at all. For little ones, see our guide to clip on earrings for kids.

The back changes how an earring wears as much as its style. Lever-backs, screw backs and threadless tops all affect security and comfort; see our guide to types of earring backs.

Choosing types of earrings for your face and day

Forget strict face-shape rules; proportion and lifestyle matter more. If you wear headphones, helmets or a phone on your shoulder all day, studs and huggies stay out of the way. If you want to draw attention upward for photos, drops and dangles bring light to the face.

Weight is the other practical consideration. Big chandeliers and heavy hoops can stretch lobes over time, so save them for occasions and give your ears rest days.

Mixing types on one ear

Curated ears work best with a mix of sizes and shapes. A simple formula:

  1. Start with an anchor in the first hole, like a drop or a medium hoop.
  2. Add something smaller in the next hole, such as a huggie or stud.
  3. Finish with a tiny stud, climber or cuff higher up.
  4. Repeat one element, like metal color or stone, to tie it together.

Then break a rule on purpose. A single chandelier on one side and tiny studs on the other is exactly the kind of charming mismatch that makes an ear memorable.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main types of earrings?

The main types are studs, hoops, huggies, drops, dangles, chandeliers, threaders, ear cuffs, climbers, jackets and clip-ons.

What is the difference between a drop and a dangle earring?

A drop hangs just below the lobe and moves little. A dangle is usually longer and swings freely.

What earrings can you wear without piercings?

Clip-ons, magnetic earrings and ear cuffs can all be worn without a piercing.

What are huggie earrings?

Huggies are small hoops that sit snugly against the lobe, usually with a hinged closure. They're popular for stacking.
