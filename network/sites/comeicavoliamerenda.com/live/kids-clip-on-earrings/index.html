Clip On Earrings for Kids: A Parent's Guide

Clip on earrings for kids: how to pick comfy, safe pairs, avoid choking hazards and nickel, lead or cadmium, compare magnetic and sticker earrings and more.

Guides4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • Look for padded, adjustable clips and lightweight designs for comfort.
  • Small parts are a choking hazard; supervise younger children.
  • Avoid cheap metal jewelry that may contain nickel, lead or cadmium.
  • Magnetic earrings carry extra risk if swallowed; keep them away from little kids.

Clip on earrings for kids are a fun way to play with style before (or instead of) getting ears pierced. They suit dress-up, recitals, holidays and children who simply aren't ready for a piercing. Choosing the right pair comes down to comfort, safety and realistic expectations about how long a child will happily wear them.

Choosing comfortable clip on earrings for kids

Children have small, sensitive lobes, and an adult clip can pinch hard enough to put them off earrings entirely. Look for designs made with kids' ears in mind.

  • Padded clips: silicone or rubber pads cushion the lobe and add grip.
  • Adjustable tension: screw-style clips let you loosen the pressure.
  • Lightweight designs: small, flat shapes are easier to wear than heavy dangles.
  • Short wear time: start with 20 to 30 minutes, and take them off if lobes turn red or sore.

Pretty dangles like crystal hearts look adorable but pull harder on the lobe. For everyday play, smaller clip-on studs are usually more comfortable.

Safety first: choking, small parts and metals

Choking and small parts

Earrings are small, and clip-on parts, beads and stones can come loose. They are a choking hazard for babies and toddlers, and many kids' jewelry products carry age warnings for this reason. Supervise younger children, check pieces for loose parts, and put earrings away after play.

Magnets

Magnetic earrings deserve extra caution. If a child swallows more than one small magnet, they can attract each other through the intestines and cause serious injury. Keep magnetic earrings away from young children altogether, and seek medical help immediately if you suspect one has been swallowed.

Nickel, lead and cadmium

Inexpensive jewelry, including some children's jewelry, has been found to contain lead or cadmium, which are toxic, especially if mouthed or swallowed. Nickel is a common cause of skin allergy, and early exposure can lead to lifelong sensitivity.

  • Buy from reputable sellers that state the materials clearly.
  • Look for nickel-free, lead-free and cadmium-free labeling.
  • Prefer plastic, resin, fabric or coated designs made for children.
  • Discourage chewing or sucking on jewelry.

If you suspect a child has swallowed jewelry, especially a magnet or a metal charm, contact a doctor or poison control right away rather than waiting for symptoms.

Sticker earrings are often the easiest starting point for younger kids. Clip-ons suit children who want something that looks more like real jewelry. For a broader look at how closures work, see our guide to types of earring backs.

Checking fit and wear time

Before a big event, do a short trial at home. Clip the earrings on, set a timer for 15 minutes, and check the lobes afterward. Light pink marks that fade quickly are fine; deep red marks, numbness or tears mean the clip is too tight.

For clips with a screw adjustment, loosen until they just stay put when your child shakes their head. Pack a small pouch so earrings can come off as soon as they're uncomfortable, rather than ending up in a pocket or on the floor.

When to consider piercing

There's no single right age to pierce ears. Many families wait until a child asks for it, can say yes clearly, and is ready to help with aftercare. Pierced ears need daily care for weeks, and children need to keep hands, hair and toys away from fresh piercings.

If you decide to go ahead, choose a professional piercer, ideally an APP member, who uses a sterile needle and implant-grade titanium or 14k+ solid gold jewelry. Lobe piercings typically heal on the surface in about 6 to 8 weeks, with full healing taking a few months. Avoid at-home methods such as self piercing earrings.

Once ears are healed, small, flat studs with secure screw backs are a practical first choice. Our 5mm earrings actual size guide shows how small studs like 3mm and 4mm look in real life.

Making it fun

Clip-ons can be a lovely way to explore taste. Let kids pick colors and shapes, mix and match, or wear one star and one heart. Store their collection in a small tray or box so pieces don't wander into the toy bin. Mismatched is not a mistake here; it's a style.

Frequently asked questions

Are clip on earrings safe for kids?

Yes for older children with supervision, as long as they're comfortable, made from safe materials and checked for loose parts. Keep them away from babies and toddlers.

Do clip on earrings hurt kids?

They can pinch if the clip is tight. Padded or adjustable clips and short wear times help keep them comfortable.

Are magnetic earrings safe for children?

Small magnets can cause serious internal injuries if more than one is swallowed, so keep magnetic earrings away from young children.

What age can kids get their ears pierced?

There's no single right age. Many families wait until a child asks and can help with aftercare, then visit a professional piercer.
