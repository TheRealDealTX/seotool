Kylie Jenner Ear Piercings: Building Your Own Curated Ear

Kylie Jenner ear piercings helped popularize the curated ear: stacked lobe studs and tiny hoops. Here's the look and how to plan your own stack safely.

Celebrity4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • Kylie Jenner is often photographed with multiple lobe piercings styled as a delicate stack.
  • The look is minimal and polished: small studs and tiny hoops, usually in gold or diamond.
  • It's part of the wider curated ear trend — planning piercings and jewelry as one composition.
  • Start with two or three lobe piercings and add pieces over time once each one heals.

Kylie Jenner ear piercings are a big part of why the "curated ear" went mainstream. In photos and social posts she's often seen with several piercings along the lobe, styled with small studs and tiny hoops rather than one big statement earring. It's a polished, minimalist approach — and one of the easiest celebrity looks to adapt for yourself.

We won't guess at an exact count or list of her piercings, since that isn't consistently documented and her jewelry changes often. Instead, here's how the look works and how to build your own version.

What Kylie Jenner's ear piercings look like

The style fans most often try to copy has a few consistent features:

  • Multiple lobe piercings arranged in a line, from the main lobe up toward the upper lobe.
  • Graduated sizes — slightly larger pieces at the bottom, smaller ones higher up.
  • Tiny hoops and studs, often in gold or set with diamonds.
  • A cohesive palette — one metal tone so the stack reads as a set.

It's less about any single earring and more about how they work together. That's the heart of the curated ear.

What is a curated ear?

A curated ear treats your piercings like a small composition. Instead of matching pairs, you plan placement and jewelry so the whole ear looks intentional — sometimes even different on each side. The trend grew through high-end piercing studios and social media in the 2010s and has stayed popular since.

It fits our whole philosophy here: mismatched can be beautiful, as long as it's thoughtful.

How to build a Kylie-style stack

Here's a simple lobe-focused layout that gets you close to the look:

PositionJewelryTypical size
First lobeSmall hoop or huggie8–10 mm
Second lobeDiamond or CZ stud3–4 mm
Third / upper lobeTiny stud or dot2–3 mm
Optional cartilageFlat-back stud or small hoop2–3 mm stud / 6–8 mm hoop

For more detail on spacing and jewelry for the second hole, see our guide to double lobe piercing earrings. If you want to extend the stack into cartilage, our helix earrings guide covers the next step.

Tip: bring photos of looks you like to your piercer, but let them adjust the placement to your ear's anatomy. The same layout can look very different on a small or large lobe.

Getting pierced safely

  1. Choose a professional. Look for an APP (Association of Professional Piercers) member who uses a sterile needle, not a piercing gun.
  2. Start with good metal. Implant-grade titanium or 14k-plus solid gold reduces irritation in fresh piercings.
  3. Pace yourself. Getting a couple of piercings at once is common, but adding more over time makes aftercare easier.
  4. Know the healing times. Lobes typically take around 6–8 weeks to heal on the surface; cartilage often takes 6–12 months.

Clean with sterile saline, don't sleep on fresh piercings and don't change jewelry until you're healed. See a doctor if you notice spreading redness, heat, pus or fever.

Common curated-ear mistakes

  • Crowding the lobe. Piercings placed too close together leave no room for hoops. A good piercer will space them for the jewelry you want.
  • Changing jewelry too early. Swapping studs for hoops before healing is a common cause of irritation bumps.
  • Hoops that are too big. Oversized hoops in upper positions twist and catch. Measure, then size down.
  • Copying exactly. Every ear is shaped differently, so a celebrity layout may not fit yours.

Most of these come down to patience. A curated ear is built over months, not in a single appointment, and the best-looking stacks are usually the ones that grew slowly. Keep a photo note of what you have and what you want next, and plan each new piercing around the jewelry you already love.

Choosing metals and finishes

A curated ear looks most polished when the metals agree. Most stacks in this style stick to yellow gold, sometimes with a little sparkle from diamonds or CZ. Mixing metals can look great too, but it works best when it's clearly deliberate — for example, alternating gold and silver in a regular pattern.

For healed piercings, solid gold lasts longest and is least likely to irritate. Gold-plated pieces are cheaper but can wear through to the base metal, which may bother sensitive ears.

Making it your own

The best curated ears don't copy anyone exactly. Mix in a charm, a colored stone or a single pearl, or try a different arrangement on each ear. If you're unsure about sizes, the earring size tool shows small studs and hoops at actual scale so you can plan the stack before you buy.
