Earring Piercing Guide: Every Ear Placement, With Healing Times

Come i Cavoli a Merenda · October 26, 2026

This earring piercing guide covers every standard placement on the ear, in the order most people get them, with the measurements and healing times that matter when you are choosing jewelry. The numbers below are typical ranges from professional piercers, not promises; healing depends on the placement, the jewelry, the aftercare and the person. Where a placement has a sizing quirk we have linked to the detailed page for it.

A note before the list. Piercings done with a hollow needle by a trained piercer heal more predictably than piercings done with a gun, especially anywhere in cartilage. The Association of Professional Piercers explains why in its piercing basics, and it is worth reading once before you book anything.

The ear, mapped

Think of the ear in three zones. The lobe at the bottom, soft tissue, fast to heal. The outer rim of cartilage, called the helix, running from above the lobe up and around to the top. And the inner cartilage: the small shelves and folds inside the rim, which are harder to pierce and slower to heal but carry the most distinctive jewelry.

Lobe

Where: the soft tissue at the bottom of the ear. Healing: six to eight weeks, sometimes longer before you can leave jewelry out overnight. Pain: the lowest on the ear. Starter jewelry: 20 gauge to 18 gauge studs, 6 to 8 millimetre post. Notes: the first and simplest piercing. A second or third lobe piercing sits a few millimetres along from the first, toward the face or up the lobe. Our 5mm earrings actual size page is the usual reference for stud sizes here.

Upper lobe and transverse lobe

Where: the upper lobe sits where the soft tissue meets the cartilage. A transverse lobe runs horizontally through the lobe, with a bar rather than a stud. Healing: eight to twelve weeks for the upper lobe; three to six months for a transverse. Pain: low to moderate. Starter jewelry: 18 or 16 gauge; a transverse uses a curved or straight barbell.

Helix

Where: the outer rim of cartilage, usually in the upper third. Healing: three to six months, often longer. Pain: moderate, with a pressure feeling for a few days. Starter jewelry: 16 gauge flat-back labret stud, 6 to 8 millimetre post, downsized after the swelling settles. Notes: the most common cartilage piercing and the one most often done with a gun, which is the main reason helix piercings have a reputation for bumps. Sleep on the other side.

Forward helix

Where: the front of the rim, near where the ear meets the head, just above the tragus. Healing: three to nine months. Pain: moderate to higher, because the tissue is thick. Starter jewelry: 16 or 18 gauge flat-back stud with a short post. Double and triple forward helix stacks are placed at the same time, evenly spaced.

Flat

Where: the flat area of cartilage inside the upper rim. Healing: six to twelve months. Pain: moderate. Starter jewelry: 16 gauge flat-back stud. The flat is the best placement for a decorative top because it sits on a plain surface. The cute cartilage earrings page shows the styles that suit it.

Industrial

Where: two piercings through the upper cartilage, joined by one straight bar. Healing: nine months to a year. Pain: higher, and the bar can catch on hair and clothing while healing. Starter jewelry: 14 gauge straight barbell, usually 32 to 38 millimetres long; it depends on the ear. Notes: not every ear has the anatomy for it. A good piercer will tell you if the angle will not work.

Tragus

Where: the small flap of cartilage in front of the ear canal. Healing: six to twelve months. Pain: moderate; the sound of the needle is the part people remember. Starter jewelry: 16 gauge flat-back stud, 6 millimetre post. Our tragus earrings page covers studs and hoops for after healing.

Anti-tragus

Where: the ridge of cartilage opposite the tragus, just above the lobe. Healing: six to twelve months. Pain: moderate to higher. Not everyone has enough ridge for it. Starter jewelry: 16 gauge curved barbell or flat-back stud.

Daith

Where: the innermost fold of cartilage, above the ear canal. Healing: six to nine months. Pain: moderate; a deep pressure rather than a sharp pain. Starter jewelry: 16 gauge curved barbell or a seamless ring, 8 to 10 millimetres. Notes: often chosen for its look with a heart-shaped ring. Claims about migraines are not supported by evidence; get it because you like it.

Rook

Where: the fold of cartilage above the daith, in the upper inner ear. Healing: six to twelve months. Pain: among the higher on the ear, because the fold is thick. Starter jewelry: 16 gauge curved barbell, 6 to 8 millimetres. Needs enough fold to hold a bar; not every ear qualifies.

Snug

Where: the inner ridge of cartilage that runs parallel to the outer rim, at the middle of the ear. Healing: six to twelve months, with more swelling than most. Pain: high. Often described as the most painful ear placement. Starter jewelry: 16 gauge curved barbell. Anatomy-dependent; many piercers decline it for ears without a defined ridge.

Conch

Where: the large bowl of cartilage in the centre of the ear. An inner conch sits in the bowl; an outer conch sits toward the rim. Healing: six to twelve months. Pain: moderate. Starter jewelry: 16 or 14 gauge flat-back stud. A conch ring, which wraps around the rim, is swapped in after healing and needs a diameter measured to the ear.

Orbital

Where: two piercings, usually in the lobe or conch, joined by a single ring. Healing: follows the placement, lobe fast, cartilage slow. Starter jewelry: usually pierced with two studs and joined with a ring after healing.

Dermal and surface placements

A dermal is a single-point anchor under the skin, sometimes placed near the ear. It is a different procedure with different risks and its own page: see the dermal piercing guide.

Pain, in one chart

From least to most, as most piercers rank them: lobe, upper lobe, helix, tragus, conch, forward helix, flat, daith, anti-tragus, industrial, rook, snug. Individual experience varies more than the chart does.

Aftercare that actually matters

Sterile saline spray, twice a day, no twisting, no touching, no sleeping on it. No alcohol, peroxide or ointment. Leave the jewelry in for the full healing period. Downsize cartilage posts at the piercer's recommended time, usually four to eight weeks in, because a long post that was right for swelling will catch and tilt once the swelling is gone.

A bump on a cartilage piercing is usually irritation from movement, pressure or a post that is too long, and it resolves when the cause is removed. Discharge, heat and spreading redness are different, and that is the point to see a doctor rather than a piercer.

Choosing the first jewelry

Implant-grade titanium or 14-karat gold, with a flat-back or a smooth seamless ring. No plated metals, no nickel, no mystery alloys from a market stall. Our types of earrings page explains the difference between the jewelry you heal in and the jewelry you wear once healed, which is where the fun begins.

Planning a curated ear

If the plan is several piercings, map them first. Space the placements so the jewelry has room, mix one or two statement pieces with plain studs, and pierce in stages: a lobe and a helix at once is fine, a rook and a snug at once is a long, sore year. Most piercers will sketch a plan on a photo of your ear, and it is the best five minutes you can spend before the needle.
