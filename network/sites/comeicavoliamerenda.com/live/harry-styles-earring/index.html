The Harry Styles Earring: Why One Pearl Made Headlines

The Harry Styles earring moment — a single pearl drop at the 2019 Met Gala — helped make pearls genderless. Here's the look and how to wear one yourself.

Celebrity4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • The best-known Harry Styles earring is the single pearl drop he wore at the 2019 Met Gala, styled with a sheer Gucci look.
  • It helped push pearls as genderless jewelry into the mainstream.
  • To copy it, wear one pearl drop (about 8–12 mm pearl) and keep the other ear simple.
  • He's also known for pearl necklaces and playful jewelry, so mixing pearls with casual clothes fits his style.

The Harry Styles earring that people still talk about is a single pearl. At the 2019 Met Gala, where he co-chaired the "Camp: Notes on Fashion" event, Styles wore a sheer black Gucci outfit with one pearl drop earring. It was a small detail on a very big night, but it became one of the most-discussed jewelry moments of the year.

That one pearl helped shift how people think about earrings and gender. Here's why it worked, and how to wear a similar look in real life.

The 2019 Met Gala pearl earring

The earring itself was simple: a single pearl hanging from the lobe, worn on one side only. Against the high-waisted trousers, ruffled sheer blouse and heeled boots of the outfit, the pearl added a classic, almost old-fashioned touch — which is exactly what made it feel so fresh.

Fashion writers at the time connected it to the long history of men wearing pearl earrings in portraiture, from Elizabethan-era courtiers onward. Whether or not that was the intent, the comparison stuck.

Why the Harry Styles earring mattered

Pearls had long been coded as "polite" women's jewelry. Seeing one on a male pop star at fashion's biggest event helped normalize pearls for everyone. In the following years, pearl necklaces and earrings showed up on many more men on red carpets and in street style.

Styles has kept pearls in his own rotation too, often wearing pearl necklaces with tailoring or casual knits. The common thread is a playful, unfussy attitude: pearls aren't saved for formal occasions.

Style note: a single earring always looks intentional. If you're nervous about pearls, wearing just one is a softer way in than a matching pair.

Real, cultured or faux?

You don't need expensive pearls to get the look. Here's how the main options compare:

  • Freshwater cultured pearls: real pearls at an approachable price; often slightly irregular, which can look more modern.
  • Akoya or saltwater pearls: rounder and glossier, closer to a classic look, and more expensive.
  • Shell pearls or glass pearls: uniform and inexpensive; great for trying the look first.
  • Baroque pearls: irregular shapes that feel artsy and less formal.

Whatever you choose, check the metal on the post or hook. Sterling silver, 14k gold and titanium are kinder to sensitive ears than plated base metals.

Pearl studs, drops or hoops?

If a drop feels like too much, a pearl stud is the easiest entry point: it's quiet, comfortable and works with almost anything. A pearl on a small hoop sits in the middle, adding a little movement without the length of a full drop. The drop is the most dramatic and the closest to the Met Gala moment.

Think about your hairstyle, too. Short hair or hair tucked behind the ear shows off a drop beautifully, while long, loose hair can hide a small stud entirely. If you wear your hair down most days, a slightly larger pearl or a drop will actually get seen.

Building a pearl ear beyond one earring

Once you're comfortable with a single pearl, it's easy to build on it. A pearl drop in the first lobe with a tiny gold stud above it looks polished and modern. You can also mix a pearl on one ear with a small hoop on the other — the kind of thoughtful mismatch we love.

If you have more than one lobe piercing, try graduating pearl sizes, largest at the bottom. It's a softer take on the curated-ear trend, and it works just as well with a suit as with a hoodie.

Wearing pearls every day

The fastest way to make a pearl earring feel like yours is to pair it with something unexpected: a T-shirt, a denim jacket, a chunky knit. Keep the rest of your jewelry light, or add a thin chain for a little extra.

Pearls are soft, so put them on after perfume and hair products, and wipe them gently with a soft cloth after wearing. For more on wearing earrings with confidence, see our guide to guys with earrings.

Frequently asked questions

What earring did Harry Styles wear to the Met Gala?

At the 2019 Met Gala he wore a single pearl drop earring with a sheer black Gucci outfit.

Does Harry Styles have pierced ears?

He has worn pierced earrings in public, including the 2019 Met Gala pearl, but he's better known day-to-day for necklaces and rings than for earrings.

Can men wear pearl earrings?

Yes. Pearl earrings have a long history on men, and the Harry Styles earring moment helped bring them back into mainstream menswear.

What size pearl should I choose?

For a look close to his Met Gala earring, an 8–12 mm pearl drop works well. Go smaller for a subtle stud.
