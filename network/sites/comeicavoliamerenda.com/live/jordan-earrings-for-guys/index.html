Jordan Earrings for Guys: Jumpman Studs, Sizing and Materials

Jordan earrings for guys: Jumpman studs are mostly third-party, not official. Learn the best stud sizes for men, metals to choose and how to avoid knockoffs.

Styles4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • Most Jordan earrings for guys (Jumpman-logo studs) are third-party, not official Jordan Brand products.
  • Common men's stud sizes run about 5–8 mm; 6–7 mm is a versatile middle.
  • Choose solid gold, sterling silver, titanium or surgical steel posts over vague alloys.
  • Prong-set stones and screw-backs last longer than glued stones and loose push-backs.

Jordan earrings for guys usually means Jumpman-logo studs: the silhouette of Michael Jordan mid-dunk, often iced out with cubic zirconia. They're popular with sneakerheads who want their jewelry to match their kicks. Most of these earrings, though, are made by third-party sellers rather than Jordan Brand. We're independent and unaffiliated, so here's an honest guide to what you're buying, which sizes work and which metals to pick.

Are Jumpman earrings official?

Jordan Brand, part of Nike, is known for sneakers and apparel. Jumpman earrings you see online are mostly made by independent jewelers and mass-market sellers using the logo or a logo-like shape. Treat claims of "official" or "authentic Jordan" earrings with caution unless there's clear licensing information.

Using a trademarked logo without permission can be a legal issue for sellers, which is why these listings vary in quality and sometimes disappear. For you as a buyer, the practical upshot is simple: judge the earrings on materials and craftsmanship, not the logo.

Stud sizes for Jordan earrings for guys

Size is the biggest style decision. A stud that's too small disappears; too big can look costume-y.

Logo studs are measured across the widest point, so a 10 mm Jumpman may look smaller than a 10 mm round stud. Our 8mm actual-size guide helps you picture sizes on screen.

Face shape matters a little too. Broader faces and larger frames carry bigger studs easily, while slimmer faces often look best with 5–7 mm. If you wear glasses or a cap a lot, slightly larger studs keep the earrings from getting lost. When in doubt, buy a mid size first and size up later.

Materials that hold up

  • Solid gold (10k, 14k): durable and good for sensitive ears, especially 14k.
  • Sterling silver (925): bright and affordable; can tarnish but polishes up.
  • Titanium or surgical steel: tough and usually hypoallergenic.
  • Gold-plated brass or "alloy": cheapest; plating wears and may contain nickel.

For stones, cubic zirconia is the usual choice on logo studs. Look for prong or channel settings rather than glued stones, which fall out quickly. Moissanite and lab-grown diamonds appear on higher-end custom pieces.

The post matters more than the face. A great-looking stud on a mystery-metal post can still leave your lobes itchy. If in doubt, ask the seller what the post and back are made of.

Caring for iced-out studs

Stone-set studs collect lotion, sweat and hair product fast, which dulls the sparkle. Soak them for a few minutes in warm water with a drop of mild dish soap, brush gently with a soft toothbrush behind the stones, rinse and dry thoroughly.

Skip harsh chemicals and ultrasonic cleaners on plated or glued pieces, since they can strip finishes and loosen stones. Store studs in a small box or pouch so stones don't scratch other jewelry, and check prongs now and then for anything bent or loose.

For gold studs, a quick wipe with a soft cloth after wearing keeps them bright between deeper cleans.

How to avoid cheap knockoffs

  1. Read the material line: "925 sterling" or "14k solid gold" beats "gold color."
  2. Check the backs: screw-backs are best for studs you don't want to lose; see our earring backs guide.
  3. Zoom in on stones: uneven, cloudy or missing stones in listing photos are a red flag.
  4. Read reviews with photos: real-world shots show plating and sparkle honestly.
  5. Prefer clear returns: sizing is hard to judge online.

Styling Jumpman and sneaker-themed studs

Match metal to the rest of your jewelry: gold studs with a gold chain, silver or white with a silver watch. One logo is plenty, so if your hoodie or cap is branded, consider a plain round stud instead.

Wear one stud for a relaxed look or a pair for symmetry. For more ideas, see our guide to guys with earrings.

Alternatives to logo studs

If you want sneakerhead energy without logo questions, try a classic round CZ or diamond stud, a black stud that matches a black-and-red colorway, or a small basketball charm on a huggie. Halo studs, a round center stone surrounded by smaller stones, give the iced-out effect in a timeless shape. Keep fresh piercings in titanium or solid gold starter jewelry from a professional piercer until healed, and see a doctor if you notice signs of infection.

Frequently asked questions

Does Jordan Brand make official earrings?

Jumpman earrings sold online are mostly third-party products. Be cautious about claims of official status without clear licensing information.

What size stud earrings should a guy wear?

Around 5–8 mm suits most men; 6–7 mm is a versatile middle. Go smaller for work and larger for a bold, iced-out look.

What metal is best for Jordan earrings?

Solid 14k gold, sterling silver, titanium or surgical steel. Avoid vague alloys if your ears are sensitive.

Do screw-back earrings stay in better?

Yes. Screw-backs thread onto the post and are much less likely to fall off than standard push-backs, which makes them good for studs.
