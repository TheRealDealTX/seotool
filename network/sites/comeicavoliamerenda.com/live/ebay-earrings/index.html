Buying Earrings on eBay and Resale Sites: A Safe-Shopping Guide

Buying earrings on eBay or other resale sites? Learn to read hallmarks like 925, 14k and 585, vet sellers and photos, avoid fakes and dodge nickel.

Styles4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • Check hallmarks: 925 = sterling silver, 14k or 585 = 14 karat gold, 18k or 750 = 18 karat gold.
  • Read seller feedback, return policy and full item description before buying earrings on eBay.
  • Ask for close-up photos of stamps, posts and backs in natural light.
  • Vague terms like "alloy" or "gold tone" usually mean plated base metal, which may contain nickel.

Buying earrings on eBay and other resale marketplaces can be a great way to find vintage pieces, discontinued designs and solid gold for less. It can also mean fakes, mislabeled metals and itchy ears. We're an independent guide with no affiliation with any marketplace. Here's how to read listings, decode hallmarks and protect yourself.

Why buy earrings on eBay or resale sites?

Resale marketplaces are full of jewelry that isn't sold new anymore: vintage clip-ons, estate gold, old designer collections and single earrings to replace a lost one. Prices can be good because sellers range from professional dealers to people clearing out a drawer.

That variety is also the risk. Sellers may not know what they have, or may know and not say. Your job is to read listings like a jeweler would.

Decoding hallmarks and metal stamps

Hallmarks are the small stamps that indicate metal content. They're your first clue, though stamps can be faked, so treat them as evidence rather than proof.

StampMeansNotes
925 or STERSterling silver, 92.5% silverCan tarnish; usually fine for most ears
10k or 41710 karat gold, about 41.7% goldDurable, more alloy
14k or 58514 karat gold, about 58.5% goldA popular choice for everyday earrings
18k or 75018 karat gold, 75% goldRicher color, softer
PT or PT950PlatinumHeavy, hypoallergenic for most people
GF or 1/20 GFGold-filledThick gold layer over base metal
GP, GE, HGE, RGPGold-plated or electroplatedThin layer; wears off

Earring posts are thin, so a stamp may be on the back, the clip or the backing instead. Some genuine vintage pieces aren't stamped at all; that's where seller reputation and testing come in.

Checking the seller and listing

  1. Feedback: look at recent feedback for jewelry specifically, not just overall percentage.
  2. Returns: prefer listings with clear return policies, and know the platform's buyer protection terms.
  3. Description: exact metal, weight in grams, measurements and condition notes are good signs.
  4. Language: "gold tone," "silver color," "alloy" or "looks like" usually means plated base metal.
  5. Stock photos: be careful with listings that only use catalog images.
  6. Questions: message the seller. A quick, specific answer is reassuring.

Ask for three photos before buying anything pricey: the hallmark up close, the post and back, and the earring next to a ruler or coin. A US dime is 17.91 mm across, which makes a handy size check.

Weight is another useful detail. Sellers of genuine solid gold often list the weight in grams, because it reflects the metal's value. If a pair of "solid gold" hoops has no weight listed and the seller won't provide one, be cautious.

Spotting fakes and misdescribed jewelry

Designer earrings are heavily counterfeited, and "solid gold" is sometimes plated. Warning signs include brand-new designer pieces at a fraction of the usual price, many identical "authentic" items from one seller, blurry logos, uneven plating and stamps that look shallow or crooked.

For designer pieces, see our guides to Chanel earrings and Coach earrings for brand-specific style notes. For anything valuable, consider a professional authentication service or a local jeweler who can test the metal.

Nickel, sensitivity and hygiene

Nickel is one of the most common causes of earring reactions, and unlabeled base metals often contain it. If your ears are sensitive, stick to stamped sterling, solid 14k gold or higher, platinum, or titanium posts, or plan to swap hooks for hypoallergenic ones.

  • Clean pre-owned earrings with mild soap and warm water, then dry well.
  • Wipe posts with isopropyl alcohol before wearing.
  • Replace old backs with new ones; they're cheap and more hygienic.
  • Skip pre-owned jewelry for fresh piercings; a professional piercer should handle starter jewelry.

If pre-owned earrings smell or itch, our guide to smelly earrings explains why. See a doctor if your ear shows signs of infection.

Sizing earrings from a listing

Size is one of the hardest things to judge from photos. Look for listings that give measurements in millimeters, and ask if they don't. Remember that 1 mm is about 0.039 inches, so a 10 mm stud is roughly 0.39 inches across.

For studs, compare the measurement with our 5mm actual-size guide or the earring size tool. For hoops, check whether the seller is giving inner or outer diameter, and the wire thickness. For dangles, ask for the total drop length from the top of the hook.

Smart things to buy secondhand

Some earrings are especially good resale finds: vintage clip-ons, plain solid gold hoops and studs, sterling statement pieces and single replacement earrings. Mixed lots can be fun for building a mismatched ear, just check every post before it goes near your skin.

Frequently asked questions

What does 585 mean on earrings?

585 means the metal is about 58.5% gold, the same as 14 karat. Similarly, 750 equals 18 karat and 417 equals 10 karat.

Is it safe to buy earrings on eBay?

It can be, if you check seller feedback, read descriptions carefully, ask for hallmark photos and use listings with returns and buyer protection.

How can I tell if secondhand earrings are real gold?

Look for karat stamps like 14k or 585, but remember stamps can be faked. A jeweler can test the metal for certainty.

Should I clean secondhand earrings before wearing them?

Yes. Wash them with mild soap and water, dry well, wipe posts with isopropyl alcohol and replace the backs.
