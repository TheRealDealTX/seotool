Rook Earrings: Choosing Curved Barbells, Clickers and Sizes

Find the right rook earrings: curved barbell sizes (16g, 5/16"), when to switch to clickers, whether your anatomy fits, healing time and how to spot quality.

Piercings4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • The rook is the fold of cartilage above your inner ear, just above the tragus ridge.
  • Starter rook earrings are usually 16g curved barbells, often 5/16" (about 8mm) long.
  • Switch to a clicker ring only once healed, which typically takes 6–12 months.
  • Buy implant-grade titanium or 14k+ solid gold from a reputable body jewelry source.

Rook earrings sit in one of the most sculptural spots on the ear: the inner ridge of cartilage that folds over just above your ear canal. Because the piercing goes through a curved fold rather than a flat surface, rook jewelry has its own shapes and sizes. Here is how to choose rook earrings that fit, heal well and look great.

What is a rook piercing?

The rook passes vertically through the antihelix fold, the ridge inside your upper ear that sits above the tragus and the small bump called the daith. The jewelry enters at the top of the fold and exits underneath, so only the ends show from the side.

Does your anatomy fit?

Not every ear has a pronounced enough ridge for a rook. A professional piercer will pinch the fold to see if there is enough tissue to hold jewelry securely without pressure. If the ridge is shallow, they may suggest a different placement, such as a forward helix or conch. That is not a failure; it is good piercing practice.

Starter rook earrings: curved barbells

The go-to first piece for a rook is a curved barbell, a slightly bent bar with a ball or gem on each end. The curve follows the shape of the fold, so it puts less pressure on the tissue than a straight bar would.

DetailCommon size
Gauge16g (1.2mm), occasionally 14g (1.6mm)
Bar length5/16" (about 8mm) is common; 1/4" (about 6mm) or 3/8" (about 9.5mm) for some ears
End size3mm balls or gems, often a slightly larger gem on the bottom

Length matters more in the rook than almost anywhere else. A bar that is too short pinches the fold and can cause the jewelry to sink into the tissue, while a bar that is too long tilts forward and catches on hair, hats and earbuds. Because every ridge is shaped differently, a piercer measuring your ear beats guessing from a chart.

Your piercer may start you on a slightly longer bar for swelling, then downsize. If gauges are new to you, our ear gauge size chart converts every size to millimeters.

The bottom end of a rook barbell is the one people see most. Many piercers put the decorative gem on the bottom and a simple ball on top.

Clickers and rings once you are healed

Once your rook is fully healed, many people switch to a clicker, a hinged ring that snaps shut. Rook clickers are often around 8mm in diameter, but the right size depends on your fold, so have a piercer measure you.

Clickers swing and rotate more than barbells, which is why they belong in healed piercings only. Other healed options include:

  • Seamless rings: a clean, minimal look that hugs the ridge.
  • Curved barbells with fancy ends: opals, marquise stones or tiny charms.
  • Clickers with dangles: a small charm hanging from the bottom for movement.

Healing a rook piercing

The rook is known as a slower healer. Expect 6–12 months on average, with some people taking longer. It is also known to be among the more tender piercings for the first few weeks, partly because the cartilage is thick.

  • Rinse with sterile saline once or twice daily.
  • Do not rotate the jewelry or remove it yourself.
  • Avoid earbuds that press against the inner ear while it heals.
  • Keep hair spray, shampoo and phone screens away from the area.

Irritation bumps can happen and often settle with gentler care and correctly sized jewelry. Spreading redness, heat, thick discolored discharge or fever are signs of infection, so see a doctor.

Where to buy quality rook earrings

We are an independent guide and do not sell jewelry, but here is what to look for wherever you shop.

  • Material listed specifically: "implant-grade titanium (ASTM F-136)" or "14k/18k solid gold," not just "gold tone" or "surgical steel."
  • Internally threaded or threadless ends: smoother to insert and less likely to scratch the channel.
  • Clear gauge and length: a reputable seller lists both precisely.
  • Your piercer's studio: many professional studios stock high-quality brands and can fit you on the spot.

Cheap mixed-metal barbells from marketplace listings may be fine for a long-healed rook in a pinch, but they are a common culprit for irritation. Our guide to buying earrings on eBay covers how to vet sellers if you do shop online.

Styling rook earrings

Because the rook sits in the middle of the ear, it pairs beautifully with a daith, a conch stud or a tragus gem. For a warm, cohesive ear, keep everything gold; for something more playful, mix a gold rook clicker with an opal tragus and silver lobes. Frequently asked questions

What size are rook earrings?

Most rook piercings use 16g (1.2mm) curved barbells, often 5/16" (about 8mm) long, though your exact length depends on your anatomy.

Can I put a hoop in my rook right away?

No. Wait until it is fully healed, typically 6–12 months, before switching to a clicker or ring, and let your piercer size it.

Why is my rook taking so long to heal?

The rook goes through thick, folded cartilage, so it is naturally slow. Pressure from earbuds, sleeping on it or poorly fitting jewelry can slow it further.

What metal should rook earrings be?

Implant-grade titanium or 14k+ solid gold are the safest choices, especially while healing.
