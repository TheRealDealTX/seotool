Nike Earrings: The Truth About Swoosh Studs

Nike earrings explained: most swoosh studs are fan-made or third-party, not official. Learn what to check, which metals to choose and sporty alternatives.

Styles4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • Most Nike earrings and swoosh studs are fan-made or third-party, not official Nike products.
  • Logo jewelry can raise trademark issues, so listings vary widely in quality and legitimacy.
  • Pick posts in titanium, surgical steel, sterling silver or solid gold for comfort.
  • Sporty alternatives: small huggies, plain studs or flat-back earrings for workouts.

Nike earrings are a popular search, especially swoosh-shaped studs that match sneakers and athleisure. The catch is that most of what you'll find isn't made by Nike. Swoosh earrings are largely fan-made or third-party products, so it pays to know what you're buying. We're an independent guide with no connection to Nike, and this page sticks to what's useful: who makes these pieces, what to check and what to wear instead.

Does Nike make earrings?

Nike is a sportswear and footwear company, and earrings aren't a regular part of its lineup. Most "Nike earrings" sold online are made by independent jewelers or mass-market sellers who use the swoosh or a swoosh-like shape.

That matters for two reasons. First, quality varies a lot. Second, using a famous logo without permission can be a trademark issue, so listings may appear and vanish, and some sellers rename products with phrases like "swoosh-style" or "check" earrings.

Types of swoosh earrings you'll see

  • Flat swoosh studs: the most common style, in gold, silver or black tone.
  • Iced-out swoosh studs: covered in cubic zirconia or crystals, often marketed for men.
  • Enamel colorway studs: colors chosen to match a favorite sneaker.
  • Swoosh dangles and huggies: a small swoosh charm hanging from a hoop.
  • Mismatched sets: a swoosh in one ear and a sneaker or basketball charm in the other.

What to check before buying Nike earrings

Since you're usually buying third-party jewelry, judge the earrings on their own merits rather than the logo.

"Alloy" with no further detail often means mixed base metal that may contain nickel, a common cause of itchy ears. If you're unsure about backs, our earring backs guide explains the options.

Avoid any listing that claims a swoosh stud is "official Nike" without proof. Even if the price looks fair, it's a sign the seller is loose with the truth in other ways too.

A quick tip on reviews: photos from real buyers tell you far more than polished product shots. Look for close-ups after a few weeks of wear, since that's when cheap plating starts to show its true color. If several reviewers mention green ears, itching or stones falling out, believe them and move on to another listing.

Matching earrings to your sneakers

The fun of swoosh earrings is coordinating with your shoes. You can do that without any logo at all by matching color and finish instead.

  • White sneakers: silver, white enamel or clear CZ studs.
  • Black sneakers: matte black or gunmetal studs.
  • Bold colorways: one small enamel stud in the accent color, with a plain stud in the other ear.
  • Gold details: small gold huggies keep things tied together.

Keeping it to one accent color stops the look from feeling too matchy, and a mismatched pair adds a little personality.

Size and fit

Swoosh studs usually range from tiny 6–8 mm pieces to bold 15 mm-plus statements. Small sizes look clean in a second hole or cartilage piercing. Larger flat shapes can flop forward on thin posts, so look for a sturdier backing.

Wearing earrings while working out

If the appeal of Nike earrings is wearing them to the gym, think about safety first. Dangles and hoops can snag on clothing, headphones or equipment. Flat studs are the lowest-risk choice for most workouts.

  1. Choose small studs or snug huggies.
  2. Use hypoallergenic metals, since sweat speeds up irritation from base metals.
  3. Clean posts and backs regularly to avoid buildup and odor.
  4. Remove earrings for contact sports or when your coach or league requires it.
  5. Keep fresh piercings in their starter jewelry until fully healed.

Sweat and friction are hard on plated metal, so earrings you wear to train will age faster than ones you wear to brunch. Rinse them with mild soap and water after sweaty sessions and let them dry fully before storing.

Sporty alternatives to swoosh earrings

If you want the clean, athletic mood without logo questions, there are easy swaps. Small plain gold or steel studs, matte black studs and minimal huggies all pair well with sneakers. Tiny geometric shapes like a bolt, star or bar give you a graphic look that isn't anyone's trademark.

Frequently asked questions

Does Nike sell official earrings?

Earrings aren't a regular Nike product. Most swoosh earrings you'll find are fan-made or third-party, so treat claims of official status with caution.

Are swoosh earrings legal to sell?

Using a trademarked logo without permission can raise legal issues, which is why such listings vary and sometimes disappear. That's a question for the seller, but it's worth knowing as a buyer.

What metal is best for swoosh stud earrings?

Titanium, surgical steel, sterling silver or solid gold posts are the most comfortable. Vague alloys may contain nickel.

Can I wear earrings to the gym?

Small flat studs are usually fine for general workouts. Remove them for contact sports and keep them clean, since sweat can increase irritation.
