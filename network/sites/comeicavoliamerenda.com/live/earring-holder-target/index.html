How to Choose an Earring Holder (and 4 DIY Ideas)

How to choose an earring holder: tiered stands, mesh frames, post trays, wall organizers and travel cases, what to check at big-box stores, plus 4 DIY ideas.

Guides4 min readUpdated October 17, 2026

Quick answer

  • Match the earring holder to your collection: hooks need hanging space, studs need holes or posts.
  • Popular types: tiered stands, mesh frames, post trays, wall organizers and travel cases.
  • At big-box stores, check hole size, stability and finish before you buy.
  • DIY options like a picture frame with mesh cost very little.

A good earring holder does two jobs: it keeps pairs together, and it lets you see what you own. That second part matters more than people expect, because earrings you can't see tend not to get worn. This guide covers the main types of holders and stands, what to check when you're shopping at big-box stores like Target, and four easy DIY versions.

We're an independent guide, so there are no prices or product picks here, just what makes a holder actually work.

Start with what you own

Before choosing a holder, sort your earrings into rough groups. The mix tells you which design will suit you.

  • Studs: need small holes, foam slots or a backed surface to hold the backs.
  • Hooks and dangles: need hanging space and enough drop so they don't tangle.
  • Hoops: hang well on bars or pegs, or lie flat in shallow compartments.
  • Precious pieces: deserve a lined, lidded spot away from dust and light.

Tiered stands

Stands with rows of holes or bars look great on a dresser and let you see everything at once. They work best when you hang earrings evenly so the stand stays balanced.

Mesh frames

A frame with a metal or fabric mesh panel holds studs and hooks in neat rows. Some lean, some stand on a foot, and some hang on the wall.

Trays with posts

Velvet or foam trays with slots are perfect for studs and fit neatly in drawers. Compartment trays like the one in a jewelry box hold hoops and loose pairs.

Wall organizers and travel cases

Wall organizers turn a collection into décor and save counter space. Travel cases are small, zippered and usually have stud rows plus a compartment or two.

What to look for at big-box stores like Target

Large retailers usually stock a rotating mix of stands, trays and cases. Since selection changes, focus on the features rather than a specific product.

  1. Hole size: standard lobe earrings are usually 20g or 18g. If you wear thicker body jewelry, check that holes fit; our guide to what gauge regular earrings are helps.
  2. Stability: press on one corner. A stand that wobbles empty will tip when full.
  3. Finish: smooth edges protect delicate wires and plating from scratches.
  4. Size and capacity: count your pairs and leave room for about a third more.
  5. Cleaning: wipeable surfaces beat fabrics that trap dust.

Store earrings clean and dry. An open, ventilated holder is better than a sealed bag for everyday pairs, and it helps prevent the buildup that makes earrings smell.

4 DIY earring holder ideas

1. Picture frame with mesh

Remove the glass from an old frame and staple or glue a sheet of craft mesh, window screen or lace across the back. Prop it on a dresser or hang it on the wall. Hooks go through the mesh; studs push straight in.

2. Cork board panel

A cork board or cork trivet makes a soft surface for studs. Add a few small hooks along the bottom edge for hoops and dangles.

3. Ice cube or egg tray

A clean ice cube tray or ceramic egg holder keeps pairs separated in a drawer. It's ideal for small studs and huggies.

4. Tension rod or branch

Mount a short curtain rod, a wooden dowel or a smooth branch on two hooks. Hang hooks and hoops along it for a pretty, visible display.

Choosing an earring holder for small spaces

In a small bedroom or dorm, go vertical. A slim wall organizer or an over-the-door hanger keeps the dresser clear, while a lidded tray stacks neatly in a drawer. Whatever you choose, aim to see every pair at a glance.

Organizing tips

Group earrings by type, then by color or metal, so you can build a look at a glance. Keep your everyday favorites front and center, and rotate the rest. A small dish by the sink catches pairs you take off at night.

And don't forget the backs. Spare butterflies and screw backs disappear easily, so give them a little container of their own. If you're not sure which you need, our guide to types of earring backs explains every style.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best type of earring holder?

It depends on your collection. Tiered stands and mesh frames suit hooks and dangles; trays with slots suit studs.

How do I store stud earrings so I don't lose them?

Use a tray with foam slots, a mesh frame where backs can be reattached, or a travel case with stud rows.

Can I make my own earring holder?

Yes. A picture frame with mesh, a cork board, an ice cube tray or a dowel on hooks all work well.

Should earrings be stored in a closed box?

Precious pieces benefit from a lined, lidded box. Everyday earrings are fine on an open holder if they're clean and dry.
