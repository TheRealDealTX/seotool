How to measure a TV screen is a question we get for three different reasons, and the answer is different for each. Someone buying a TV wants to know if a 65-inch set will fit the cabinet. Someone fitting a wall bracket wants the mounting pattern. Someone with a cracked panel wants the number that tells them whether a replacement exists and what it costs. The one thing all three have in common is the trap at the start: the size printed on the box is the diagonal of the visible picture, measured corner to corner, and it is not the width of the TV. A 65-inch TV is about 57 inches wide. This guide gives the method, the real dimensions of every common size, and the measurements each of the three situations actually needs.

## The advertised size is a diagonal

TV sizes have been quoted as a diagonal since the days of round cathode ray tubes, when the diagonal and the diameter were the same thing. The habit stuck. Today the number is the length of a straight line from the bottom-left corner of the visible picture to the top-right corner, in inches, usually rounded. A "55-inch" panel often measures 54.6 inches on the diagonal; a "65" measures 64.5. Manufacturers call this the "screen class."

Because nearly every modern TV has the same shape, a 16:9 widescreen rectangle, the diagonal fixes the width and the height. The width is about 87 percent of the diagonal and the height about 49 percent. That is the arithmetic behind the table below.

| 85 inches | 74.1 in / 188.2 cm | 41.7 in / 105.9 cm | 239 percent |

Two things stand out. Going from 55 to 65 inches adds 40 percent more picture, not 18 percent, because area grows with the square of the diagonal. And a 65-inch TV is 57 inches wide before its frame, so a cabinet described as "fits up to 65 inches" needs to be 60 inches wide or more once the bezel and the feet are counted.

## How to measure a TV you already own

For the size class: measure diagonally from the bottom-left corner of the picture (not the frame) to the top-right corner, in inches. Round to the nearest standard size. If you get 54.6, you have a 55.

For the overall dimensions: measure the full width and height of the outer frame, and the depth including the thickest point at the back, where the boards and the connections sit. Modern TVs are thin at the edge and 5 to 9 centimetres thick in the middle.

For the stand footprint: measure between the outer edges of the feet, and from the back of the feet to the front. TVs with feet at the far corners need a surface nearly as wide as the TV; TVs with a central pedestal need far less. This is the measurement most people forget until the TV is on the cabinet with one foot hanging off.

## Measuring for a wall bracket

A wall bracket does not care about the diagonal. It cares about the VESA pattern: the distance between the four threaded holes on the back of the TV, in millimetres, horizontal by vertical. Measure centre to centre. Common patterns are 200 by 200, 300 by 300, 400 by 400 and 600 by 400. Every bracket lists the patterns it accepts and the maximum weight; match both. The TV's weight is on the label on the back and in the manual.

Then measure the room: viewing distance from the seat to the wall, and the height of seated eye level. The centre of the screen should sit at or slightly below eye level, which for most sofas is about 100 to 110 centimetres from the floor. Mounting a TV above a fireplace puts the centre at 150 centimetres or more and is the most common cause of the neck ache customers blame on the TV. The Consumer Technology Association publishes [guidance on viewing distance and screen size](https://www.cta.tech/) that we use on site: for 4K, a viewing distance of 1 to 1.5 times the diagonal is comfortable, so a 65-inch set suits a seat 1.7 to 2.5 metres away.

## Measuring for a cabinet or an alcove

Add to the TV's outer width: at least 5 centimetres on each side for air and for fingers, and more if the TV will be angled. Add to the height: the feet (usually 6 to 10 centimetres) plus 5 centimetres of clearance above for heat. TVs that sit tight in a closed alcove run hot, and heat is what kills backlight LEDs and power boards, the two repairs we do most. If the alcove is closed at the back, leave the full depth of the TV plus 10 centimetres for cables and air.

## The measurement a technician needs

When a screen is damaged and you call for a quote, the diagonal is the least useful number you can give us. Three things decide whether a panel can be replaced and what it costs:

1. The model number from the label on the back of the TV. Not the size, the full code: it tells us the series, year and region.
2. The panel code, if the TV is already open or if the label lists it. It is printed on the panel itself inside the TV and begins with letters that identify the manufacturer and the exact glass. Two 55-inch TVs from the same brand can use different panels that are not interchangeable.
3. Whether the glass is cracked. A crack means panel replacement; everything else may be a board or backlight repair, as our guide to [how to fix a TV screen](/how-to-fix-tv-screen/) explains.

## Quick answers

- A 55-inch TV is about 48 inches (122 cm) wide and 27 inches (69 cm) high before the frame.
- A 65-inch TV is about 57 inches (144 cm) wide and 32 inches (81 cm) high.
- The diagonal is measured corner to corner of the picture, not the frame.
- Measure the feet and the VESA pattern before you buy a cabinet or a bracket.
- For a repair quote, send the model number, not the size.
