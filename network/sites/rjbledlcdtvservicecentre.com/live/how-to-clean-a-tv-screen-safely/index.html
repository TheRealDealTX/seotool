How to Clean a TV Screen Safely (OLED, QLED and LCD)

RJB TV Service · October 26, 2026

Knowing how to clean a TV screen safely is worth more than it sounds. In our workshop we see panels every month with permanent streaks, cloudy patches and scratched anti-glare coatings, and the cause is almost always a cleaning product, not a fault in the set. The panel coating on a modern TV is thin and chemically sensitive, and once it is damaged there is no repair except a new panel, which on most sets costs more than the TV. So this guide is short and strict, and it applies to every brand we service, from Samsung and Sony to LG and Videocon.

What is on the surface of the screen

Every LED, LCD, QLED and OLED television has a front layer that is not plain glass. It is a polariser with an anti-glare or anti-reflective coating applied on top. That coating is what makes the picture visible in a bright room. It is also what glass cleaners, alcohol, ammonia and rough cloths destroy.

OLED panels have the thinnest front layer and are the most sensitive to pressure. QLED and standard LCD sets have a slightly tougher front but the same coating problem. Older plasma and CRT sets had glass fronts and could take more abuse. Nothing sold in the last ten years should be treated that way.

The only things you need

  • One clean microfibre cloth, the kind sold for spectacles or camera lenses
  • A second dry microfibre cloth
  • Distilled or filtered water, a few drops, only if dry cleaning does not work
  • For stubborn marks, a screen cleaning solution made for LCD and OLED panels, applied to the cloth, never to the screen

That is the complete list. Everything else in the cupboard is a risk.

Step by step

  1. Switch the TV off and unplug it. A dark screen shows dust and fingerprints clearly, and an unplugged set cannot be damaged by a wet cloth near the connectors.
  2. Let it cool for a few minutes. Warm panels streak.
  3. Wipe the screen dry with the first microfibre cloth. Use light pressure, long straight strokes, top to bottom. Most dust and light marks come off at this stage.
  4. For fingerprints and oily marks, dampen a corner of the cloth with distilled water. Dampen, not wet. Wring it until no water drips. Wipe the mark in one direction, then dry immediately with the second cloth.
  5. For marks that remain, use a few sprays of panel-safe screen cleaner on the cloth, not on the screen. Wipe, then dry.
  6. Clean the bezel and the stand with the same cloths last. Dust on the bezel falls onto the screen if you do it first.
  7. Leave the set to dry fully before plugging it in.

Never use these

  • Glass cleaner or any product with ammonia
  • Alcohol, including spirit-based wipes and hand sanitiser
  • Kitchen cleaners, soap, detergent or washing-up liquid
  • Paper towels, tissues, newspaper or any paper product; they are abrasive
  • Tap water in hard-water areas; the minerals leave rings
  • Sprays applied directly to the screen; liquid runs into the lower bezel and onto the T-con board and backlight connectors
  • Pressure of any kind on an OLED. Pressing on the panel can leave permanent marks or bright spots

We repair sets every week where liquid has run down the panel into the edge connectors. On an LED TV that shows as a vertical band or a dark strip along the bottom. On an OLED it can be the end of the panel.

Brand and panel notes

Samsung QLED. The front coating is tough but the sets are large and the stand is often narrow. Steady the set with one hand on the back while cleaning. Samsung's own guidance is dry microfibre only; our page on Samsung LED and LCD service covers the faults that follow from liquid damage on these sets.

Sony Bravia. Sony fits an anti-reflective layer that marks easily with alcohol. Water on the cloth and nothing else. See the Sony Bravia service page for the symptoms of coating damage, which Sony owners often mistake for a panel fault.

LG OLED. The most sensitive panel we handle. No pressure, no liquid near the edges, and never clean a warm screen. LG also recommends against any cleaning solution at all.

Videocon and older LED sets. Thicker front layers and more tolerant of a damp cloth, but the same rules about ammonia and alcohol apply.

How often

Dust weekly with a dry cloth if the set is in a busy room. Damp-clean only when there are visible marks, which for most homes is every month or two. More frequent wet cleaning wears the coating faster.

What a damaged coating looks like

A cloudy patch that does not change when the picture changes. A rainbow sheen at an angle. Fine scratches that catch the light with the set off. Streaks that return after every clean because the coating itself is uneven. None of these are faults in the electronics, and none can be polished out. If you see them, stop cleaning and accept the panel as it is; further rubbing makes it worse.

If instead you see lines, bands, flicker or dark areas that move with the picture, that is an electronics or backlight problem, and it is repairable. Our LED and LCD TV repairing page describes the common faults and what a board-level repair involves.

A note on screen protectors and covers

Clip-on acrylic screen protectors sold for large TVs protect against children and pets but reduce picture quality and trap dust between the layers. If you fit one, clean both faces of it with the same method and the same cloths. Dust covers for the set when not in use are a good idea in a dusty flat or on a ground floor near a road.

Summary

Unplug. Dry microfibre first. Distilled water on the cloth for marks. Panel-safe cleaner only as a last step and never on the screen itself. No glass cleaner, no alcohol, no paper, no pressure. Dry before switching on.

The manufacturers say the same. Samsung's support page on cleaning a TV screen is a good reference for the method above, and the advice applies equally to other brands. If you have already used the wrong cleaner and the picture has changed, contact us with the model number and a photo of the screen, and we can tell you quickly whether the damage is cosmetic or electronic.

Cleaning the back and the vents

Dust on the back of the set matters more than dust on the front. The vents along the top and rear let heat out of the power board and backlight driver, and a set that runs hot fails early. Twice a year, unplug it, pull it a little away from the wall, and run a soft brush or a vacuum with a brush attachment along the vents. Do not open the back cover; there is nothing in there to clean by hand and the backlight supply holds a charge after the set is off. Wipe the ports with a dry cloth, check that the HDMI plugs sit straight, and push the set back no closer than ten centimetres from the wall. A TV in a cabinet with the doors closed is the single most common reason we see power board failures in sets under five years old.
