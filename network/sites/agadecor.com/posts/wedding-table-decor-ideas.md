Wedding table decor ideas usually arrive as a mood board: a tall arrangement here, a runner of greenery there, a forest of candles. What the board never shows is the table underneath, and the table decides almost everything. A centerpiece that looks generous on a 60-inch round disappears on a 10-foot banquet. A candle count that feels romantic on a long table becomes a hazard on a round where guests reach across. We design tablescapes for Chicago and North Shore weddings every weekend, and the first question we ask a couple is not "what flowers do you like" but "what shape are your tables."

So this guide is organized that way. Four table shapes, the decor that works on each, the proportions we actually use, and realistic Chicago pricing for 2026. Use it alongside our [wedding flower decoration ideas](/blog/wedding-flower-decoration-ideas/) if you are still choosing the flowers themselves.

## Start with the venue's tables

Most Chicago venues own their tables, and that inventory shapes your options. Hotel ballrooms and country clubs stock 60-inch and 72-inch rounds, usually seating eight and ten. Loft venues in the West Loop and Fulton Market tend toward 8-foot rectangular banquets, often farm-style wood that photographs beautifully bare. Historic venues sometimes have a mix, which is where a good floor plan earns its keep.

Ask the venue for a table inventory with dimensions before you fall in love with any decor idea. If you want long tables in a round-table ballroom, rented 8-foot farm tables run 85 to 150 dollars each in Chicago, delivered, and you will need linens or not depending on the finish.

## Round tables: the workhorse

A 60-inch round seats eight comfortably and leaves a center circle about 24 inches across once place settings are down. A 72-inch round seats ten and gives you roughly 30 inches. That center circle is your whole canvas, and the mistake we see most is overfilling it.

### Low and lush

One compact arrangement, 10 to 14 inches tall and 14 to 18 inches wide, is the classic for a reason. Guests can see each other, the arrangement reads from every seat, and the flower cost stays reasonable: 125 to 250 dollars per table for a full garden-style piece in seasonal blooms, less with more greenery. Add three to five votives and a pair of taper candles in low holders and the table is done.

### Tall and airy

An elevated arrangement on a 24- to 30-inch stand lifts the flowers above eye level, so conversation passes underneath. This is the look for ballrooms with high ceilings. Keep the stand narrow (a trumpet vase or a slim gold stand) and the arrangement 20 to 26 inches wide. Budget 275 to 500 dollars per table. We rent stands from our [vase and glass collection](/vases/) so couples are not buying 20 trumpet vases they will never use again.

### Mixed heights

Alternate tall and low tables in a checkerboard across the room. It gives the photographs depth and cuts the flower budget, because the low tables cost half of the tall ones. Our usual ratio is one tall table for every two low.

### Candles only

For couples who want atmosphere more than flowers, a round can carry 15 to 25 candles of mixed heights in glass: pillars in hurricanes, tapers, and votives in a loose cluster with a few stems tucked in. Candle-only tables run 60 to 140 dollars with rentals, and most Chicago venues require real flame inside glass or LED only, so check the policy before you commit.

## Long banquet tables: rhythm, not centerpieces

A long table does not want a centerpiece. It wants a rhythm that repeats down its length so the eye travels. An 8-foot banquet seating eight gives you a strip about 18 to 24 inches wide down the middle once settings are in place.

### The garland runner

Fresh greenery (eucalyptus, Italian ruscus, olive) laid end to end with flowers tucked in at intervals is the most requested long-table look in Chicago for the past several seasons. A full garland is 25 to 45 dollars per linear foot depending on how much bloom is worked in, so a 10-table loft reception at 8 feet each is 2,000 to 3,600 in garland alone. A lighter version with greenery every other table and bud vases between is a third of that.

### Bud vase clusters

Groups of three to seven small vases, each holding one to three stems, repeated every 24 to 30 inches. Flexible, inexpensive (8 to 20 dollars per vase filled), and excellent for mixing in heirloom glass or colored bottles. This is also the look that survives being moved by guests, which matters at a long table.

### Compote and candle repeat

A low footed compote arrangement every four to five feet with taper candles between, usually in brass or glass holders at two heights. Elegant, structured, and it photographs like a magazine. 110 to 200 dollars per arrangement plus candle rentals.

### Fabric runners

A linen or gauze runner changes the whole table for 15 to 35 dollars rented. On bare wood farm tables we often skip it. On a standard rectangular table under a full-length linen, the runner is what gives the table a spine. Our [linen rental](/linens/) page lists what we carry, and the colors couples choose most are oatmeal, sage and dusty blue.

## King's tables and head tables

A king's table, the extra-wide long table (usually 48 to 60 inches wide) that seats the wedding party and family together, has room for real volume. This is where we put the statement pieces: a long, low meadow of flowers running the full length, or two or three raised arrangements with candles filling the gaps. Because everyone faces inward and the table is wide, you can go to 12 to 16 inches tall without blocking anyone.

A head table against a wall is different. It is seen from one side, so it should be decorated like a stage. Flowers forward and low so the couple is visible, a backdrop or [flower wall](/blog/flower-wall-rental-guide/) behind, and floor arrangements at either end to frame it. Budget 600 to 1,800 dollars for a head table that photographs well, more with a full backdrop.

## Sweetheart tables

A sweetheart table for two is the most photographed table of the night and the easiest to over-decorate. Keep the top nearly clear: one small low arrangement, two candles, the couple's place settings. Put the volume on the floor and behind: a pair of floor arrangements or lanterns, a draped backdrop or greenery arch, a specialty linen that pools to the floor. Chicago couples typically spend 400 to 1,200 on the sweetheart setting, and most of that is the backdrop.

## Linens, chargers and the layer most people forget

Decor sits on linen, and linen is the single largest visual surface in the room. Three rules we follow:

- Floor-length linens on round tables, always. A lap-length cloth that shows the table legs makes a 300-dollar centerpiece look cheap.
- Match the napkin to the mood, not the tablecloth. A velvet or gauze napkin in a second color does more than any other 2-dollar item on the table.
- Chargers earn their place when the menu is plated and the settings are sparse. Gold, rattan and glass chargers rent for 1.50 to 3 dollars each in Chicago. Skip them for family-style service, where the platters need the room.

Our rentals page covers [up-lighting, crystal and stands](/extras/), which is the other layer: a tabletop looks completely different under warm amber uplight than under a ballroom's white overheads, and lighting is often the better spend than more flowers.

## Candle counts and venue rules

As a rule of thumb, we place one candle per guest on long tables and six to ten per round. Mixed heights read richer than a single row. Before you buy or rent anything, confirm the venue's open-flame policy. Many Chicago historic venues allow real flame only in glass taller than the flame; some hotels require LED. Our LED tapers and pillars have improved enough that guests rarely notice, and they save the cleanup.

## What it costs: a Chicago reception of 150 guests

Fifteen 72-inch rounds plus a head table, mid-range, 2026 pricing:

- Low garden arrangements, 15 tables: 2,250 to 3,750
- Candles and holders, rented: 600 to 1,100
- Floor-length linens and napkins: 700 to 1,300
- Chargers, 150: 225 to 450
- Head table flowers and backdrop: 900 to 1,800
- Delivery, setup and strike: 600 to 1,200

That is roughly 5,300 to 9,600 dollars for tables alone, before ceremony and cocktail-hour decor. Nationally, The Knot's [Real Weddings Study](https://www.theknot.com/content/wedding-data-insights/real-weddings-study) puts average flower and decor spending in the mid four figures, and Chicago runs above the national line. Long tables with garland push the total up; bud vases and mixed heights bring it down.

## Decisions in order

If you take one thing from this: choose the table shape first, then the linen, then the candle plan, and only then the flowers. Couples who start with the flowers spend the most and change their minds the most. Couples who start with the table end up with a room that looks designed, because it was.
