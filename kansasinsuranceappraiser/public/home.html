01 Covered, but underpaid

Why Kansans call a Kansas Insurance Appraiser

Most short claims aren’t denied. They’re just short: a few slopes approved on a roof the hail hit everywhere, siding patched where it can’t be matched, gutters and screens left off, an old roof pushed onto a payment schedule with the wrong age. A Kansas Insurance Appraiser exists for that gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

03 The process

How a Kansas Insurance Appraiser moves a stuck claim

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here is the path from first call to signed award.

  1. 1

    Free claim check

    We read your policy, the insurer’s estimate and your photos, then tell you whether appraisal makes sense.

  2. 2

    Our own valuation

    We inspect and measure the property and build a line-item estimate priced for your part of Kansas.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and you name a Kansas Insurance Appraiser as your appraiser on time.

  4. 4

    Panel work

    We work through the differences with the insurer’s appraiser, line by line, with photos and measurements.

  5. 5

    Signed award

    Open items go to a neutral umpire. Two signatures set the amount of loss and payment follows the policy.

08 Before you demand

Three questions to answer first

1

Is coverage accepted?

Appraisal values a covered loss. If the insurer denied the claim or cited an exclusion, talk to a Kansas attorney before anything else.

2

Is the gap big enough?

Fees come out of the increase. A gap of a few hundred dollars rarely justifies appraisal; a missed roof replacement often does.

3

Is your file complete?

Both line-item estimates, dated photos and your policy. The appraisal prep scorecard shows what is missing.

09 FAQ

Questions people ask a Kansas Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it is the wrong move.

All FAQs Glossary
What does a Kansas Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree a loss is covered but disagree on the cost. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, present open items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster manages and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question, the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When should I call a Kansas Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the insurer’s estimate and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful, generally more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is the appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, yes, once any two of the three panel members sign it. That is why documentation matters before appraisal is demanded.

What will it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my part of Kansas?

Yes. We work in all 105 Kansas counties, including Wichita, the Kansas City suburbs, Topeka, Lawrence, Manhattan, Salina, Hays, Dodge City and Garden City.
