What we do

What a Colorado Insurance Appraiser does when the numbers don’t match

Most disputed claims in this state aren’t denials. They’re short payments: a roof priced as a repair when the shingles are discontinued, a hail claim that skipped the gutters and window wraps, a wildfire smoke loss that cleaned surfaces nobody tested. Colorado Insurance Appraiser works in that gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

  1. We scope the loss ourselves. Our own inspection, measurements and photos — not an edited copy of the carrier’s estimate.
  2. We price it for where you live. Labor and material costs in Breckenridge are not Pueblo’s. A Colorado Insurance Appraiser estimate reflects your market.
  3. We tell you when appraisal is the wrong tool. Coverage denials and legal questions belong with an attorney, and we’ll say so on the first call.

Statewide knowledge

Five regions, five kinds of Colorado claims

A hailstorm in Aurora, a frozen pipe in a Vail condo and a wildfire on the Western Slope produce very different estimates. A Colorado Insurance Appraiser has to know how each region’s weather shows up on a building — and in the carrier’s numbers.

Denver · Colorado Springs · Fort Collins

Front Range

Most of the state’s population lives along the I-25 corridor, and so do most of its hail claims. Storms that form over the foothills on summer afternoons drift east over Jefferson, Denver, Arapahoe, Douglas, El Paso, Larimer and Weld counties, often hitting the same neighborhoods year after year.

  • Large hail
  • Downslope wind
  • Wildland-urban fire
  • Spring snow

Greeley · Sterling · Lamar

Eastern Plains

East of the urban corridor, open farm country produces the state’s tornadoes and some of its largest hailstones. Claims often blend a farmhouse, machine sheds, grain bins and fencing under one policy, with long drives between adjusters.

  • Hail
  • Tornadoes
  • Straight-line wind
  • Ag buildings

Summit · Eagle · Pitkin · Grand

Mountains & resort towns

High-country claims follow the calendar: heavy snow on roofs in winter, burst pipes in unoccupied second homes, and wildfire threat in summer and fall. Remote sites, short building seasons and resort-area labor costs all push repair prices above metro levels.

  • Snow load
  • Ice dams
  • Frozen pipes
  • Wildfire

Grand Junction · Montrose · Glenwood Springs

Western Slope

Mesa, Delta, Montrose and Garfield counties see strong canyon winds, summer fire on the surrounding mesas and occasional damaging hail. Contractor availability and material delivery from the Front Range can stretch repair timelines.

  • Wind
  • Wildfire
  • Hail
  • Water

Pueblo · Durango · Trinidad · Alamosa

Southern Colorado

From the Pueblo area to La Plata County and the San Luis Valley, the south mixes high-desert hail and wind with forest fire risk around Durango and the Sangre de Cristo foothills.

  • Hail
  • Wildfire
  • Wind
  • Freeze

Hail Alley

The Front Range sits in the middle of Hail Alley

Forecasters use the name for the high plains where the country’s most frequent large hail falls, and metro Denver sits squarely inside it. The May 8, 2017 storm that battered the west metro is often cited as the costliest insured event in state history, and a 4.83-inch stone that fell near Bethune in 2019 set the state record.

For a Colorado Insurance Appraiser, that history matters: repeat storms create arguments about old versus new damage, discontinued shingles and whether a roof can really be repaired.

The process

How Colorado Insurance Appraiser takes a claim from dispute to award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result on the amount of loss. Here is the path, start to finish.

  1. 1

    Free claim review

    We read the policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos, then tell you plainly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 2

    Independent valuation

    An on-site inspection and a line-item estimate priced for your part of Colorado.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is invoked in writing and we’re named as your appraiser within the policy’s time frame.

  4. 4

    Panel work

    We work through the estimates with the carrier’s appraiser. Most disputes narrow sharply here.

  5. 5

    Binding award

    Remaining items go to a neutral umpire. Two signatures set the amount of loss.

Choosing help

Appraiser, public adjuster or attorney?

Each solves a different problem. Picking the right one first saves time and fees, and Colorado Insurance Appraiser will point you elsewhere when appraisal isn’t the right fit.

Colorado Insurance Appraiser

Values the amount of loss

  • Coverage accepted, number disputed
  • Binding award on two of three signatures
  • Doesn’t decide coverage

Public adjuster

Handles the whole claim

  • Often hired early, from first notice
  • Negotiates a settlement
  • Doesn’t sit on an appraisal panel as a neutral

Attorney

Handles legal disputes

  • Denials, bad faith, suit deadlines
  • Can litigate and advise on statutes
  • Usually not needed for a pure pricing gap

FAQ

Questions people ask a Colorado Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All FAQs Claim glossary
What does a Colorado Insurance Appraiser do?

A Colorado Insurance Appraiser values property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. Once appraisal is invoked, we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is appraisal the same as hiring a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question — the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When is the right time to call?

Usually after the carrier has issued its estimate and you have a contractor’s number that is meaningfully higher. Call sooner if a deadline in your policy or a letter from the insurer is approaching.

Is an appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, generally yes, once two of the three panel members sign. Appraisal doesn’t decide coverage questions, which is why the file should be well documented before anyone invokes it.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and typically shares the umpire’s fee. We put our fee in writing before work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my part of Colorado?

Yes. We work in all 64 counties, from the Denver metro and Colorado Springs to Grand Junction, Durango, the mountain resort towns and the Eastern Plains.
