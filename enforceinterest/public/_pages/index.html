I.What we do

Enforce Interest turns a slow claim into a documented one.

Insurance claims rarely fail all at once. They stall: an inspection that takes a month, a desk review that cuts the estimate, a document request for something already in the file, a supplement that sits for a season.

Texas lawmakers anticipated that. Since 1991 the state has had a prompt-payment statute — first Article 21.55, now Chapter 542, Subchapter B of the Insurance Code — that puts insurers on a clock and charges them for running past it. Enforce Interest exists because that clock is almost never raised by the people it protects. We read the claim file the way the statute does: what was requested, when it arrived, when payment was due and when it actually came.

The result is a dated, sourced timeline and an interest figure the carrier can check line by line. That is what Enforce Interest presents — not a feeling that the claim took too long, but the statutory math of how long, on how much, at what rate.

II.The clock

Texas built a payment clock into every property policy.

Scroll the statute. Each step is a separate deadline in Chapter 542 — and a missed one is what Enforce Interest documents.

Statutory clockDay 0
NoticeDay 60Paid

The claim is reported. Nothing is late yet.

Day 0 · Notice of claim

You give the insurer written notice.

“Notice of claim” means any written notice that reasonably tells the insurer the facts of the claim (§542.051). Keep the date and a copy — every later deadline is measured from a document like this one.

By day 15 · §542.055

Acknowledge, investigate, ask for what it needs.

Within 15 days (30 business days for an eligible surplus lines insurer) the carrier must acknowledge the claim, begin its investigation and request every item, statement and form it reasonably believes it will need. It can ask for more later only if the investigation makes that necessary.

15 business days after items arrive · §542.056

Accept or reject — in writing.

Once it has the items it requested, the insurer has 15 business days to accept or reject the claim in writing (30 days if it reasonably suspects arson). If it needs more time it must say why within that window, and then decide within 45 more days. A rejection must state its reasons.

5 business days after acceptance · §542.057

Accepted means paid — within five business days.

After notifying you that it will pay all or part of the claim, the insurer must pay within 5 business days (20 business days for an eligible surplus lines insurer), or within 5 business days after you perform any act the payment was conditioned on.

60 days after items arrive · §542.058

The 60-day backstop.

Whatever happens with acceptance, an insurer that delays payment for more than 60 days after receiving all the items it reasonably requested is liable under §542.060. The exception: if arbitration or litigation finds the claim invalid. In a declared weather catastrophe the claim-handling deadlines extend by 15 days (§542.059(b)).

Every day after · §542.060

Interest runs until the money is paid.

From the date the claim was required to be paid, the insurer owes interest on the late amount as damages — 18% a year, or the floating rate for qualifying weather claims — plus reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees. Partial payments shrink the balance the interest runs on; they don’t stop it.

III.Two rates

Standard 18% or the weather floating rate.

Since 2017, Texas has applied two different interest rates depending on the claim. Picking the right one is the first thing Enforce Interest checks.

§542.060(a) · standard
18%

Fixed by statute. Simple interest, per year.

  • Fire, smoke, water, plumbing, theft, vandalism and other non-weather losses
  • Weather claims made before September 1, 2017
  • Plus reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees
How the 18% rate works
§542.060(c) · Chapter 542A
12.00%

§304.003 judgment rate (7.00%) + 5%, November 2026.

  • Claims for damage caused wholly or partly by forces of nature — hail, wind, hurricane, tornado, flood, lightning, wildfire, snowstorm, rainstorm, earthquake
  • Claims made on or after September 1, 2017, in an action governed by Chapter 542A
  • In court the rate is set on the date of judgment; it can range from 10% to 20%
How the floating rate works

On a $50,000 claim paid 90 days late: $2,219.18 at 18% versus $1,479.45 at 12.00%. See every monthly §304.003 rate since 2005.

V.Appraisal & late payment

The appraisal award came in higher. The interest may still be owed.

For years carriers treated paying an appraisal award as the end of the claim. The Texas Supreme Court disagreed.

Step 1

Claim stalls; carrier pays a low figure.

Step 2

Policyholder invokes appraisal.

Step 3

Panel awards more; carrier pays the award.

Result

Carrier argues the claim is closed and no prompt-payment damages are owed.

2019

Barbara Technologies: paying an award is not an acknowledgment of liability and doesn’t bar prompt-payment claims.

2020

Alvarez: same rule applied to an award paid on a disputed claim.

2021

Hinojos: a reasonable, timely partial payment doesn’t shield the unpaid balance.

Result

Interest can run on whatever was owed but unpaid at the deadline, through the date the award is paid.

On a $100,000 underpayment paid eighteen months (548 days) late at 18%, the interest alone is $27,024.66. Enforce Interest calculates it from the original deadline to the award payment date — and checks whether any interest the carrier did pay used the right rate and dates.

VI.Does your claim qualify?

Most Texas first-party property claims can.

Whether interest is owed depends on coverage, what the insurer requested and when, and why the payment was late. Here is the quick version.

Likely worth a review

  • The carrier accepted the claim but paid part of it later, or paid it all late.
  • No decision arrived within the statutory windows after you sent what was requested.
  • A supplement for additional damage sat unpaid for months.
  • Appraisal produced an award higher than what the carrier had paid.
  • The claim was denied, then paid after you pushed back.

Probably not

  • The claim was paid in full within the deadlines.
  • The claim was denied and the denial was never reversed or found wrong.
  • The delay was caused by the policyholder — no access, no documents, no response.
  • Workers’ comp, title, mortgage guaranty, fidelity/surety bonds or marine insurance (excluded by §542.053).

X.FAQ

Questions policyholders ask Enforce Interest.

What is Enforce Interest?

Enforce Interest is a Rise Public Adjusting service built around one part of Texas law: the prompt-payment rules in Chapter 542 of the Texas Insurance Code. When a carrier pays a covered property claim late, §542.060 adds statutory interest as damages. We rebuild the claim timeline, calculate that interest and present it to the carrier on the policyholder’s behalf.

What is the Texas 60-day rule?

Under Tex. Ins. Code §542.058, once an insurer has received all the items, statements and forms it reasonably requested, it generally has 60 days to pay a covered claim (unless another statute sets a different period). If it delays longer, it owes the damages in §542.060: interest on the claim amount plus reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees.

How much interest does a Texas insurer owe for paying late?

For most claims, 18% a year, simple interest, on the amount that was paid late. For weather-related property claims made on or after September 1, 2017 (in an action governed by Chapter 542A), the rate is the Finance Code §304.003 judgment rate plus 5%. The November 2026 judgment rate is 7.00%, which makes the weather rate 12.00%. In a lawsuit, that rate is fixed on the date of judgment.

Does paying an appraisal award end the insurer’s liability for interest?

No, not automatically. In Barbara Technologies Corp. v. State Farm Lloyds (Tex. 2019) and Hinojos v. State Farm Lloyds, 619 S.W.3d 651 (Tex. 2021), the Texas Supreme Court held that paying an appraisal award does not, by itself, protect an insurer from prompt-payment damages. If money was owed and unpaid when the statutory deadline passed, interest can be owed on it even though the award was later paid.

Do I need a lawyer to recover prompt-payment interest?

Not always. Many carriers resolve the issue once the dates and the math are documented and presented. If a carrier refuses and a lawsuit is needed, that is work for a licensed Texas attorney; Enforce Interest is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

What does it cost?

The first claim review is free. If we take the engagement, the fee is a contingency percentage of what is recovered, and Texas law caps public adjuster compensation at 10% of the insurance settlement on the claim (§4102.104). If nothing is recovered, you owe nothing.
