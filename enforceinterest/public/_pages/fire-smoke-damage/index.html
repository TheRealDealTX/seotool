Fire & smoke damage claims

Non-weather losses carry the full 18% when paid late. Fire claims stall on scope, contents and investigations — here is how to keep the clock on the record.

Fire claims get the full 18%

Fire is not a “force of nature” under Chapter 542A unless it is a wildfire or lightning-caused fire. A house fire from cooking, wiring, a dryer or arson is governed by the standard §542.060(a) rate: 18% a year on whatever was paid late, plus reasonable attorney’s fees. On a $150,000 fire claim paid 90 days late, that is $6,657.53.

One timing difference: if the insurer has a reasonable basis to believe the loss resulted from arson, it gets 30 days (instead of 15 business days) after receiving the requested items to accept or reject (§542.056(b)). The 60-day payment backstop in §542.058 is unchanged.

Smoke damage: the scope fight

Smoke and soot travel well beyond the room that burned — through the HVAC system, into wall cavities and attics, and onto contents in closed drawers. Carriers often scope cleaning for the visibly affected rooms only. The evidence that widens scope:

  • Smoke damage investigations. An industrial hygienist or environmental consultant samples surfaces (tape lifts and wipe samples) and air to identify char, soot and ash throughout the structure.
  • HVAC inspection. Ductwork and air handlers that ran during or after the fire distribute residue.
  • Odor testing and contents. Textiles and porous contents often can’t be restored; a contents specialist documents what can be cleaned and what must be replaced.
  • Wildfire smoke. Even without flames on the property, wildfire smoke infiltration can be a covered loss; wildfire is a force of nature, so those claims fall under Chapter 542A and the floating rate.

Each investigation report is a document the insurer may request. Send it with a dated cover letter: the date the carrier receives the last reasonably requested item is what starts the 60-day clock. More on this in our guide to smoke damage claims in Texas.

Where fire claims slow down

  • Cause-and-origin investigations that run for months with no written explanation of what is still needed.
  • ACV versus replacement cost. Depreciation is withheld up front, and the holdback becomes payable only after rebuilding — another payment with its own deadline.
  • Contents inventories of thousands of items, reviewed line by line.
  • Code upgrades (ordinance or law coverage) when the rebuild must meet current code.
  • Additional living expense paid in slow, partial increments while the family is displaced.
  • Examinations under oath and recorded statements requested late in the claim.

Total losses and the 72-hour rule

If the insurer pays, or commits in writing to pay, the policy limit for a total fire loss of real property within 72 hours after the loss is reported, Texas bars a public adjuster from charging a percentage fee on that claim (§4102.104(b)). That is a consumer protection worth knowing. Most fire claims are not resolved that quickly, and that is where documenting the timeline matters.

What to keep after a fire

  1. The fire department report number and date.
  2. Photos and video before debris removal.
  3. Every ALE receipt (hotel, rent, meals over normal, mileage).
  4. The contents list, updated as you remember items.
  5. Copies of all hygienist, contractor and engineer reports with the date each was sent to the carrier.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How long does an insurance company have to pay a fire claim in Texas?

The general rules apply: 15 business days to accept or reject after receiving requested items (30 days if arson is reasonably suspected), 5 business days to pay after acceptance, and 60 days after receiving requested items in any event (§§542.056–.058).

What is a smoke damage investigation?

A professional assessment — usually by an industrial hygienist — that samples surfaces and air to find and map soot, char and ash residue, so the cleaning and replacement scope is based on evidence rather than what is visible.

Is fire claim interest 18%?

Yes for most fires. Wildfire and lightning are forces of nature, so those claims (made on or after September 1, 2017) use the floating rate, currently 12.00%.
