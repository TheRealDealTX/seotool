The three things that have to be true

1. A covered first-party claim

Your own property policy — homeowners, landlord, condo, renters or commercial property — for damage the policy covers: hail, wind, hurricane, fire, smoke, water, theft and so on. Claims against someone else’s insurer, and the lines excluded by §542.053 (workers’ comp, title, mortgage guaranty, bonds, marine), don’t qualify.

2. Money that was owed

Interest applies to amounts the insurer was liable for. That includes underpaid initial estimates, supplements, and differences resolved through appraisal — whenever the final amount paid was higher than what was paid by the deadline.

3. A missed deadline

The most common is §542.058: payment more than 60 days after the insurer received everything it reasonably requested. Others are the 5-business-day payment deadline after acceptance (§542.057) and the decision deadlines in §542.056.

Why people don’t realize they qualify

Because the claim feels finished. The roof is on, the checks cleared, and the timeline is forgotten. But that timeline is exactly what decides whether interest is owed. FAQ

Qualification questions

What makes a Texas insurance claim qualify for statutory interest?

Two things: the insurer is liable for the claim (it was covered and owed), and it failed to comply with a deadline in Chapter 542, Subchapter B — most often by paying more than 60 days after receiving the items it reasonably requested (§542.058).

Does a denied claim qualify?

Only if the denial turns out to be wrong — for example, the insurer later pays or a court or appraisal finds money was owed. A claim found invalid in arbitration or litigation doesn’t qualify (§542.058(b)).

Does my claim qualify if I was paid in full?

It can. Interest depends on when payment was made, not on whether you were eventually paid. A claim paid in full but late can still carry interest for the period of delay.
