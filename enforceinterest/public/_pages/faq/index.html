The 60-day rule

What is the Texas 60-day rule for insurance claims?

Texas Insurance Code §542.058 says that once an insurer has received all items, statements and forms it reasonably requested, it may not delay payment of a covered claim for more than 60 days (unless another statute sets a different period). If it does, it owes the damages in §542.060.

When does the 60-day clock start?

On the date the insurer received the last item it reasonably requested under §542.055 — not necessarily the date you filed the claim. If the insurer kept requesting things it didn’t need, the clock may have started earlier than it says; that is a fact question built from the claim file.

What other deadlines are there?

15 days to acknowledge, investigate and request items (§542.055); 15 business days after receiving them to accept or reject (§542.056); 5 business days to pay after accepting (§542.057). Surplus lines insurers get longer on the first and third, and a declared weather catastrophe adds 15 days (§542.059(b)).

Does the rule apply to commercial property claims?

Yes. It applies to first-party claims under most insurance policies, residential or commercial, except the lines excluded by §542.053.

What if the insurer says it needs more time?

Under §542.056(d) it must tell you, within the 15-business-day window, why it needs more time, and then accept or reject within 45 days of that notice.

Interest & rates

How much interest does a Texas insurer owe for late payment?

18% a year (simple) under §542.060(a) for most claims. For weather claims made on or after September 1, 2017, in an action governed by Chapter 542A, the §304.003 judgment rate plus 5% — 12.00% using the November 2026 rate of 7.00%.

What is the Act of God floating rate?

The informal name for §542.060(c): interest on weather (“forces of nature”) claims at the Finance Code §304.003 rate on the date of judgment plus five percent. Because §304.003 is floored at 5% and capped at 15%, it ranges from 10% to 20%. Full explanation.

Is it the date of loss or the date of the claim that decides the rate?

The date the claim was made. HB 1774, Section 4(b), applies §542.060(c) only to claims made on or after September 1, 2017.

Is the interest compounded?

No. It is simple interest on the unpaid amount, accruing daily from the date payment was required.

Do I also get attorney’s fees?

§542.060 adds reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees. In weather cases, Chapter 542A can reduce or eliminate fees depending on the pre-suit notice and the result (§542A.007).

Can I get prejudgment interest too?

The statute says nothing in §542.060 prevents prejudgment interest “as provided by law.” See our prejudgment interest guide.

Appraisal & supplements

If the carrier paid the appraisal award, is interest still owed?

It can be. Barbara Technologies (Tex. 2019) and Hinojos (Tex. 2021) hold that paying an award doesn’t by itself end prompt-payment liability; amounts owed but unpaid at the deadline can carry interest until paid.

What did Rodriguez v. Safeco decide?

In 2024 the Texas Supreme Court held that in a Chapter 542A case, an insurer that pays the appraisal award plus all statutory interest that could be owed isn’t liable for attorney’s fees under §542A.007.

Do supplements have their own deadlines?

Each supplement is evaluated on its own timeline — when the insurer received it and the items reasonably needed, and when it paid. Interest is calculated per supplement and added up.

Deadlines to act

How long do I have to sue my insurance company in Texas?

Check the policy’s “suit against us” condition — often two years and one day from when the cause of action accrues. Texas voids contractual limits shorter than two years (§16.070). Chapter 541 claims have a two-year limit (§541.162). When a claim accrues is a legal question; talk to an attorney well before any deadline.

How long do I have to file a claim?

Policies usually require prompt notice; some set a fixed window. TWIA claims must be filed within one year of the damage (§2210.573).

What is the 542A pre-suit notice?

For weather claims, written notice to the insurer at least 61 days before suit, stating the complaint, the amount owed and attorney’s fees incurred (§542A.003). Details here.
