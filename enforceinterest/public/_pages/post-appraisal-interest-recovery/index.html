Hinojos v. State Farm Lloyds · 619 S.W.3d 651

The carrier paid the appraisal. The interest is a separate question.

Appraisal sets the amount of loss — not the date it was due. Texas law can still owe you interest on every day the underpaid amount sat unpaid.

The pattern

A claim stalls. The carrier pays a figure well below the contractor’s estimate. The policyholder invokes the appraisal clause; each side names an appraiser, an umpire is chosen, and the panel issues an award — often far higher than what was paid. The carrier pays the award and closes the file. No one mentions the months between when the money was due and when it arrived.

That gap is what Enforce Interest calculates. Appraisal fixes the amount of loss. It doesn’t erase the date by which that amount should have been paid.

What the Texas Supreme Court has held

  • Barbara Technologies Corp. v. State Farm Lloyds, 589 S.W.3d 806 (Tex. 2019). Paying an appraisal award is not an acknowledgment of liability and is not a determination that the insurer complied with the prompt-payment statute. Payment of the award does not, by itself, bar TPPCA damages.
  • Ortiz v. State Farm Lloyds, 589 S.W.3d 127 (Tex. 2019). The same day, the Court held that paying the award does generally foreclose breach-of-contract and bad-faith claims for policy benefits unless the insured shows an independent injury — but the prompt-payment claim survives.
  • Alvarez v. State Farm Lloyds, 601 S.W.3d 781 (Tex. 2020). Applied Barbara Technologies to reverse a judgment for the insurer.
  • Hinojos v. State Farm Lloyds, 619 S.W.3d 651 (Tex. 2021). The court of appeals had ruled for the insurer because it made a reasonable payment within the 60-day window. The Supreme Court reversed: an insurer is liable for prompt-payment damages on amounts it owed but had not paid when the statutory deadline expired, even if its earlier partial payment was reasonable and timely.
  • Rodriguez v. Safeco Insurance Co. of Indiana (Tex. Feb. 2, 2024). In a Chapter 542A weather case, when the insurer pays the appraisal award and any statutory interest that could be owed, nothing remains to be awarded on the claim, so §542A.007 permits no attorney’s fees.
What changed after Rodriguez

Many carriers now pay the award together with an interest figure they calculate themselves. That doesn’t end the analysis — it moves it to the math. Did they start interest on the right date? Use the right rate? Credit each earlier payment on the date it was actually made? A short-paid interest figure leaves the door open.

How post-appraisal interest is calculated

  1. Amount owed: the award under the policy’s valuation, less the deductible.
  2. Deadline: usually the §542.058 date — 60 days after the insurer received the items it reasonably requested — or an earlier §542.057 date for any part it accepted.
  3. Balance at the deadline: amount owed minus everything paid by the deadline.
  4. Accrual: interest on that balance at the applicable rate, reduced each time a later payment arrives, through the date the award was paid.

Common carrier responses — and the replies

“We paid the appraisal award, so the claim is closed.”

Barbara Technologies and Hinojos answer this directly: payment of an award does not, by itself, discharge prompt-payment liability.

“Our original payment was reasonable.”

That was the reasoning the Supreme Court rejected in Hinojos. Reasonableness of an earlier payment doesn’t change that the rest of the money owed was unpaid when the deadline expired.

“We never had a complete file, so no clock started.”

This is a real factual issue. The 60-day period runs from receipt of the items the insurer reasonably requested. The claim file — what was asked for, when, and what was already in hand — decides it, which is why the timeline reconstruction matters.

“We already paid interest.”

Check the calculation line by line. The appraisal calculator has a field for exactly this.

  • The policy declarations page and the appraisal clause.
  • The appraisal demand, appointment letters and the signed award.
  • Every payment letter and check stub, with dates.
  • All correspondence with the carrier, including document requests and your responses.
  • Any 542A pre-suit notice, for weather claims.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

If the carrier paid the appraisal award, can I still collect interest?

Possibly. Under Barbara Technologies and Hinojos, payment of an award doesn’t by itself end prompt-payment liability. Interest can be owed on amounts unpaid at the statutory deadline through the date they were paid.

What is Hinojos v. State Farm Lloyds?

A 2021 Texas Supreme Court decision, 619 S.W.3d 651, holding that an insurer’s reasonable, timely partial payment doesn’t shield it from prompt-payment damages on the amounts it owed but hadn’t paid by the deadline. Read our explainer.

Does Rodriguez v. Safeco mean I can’t recover anything after appraisal?

No. Rodriguez concerns attorney’s fees in Chapter 542A cases when the insurer has paid the award and all statutory interest that could be owed. If the interest wasn’t paid, or was underpaid, the interest itself remains owed.

Is there a deadline to pursue post-appraisal interest?

Yes. Your policy’s suit-limitation clause (not shorter than two years under §16.070) and general limitations rules apply, and when the claim accrued is a legal question. Don’t wait.
