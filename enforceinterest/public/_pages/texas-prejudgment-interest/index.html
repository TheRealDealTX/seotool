What prejudgment interest is

Prejudgment interest compensates a plaintiff for the time between the injury (or notice of the claim) and the judgment — the lost use of money that should have been paid earlier. In Texas it comes from two sources:

  • Statute. Finance Code §§304.101–.108 apply to wrongful death, personal injury and property damage cases. A judgment in those cases earns prejudgment interest (§304.102).
  • Common law. For other claims, such as breach of contract, Texas courts award prejudgment interest as a matter of equity. In Johnson & Higgins v. Kenneco Energy (Tex. 1998) the Texas Supreme Court held that those awards should follow the statutory accrual rules and rate.

Prejudgment interest on insurance claims

When a Texas insurer pays a claim late, the policyholder’s main remedy is the §542.060 statutory interest — 18% a year, or the floating rate for weather claims. That interest is labeled “damages,” and the statute adds: “Nothing in this subsection prevents the award of prejudgment interest on the amount of the claim, as provided by law.” So prejudgment interest is available in principle on top of §542.060 interest. In practice, insurers argue that both should not be awarded on the same dollars for the same days, and courts have handled the overlap in different ways. Keep both numbers separate when you present a claim.

Example

A $75,000 property damage judgment is signed on November 15, 2026. The defendant received written notice on March 1, 2025 and suit was filed on January 10, 2026. Day 180 after notice is August 28, 2025, which is earlier than the filing date, so interest runs from then to the day before judgment, at the 7.00% rate in effect at judgment.

Postjudgment interest then runs on the whole judgment — including court costs and prejudgment interest — at the same §304.003 rate until it is paid (§304.003(a)).

FAQ

Texas prejudgment interest questions

When does prejudgment interest start in Texas?

In wrongful death, personal injury and property damage cases, on the earlier of the 180th day after the defendant receives written notice of the claim or the date suit is filed, ending the day before judgment (Finance Code §304.104).

What is the Texas prejudgment interest rate?

The postjudgment rate applicable at the time of judgment (§304.103) — 7.00% for judgments rendered in November 2026. It is simple interest.

Can I get both §542.060 interest and prejudgment interest?

The statute expressly allows it: both §542.060(a) and (c) say nothing in them prevents an award of prejudgment interest “as provided by law.” How the two interact for the same period is argued case by case; insurers commonly object to a double recovery.

Does prejudgment interest apply to breach of an insurance contract?

Texas courts award prejudgment interest on contract damages under common law. In Johnson & Higgins of Texas, Inc. v. Kenneco Energy, Inc., 962 S.W.2d 507 (Tex. 1998), the Texas Supreme Court adopted the statutory accrual rules and rate for those cases too.
