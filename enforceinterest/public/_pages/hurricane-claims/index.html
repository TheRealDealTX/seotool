Hurricane claims in Texas

Wind, flood and TWIA each play by different rules. Here is which deadlines apply to your hurricane claim — and what late payment costs the insurer.

Hurricane claims are three claims in one

A Gulf Coast hurricane can produce a wind claim, a flood claim and a business-income or living-expense claim from the same day — under different policies, from different insurers, with different rules. Disputes over which force caused which damage are the single biggest source of delay. Knowing which policy pays what, and which deadlines apply to each, is the first step.

Private insurers: Chapter 542 and 542A

For a hurricane wind claim against a private carrier, the full prompt-payment sequence applies, extended by 15 days when the commissioner has declared a weather catastrophe (§542.059(b)). A claim made on or after September 1, 2017 is governed by Chapter 542A: interest under §542.060(c) at the §304.003 rate plus 5% (12.00% using November 2026’s rate) and a 61-day pre-suit notice.

TWIA claims follow different rules

TWIA policies are governed by Insurance Code Chapter 2210, and Subchapter L-1 provides the exclusive remedies for TWIA claims (§2210.572). Chapter 542A does not apply to TWIA (§542A.002(b)). The key TWIA deadlines:

  • File within one year of the date of damage (§2210.573(a)); the commissioner can extend this by up to 180 days for good cause (§2210.205(b)).
  • Accept or deny within 60 days after TWIA receives the claim or the information it requested (§2210.573(d)), unless extended by the commissioner.
  • Pay within 10 days after notice of acceptance, or after you perform a condition (§2210.5731).
  • Interest is prejudgment interest at the Finance Code Chapter 304 rate from the day after payment was due (§2210.576(b)(2)) — not the 18% or §542.060(c) rate.
  • Disputes over the amount go to appraisal under §2210.574; denied coverage requires notice of intent to sue and suit within two years of the denial notice (§2210.577).

Named-storm and windstorm deductibles

Coastal policies commonly carry a percentage deductible for named storms or windstorm. On a home insured for $400,000, a 5% deductible is $20,000. Run yours through the deductible calculator before deciding how to proceed.

Wind versus water

Wind policies exclude flood; flood policies don’t cover wind. When a carrier attributes damage to surge or rising water, ask for the basis in writing, preserve water lines and debris lines with photographs, and document roof and window openings that let wind-driven rain in. For TWIA, the commissioner publishes guidelines for separating wind from water damage, and those guidelines are presumed correct in a TWIA dispute (§2210.578(h)).

Living expenses and temporary repairs

Additional living expense (ALE) and business income are part of the claim and are paid on the same statutory clock. Keep receipts, and send them in batches with a cover letter so each batch has a clear date of receipt.

After the storm: a checklist

  1. Report the claim in writing and keep proof of the date.
  2. Photograph everything before mitigation, then mitigate (tarps, water extraction) and keep invoices.
  3. Identify every policy: homeowners, TWIA, flood, and any separate contents or business policies.
  4. Log every request and response in the claim timeline builder.
  5. Check the deadlines with the deadline calculator (tick “TWIA” if it applies).

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How long does TWIA have to pay a claim?

TWIA must accept or deny within 60 days after it receives the claim or requested information, and pay an accepted claim within 10 days after notice of acceptance (§§2210.573(d), 2210.5731).

Does the 18% penalty apply to TWIA?

No. TWIA claims have exclusive remedies in Chapter 2210. A successful claimant can recover the covered loss, prejudgment interest at the Finance Code rate from the day after payment was due, costs and attorney’s fees, with enhanced damages only for proven intentional misconduct (§2210.576).

How long do I have to file a hurricane claim in Texas?

For TWIA, one year from the date of damage (§2210.573). For private insurers, check your policy’s notice condition — often “prompt notice” or a fixed period — and file as soon as you can.
