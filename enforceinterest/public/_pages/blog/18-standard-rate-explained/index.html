The 18% standard rate, explained

How the 18% prompt-payment interest rate in Texas Insurance Code §542.060(a) works: when it applies, when it starts, how to calculate it, partial payments, supplements and attorney's fees.

Interest ratesPublished April 30, 2026Updated November 4, 20264 min read

Most policyholders focus on getting a claim approved. Texas law also cares about when it is paid. If a liable insurer misses the payment deadlines in Chapter 542, §542.060(a) adds interest at 18% a year on the late amount, plus reasonable attorney’s fees. Here is exactly how that works.

In short

  • Where: Texas Insurance Code §542.060(a), part of the Prompt Payment of Claims Act.
  • Rate: 18% a year, simple interest, as damages — plus reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees.
  • Applies to: non-weather claims, and weather claims made before September 1, 2017.
  • Runs from: the date payment was required until the amount is paid.

What the 18% standard rate is

Section 542.060(a) reads, in part: if an insurer “that is liable for a claim under an insurance policy is not in compliance with this subchapter, the insurer is liable to pay the holder of the policy … in addition to the amount of the claim, interest on the amount of the claim at the rate of 18 percent a year as damages, together with reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees.”

Three details matter:

  • It is “as damages.” The 18% is a statutory consequence of non-compliance, not a contractual interest term.
  • It requires liability. The insurer has to be liable for the claim. A claim found invalid in arbitration or litigation doesn’t trigger it (§542.058(b)).
  • It is in addition to the claim. The policy benefits are paid in full; the interest comes on top.

When does 18% apply instead of the floating rate?

Since September 1, 2017, Texas has had two rates. The floating rate in §542.060(c) — the §304.003 judgment rate plus 5%, currently 12.00% — applies in actions governed by Chapter 542A: claims for damage caused wholly or partly by forces of nature, made on or after September 1, 2017. Everything else stays at 18%:

When the 18% starts: it isn’t always “day 61”

You will often read that interest “starts on day 61.” That is a useful shorthand for §542.058, but the precise rule is that interest runs from the date the claim was required to be paid, which depends on which deadline was missed:

  • §542.058: more than 60 days after the insurer received all the items, statements and forms it reasonably requested — not 60 days after you reported the claim.
  • §542.057: 5 business days after the insurer notified you it would pay all or part of the claim. That can be much earlier than 60 days.
  • Catastrophes: in a commissioner-declared weather catastrophe the claim-handling deadlines extend by 15 days (§542.059(b)).

Partial payments don’t stop the clock

A partial payment reduces the balance that interest runs on — from the date it is paid. It doesn’t end the interest on what is still owed. The Texas Supreme Court confirmed in Hinojos v. State Farm Lloyds, 619 S.W.3d 651 (Tex. 2021), that a reasonable payment made within the deadline doesn’t shield the insurer from liability on amounts it owed but hadn’t paid when the deadline passed. The statutory interest calculator handles any number of partial payments.

Supplements, appraisal and the 18% rate

When the claim grows — through a supplement for hidden damage, or an appraisal award higher than what was paid — the additional money is still money the insurer was liable for. If it was paid after its deadline, the 18% can apply to it. Paying an appraisal award doesn’t, by itself, end prompt-payment liability (Barbara Technologies Corp. v. State Farm Lloyds, 589 S.W.3d 806 (Tex. 2019)). See post-appraisal interest and supplements.

Attorney’s fees

§542.060(a) also makes the insurer liable for reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees, taxed as costs if suit is filed (§542.060(b)). That fee exposure is a major reason carriers resolve documented prompt-payment issues. (Weather claims under Chapter 542A have their own fee formula in §542A.007.)

Is the 18% automatic?

The liability arises by statute once the conditions are met — but carriers rarely calculate or pay it unprompted. In practice it is raised with a documented timeline and a calculation, and if necessary in a lawsuit. That documentation is what Enforce Interest prepares.

Protecting your right to the 18%

  1. Give notice in writing and keep proof of the date.
  2. Answer every request in writing, and note the date the insurer received your response.
  3. Keep every payment letter and check stub.
  4. Ask in writing what else the insurer needs.
  5. Don’t assume a check is the final payment — compare it to your estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 18% compounded?

No. It is simple interest on the late amount.

Does the 18% apply to hail claims?

Only to hail claims made before September 1, 2017. Later hail claims use the floating §542.060(c) rate, currently 12.00%.

Does partial payment stop the 18%?

No. It reduces the balance interest runs on from the date of payment.

Do I need an attorney to recover it?

Not always; many carriers resolve it once it is documented. If a suit is needed, that is a job for a Texas attorney. Enforce Interest is not a law firm.

Are attorney’s fees included?

§542.060 adds reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees on top of the interest.
