Smoke damage claims in Texas: investigations, scope and payment

How smoke damage insurance claims work in Texas: what a smoke damage investigation is, why scope gets disputed, wildfire smoke, contents and HVAC, and the deadlines and interest when payment is late.

Claim typesPublished November 2, 2026Updated November 4, 20263 min read

A smoke claim is one of the hardest property claims to see and one of the easiest to under-scope. Soot and odor travel far beyond the flames — through returns and ducts, into attics and closed cabinets. Here is how smoke damage claims are investigated, where they get disputed, and what Texas law says about paying them on time.

What counts as a smoke claim

  • Fire in the home — the burned area plus smoke and soot elsewhere in the structure.
  • Puff-back from a furnace or water heater that spreads soot through the house.
  • Neighboring fire — an apartment, adjoining unit or nearby building.
  • Wildfire smoke — residue and odor without flames ever reaching the property.

Most homeowners policies cover smoke as part of the fire peril (often excluding smoke from agricultural smudging or industrial operations). Check your form’s exact wording.

What a smoke damage investigation is

Because smoke residue is often invisible, the scope of a smoke claim should be based on testing rather than on what looks dirty. A smoke damage investigation typically includes:

  • Surface sampling (tape lifts and wipe samples) analyzed by a lab for soot, char and ash particles.
  • Mapping — samples from rooms near and far from the fire, the attic, wall cavities and HVAC components, to show how far residue traveled.
  • HVAC inspection of the air handler, coils and ducts.
  • Odor assessment and a contents evaluation: what can be cleaned and deodorized, and what can’t.
  • A written protocol describing the cleaning, sealing or replacement each area requires.

Investigations are usually performed by industrial hygienists or environmental consultants. Some insurers hire their own; policyholders can too. Either way, the report becomes central evidence.

Why smoke claims get disputed

  • Room-by-room scoping. The carrier pays to clean the fire room and the adjoining hallway; residue in the rest of the house is called “not affected.”
  • Clean versus replace. Porous materials — insulation, upholstery, carpet pad, clothing — often can’t be fully deodorized.
  • HVAC. Ducts that circulated smoke may need cleaning or replacement.
  • Wildfire smoke causation. Without visible damage, carriers question whether residue is from the fire at all, which is exactly what lab analysis answers.

Deadlines and interest on smoke claims

The Chapter 542 deadlines apply to smoke claims like any other: 15 days to acknowledge and request items, 15 business days to decide after receiving them (30 days if arson is reasonably suspected), 5 business days to pay after accepting, and no more than 60 days after receiving the items reasonably requested.

Two practical points:

  1. The investigation report is often the last “item”. Send it with a dated cover letter and ask the adjuster in writing whether anything else is needed. The date the insurer receives the last reasonably requested item starts the §542.058 clock.
  2. Know the rate. A house fire or puff-back is a non-weather loss: 18% a year if paid late. Wildfire smoke is a forces-of-nature loss under Chapter 542A: the floating rate, currently 12.00%, for claims made on or after September 1, 2017.

On a $60,000 smoke supplement paid 100 days late: $2,958.90 at 18%; $1,972.60 at 12.00%.

Documenting a smoke claim

  1. Photograph and video every room, closet and the attic before cleaning starts.
  2. Keep a contents list with photos; set aside items that still smell after cleaning.
  3. Ask for the insurer’s testing results if it performs any, and get your own if scope is disputed.
  4. Keep every ALE receipt while the home is uninhabitable.
  5. Log requests and responses in the claim timeline builder.

Frequently asked questions

What is a smoke damage investigation?

A professional assessment, usually by an industrial hygienist, that samples surfaces and air throughout the property to identify and map soot, char and ash, so the repair scope is based on evidence.

Is wildfire smoke damage covered in Texas?

Often, as part of the fire or smoke peril, depending on the policy wording. Wildfire is a forces-of-nature cause, so Chapter 542A’s notice rules and the floating interest rate apply to those claims.

How long does an insurer have to pay a smoke claim?

The standard Chapter 542 deadlines apply: 5 business days after acceptance, and no more than 60 days after it receives the items it reasonably requested.
