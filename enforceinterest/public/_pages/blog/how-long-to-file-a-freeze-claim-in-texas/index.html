How long do I have to file a freeze claim in Texas?

Texas freeze and burst-pipe claims: how long you have to report the claim and to sue, freeze exclusions, catastrophe extensions, which interest rate applies and how to document the loss.

DeadlinesPublished October 27, 2026Updated November 4, 20263 min read

There is no single Texas statute that says “you have X days to file a freeze claim.” The answer comes from your policy, and the policy usually says “promptly.” Here is what that means in practice, the other deadlines that matter, and how freeze claims are paid.

Short answer

  • Report it as soon as you discover the damage — most policies require prompt notice.
  • Some policies set a fixed reporting window; check the “Duties After Loss” section.
  • The deadline to sue is separate — commonly two years and one day after the claim accrues, and never less than two years (§16.070).

Reporting deadline: what “prompt notice” means

Homeowners policies typically require you to give the insurer “prompt notice” of a loss. That isn’t a fixed number of days; it means reasonably soon given the circumstances. After a widespread freeze like February 2021, a delay of days or a few weeks while repairs are arranged is common. Months of unexplained delay invite a late-notice defense, and late notice can make it hard to prove what the freeze caused. Report in writing, even if you can’t get an adjuster out yet.

Some policies now include a fixed outer limit for reporting certain claims. If yours does, that date controls.

Deadline to sue

Most policies limit the time to sue to two years and one day after the cause of action accrues; Texas won’t enforce anything shorter than two years (Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §16.070). Claims under Insurance Code Chapter 541 have a two-year limit (§541.162). Accrual is often tied to the insurer’s denial or underpayment, but get legal advice if you’re anywhere near two years from the freeze.

Is freeze damage covered?

Usually, yes — a pipe that bursts in a freeze is the classic “sudden and accidental discharge” of water. Watch for these limits:

  • Freezing exclusion for unheated or vacant homes. Many policies exclude freeze damage to plumbing unless you used reasonable care to maintain heat, or shut off the water and drained the system. During a statewide power outage, “reasonable care” is judged against what was possible.
  • The pipe itself. Some policies pay for the water damage but not the cost of repairing the pipe or appliance that froze.
  • Mold sublimits if water sat before mitigation.
  • Flood policies don’t cover burst pipes, and TWIA covers wind and hail only.

How fast must the insurer pay a freeze claim?

Chapter 542 applies: acknowledge within 15 days, decide within 15 business days of receiving what it requested, pay within 5 business days of accepting, and no later than 60 days after receiving requested items. If the commissioner declared the freeze a weather-related catastrophe, each claim-handling deadline gets 15 more days (§542.059(b)).

Which interest rate applies to a late freeze claim?

This is genuinely unsettled. The floating rate in §542.060(c) applies to claims under Chapter 542A, which covers damage “caused, wholly or partly, by forces of nature,” listing examples including a snowstorm but not a freeze. An insurer may argue a winter storm loss is a forces-of-nature claim (floating rate, currently 12.00%, plus the 61-day pre-suit notice); a policyholder may argue a burst pipe is a plumbing loss (18%). The answer can depend on the facts and the pleadings. When we calculate freeze-claim interest, we show both.

On a $35,000 freeze claim paid 150 days late: $2,589.04 at 18%, or $1,726.03 at 12.00%.

Documenting a freeze loss

  1. Photograph the burst pipe and water damage before repairs; keep the failed section of pipe.
  2. Note the outside temperatures and any power outage dates (utility notices are good evidence).
  3. Keep plumber and mitigation invoices; plumbers’ notes on the cause are valuable.
  4. Report the claim in writing and log every request and response in the timeline builder.
  5. Keep receipts for hotels and extra costs if the home was unlivable.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to file a freeze claim in Texas?

Your policy controls — usually “prompt notice,” meaning as soon as reasonably possible after you discover the damage, and some policies set a fixed outer limit. Report in writing right away.

Can I still file a claim for the February 2021 freeze?

Reporting that late will face a late-notice defense, and the time to sue under most policies has likely run. Talk to a Texas attorney about your specific situation.

Does TWIA cover freeze damage?

No. TWIA policies cover wind and hail. Freeze damage is a homeowners-policy claim.
