Stacy Adams Spring 2018 and Lifestyle-Led Footwear Photography

By the time of a Stacy Adams spring 2018 style season, menswear footwear imagery leaned toward lifestyle and social. Here is how that kind of shoot is built.

Footwear & Product3 min readFrame 12A

A shift toward lifestyle

Thinking about a Stacy Adams spring 2018 style shoot is a useful way to look at how men's footwear photography changed in that era. Product on white still mattered for retail, but more of the imagery was lifestyle-led: shoes shown on real streets, with outfits and moments around them.

Social platforms drove much of that. A single shoot had to deliver campaign heroes, retail images and a steady supply of vertical cut-downs, stories and short clips.

For photographers, that meant more variety per day: more locations, more looks, more formats. It also meant thinking about how a single frame would be cropped, captioned and posted, not just how it would look in print.

Planning for every format

Plan the deliverables first. List the formats: horizontal web banners, vertical stories, square posts, product on white and short video. Then design each setup to serve several of them at once.

Shoot wider than the final crop so vertical and square versions can be pulled from the same frame. Leave space above the model for type. For each look, add a five- to ten-second clip of walking or lacing up, which can run as a story with little editing.

Video adds different needs. Use a gimbal or tripod for smooth clips, keep shutter speed near double the frame rate for natural motion and record clean ambient sound. Even short clips benefit from a stable shot and a simple beginning and end. Shoot a few frames with the model framed in the center third, as vertical stories crop heavily on both sides.

Plan one shoot for six formats and you stop cropping the shoe out of half the pictures.

Lifestyle product frames

Lifestyle product imagery puts the shoe in a believable scene. A pair of dress shoes beside a leather bag on a café table in the Arts District, a loafer stepping onto a Metro platform at Union Station, or shoes lined up by a door all suggest a life around the product. The scene should hint at a story without needing a caption to explain it.

Keep the shoe the brightest and sharpest thing in the frame. A reflector or a small LED panel lifts the leather slightly above the scene, and a shallow depth of field at f/2.8 to f/4 softens busy backgrounds.

Props should be believable and brand-neutral. Avoid visible logos from other brands on cups, bags or watches, and keep the props consistent with the customer the footwear is aimed at.

Shooting the spring line

A Stacy Adams spring 2018 inspired line of dress shoes, loafers and two-tones photographs well in LA's spring light. Morning sessions on Broadway's historic blocks give architecture and soft light; afternoons in Venice or along the canals add a relaxed mood. Late light along the canals is warm and low, which suits loafers and suede but needs care to keep leather colors accurate.

Mix models and ages if the brief allows. Classic dress footwear has a broad audience, and lifestyle imagery reads as more honest when the people in it look like real customers.

Build in time for the unexpected. Lifestyle shoots in public places attract onlookers, traffic and noise, so allow extra minutes per setup and have a quieter backup location nearby, such as a side street or courtyard.

Social cut-downs and delivery

Edit with platforms in mind. Pick frames that work as small thumbnails, with one clear subject and strong contrast. Build sets of three to five images that tell a short story: the outfit, the step, the detail. Captions should be short and descriptive, naming the style and color rather than relying on adjectives.

Deliver a master library organized by style and format, so brand teams can find the right crop quickly through the season.

Keep track of usage too. Lifestyle images featuring people need model releases that cover social and paid use, and location releases where required. Organized paperwork makes it easier for brands to reuse imagery across a full season.

Shot list

  1. Wide frames with room for vertical and square crops
  2. Five- to ten-second clip of each look for stories
  3. Lifestyle still life: shoes with bag, table or doorway
  4. On-foot walking frame on a city street
  5. Clean product on white for retail
  6. Detail crops that read as small thumbnails
