Nike Running Photography: Shooting Runners and Shoes in Los Angeles

Nike running photography-style work is made at dawn, on real routes, with real runners. A practical guide to crews, trails, motion and getting the shoe sharp mid-stride.

Sport3 min readFrame 43A

What the genre asks for

Nike running photography, and the wider style of athletic running imagery it shaped, has a recognizable look: real runners, real routes, early light and a shoe that reads clearly even when it is moving. The pictures sell effort and community as much as product, so authenticity is the brief.

That means casting actual runners, not models who jog. Run crews meet before work in most neighborhoods of Los Angeles, from Silver Lake to Downtown, and their members know how to run in a pack, hold a pace and look natural doing it. Their form is the thing a stylist cannot fake.

Plan for three kinds of image in one session: the crew, the individual runner and the shoe. Each needs a different lens, a different shutter speed and a different place to stand.

Locations: river paths and park trails

The LA River bike path through Elysian Valley is a favorite for urban running work. Concrete channel, bridges overhead, herons in the water and a flat path that lets a crew run in formation. Early morning light comes low across the channel and catches runners from the side.

Griffith Park gives you the opposite: dirt fire roads, switchbacks and views back toward downtown. The trails up toward the observatory catch first light early. Get there before sunrise and you will have the hill mostly to yourself for twenty minutes.

The Silver Lake Reservoir loop, the bluffs in Santa Monica and the Venice boardwalk round out the options. Each one says something different about running in LA. Pick the one that fits the story rather than the one that photographs easiest. Check permit rules too: commercial shoots in city parks and on the river path need a FilmLA permit.

A stylist can dress a runner, but nobody can fake the way a real one lands.

Freezing and showing motion

For the crew, shoot at 1/1000s with a 70-200mm from the front at a slight angle, so the pack stacks up and stays sharp. Running toward the lens means continuous autofocus has to work hard; use a tracking area mode and let the runners come to you repeatedly. Most shoots run the same 50-meter stretch a dozen times.

To show speed, pan. At 1/30s to 1/60s, track a single runner side-on against a busy background like a bridge or a graffiti wall. The runner holds, the background streaks. It takes many tries, so warn your runners they will be doing a lot of repeats.

A wide lens low to the ground, 20mm or 24mm, with the camera almost on the path, puts the viewer in the stride. Pre-focus on a spot and fire as the foot lands. This is the frame that makes a shoe look fast.

The shoe in motion

Product-in-motion shots are where running photography gets technical. The shoe has to be sharp, the logo legible and the foot caught at a moment that shows the sole, the cushion or the upper. Footstrike, toe-off and midair are the three classic moments.

A high frame rate helps, but timing matters more. At 1/2000s or faster, with a fast lens stopped to f/4 for some depth, set focus on a mark on the ground and have the runner land on it. Repeat until the shoe is sharp and the gesture is right.

Off-camera flash can freeze a foot completely, but on location at dawn it often looks staged. Many shooters stick with available light and faster ISO, then use a small strobe only for the tight product frames. That balance keeps Nike running photography-style work grounded in real mornings.

Working with runners on set

Runners are athletes, not props. Warm them up properly, keep repeats short and give them rest between takes. A tired runner's form breaks down, and it shows in the shoulders and the footstrike long before they say anything.

Bring water, snacks and dry layers for after. Dawn shoots in LA can be cold until the sun clears the hills, and a crew that feels looked after will give you one more good run when the light is perfect.

Shot list

  1. Crew running toward the lens on the LA River path at sunrise
  2. Single runner panned at 1/30s against a bridge or mural
  3. Low wide angle at footstrike, pre-focused on a ground mark
  4. Shoe at toe-off frozen at 1/2000s or faster
  5. Trail runner on a Griffith Park switchback with downtown behind
  6. Post-run moments: stretching, coffee, laces untied
