Team-Color Cans and Tailgates: The Bud Light My Team Can Brief

Bud Light ran team-specific cans, and the Bud Light My Team Can idea points to a genre: beverage campaigns built on fandom, team colors and the noise of a tailgate.

Campaign & Brand3 min readFrame 24A

Fandom as the subject

Bud Light ran team-specific cans, and the Bud Light My Team Can concept stands for a wider kind of brief: a beverage campaign where the product carries a team's colors and the story is about the fans. The photography is less about the drink than about belonging, the shared ritual of game day.

That shifts the creative priorities. The images need people who look like real fans, settings that feel like real game days, and a can that is instantly recognizable in team colors, often with many team variants to cover. Each version has to feel local and specific to one fan base while fitting into a consistent national campaign.

Regional variants add logistics. If the campaign covers many teams, build a modular set where the cans, wardrobe and props change while the lighting, camera positions and core scene stay fixed. That lets a crew shoot many versions in a day with a consistent look, and it keeps the post-production pipeline simple when every variant needs the same retouching and grade.

Team colors on cans and in frames

Team-color cans are printed metal, which means reflections, curved highlights and color shifts depending on the light. For product shots, use a tall strip softbox on each side to draw clean vertical highlights down the can, a black card behind to define edges, and a spray of glycerin and water for condensation that lasts through a long session.

Color accuracy matters to fans. A team's red or navy rendered slightly off will be noticed immediately. Shoot a color checker for every setup, match against printed samples of the cans, and carry the same grade across every team variant so none looks favored.

In lifestyle frames, coordinate wardrobe, face paint, flags and props with the can's colors, but keep logos and trademarks of leagues and teams cleared by the client. Licensing in sports advertising is strict, and unapproved jerseys or stadium signage in the frame can make an image unusable. Generic team-color clothing without logos is often the safest route for wide lifestyle frames.

Fan photography is not about the drink. It is about the second right after the big play.

Tailgate and watch party scenes

Tailgates are the natural setting: open truck beds, folding chairs, grills, coolers, pop-up canopies in team colors. Stadium lots usually require permission from the venue, so many productions build a tailgate in a private lot, a park with a permit or a studio backlot. Watch parties in a living room or a backyard give a second, more intimate set.

Light for warmth and energy. Late-afternoon sun gives a golden, game-day feeling; backlight through smoke from a grill adds atmosphere. For evening scenes, mix string lights, a TV glow and a gelled strobe bounced off the ceiling. Shoot wide with a 24mm to capture the crowd, and switch to an 85mm for reactions.

Reactions are the heart of fan imagery. Play a recording of a big game moment or have a stand-in referee call fake plays, and photograph the group's response in bursts. Real cheering, groaning and hugging beat any posed toast.

Casting and alcohol advertising rules

Cast fans of different ages, backgrounds and body types, and book groups who know each other. Beer advertising in the United States follows industry self-regulation codes that set a minimum age for models, commonly 25 and older, and discourage showing excessive drinking or drinking connected to driving or sports participation. Confirm the client's rules before casting.

Practically, that means no one holds a can while playing catch, no keys or cars in frame with an open can, and no frames that suggest heavy consumption. Many campaigns keep cans in hands at rest, on tables and in coolers, which also makes the product easier to read.

Bud Light My Team Can-style work is ultimately about pride in a place and a team. If the fans in the frame look genuinely happy to be there, the can in their hand will carry the rest.
