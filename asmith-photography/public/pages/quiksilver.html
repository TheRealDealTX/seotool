Quiksilver Photography and Southern California Surf Lifestyle

Quiksilver photography stands for a whole category: surf and skate lifestyle imagery made on SoCal beaches and streets. Here is how that work is approached.

Campaign & Brand3 min readFrame 07A

The surf lifestyle brief

Quiksilver photography, in the broad sense, means the sun-bleached, active look of surf and skate lifestyle brands. Quiksilver is a surf brand with deep Southern California ties, and labels like it have built their identity on pictures of people who actually surf and skate, wearing the clothes in the places they belong.

The brief usually blends three things: action that proves credibility, lifestyle that sells a feeling, and product that shows fabric, prints and fit. A good shoot moves between those modes without the seams showing.

That also means choosing the right locations. Huntington Beach, Malibu and the South Bay each have a different character, from pier crowds to rocky points to wide sand. In Los Angeles, Venice and El Porto are within easy reach and offer both surf and skate culture.

Working with beach light

Coastal light in Southern California changes fast. Morning marine layer gives a soft, even light that flatters skin and keeps colors true. By midday the sun is overhead and harsh, with reflective sand acting as a giant fill card. Golden hour turns everything warm, which suits lifestyle but can shift product colors.

Plan the day around it. Shoot product-critical looks in the soft morning, action in the clearer late morning, and mood frames at sunset. A polarizer deepens sky and water and cuts glare off wet skin and boardshorts.

Bring sand bags and a clamp for any light stands, and keep electronics in dry bags. Salt spray and fine sand get into everything, so wipe lenses often with a microfiber cloth and avoid changing lenses in the wind.

Surf lifestyle work only lands when the people in it would be at the beach whether or not the camera showed up.

In and near the water

Surf action from the beach usually means a 400mm or longer lens on a monopod, shooting at 1/2000s to freeze spray. From the water, a camera in a housing with a wide lens puts the viewer in the lineup, but it needs a photographer who is a confident swimmer and knows the break.

Shoreline frames are a reliable middle ground: walking out with a board, waxing up, rinsing off at the showers. They carry the product and the culture without needing perfect surf.

Safety comes first in the water. Know the tides, check the swell, and have someone on shore watching. A photographer drifting into the impact zone with a housing is a danger to surfers as well as to themselves.

The skate side

Surf brands have long overlapped with skating, and a lifestyle campaign often includes a street or park session. Venice Skatepark is the obvious choice for a beach-meets-concrete look, while Downtown and the Arts District give a city contrast.

Treat the skate part with the same respect as the surf. Cast people who skate, shoot real tricks with a fisheye and flash, and keep the clothing visible in at least one clean frame per look. Quiksilver photography at its best never looks like models borrowing a board.

Board and wheel choice tells a story too. A cruiser with soft wheels on a beach path reads as lifestyle, while a street deck with hard wheels at a ledge reads as skating. Match the setup to the mood of each look so the campaign stays coherent.

Casting, styling and delivery

Casting is the backbone of surf lifestyle work. Real surfers and skaters bring posture, tan lines and confidence that are hard to fake. Keep styling loose: layers people would actually wear on a cold morning session, wet hair, sandy feet.

Deliver selects grouped by look, with action, lifestyle and product versions of each. Keep a consistent grade across the set so the campaign reads as one story from catalog to social.

Plan wardrobe changes around the light. Swimwear and boardshorts need warm sun and wet skin; jackets and fleece look natural in early fog. Grouping looks by weather saves time on set and keeps the sequence of images feeling like a real day at the coast.

  4. Skate session at Venice with fisheye and off-camera flash
  5. Sunset group frame for campaign mood
  6. Flat lays of prints and fabrics on sand or boards
