Shooting Comfort Footwear the Nunn Bush Way: Everyday and Easy

Casual men's shoes in the Nunn Bush vein are bought for comfort, not flash. The photography should feel like a good Saturday: relaxed people, real places, nothing staged too hard.

Footwear & Product3 min readFrame 15A

Selling comfort in a still frame

Comfort footwear of the kind Nunn Bush is known for, casual oxfords, slip-ons and dressy-casual sneakers for men, has a different buyer than a fashion label. He wants to know the shoe will work at a backyard barbecue, a daughter's soccer game and a casual Friday. The pictures have to answer that question without a word of copy.

Comfort is hard to photograph directly, so you show it through behavior. A man standing with his weight easy on one leg, crouching to tie a kid's lace, walking a dog on a long lead or leaning on a porch rail is wearing comfortable shoes. Stiff poses undo that message instantly.

Casting and directing real-feeling people

Cast for the actual customer rather than runway proportions. Men in their thirties to sixties with lived-in faces, a range of builds, and the ease that comes from not being professional models. Lifestyle agencies in Los Angeles carry plenty of this talent, and real couples or friends booked together bring natural chemistry.

Direct with tasks, not poses. Ask the model to carry groceries from the car, read the paper on a bench, or hunt for keys in a jacket. While he does it, shoot continuously with a 35mm or 50mm lens and wait for the moment between actions, when the body relaxes. That in-between frame is usually the one.

Keep wardrobe simple: chinos, dark denim, an oxford shirt, a quarter-zip, a lightweight jacket. Muted, believable clothing keeps attention on brown and tan leather and avoids dating the images within a season.

Comfort never shows up in a pose. It shows up in the moment between two ordinary things.

Everyday settings around Los Angeles

The best locations look like places a customer already goes. Suburban streets in Pasadena or Glendale, a farmers market, a hardware store aisle, the walking paths at Griffith Park, a coffee counter in Westwood or a driveway with a well-kept car all say ordinary life without looking cheap.

Shoot in open shade or early morning light. Comfort footwear photography benefits from soft, flattering light that keeps faces friendly and leather tones warm. A white bounce card or a 5-in-1 reflector is often all the lighting you need, and it keeps a small crew moving quickly between scenes.

Planning a seasonal casual set

A spring casual range usually means lighter suedes, canvas details, perforated uppers and softer colors like stone, tan and navy. Plan scenes around those tones: a pale stucco wall or a weathered fence flatters stone suede far more than a busy mural, and a navy shoe pops against warm wood decking.

Build the day around four or five scenes rather than a dozen. Each scene should carry two or three styles, a wide establishing frame, a medium frame with the model's full body and one low frame where the shoe leads. Moving the whole crew less often leaves more time to wait for the relaxed, unposed moments that make this kind of work believable, and it keeps talent fresh through the afternoon.

Product clarity inside lifestyle frames

Every lifestyle frame should still show the shoe well enough to recognize it on a product page. Keep at least one frame per scene where the shoe is in focus, unobstructed and lit from the front or side. Crouch to shin height for these, and avoid grass or gravel that hides the outsole.

Pair each lifestyle set with a simple on-foot series on a neutral backdrop: standing, heel lift, side step. A Nunn Bush-style comfort range often has small features such as cushioned footbeds, padded collars or flexible soles that are easier to show in a clean close-up, with the foot slightly flexed, than in a wide scene.

In post, keep the grade gentle and honest. Avoid crushed blacks and heavy teal shadows; this customer is looking for a reliable shoe, and the images should look as trustworthy as the product.
