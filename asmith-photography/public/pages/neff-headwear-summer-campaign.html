Neff Headwear Style: Shooting a Summer Headwear Campaign

Youth brands like Neff headwear built their look on color, humor and action-sports culture. Here is how a summer headwear campaign in that spirit comes together.

Campaign & Brand3 min readFrame 08A

The headwear brief

Neff headwear is known for beanies and caps rooted in snow and skate culture, often with bold color and a sense of humor. A summer campaign in that spirit has a specific challenge: making headwear the star when it is a small object sitting on top of a whole person.

The answer is a mix of framing and personality. Wide shots establish the mood, medium shots carry the outfit, and tight frames make the cap or beanie legible. Every look needs at least one frame where the logo and shape read clearly.

Small details help. Check that brims are shaped the way the brand intends, that tags are removed or hidden, and that beanies are folded consistently. In tight frames, lint and a crooked logo stand out immediately, so keep a lint roller and mirror close.

Color and summer light

Bright headwear and summer light are a strong combination, but hard midday sun throws dark shadows under brims. Fill those with a reflector or a small strobe, or shoot in open shade and add a hint of flash for punch.

Plan the palette with the location. A saturated cap pops against a pale wall in Venice or the clean blue of a pool, while muted colorways sit well against the brick and murals of the Arts District. A polarizer deepens sky and helps colors hold.

Do not ignore the beanie in summer. Many youth headwear lines sell beanies year round, and showing them in warm light, worn loose with a T-shirt or by a pool, is part of the brand’s playful tone.

Headwear is the smallest thing in the frame and the reason the picture exists, so every look needs one shot where it wins.

Casting and styling

Youth headwear campaigns work best with people who already live the culture: skaters, snowboarders in their off season, BMX riders, musicians. Their ease with the product shows. Give them room to be playful; group shots with genuine laughter are often the frames that end up used.

Styling should support, not compete. Keep tops simple when the headwear is busy, and think about hair: caps sit differently on long hair, short hair and under hoods. Bring duplicates of hero pieces in case one gets crushed or soaked.

Think about the group dynamic. Three or four friends bring more energy than one model, and caps look more natural when people are joking around, sharing food or watching someone skate. Keep the camera up during breaks; that is when real moments happen.

Action and lifestyle

Action frames keep the campaign credible. A skate session at a park or a curb in Silver Lake, shot with a fisheye and a single off-camera flash, gives energy. Make sure caps stay on; a backward cap or a tight beanie usually survives a trick better than a loose snapback.

Lifestyle moments fill the rest: a group on a rooftop in Downtown, a drive to the beach, a taco stop. These show how the product fits into a day, which is what a summer campaign is selling.

Shoot some of the lifestyle on a longer lens from a distance. People relax when the camera is across the street, and an 85mm or 135mm at f/2 isolates a bright cap against a soft background of city or beach.

Product and delivery

Shoot clean product frames alongside the lifestyle: flat lays on concrete or grip tape, caps stacked on a board, beanies hanging from a fence. Natural light and a reflector are enough for most of it.

Deliver a set with consistent color and grade, organized by product, plus vertical crops for social. For Neff headwear style campaigns, short, playful sequences and behind-the-scenes frames often do as well online as the heroes.

Keep logo legibility in mind for social crops. A cap brim pointed straight at the lens hides the front panel; a slight turn of the head shows the logo and the shape. Shoot a few safe frames for the product team before experimenting with angles.

Kit list

  1. Reflector or small strobe to fill shadows under brims
  2. 35mm and 85mm primes for lifestyle and tight headwear frames
  3. Fisheye and speedlight for skate action
  4. Duplicate hero pieces in case of damage
  5. Lint roller and steamer for beanies and fabric caps
  6. Polarizer for deep summer skies and saturated color
