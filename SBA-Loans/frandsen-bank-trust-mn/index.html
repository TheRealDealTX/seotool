Frandsen Bank & Trust SBA loans (MN)

Frandsen Bank & Trust is a privately owned community bank headquartered in Lonsdale, Minnesota, with offices across Minnesota, Wisconsin and North Dakota.

Frandsen Bank & Trust is headquartered in Lonsdale, Minnesota, a small city in Rice County south of the Twin Cities. It is part of Frandsen Financial Corporation, a family of businesses built by Minnesota entrepreneur Dennis Frandsen. Unlike many banks its size, it is privately held, and it has grown largely by acquiring small-town banks and keeping them local.

The bank operates a large network of community offices, most of them in rural and small-city Minnesota, with additional locations in western Wisconsin and North Dakota. Many of those towns have few other lending options. Frandsen offers agricultural, commercial, real estate and consumer lending, plus trust and insurance services through affiliates.

Its business clients are typical of small-town Main Street: farm operations and ag suppliers, auto and equipment dealers, contractors, clinics, restaurants and family-owned shops. For many of them, Frandsen is the bank that knows the family and the local market. SBA loans give it room to help with deals that need more time or less down payment.

How business owners use SBA loans here

A rural Minnesota business owner might use an SBA 7(a) loan from Frandsen to buy a retiring owner's business, expand a building, or finance equipment when local collateral values are thin. The SBA guarantee lets a community bank stretch on a deal it believes in. Ask whether SBA loans are handled at your local office or by a central team in Lonsdale, whether it is an SBA Preferred Lender, and whether it works with a Minnesota CDC on 504 loans.

SBA programs to ask Frandsen Bank & Trust about

  • 7(a). The SBA's main loan, up to $5 million, for working capital, equipment, buying a business, refinancing and real estate.
  • 504. Long-term fixed-rate financing for owner-occupied real estate and heavy equipment. The bank funds about 50%, a CDC up to 40%, and you put in about 10%.
  • SBA Express. A 7(a) loan up to $500,000 that the lender approves under its own authority, so it closes faster. The rate cap is the same as standard 7(a).

Program availability changes. Ask the lender which SBA programs it closes most often and whether it has SBA Preferred Lender status, which lets it approve loans in-house.

What rate will Frandsen charge on an SBA loan?
