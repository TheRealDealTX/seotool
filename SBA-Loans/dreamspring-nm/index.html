DreamSpring SBA loans (NM)

DreamSpring is a nonprofit CDFI in Albuquerque, formerly part of the Accion network, that makes SBA microloans and other small-business loans to entrepreneurs banks often turn away.

DreamSpring is a nonprofit community development financial institution headquartered in Albuquerque, New Mexico. For decades it was known as Accion, first as Accion New Mexico and later under names that listed the growing set of states it served, such as Arizona, Colorado, Texas and Nevada. It adopted the DreamSpring name in 2020.

The organization lends to small-business owners who have trouble getting bank credit: start-ups, sole proprietors, owners with limited credit history or collateral, and entrepreneurs in low-income, rural and Native American communities. Typical borrowers include food businesses, retailers, trades, transportation and personal-service businesses. It also provides coaching and other business support.

DreamSpring has been an SBA Microloan intermediary, which means it borrows from SBA and relends to small businesses in amounts up to $50,000. It has also used SBA's mission-lender programs to make larger guaranteed loans. Its service area has expanded well beyond New Mexico. Today it lends in a number of states, and it works with partners to reach owners in underserved markets.

How business owners use SBA loans here

DreamSpring fits a borrower who is too small, too new or too thin on credit for a bank. An SBA microloan up to $50,000 can cover equipment, inventory, a food truck build-out or working capital, and often comes with business coaching. Rates on microloans are typically higher than bank SBA loans because the loans are small and riskier, but they can build the track record a business needs to qualify for a bank 7(a) loan later. Ask about any technical assistance requirements and how fast funds can close.

SBA programs to ask DreamSpring about

  • Microloan. Loans up to $50,000 through nonprofit intermediaries, usually with free coaching. Good for start-ups and very small businesses.
  • Community Advantage. Mission-based 7(a) lending for borrowers that banks often turn down: newer businesses, thin credit files and underserved communities.
  • 7(a). The SBA's main loan, up to $5 million, for working capital, equipment, buying a business, refinancing and real estate.

Program availability changes. Ask the lender which SBA programs it closes most often and whether it has SBA Preferred Lender status, which lets it approve loans in-house.

Questions

Is DreamSpring the same as Accion New Mexico?

Yes. DreamSpring is the current name of the Albuquerque-based nonprofit lender long known as Accion New Mexico and later Accion Serving New Mexico, Arizona, Colorado, Texas and Nevada. It renamed itself DreamSpring in 2020 and continues to make small-business loans, including SBA microloans.

How much can I borrow from DreamSpring through the SBA Microloan program?

SBA microloans go up to $50,000, and many are much smaller. They can fund equipment, inventory, supplies and working capital, but not real estate purchases. DreamSpring sets its own rates and terms within SBA rules. Microloan terms run up to seven years.

Can a start-up with bad credit get a loan from DreamSpring?

DreamSpring was built to serve owners that banks often decline, including start-ups and people with limited or damaged credit. It still looks for the ability to repay, a realistic plan and some commitment from the owner. Expect a conversation about your story and cash flow, not only a credit score.

What rate will I get on an SBA loan from DreamSpring?

DreamSpring sets your rate at or below the SBA maximum. With prime at 7.00%, variable 7(a) caps run from 10.00% on loans over $350,000 to 13.50% on loans of $50,000 or less. Your rate depends on loan size, term, collateral, credit and cash flow.
