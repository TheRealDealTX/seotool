John Marshall Bank SBA loans (VA)

John Marshall Bank is a Reston, Virginia business bank that lends to companies and professionals across the Washington, D.C. region.

John Marshall Bank is a commercial bank headquartered in Reston, Virginia. It is the banking subsidiary of John Marshall Bancorp, which trades on Nasdaq. The bank has built its business around relationship banking for local companies, professional practices, nonprofits and real estate investors rather than a large consumer branch network.

Its offices are concentrated in Northern Virginia, Washington, D.C. and Montgomery County, Maryland. In September 2026 John Marshall Bancorp agreed to combine with Eagle Financial Services, the parent of Bank of Clarke, in a deal expected to close in early 2027. Both bank brands are expected to stay in use, and the merger would extend the footprint west into the Shenandoah Valley.

Typical clients include government contractors, law and medical practices, accounting firms, property owners and family-owned service companies. For an SBA borrower, that means a lender used to cash-flow-based commercial credit and owner-occupied real estate in one of the most expensive property markets in the country. Deposit relationships usually come with the loan, so expect the bank to ask for your operating accounts as part of the deal.

How business owners use SBA loans here

In the D.C. market, the most common SBA use with a bank like John Marshall is buying a practice or service business, where a 7(a) loan can stretch repayment to 10 years and limit the down payment. Owner-occupied office or flex space is the other big one: a 504 loan, with the bank in first position and a Virginia or Maryland CDC on the second lien, can lock in a long fixed rate on a pricey building. Government contractors with receivables can also ask about SBA working capital lines.

SBA programs to ask John Marshall Bank about

  • 7(a). The SBA's main loan, up to $5 million, for working capital, equipment, buying a business, refinancing and real estate.
  • 504. Long-term fixed-rate financing for owner-occupied real estate and heavy equipment. The bank funds about 50%, a CDC up to 40%, and you put in about 10%.
  • SBA Express. A 7(a) loan up to $500,000 that the lender approves under its own authority, so it closes faster. The rate cap is the same as standard 7(a).

Program availability changes. Ask the lender which SBA programs it closes most often and whether it has SBA Preferred Lender status, which lets it approve loans in-house.

Questions

Does John Marshall Bank make SBA loans?

John Marshall Bank appears in SBA lending data as a participating lender. Ask its business bankers which programs they offer today, whether they hold Preferred Lender status for faster approvals, and whether they handle 504 first liens alongside a local CDC.

Where does John Marshall Bank lend?

The bank is based in Reston, Virginia and focuses on Northern Virginia, Washington, D.C. and suburban Maryland. Businesses outside that region can still ask, but community banks usually prefer borrowers they can visit and serve with a local relationship manager.

Is John Marshall Bank merging with Bank of Clarke?

In September 2026 John Marshall Bancorp agreed to combine with Eagle Financial Services, parent of Bank of Clarke. The deal was expected to close in early 2027 pending approvals, with both bank names kept. A loan in process should not be affected, but ask your banker about timing.

What rate will I get on an SBA loan from John Marshall Bank?
