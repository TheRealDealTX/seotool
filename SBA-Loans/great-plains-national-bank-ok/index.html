Great Plains National Bank SBA loans (OK)

Great Plains National Bank is an Oklahoma community bank based in Elk City that lends to farm, energy and small-business customers.

Great Plains National Bank is an Oklahoma community bank headquartered in Elk City, in the western part of the state. Its website is gpbankok.com, which is how many customers search for it. The bank is locally focused and serves communities across western Oklahoma, and it has also built a presence in the Oklahoma City area.

Western Oklahoma's economy leans on agriculture, oil and gas, trucking and the service businesses that support them. The bank's commercial customers reflect that: oilfield service companies, equipment dealers, contractors, restaurants, retailers, healthcare providers and real estate owners, along with farm and ranch operations. Many of these customers have banked with Great Plains for years.

The bank offers commercial, agricultural and consumer lending, plus business deposit accounts and online banking. SBA loans help it finance businesses whose collateral or cash flow history does not fit a conventional loan, which can matter in an energy-driven economy that moves in cycles. Loan officers in its western Oklahoma markets tend to know local industries closely.

How business owners use SBA loans here

In western Oklahoma, energy price swings can make conventional lenders cautious. An SBA 7(a) loan from Great Plains National Bank can give an oilfield service or trucking company longer terms on equipment, which lowers monthly payments when activity slows. Owners buying a local business or building can use 7(a) or a 504 loan with an Oklahoma CDC. Show the bank how your revenue held up in past downturns, and be realistic in your projections.

SBA programs to ask Great Plains National Bank about

  • 7(a). The SBA's main loan, up to $5 million, for working capital, equipment, buying a business, refinancing and real estate.
  • 504. Long-term fixed-rate financing for owner-occupied real estate and heavy equipment. The bank funds about 50%, a CDC up to 40%, and you put in about 10%.
  • SBA Express. A 7(a) loan up to $500,000 that the lender approves under its own authority, so it closes faster. The rate cap is the same as standard 7(a).

Program availability changes. Ask the lender which SBA programs it closes most often and whether it has SBA Preferred Lender status, which lets it approve loans in-house.
