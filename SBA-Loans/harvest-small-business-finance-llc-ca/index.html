Harvest Small Business Finance SBA loans (CA)

Harvest Small Business Finance is a nonbank SBA 7(a) lender based in Laguna Hills, California, that makes SBA loans to businesses across the country.

Harvest Small Business Finance, LLC is a nonbank lender headquartered in Laguna Hills, in Orange County, California. It is not a bank and does not take deposits. Instead it holds one of the few Small Business Lending Company (SBLC) licenses that allow a nonbank to make SBA 7(a) loans. Harvest acquired that license in 2016, with backing from investment firm Medalist Partners.

Harvest lends nationwide through business development officers in many states. A large part of its lending is 7(a) loans secured by owner-occupied commercial real estate, and it has issued several securitizations backed by the unguaranteed portions of those loans. It is also licensed in California for 504 and conventional commercial lending.

Its borrowers include owner-operators buying their building, buyers of established businesses, and companies refinancing higher-cost debt. Because it works only in business lending, Harvest focuses on deal structure rather than deposit relationships. Industry publication Coleman Report has named it a top nonbank SBA lender. Loans are typically sized for established businesses with real estate collateral.

How business owners use SBA loans here

Harvest is a fit when you want an SBA specialist rather than a full-service bank. Common deals are 7(a) loans to buy owner-occupied real estate with a longer term and smaller down payment, business acquisitions with real estate, and refinancing. A nonbank lender will not require you to move your checking accounts, but you will still need a bank for daily operations. Ask about the rate type, prepayment terms and fees, and compare them with a bank's offer.

SBA programs to ask Harvest Small Business Finance about

  • 7(a). The SBA's main loan, up to $5 million, for working capital, equipment, buying a business, refinancing and real estate.
  • 504. Long-term fixed-rate financing for owner-occupied real estate and heavy equipment. The bank funds about 50%, a CDC up to 40%, and you put in about 10%.

Program availability changes. Ask the lender which SBA programs it closes most often and whether it has SBA Preferred Lender status, which lets it approve loans in-house.

Questions

Is Harvest Small Business Finance a bank?

No. Harvest is a nonbank lender. It makes SBA 7(a) loans under a Small Business Lending Company license issued by the SBA, one of only a small number of such licenses. It does not offer checking or savings accounts, so you keep your existing bank for daily banking.

Does Harvest lend outside California?

Yes. Although it is headquartered in Laguna Hills, California, Harvest makes SBA loans nationwide through business development officers in many states. Ask whether it has an officer covering your state and whether your property type and industry fit its lending focus.

What rates does Harvest charge on SBA loans?

Harvest prices within SBA maximums like any 7(a) lender. With prime at 7.00%, variable caps run from 10.00% for loans over $350,000 to 13.50% for $50,000 or less. Fixed-rate caps are set separately. Get the spread, fees and prepayment terms in writing.

What rate will I get on an SBA loan from Harvest Small Business Finance?

Harvest Small Business Finance sets your rate at or below the SBA maximum. With prime at 7.00%, variable 7(a) caps run from 10.00% on loans over $350,000 to 13.50% on loans of $50,000 or less. Your rate depends on loan size, term, collateral, credit and cash flow.
