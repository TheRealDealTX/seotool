How to Travel With a Wedding Dress (Without Wrinkles or Panic)

September 12, 20267 min readBy the Zibetti team

As destination photographers, we’ve seen dresses arrive perfect and dresses arrive… less perfect. Here’s everything we tell our couples about traveling with a wedding dress and formal attire.

Before you fly

  1. Call your airline. Ask how they handle wedding dresses. Many airlines will let you hang a gown in a crew closet on larger aircraft if space allows, but nothing is guaranteed — plan to carry it on.
  2. Ask your boutique to pack it. Bridal shops fold dresses for shipping all the time. Ask for acid-free tissue paper between every fold.
  3. Schedule your final fitting early. Leave a buffer so you’re not picking the dress up the day before your flight.
  4. Consider shipping. For big ball gowns going abroad, a tracked, insured courier shipment to your venue can be less stressful than an overhead bin. Confirm the venue can receive and store it.

How to pack it

For most dresses, a breathable garment bag (not plastic, which traps moisture) folded into thirds fits in an overhead bin. For voluminous gowns, a dedicated wedding dress travel box or a large carry-on with the dress rolled in tissue works better.

  • Turn the dress inside out to protect beading and lace.
  • Stuff the bodice with tissue to keep its shape.
  • Fold along existing seams, never across structured bodices.
  • Pack your veil separately, rolled loosely around a towel or cardboard tube.

At the airport

  1. Board early. Pay for priority boarding so overhead space isn’t gone when you get on.
  2. Tell the crew. A friendly “I’m flying with my wedding dress” often earns a closet spot or a careful hand.
  3. Never check it. Lost luggage is a nightmare on any trip; on this trip it’s not an option.
  4. Keep the essentials with you: rings, vows, marriage paperwork, shoes and any jewelry go in your personal item.

When you arrive

  1. Unpack immediately and hang the dress somewhere high so the train hangs free.
  2. Steam, don’t iron. Bring a travel steamer or ask your hotel. A steamy bathroom works in a pinch for light fabrics. Test on an inside seam first.
  3. Find a local pro. For destination weddings, ask your planner or venue for a local seamstress or steaming service on standby.
Photographer tipLeave the dress on its prettiest hanger. We photograph it during the details portion of getting ready — hanging in a window, it’s often one of the most-loved images in the gallery.

Suits & other formalwear

Suits and tuxedos travel well in a carry-on garment bag. Roll shirts instead of folding them, pack ties flat inside a book, and stuff dress shoes with socks to keep their shape. If you’re renting, check whether the rental company has a location near your destination — it’s often easier to pick up there.

Bridesmaids and groomsmen should follow the same rule: carry it on, unpack on arrival, steam the day before. Planning a destination wedding? See our guide to destination photography costs, or send us your date.
