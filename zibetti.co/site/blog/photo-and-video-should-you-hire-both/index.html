Wedding Photo and Video: Should You Hire Both?

July 30, 20266 min readBy the Zibetti team

It’s one of the most common budget questions we hear. And the most common regret we hear from married couples? “I wish we’d gotten video.”

What film captures that photos can’t

  • Voices. Your vows in your own words. Your grandmother’s toast.
  • Movement. The way the veil caught the wind, the spin on the dance floor.
  • Sound and music. The song you walked in to, the laughter, the cheering.

Photographs are what you’ll hang on the wall and print in albums. Film is what you’ll watch on anniversaries and show your kids. They do different jobs.

Why one team works better than two

When a photographer and videographer have never met, they compete for the same angles, step into each other’s shots and plan two separate timelines. A single photo + film team plans one schedule, knows where the other will stand and moves as one quiet crew. You get better coverage with fewer people in your space.

What it costs

Nationally, wedding videography often costs about as much as photography. Booking both from one studio usually saves money because travel, planning and coordination are shared. At Zibetti, adding film to any photo collection is a $2,000 add-on that covers the same hours as your photos, and film-only collections start at $3,200. You can try combinations in our pricing estimator.

When photo-only makes sense

  • Very short elopements where it’s just the two of you and an officiant.
  • Couples who genuinely never re-watch videos.
  • A tight budget where the choice is film vs. a great photographer. Choose the great photographer.

How to decide

Ask yourself: twenty years from now, would you rather hear your vows or not? If the answer is “hear them,” at least add a short highlight film or a ceremony-only edit. Learn more on our wedding film page.
