The 15 Best Places to Elope in Utah (By Season and Scenery)

September 24, 202610 min readBy the Zibetti team

We could fill a book with Utah locations. Instead, here are fifteen we come back to again and again — grouped by the kind of scenery you’re dreaming about, with the season we’d pick for each.

InteractiveWant to narrow this list down fast? The Utah elopement location finder filters these spots (plus a few more) by season, scenery, hiking and drive time.

Red rock & desert

1. Arches National Park

Sandstone arches, balanced rocks and fins glowing orange at sunrise. Arches is iconic for a reason, which also means crowds — choose a weekday sunrise and a less famous viewpoint. Best: March–May, late September–November. Permit: national park special use permit.

2. Dead Horse Point State Park

A rim-top overlook above a gooseneck bend of the Colorado River. Massive views with nearly zero hiking — perfect for grandparents or a small group. Best: spring and fall; winter is quiet and beautiful. Permit: state park.

3. Canyonlands — Island in the Sky

Layer upon layer of canyons stretching to the horizon. Grand View Point at sunset feels like standing on the edge of the world. Best: spring and fall.

4. Capitol Reef National Park

White domes, red cliffs, historic orchards and far fewer visitors than the “Mighty Five” neighbors. Great for couples who want quiet. Best: April–May and October.

5. Goblin Valley State Park

Hundreds of mushroom-shaped hoodoos that look like another planet. Playful, surreal and endlessly photogenic. Best: spring and fall.

Canyons & hoodoos

6. Zion National Park

Thousand-foot cliffs, the Virgin River and cottonwoods that turn gold in early November. Canyon walls block the sun early, so plan for open light rather than a horizon sunset. Best: spring, late fall and winter.

7. Bryce Canyon National Park

An amphitheater of orange hoodoos at around 8,000 feet. Cool in summer and unforgettable with fresh snow. Sunrise at the rim is the move. Best: any season — winter is magic.

8. The San Rafael Swell slot canyons

Narrow, glowing slot canyons for adventurous couples who don’t mind a little scrambling. Never enter a slot canyon with rain anywhere in the forecast. Best: spring and fall.

Alpine & lakes

9. Mirror Lake Highway & the Uinta Mountains

High alpine lakes, pine forests and summer wildflowers about two hours from Salt Lake City. The highway closes in winter. Best: July–September.

10. The Alpine Loop & Mount Timpanogos

Our backyard. Aspen groves, meadows and big granite peaks minutes from Provo. Peak fall color usually lands in the last week or two of September. Best: summer and late September.

11. Tibble Fork Reservoir

A calm mountain reservoir in American Fork Canyon with easy access — ideal when you have family who can’t hike. Best: June–October.

12. Sundance Mountain Resort

Rustic-luxe lodging, dining and ceremony sites at the base of Timpanogos. A great pick for intimate weddings with a few dozen guests. Best: year-round.

13. Guardsman Pass, Park City

Rolling ridgelines and aspen groves with roadside access — plus all of Park City’s restaurants for your dinner afterward. Best: summer and fall.

The unexpected

14. Bonneville Salt Flats

A blindingly white, perfectly flat horizon. Minimal and modern, and after a rain it becomes a mirror. Bring sunglasses and be ready for heat. Best: late summer and fall when the flats are dry.

15. Antelope Island State Park

Golden grasslands, the Great Salt Lake shoreline and free-roaming bison, about an hour from Salt Lake City. Bugs can be intense in late spring. Best: April and September–October.

How to choose your spot

  • Start with the season. Desert in spring and fall, mountains in summer and early fall.
  • Be honest about hiking. A mile in wedding shoes feels like three.
  • Count your guests. National parks have group limits; private venues and state parks are more flexible.
  • Check the light. Use the golden hour calculator — canyon locations lose the sun early.
