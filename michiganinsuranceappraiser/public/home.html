Who we are

What a Michigan Insurance Appraiser actually does

Most Michigan homeowners and business owners never think about the appraisal clause until a claim goes sideways. The carrier agrees the loss is covered, then sends an estimate that won’t rebuild the roof or dry out the basement. That gap is where Michigan Insurance Appraiser works.

  1. Measure and price it ourselves. We inspect, measure every roof plane and room, and write a line-item estimate with current local pricing, so the number on the table is grounded in your property, not a desk review.
  2. Tell you whether appraisal fits. Appraisal settles the amount of loss. If the real fight is coverage, a Michigan Insurance Appraiser will say so and point you toward the right help.
  3. Serve as your appraiser. When appraisal is demanded, we are the competent, independent appraiser you name, and we work the panel through to a signed award.
  4. Act as a neutral umpire. On other claims, we sit in the middle and decide the items two appraisers can’t settle.

Every Michigan Insurance Appraiser file starts the same way: a free conversation about what happened, what the carrier paid and what you think it missed.

The process

How appraisal runs, from demand letter to award

Michigan’s Insurance Code sets the basic shape: a written demand, two independent appraisers, an umpire if needed, and an award signed by any two of the three. Here is how a Michigan Insurance Appraiser moves a claim through it.

Read the full process guide
  2. 01

    Document the gap in numbers

    Put your figure and the insurer’s figure side by side, line by line, so the dollar gap is clear. Appraisal works best when the argument is about price and quantity, not about whether the loss is covered.

  3. 02

    Read the appraisal clause

    Find the appraisal provision in your policy and note its exact wording, notice rules and any timing language. Michigan law requires this provision in property policies, but the details still come from your contract.

  4. 03

    Send a written demand

    Either side can start the process with a clear written demand for appraisal sent to the insurer. Keep proof of when and how it was delivered.

  5. 04

    Each side names an appraiser

    You and the insurer each pick a competent, independent appraiser, generally within 20 days of the demand. Your appraiser builds and presents the valuation for your side.

  6. 05

    Appraisers select an umpire

    The two appraisers try to agree on a neutral umpire, generally within 15 days. If they can’t agree, either side can ask a Michigan court of record to choose one.

  7. 06

    Inspect and exchange estimates

    The appraisers walk the property, review photos, invoices and estimates, and trade detailed line-item valuations. Many items settle at this stage once both sides see the same evidence.

  8. 07

    Resolve the remaining items

    Items the two appraisers can’t agree on go to the umpire with supporting documents. The umpire weighs each disputed item and sides with the better-supported number or sets a figure between them.

  9. 08

    Sign the award and get paid

    A written award signed by any two of the three sets the amount of loss. The insurer then pays under the policy terms, less the deductible and prior payments, with any holdback handled as the policy allows.

Questions

What owners ask a Michigan Insurance Appraiser

Short answers to common questions. The full FAQ goes deeper on costs, timing and Michigan specifics.

What does a Michigan insurance appraiser do?

An insurance appraiser builds an independent valuation of a covered loss and, when either side demands appraisal under the policy, serves on the panel that sets the amount of loss. We also review estimates before a dispute escalates and serve as neutral umpires.

Is appraisal required in Michigan policies?

Michigan’s Insurance Code requires property policies covering fire to include an appraisal provision, and most homeowners and commercial policies contain one. Read your own policy’s appraisal condition for the exact wording and deadlines.

Can appraisal decide whether my claim is covered?

Generally no. Appraisal decides the amount of loss. If the insurer denied coverage, talk with a Michigan attorney about your options.

How much does an appraiser cost?

Fees vary by claim size and complexity and may be flat, hourly or a percentage of the increase. Each side typically pays its own appraiser and splits the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work starts.

Do you work in the Upper Peninsula?

Yes. We work in all 83 counties on both peninsulas, from Detroit and Grand Rapids to Marquette, Houghton and Sault Ste. Marie.

Are you a public adjuster or a law firm?

Neither. We provide appraisal, estimating and umpire services. If your claim needs a public adjuster or an attorney instead, we’ll tell you.
