The short version

Covered isn’t the same as paid

Most underpaid claims in Rhode Island aren’t denied. They’re short: a slope left off the roof, plaster priced as drywall, a hurricane deductible applied to an incomplete scope. A Rhode Island Insurance Appraiser works in the space between the carrier’s number and the real cost of the repair.

We inspect, measure and price the loss from scratch, then put that valuation in front of the carrier’s appraiser line by line. When we can’t agree, a neutral umpire decides the difference.

What each season sends a Rhode Island Insurance Appraiser

Rhode Island’s weather runs on a calendar. Knowing when and how damage happens helps us scope it, and helps you document it.

  1. Jan–Mar

    Blizzards & ice dams

    Heavy snow, roof loads and meltwater backing up under shingles.

  2. Mar–May

    Spring rain & nor’easters

    Saturated ground, river flooding and late-season coastal storms.

  3. Jun–Aug

    Thunderstorms & hail

    Downbursts, lightning and occasional hail crossing from the west.

  4. Aug–Oct

    Hurricane season peak

    Tropical systems funneling wind and surge up Narragansett Bay.

  5. Oct–Dec

    Fall nor’easters

    Long-duration wind, falling trees and wind-driven rain.

How it works

From written demand to signed award

The appraisal provision turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result on the amount of loss. Here is how a Rhode Island Insurance Appraiser takes a file through it.

Read the full appraisal sequence
  1. 1

    Send the written demand

    Either side demands appraisal in writing, following the policy’s appraisal paragraph.

  2. 2

    Each side names an appraiser

    You and the insurer each choose a competent, disinterested appraiser, usually within a set number of days.

  3. 3

    The appraisers choose an umpire

    If they can’t agree on one, many policies let either side ask a court to appoint one.

  4. 4

    The appraisers compare estimates

    They inspect, exchange estimates and work through the differences line by line.

  5. 5

    The umpire resolves what’s left

    Items the appraisers can’t agree on go to the umpire, who reviews both positions.

Three-question test

Should you call a Rhode Island Insurance Appraiser?

Answer these honestly. If all three are “yes,” appraisal is worth a conversation. If not, we’ll point you to the right kind of help.

1

Has the insurer accepted coverage?

Appraisal values a covered loss. If the claim was denied outright, a Rhode Island attorney is usually the first call.

2

Do you have two itemized estimates?

The carrier’s full line-item estimate and a credible one of your own. A one-line bid isn’t enough for a panel.

3

Is the gap worth the cost?

Generally more than about 10% of the carrier’s estimate or several thousand dollars. Smaller gaps often close with a supplement.

What does a Rhode Island Insurance Appraiser do?

A Rhode Island Insurance Appraiser values property damage when you and your insurer agree a loss is covered but disagree on the cost. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work through it with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to the umpire.

How is an appraiser different from a public adjuster?

A public adjuster represents you in preparing and negotiating the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal process on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When does appraisal make sense?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s itemized estimate and a credible estimate of your own, and the difference is meaningful: generally more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is the appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, generally yes, once any two of the three panel members sign. That’s why the file should be well documented before appraisal starts.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work begins, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover Block Island and the rest of the state?

Yes. We work in all 39 cities and towns across Rhode Island’s five counties, and on Block Island by arrangement.
