Appraisal Clause Representation for Nevada Policyholders

Your claim was accepted, but the number is wrong. The appraisal provision in most Nevada property policies replaces open-ended back-and-forth with a three-person panel. We are the appraiser you put on it.

The short paragraph that changes a dispute

Look near the end of the conditions section of a Nevada homeowners, landlord or commercial property policy and you will usually find a provision titled “Appraisal.” The wording varies by carrier, but the structure is consistent: if you and the insurer fail to agree on the amount of loss, either side can demand appraisal in writing, each side names a competent and impartial appraiser, those two pick an umpire, and a written agreement signed by any two of the three sets the amount.

That two-of-three rule is the whole point. Once an award is signed, the amount of loss is no longer a matter of opinion. It is also why the appraiser you name has to bring a valuation that holds up when someone challenges it.

How we work a Nevada appraisal

  1. Policy and deadline check. We read the appraisal wording, any time limit for naming appraisers and the policy’s suit-limitation clause before anything else.
  2. A fresh inspection. We measure and photograph the loss ourselves. In the Las Vegas Valley that often means walking concrete tile and checking underlayment; around Reno and Lake Tahoe it may mean attic framing, ice-dam paths or smoke residue in ductwork.
  3. Line-item pricing. We build an estimate in standard estimating software, priced for your area. Clark County and Washoe County labor rates differ, and rural jobs in Elko or Nye County carry travel and mobilization costs that generic price lists miss.
  4. Panel work. We meet the insurer’s appraiser, walk the property together where possible and narrow the disagreement to specific line items.
  5. Umpire submission. Anything still unresolved goes to the umpire with photos, measurements and pricing support.
  6. Award review. Before signatures, we check that replacement cost, depreciation and actual cash value are stated clearly so payment follows without a second argument.
Scope of appraisalAn appraisal panel values the loss. It does not decide coverage questions, interpret exclusions or award damages for claim handling. If your insurer has denied the claim, or you believe it has acted in bad faith, speak with a Nevada attorney.

Disputes that suit appraisal

Appraisal works well for disagreements over how much damage exists and what it costs: whether a tile roof needs a full underlayment replacement or a spot repair, how many rooms smoke reached, whether a discontinued floor can be matched, or what a contractor in Henderson actually charges for stucco and paint this year. It is a poor fit when the argument is about whether the loss is covered at all.

Our standard

We would rather bring a number we can defend than a number that looks large. Umpires notice inflated estimates, and a credible appraiser is the one whose line items get accepted.

Questions about this service

Who picks my appraiser?

You do. Most policies require your appraiser to be competent and impartial, so someone with a financial stake in the repair contract can create problems for the award.

What if the insurer’s appraiser and ours can’t agree on an umpire?

Most policies let either side ask a court to appoint one. In Nevada that is typically the district court for the county where the property is located.

Does appraisal stop the clock on my policy’s lawsuit deadline?

Not automatically. Read your policy and talk to a Nevada attorney about any suit-limitation date. Appraisal is not a substitute for protecting that deadline.
