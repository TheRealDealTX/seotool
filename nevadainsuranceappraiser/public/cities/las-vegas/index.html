A city built fast, in a hard climate

Most of the Las Vegas Valley was built in a few decades. Neighborhoods like Huntridge, the Scotch 80s and Rancho Circle date from the 1940s to the 1960s, with block construction, flat or low-slope roofs and mature trees. Summerlin, Centennial Hills, The Lakes and the southwest valley filled in during the 1990s and 2000s with stucco homes, concrete tile roofs and HOA-maintained walls. Downtown and the resort corridor add condominium towers where the association’s master policy and the owner’s HO-6 policy split the loss.

The climate tests all of it. Summer highs routinely pass 110°F; the airport recorded 120°F on July 7, 2024. That heat ages underlayment, sealants and roof membranes faster than in milder states, which insurers sometimes use to argue that damage is wear rather than a storm. The valley’s very hard water corrodes copper lines and water heaters, making slab and supply-line leaks some of the most common claims.

Storm history that shows up in claims

  • July 8, 1999: a severe monsoon storm flooded large parts of the valley, damaging hundreds of homes.
  • July 2022: intense monsoon storms sent water through streets and into buildings on and off the Strip.
  • August 2023: Tropical Storm Hilary’s remnants brought heavy rain and wind across Southern Nevada.
  • Most summers bring localized microbursts that lift tile, topple block walls and drop mature trees.

Where we work in Las Vegas

Summerlin, The Lakes, Desert Shores, Centennial Hills, Skye Canyon, Spring Valley, Enterprise, Mountain’s Edge, Rhodes Ranch, Southern Highlands, Paradise, Huntridge, the Scotch 80s, the Arts District and downtown high-rises. Much of the valley is unincorporated Clark County rather than the City of Las Vegas, which matters for permits and code requirements.

Questions from Las Vegas property owners

Most of my neighbors have tile roofs. Why do tile claims get disputed so often?

Because the tile is only part of the system. Broken tile can be easy to see, but the underlayment beneath may be the real damage, and old tile is often discontinued. Both drive the cost up and invite disagreement.

I live in Spring Valley. Is that Las Vegas for my claim?

Spring Valley, Paradise and Enterprise are unincorporated Clark County. Permits and code requirements come from the county, which can affect what a repair must include.
