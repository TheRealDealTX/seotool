The short version

What a Nevada Insurance Appraiser actually does

Most underpaid claims are not denials. They are short: a tile roof priced as a patch, a smoke claim reduced to a wipe-down, a slab leak with no allowance for the floor. Nevada Insurance Appraiser exists for the gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

When the appraisal clause is invoked, each side names an appraiser. As your Nevada Insurance Appraiser, we inspect the property ourselves, price every line for your area and defend that number in front of the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, a neutral umpire.

  • We start from scratch. Our own measurements, not an edited copy of the insurer’s estimate.
  • Defensible, not inflated. Numbers built to survive an umpire’s review.
  • Straight answers. If your claim needs an attorney or a supplement instead, we say so on the first call.

Four claim climates

One state, very different losses

A Nevada Insurance Appraiser has to know how a monsoon microburst in Henderson differs from a snow load in Incline Village or a smoke claim in southwest Reno, and how each one shows up in an estimate.

Las Vegas Valley

  • Monsoon storms
  • Microbursts
  • Tile roofs
  • Slab leaks

About three of every four Nevadans live in Clark County, most of them in stucco homes with concrete tile roofs built since the 1980s. Extreme heat ages roofing fast, very hard water corrodes plumbing, and summer monsoon storms bring sudden wind, hail and flooding.

Las Vegas · Henderson · North Las Vegas · Boulder City · Mesquite

Reno–Sparks

  • Wildfire & smoke
  • Zephyr winds
  • River floods
  • Freeze

The Truckee Meadows sit where the Sierra meets the Great Basin. Foothill neighborhoods face wildfire, the Washoe Zephyr tests roofs every summer afternoon, and the Truckee River has flooded downtown Reno and Sparks more than once.

Reno · Sparks

Carson Valley & Lake Tahoe

  • Snow load
  • Ice dams
  • Wildfire
  • Downslope wind

Nevada’s capital and the Carson Valley sit at the foot of the Carson Range; Lake Tahoe’s Nevada shore sits above them in heavy-snow country. Claims here involve fire at the wildland edge, winter snow loads and high-value mountain construction.

Carson City · Minden & Gardnerville · Incline Village

Rural & Great Basin Nevada

  • Extreme cold
  • Rangeland fire
  • Wind
  • Earthquake

Most of Nevada’s land is open basin and range. In Pahrump, Elko and the towns between, homes sit on large lots with outbuildings and wells, winters can be brutal, and the 2008 Wells earthquake showed that the Great Basin can shake.

Pahrump · Elko

The process

From first call to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here is how Nevada Insurance Appraiser moves a file through it.

  1. 01

    Free review

    We read your policy, the insurer’s estimate and your photos, then tell you plainly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 02

    Independent scope

    We inspect, measure and build a line-item estimate priced for your part of Nevada.

  3. 03

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and we are named as your appraiser within the policy’s time frame.

  4. 04

    Panel work

    We work line by line with the insurer’s appraiser. Most disputes narrow sharply here.

  5. 05

    Signed award

    Anything left goes to a neutral umpire. Two signatures set the amount of loss.

What people ask a Nevada Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it is the wrong move.

All questions Claim dictionary
What does a Nevada Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree a loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

How is that different from a public adjuster?

A public adjuster manages and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When should I call a Nevada Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the insurer’s estimate and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful, generally more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is an appraisal award binding?

Yes, on the amount of loss, once any two of the three panel members sign. That is why documentation matters so much before appraisal is demanded.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you work outside Las Vegas and Reno?

Yes. We work in all 16 counties + Carson City, including Carson City, the Carson Valley, Lake Tahoe, Pahrump, Mesquite and Elko.
