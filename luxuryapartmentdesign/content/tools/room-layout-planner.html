How to Use the Room Layout Planner

Start by measuring your room wall to wall and entering the width and length in feet. The planner draws a scaled grid so every piece of furniture appears at its true size relative to the room. Drag pieces in from the furniture library, rotate them to try different orientations, and delete anything that does not fit. As you move things, the planner checks the space between pieces and walls and warns you when a walkway or clearance falls below the recommended minimum.

Before you start, note where doors swing, where windows and radiators sit, and where outlets and cable jacks are. A layout that looks perfect on the grid but blocks the only outlet behind a dresser is not perfect. Measure your actual furniture too: the sizes in the library are typical dimensions, and your pieces may differ.

Clearance Rules the Planner Checks

Good layouts are mostly about the empty space between furniture. These are the guidelines the planner uses for its warnings:

ClearanceRecommendedWhy it matters
Main walkways30–36 in. (76–91 cm)Lets people pass without turning sideways
Sofa to coffee table14–18 in. (36–46 cm)Within reach, with room for legs
Behind dining chairs (to pass)36 in. (91 cm)Someone can walk behind a seated guest
Behind dining chairs (minimum)24 in. (61 cm)Just enough to pull out a chair and sit
Sides of bed24–36 in. (61–91 cm)Room to get in and out and make the bed
Conversation areaWithin about 8–10 ft (2.4–3 m)Seats close enough to talk comfortably

In small apartments, you will often have to choose which clearance to compromise on. Protect the main walkway first, then the space around the bed or dining table. A coffee table can always be swapped for a smaller round one or a pair of nesting tables.

Layout Strategies for Living Rooms and Bedrooms

In a living room, start by choosing a focal point, such as a window, TV wall, or art wall, and face the sofa toward it. Add chairs to form a U or L shape, keeping all seats within about 8 to 10 feet of each other so conversation feels natural. Pull furniture off the walls if the room allows; even a few inches of space behind a sofa makes a room feel more considered. Then size the rug to connect the group. Our apartment living room ideas include more layout examples.

In a bedroom, place the bed on the wall opposite or beside the door, not under a window if you can avoid it, and aim for 24 to 36 inches on each side. If the room is too narrow for both, keep the clearance on one side and push the other closer to the wall. See our apartment bedroom ideas for small-room solutions.

Designer tip: Once you have a layout you like on screen, mark the outline of each major piece on your floor with painter's tape and walk through the room for a day. You will quickly notice any path that feels tight or a chair that blocks a door, and painter's tape will not harm rental floors.

Planning Small Spaces and Studios

In a studio or small apartment, use furniture to create zones: a rug and sofa define the living area, a bookcase or console behind the sofa separates it from the bed, and a small round dining table keeps traffic flowing around the edges. Choose pieces with exposed legs and lower backs to keep sightlines open, and consider multifunctional pieces such as storage beds and extendable tables.
