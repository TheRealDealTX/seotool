How the Interior Design Style Quiz Works

Every interior design style is really a set of repeatable decisions: a color family, a handful of materials, a furniture silhouette, and a mood. The quiz asks eight questions that map to those decisions, covering color, materials, furniture shapes, the mood you want at home, how you spend a weekend, the art you gravitate toward, and where you would rather spend your budget. Each answer adds points to one or more styles, and the style with the highest score becomes your result.

Because many styles share traits (Japandi and Organic Modern both love natural wood, for example), it is normal for your top two results to be close. Treat a near-tie as permission to blend, letting one style lead and the other supply accents.

Quiet Luxury, Japandi and Modern Minimalist

Quiet Luxury is about restraint and touch. Think a tonal palette of ivory, oatmeal, and taupe, with interest coming from texture: bouclé, heavy linen, honed stone, and wool. It looks expensive because nothing shouts. Our quiet luxury interior design guide breaks down how to build it piece by piece.

Japandi blends Japanese calm with Scandinavian warmth: light oak, paper lanterns, low furniture, and a muted palette with charcoal accents. Natural, flat-weave rugs anchor it; see our guide to choosing a Japandi rug.

Modern Minimalist favors crisp white walls, black accents, and clean lines with everything tucked away. If you scored here, our breakdown of the types of minimalism will help you decide how stark or soft to go.

Warm Contemporary, Art Deco Glam and Organic Modern

Warm Contemporary keeps modern lines but softens them with curves, walnut, cognac leather, and creamy neutrals. It is the most forgiving style for mixing what you already own.

Art Deco Glam leans into saturated jewel tones, velvet, marble, and polished brass, with geometric patterns such as fluting, fans, and chevrons. Keep it in balance by limiting metallics to one finish and grounding bold colors with black.

Organic Modern pairs simple modern forms with raw, earthy materials: jute, rattan, limewash-look walls, stoneware, and lots of greenery. Rounded shapes and clay tones keep it warm rather than rustic.

Classic Parisian and Mid-Century Modern

Classic Parisian is the look of a Haussmann apartment: tall mirrors, wall paneling, herringbone floors, marble mantels, and a mix of antique and modern pieces. In a rental, you can fake the architecture with peel-and-stick molding painted the wall color and a large leaning mirror.

Mid-Century Modern draws on 1950s and 1960s design: tapered legs, teak and walnut, organic curves, and pops of mustard, olive, or orange. One iconic-shaped lounge chair or credenza sets the tone; avoid filling the room with reproductions, which can feel like a showroom.

Designer tip: Whatever your result, start with the largest, most-used piece in the style (usually the sofa or bed) and the lighting. Those two decisions define the room more than any accessory, and getting them right makes smaller additions look intentional.

Turning Your Result Into a Room

Once you know your style, write down its three signature colors and two signature materials, and check every purchase against that list. This keeps the room cohesive even when you buy pieces slowly over months.

  • Set the palette first. Translate your style colors into a 60-30-10 scheme for walls, furniture, and accents.
  • Repeat materials. Use your signature wood or metal at least three times around the room.
  • Edit ruthlessly. Remove anything that clearly belongs to a different style or move it to another room.
  • Go renter-friendly. Lean on textiles, lighting, mirrors, and removable details instead of permanent changes. Our list of renter friendly upgrades works for every style above.
