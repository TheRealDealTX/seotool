The best home refresh ideas are not about buying a new room's worth of furniture. They are about resetting what you already have: clearing out, deep cleaning, rearranging, and then adding a few well-chosen updates in color, textiles and light. Done in the right order, a full refresh fits into a single week of evenings plus one weekend, and it can make a tired apartment feel brand new.

This guide gives you a day-by-day, 7-day plan for how to refresh your home, with a compressed weekend version if you are short on time. You will also find budget ranges, seasonal swaps for spring, summer, fall and winter, and specific ways to refresh a living room on a budget.

Before You Start: Set a Goal and a Budget

Spend 15 minutes walking through your apartment with your phone. Photograph each room from the doorway and jot down the three things that bother you most in each. Common answers: clutter, dim light, a layout that does not work, worn textiles, and walls that feel bare or dated.

Then choose a budget tier. These are typical ranges for an apartment refresh and vary by region and retailer:

Pick your goal for the week as a single sentence, such as "make the living room feel calm and warm" or "make the whole apartment feel lighter for spring." It will help you say no to purchases that do not serve it.

Day 1: Declutter Room by Room

Everything else goes faster once the clutter is gone. Work through one room at a time with three bags or boxes: keep, donate or sell, and trash or recycle.

  • Clear every flat surface, then put back only what earns its place.
  • Empty one drawer or shelf at a time rather than an entire closet at once.
  • Remove anything broken, expired or unused for a year.
  • Take the donation bags out of the apartment the same day so they do not creep back in.

Our printable declutter checklist breaks this into room-by-room tasks if you want a structured list.

Day 2: Deep Clean the Things You Stopped Noticing

A refreshed room has to feel clean, not just tidy. Focus on the surfaces regular cleaning skips:

  • Wash windows inside and wipe the sills and tracks.
  • Dust light fixtures, lampshades, ceiling fan blades and the tops of door frames.
  • Wipe baseboards, switch plates and door handles.
  • Wash or steam curtains, and vacuum upholstery and under cushions.
  • Launder pillow covers, throws and bath mats.
  • Clean the grout and re-caulk tired spots in the bathroom if your lease allows small maintenance.

Clean windows and dust-free lampshades alone make rooms noticeably brighter, which sets up everything that follows.

Day 3: Rearrange the Layout

With surfaces clear and floors clean, it is the perfect moment to try a new layout. A fresh arrangement makes a room feel different without spending anything.

  • Find the focal point, such as a window, fireplace or large wall, and orient the main seating toward it.
  • Pull furniture off the walls where space allows, and group seating into a conversation zone.
  • Check the clearances: about 16 to 18 inches between sofa and coffee table, and 30 to 36 inches for main walkways.
  • Swap pieces between rooms: a chair, lamp or side table often looks new somewhere else.

To test arrangements without moving heavy furniture, sketch the room in the room layout planner first. For more layout ideas for smaller spaces, see our apartment living room ideas.

Day 4: Choose a Fresh Color Palette

Color is what makes a refresh feel like a real change. You do not need to paint; you can shift a room's palette with textiles, art and accessories alone.

Pick one base color, from a sofa, rug or wall you are keeping, and build around it with the 60-30-10 approach: about 60 percent dominant color, 30 percent secondary and 10 percent accent. The room color palette generator creates a 60-30-10 palette from a single base color so you can see combinations before buying.

Paint, if your lease allows it

If you have landlord permission, one painted accent wall or a freshly painted bedroom is the biggest single change you can make. Warm whites, soft greiges and muted greens are easy, versatile choices; our guide to pastel colors for home covers softer palettes that still feel sophisticated.

Renter note: Many leases require written approval before painting and may ask you to repaint the original color when you move out. If painting is off the table, shift the palette with curtains, pillows, a throw, a rug and art instead.

Day 5: Swap Textiles and Soft Furnishings

Textiles carry the new palette into every room. Keep your pillow inserts and change only the covers, which is cheaper and easier to store.

  • Pillows: two to five per sofa, in at least two textures and a mix of sizes.
  • Throw: one textured throw draped over an arm or corner.
  • Curtains: floor-length panels, with the rod mounted at least 4 to 6 inches above the window frame and extending 8 to 12 inches beyond it on each side.
  • Bedding: fresh sheets, a duvet cover in the new palette and a textured coverlet at the foot of the bed.
  • Rug: if your current rug is too small, sizing up is one of the most noticeable upgrades. In most apartment living rooms, an 8 x 10 ft rug lets the front legs of the seating sit on it.

Day 6: Fix the Lighting

Lighting affects how every color and texture you have chosen looks. Spend day six making the apartment glow.

  • Swap bulbs to warm white, around 2700K, in living areas and bedrooms, and choose a CRI of 90 or higher.
  • Make sure every room has at least three light sources at different heights: overhead, a floor lamp and a table lamp or sconce.
  • Add plug-in dimmers or smart bulbs so you can lower the light in the evening.
  • Clean or replace yellowed lampshades with linen or pleated fabric shades.

Designer tip: Look at each room after dark with only the lamps on. Any corner that falls into shadow is a spot for a small lamp or plug-in sconce. Evenly lit corners make a room feel bigger and more finished.

Day 7: Style, Add Greenery and Finishing Touches

The final day is about polish. Restyle shelves and surfaces using groups of three with varied heights, and leave some negative space. Add one large plant to the living room and a smaller one to the bedroom or bathroom. Hang or lean art, aiming for the center of each piece around 57 to 60 inches from the floor, or 8 to 10 inches above furniture; our guide on how to hang wall art covers the details.

Finish with small sensory details: a candle or diffuser by the entry, fresh flowers on the table, matching soap dispensers in the kitchen and bath, and a tray to corral keys and mail.

Seasonal and Budget Home Refresh Ideas

Seasonal home refresh

A seasonal home refresh keeps an apartment feeling current without redecorating. Keep your furniture and base palette neutral, then rotate a small set of textiles and accents a few times a year. Store off-season pieces in labeled bins under the bed or in a closet.

How to refresh your living room on a budget

If you can only refresh one room, make it the living room. These low-cost moves deliver the most change for under about $300:

  • New pillow covers in two textures and one throw ($50–$120).
  • Warm bulbs and one additional floor or table lamp ($40–$150).
  • One large floor plant in a simple pot ($40–$100).
  • A large mirror or piece of art leaned on a console ($60–$200, or free if you move one from another room).
  • A tray and a few books or objects for the coffee table (often free from what you own).
