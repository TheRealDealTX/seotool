A good bathroom drawer organizer turns a deep, jumbled drawer into a set of clearly defined compartments where every product has a spot and you can see it at a glance. The most useful types are modular trays that you combine to fit your drawer exactly, and double layer drawer organizers with a sliding top tray that doubles the usable surface in deeper drawers. Pair them with an under-sink system and your vanity can hold far more than you think, while the counter stays clear.

This guide covers how to measure, which organizer types suit which drawers, how to lay out a vanity drawer by routine, under sink organization around awkward plumbing, and toiletry storage ideas that make a small rental bathroom feel like a hotel suite. Most of it costs $30 to $150 per vanity.

How to Measure for a Bathroom Drawer Organizer

The number one reason organizers fail is that they do not fit. Measure every drawer before you buy anything.

  • Interior width: Measure side to side inside the drawer box, not the drawer front.
  • Interior depth: Measure front to back inside the box. Many vanity drawers are 16 to 19 in. deep inside.
  • Usable height: Measure from the drawer bottom to the underside of the counter or the frame above, minus about 1/4 in. so things do not catch when the drawer closes.
  • Obstructions: Note any U-shaped cutouts for plumbing, slide hardware that intrudes inside, or a lip at the front.
  • Your tallest item: Measure your tallest bottle that needs to stand upright in that drawer.

Write the measurements on a sketch of each drawer. When shopping, choose trays that fill the width and depth with no more than about 1/2 in. of slack in total, or the trays will slide around every time you open the drawer.

Materials: what holds up in a bathroom

Plastic and acrylic are waterproof and easy to wipe out, which makes them the most practical choice near the sink. Bamboo looks warmer and tolerates humidity better than most woods, but dry it if it gets wet. Avoid unsealed wood and fabric bins in drawers that get damp items. Add a washable silicone or cork liner underneath to catch spills and stop trays sliding.

How Double Layer Drawer Organizers Work

A double layer drawer organizer splits one deep drawer into two levels. The upper tray sits on rails or ledges and slides back to reveal a lower compartment. Some versions use two stacking trays instead of rails, where you lift the top one out.

When a double layer makes sense

  • Your drawer has at least 5 to 6 in. of usable height.
  • You have lots of small items you use daily (top layer) and backups or less frequent items (bottom layer).
  • You share the vanity and want to split items by person: one layer each.

How to load it

Put your daily items, such as toothbrush case, skincare, deodorant and a hairbrush, in the top tray so you can grab them without moving anything. Use the lower level for refills, travel minis, first aid, nail tools and items you reach for weekly. Keep bottles that need to stand upright out of the double layer; they belong in a deeper drawer or under the sink.

Designer tip: Make sure the sliding top tray is no more than half the drawer's depth, or you will not be able to reach the lower level with the drawer only partly open.

Under Sink Organization Around Pipes

The cabinet under the sink is usually the largest storage space in a small bathroom, and the most wasted. The P-trap (the curved drain pipe) splits it in two, so standard shelves rarely fit.

Best under-sink solutions

  • Two-tier pull-out organizers sit on either side of the drain. Look for ones about 10 to 12 in. wide each that slide out on runners.
  • U-shaped or adjustable shelves wrap around the pipe with removable sections.
  • Clear stacking bins with handles pull out like drawers and keep categories together: cleaning, hair care, backups.
  • Door-mounted racks on the inside of the cabinet doors hold hair tools, a small trash bag roll or spray bottles. Check that they clear the pipe when the door closes.
  • A tension rod mounted across the top of the cabinet holds spray bottles by their triggers.

Renter note: Keep the shut-off valves under the sink accessible so you or maintenance can reach them quickly in a leak. Place a waterproof tray or mat on the cabinet floor; it protects the base from drips and is something many landlords appreciate. Toiletry Storage Ideas Beyond the Drawers

If your vanity has few drawers, or none, these toiletry storage ideas fill the gap.

  • A tray on the counter: One stone, marble-look or wooden tray holding a soap pump and two or three daily products looks curated. Everything else goes away.
  • Medicine cabinet organizers: Small acrylic risers and magnetic strips (for tweezers and clippers) double the space in a mirrored cabinet.
  • A slim rolling cart: A narrow cart beside the toilet or vanity fits a 6 to 8 in. gap and holds backups.
  • Over-the-toilet shelving: A freestanding unit uses dead wall space with no holes.
  • Matching dispensers: Decanting shampoo, conditioner, body wash and hand soap into matching bottles transforms a cluttered shower or counter.
  • Lidded jars: Glass apothecary jars for cotton pads, swabs and bath salts look elegant and keep things dry.

How to Make Your Bathroom Storage Look High-End

Organized and beautiful are not always the same thing. A few choices make your bathroom drawers and counters feel like a boutique hotel.

  • Stick to one material per drawer. All clear acrylic, all white, or all bamboo. Mixing colors and textures makes even a tidy drawer look busy.
  • Decant whenever possible. Packaging is designed to stand out on a store shelf, which is exactly what you do not want at home.
  • Use labels sparingly. Small, simple labels on bins under the sink are helpful; labels on every tray in a drawer are not needed.
  • Edit the counter to three items or fewer. A soap pump, a small vase and a candle, for example.
  • Choose rolled towels and a matching set. White or one soft neutral, stored rolled in a basket or on a shelf.

The most luxurious bathroom counter is the one with almost nothing on it.

These habits follow the same principles as quiet luxury interior design: fewer, better things, and plenty of breathing room.

Putting It All Together: Organize a Vanity in an Afternoon

  • Empty every drawer and the under-sink cabinet onto a towel.
  • Edit: toss expired products, combine duplicates, and relocate anything that does not belong in the bathroom.
  • Measure each drawer's interior width, depth and usable height, plus the space around the pipe.
  • Group items by routine: morning, grooming, makeup, hair, hot tools, backups.
  • Buy organizers to fit: modular trays for shallow drawers, a double layer organizer for medium drawers, upright bins for deep ones, pull-outs under the sink.
  • Line drawers with a washable liner, then load daily items at the front of the top drawer.
  • Style the counter with one tray and no more than three items.

Expect to spend roughly $30 to $150 for a typical single vanity, depending on how many drawers you have and whether you choose acrylic, bamboo or custom inserts.
