The best small laundry room ideas do three things: use every inch of wall above and beside the machines, hide the visual clutter of detergents and baskets behind doors or in matching containers, and give you a surface to fold on. Whether you have a true laundry room, a laundry closet in a hallway, or a stackable washer and dryer tucked into an apartment nook, the same principles apply. The space is small, but it is also used almost every day, so a few smart changes pay off quickly.

Below you will find clearance measurements for stacked and side-by-side setups, laundry room storage ideas sorted by where they go, renter-friendly makeover moves, paint and lighting guidance, and how to make a laundry area that doubles as guest or utility space feel considered rather than leftover.

How Much Space Do You Need for a Laundry Closet?

Before buying storage, measure. Machine sizes vary by model, so always check the installation guide for your specific units, but these typical ranges help you plan.

Allow roughly 1 in. of air space on each side of the machines, and several inches behind for hoses, the drain and the dryer vent. In front, plan for the door swing plus about 36 in. (91 cm) of standing room so you can load and unload comfortably.

Designer tip: Measure the closet doorway as well as the closet. Some laundry closets are wide inside but have a narrow opening, which limits what you can get in and how far the machine doors can open.

Stackable Washer Dryer Closet Ideas

A stackable washer dryer closet is the most common apartment laundry setup, and it leaves a tall, narrow column of space that is easy to waste.

Use the space above

Most stacked units stop well below the ceiling. Add one or two shelves above the dryer, leaving at least a few inches of clearance so the top unit can be pulled out for service. Store detergent and dryer sheets in a lidded bin on the lower shelf and spares above.

Use the sides

Gaps of 3 to 8 in. beside the machines fit a slim rolling cart for detergents and stain removers. Wider gaps of 10 to 12 in. can take a narrow shelving tower or a hanging rod for drip-dry items.

Use the door

An over-the-door organizer with clear or fabric pockets holds stain sticks, lint rollers, clothespins and sewing kits. On bifold doors, use slim adhesive hooks for an ironing board or a pressing cloth.

Ventilation for closets

Dryers need airflow. Louvered or vented closet doors help, and many manufacturers specify minimum openings for closet installations, especially for gas dryers. Check your dryer's manual before swapping solid doors in. If venting outside is not possible, ventless heat pump or condenser dryers are designed for closets and are common in apartments.

Decant into matching containers

Brightly labeled detergent jugs are the biggest source of visual noise in a small laundry space. Decant powder or pods into glass jars or ceramic canisters and liquid detergent into a dispenser with a spout. Matching containers make an open shelf look calm and intentional.

Plan a sorting system

A two- or three-section hamper lets you sort as you go, which saves floor space and time on laundry day. In a closet, a tall narrow hamper fits beside the machines or can live in a nearby bedroom closet instead.

Apartment Laundry Room Ideas for Renters

Apartment laundry room ideas need to come off cleanly when you move. These upgrades make a big difference without permanent changes.

  • Tension-rod shelves: A tension rod between closet walls holds hangers, and tension-mounted shelving units need no screws.
  • Freestanding shelving: An over-the-washer shelving unit stands on the floor and straddles the machines.
  • Peel-and-stick wallpaper: A small laundry closet is the perfect place for a bold pattern because you only need a roll or two. Test adhesive on a hidden spot first.
  • Plug-in lighting: A battery or plug-in LED bar under a shelf brightens a dim closet.
  • A curtain instead of doors: If closet doors are broken or awkward, a ceiling- or tension-mounted curtain hides the machines softly. Keep the original doors.
  • Removable floor tiles or a washable runner: Covers a worn floor and makes standing more comfortable.

Renter note: Never block the dryer vent or crush the vent hose when adding shelves or carts, and do not change gas, water or vent connections yourself. How to Add a Folding Surface in a Small Laundry Room

A folding surface is the one feature that makes a laundry space feel like a room rather than a closet.

Counter over front-loaders

If your front-loaders sit side by side, a countertop across the top creates a long folding station. Mount it on cleats or brackets attached to the walls, not resting on the machines, leaving about 1 in. of clearance above the units so vibration does not transfer. Butcher block, laminate or a sealed plywood slab all work.

Fold-down wall table

In a laundry room with a free wall, a wall-mounted drop-leaf table folds flat to about 3 to 5 in. deep when not in use.

Pull-out shelf

In a closet with a cabinet, a pull-out board on drawer slides works as a temporary folding surface and slides away.

Use another room

If your laundry is a stacked closet in a hallway, a dining table or bed is your folding surface. Keep a lightweight folding board or a large tray handy so the process stays quick.

Best Paint Colors and Finishes for a Laundry Room

Paint is the easiest way to make a small laundry room look finished, and you need very little of it. Use our paint calculator to work out how much you need; a typical closet takes less than a gallon for two coats.

Choose a moisture-friendly finish

Laundry areas see humidity, splashes and scuffs. A satin or semi-gloss finish wipes clean and holds up better than flat paint. Use a scrubbable formula if you have the choice.

Color ideas

  • Crisp white or soft warm white: Brightens a dim closet and looks clean.
  • Pale blue or sage: Fresh, calm colors that suit the space. Our guide to pastel colors for home has more soft options.
  • Deep green, navy or charcoal: A dark color in a closet with good lighting looks rich and hides scuffs.
  • Color drenching: Painting walls, trim and even the ceiling in one color makes a small laundry room feel bigger and more deliberate.

Renters: ask before painting, and keep the original color name so you can repaint before you leave if required.

Lighting a Small Laundry Room or Closet

Laundry closets are often lit by a single hallway fixture or nothing at all, which makes stain spotting hard.

  • Use bright, neutral light around 3000K to 4000K with a high CRI (90+) so you can see stains and match colors.
  • Add an LED strip under a shelf above the machines to light the work area directly.
  • Battery-powered motion-sensor lights work well inside closets with no wiring.
  • For a laundry room with a ceiling fixture, a simple flush mount in your chosen metal finish looks more polished than a bare bulb.

Laundry in Guest Rooms and Shared Spaces

In many homes and larger apartments, the laundry shares space with a guest room, a home office or a utility hallway. When that happens, it should blend in.

  • Hide the machines: Closet doors, a curtain or a sliding panel keeps the room feeling like a bedroom or office when not in use.
  • Stock a guest basket: Small labeled containers of detergent pods, dryer sheets and a stain stick let visitors do their own laundry without asking.
  • Add hanging space: A wall hook rail or a slim garment rack doubles as a drying rack and somewhere for guests to hang clothes.
  • Soften the noise: Anti-vibration pads under the machines and a rug on hard floors reduce sound, which matters when someone is sleeping nearby.

Putting It All Together: A Small Laundry Room Checklist

  • Measure the space, the doorway and the clearances behind and in front of the machines.
  • Check your dryer manual for closet ventilation requirements.
  • Add shelves above the machines, leaving service clearance.
  • Fill side gaps with a slim rolling cart or narrow tower.
  • Decant detergents into matching containers.
  • Add a folding surface: countertop, fold-down table or pull-out board.
  • Paint in a satin or semi-gloss finish, or add peel-and-stick wallpaper.
  • Install bright, high-CRI task lighting.
  • Keep a sorting hamper and a small bin for lint and dryer sheets.

A small laundry room or closet rarely needs a big budget. A shelf, a few matching containers, good light and a fresh coat of paint typically cost $150 to $500 together and make laundry day noticeably easier.
