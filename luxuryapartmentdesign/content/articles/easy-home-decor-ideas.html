The best easy home decor ideas need no tools, no skills and very little money. They are styling moves: rearranging what you own, editing surfaces, adding light and texture, and putting things at the right height and scale. Done together, they can make an apartment feel freshly decorated in a single weekend.

Below are simple home decor ideas grouped by how long they take, from 15-minute fixes to weekend decor projects, with typical costs and the exact measurements designers use. Everything here works in a rental, and nothing requires a drill, a paintbrush or a sewing machine.

Quick Decorating Ideas You Can Do in 15 Minutes

Start here. These quick decorating ideas cost nothing and you will notice the difference immediately.

  • Clear one surface completely, then put back only one to three objects. Coffee tables, kitchen counters and nightstands make the biggest difference.
  • Fold and drape a throw over the arm or back corner of the sofa instead of bunching it on a cushion.
  • Turn on lamps, not the overhead light, in the evening. Instantly warmer.
  • Hide the cords behind furniture or bundle them with a velcro tie.
  • Remove product packaging from view: tissue boxes, cleaning sprays, branded bottles.
  • Open the curtains fully and push them past the window frame so you get every bit of daylight.
  • Plump and chop the pillows, or simply line them up neatly by size from the arms inward.

If you only have time for one, clear the surfaces. Clutter hides good furniture, and an empty, wiped-down table looks more considered than any accessory you could buy.

Rearrange the Furniture You Already Own

A new layout feels like a new room, and it is free. Spend an hour trying a different arrangement in your living room or bedroom.

Living room layout tips

  • Pull the sofa a few inches off the wall if you can spare the space. Even 3 to 4 inches stops a room looking like a waiting area.
  • Angle seating toward each other to create a conversation zone, rather than lining every piece up facing the TV.
  • Keep about 16 to 18 inches between the sofa and coffee table, and walkways around 30 to 36 inches wide.
  • Place the largest piece of furniture on the longest wall or facing the room's focal point, such as a window or fireplace.

Swap items between rooms

Move a lamp from the bedroom to the living room, a chair from the dining table to a reading corner, or a plant from the kitchen to the entry. Things you have stopped seeing look new in a different context. Before you move anything heavy, sketch the room or test ideas in the room layout planner.

Restyle Shelves, Tables and Surfaces

Styling is the difference between "stuff on a shelf" and a shelf that looks designed. A few simple rules do almost all the work.

The rule of three

Group objects in odd numbers, usually three, with varied heights: something tall (a vase or lamp), something medium (a stack of books), and something low (a small bowl or candle). Vary shapes too, mixing round, square and organic forms.

Shelf styling formula

  • Remove everything, dust, and put back about two-thirds of what was there.
  • Alternate vertical book rows and horizontal stacks of two or three books.
  • Place an object on top of each horizontal stack.
  • Leave some negative space on every shelf. Empty space makes objects look more valuable.
  • Zigzag the eye: if a large piece sits on the left of one shelf, put one on the right of the shelf below.

Use trays

A tray turns loose items into a vignette. Put one on the coffee table, the kitchen counter by the stove for oils and salt, or a dresser for perfume and jewelry. Wood, marble, rattan and brass trays all look more expensive than plastic.

Designer tip: Take hardcover books out of their dust jackets. The cloth covers underneath are often a pleasing neutral, and they instantly look more collected and less like a bookstore display.

Add Texture and Softness With Textiles

Swapping textiles is the quickest way to change a room's mood without buying furniture. Look for natural, matte materials such as linen, cotton, wool and jute; they read as higher-end than shiny synthetics.

  • Pillow covers: keep your inserts and swap only the covers. Choose two to five pillows in a mix of sizes and at least two textures. Our guide on how to style pillows on a couch has layouts for every sofa.
  • Throw blanket: one chunky knit, linen or wool throw is enough.
  • Bedding: crisp white or tonal sheets, plus a textured coverlet folded at the foot of the bed, look like a boutique hotel.
  • Rug: layer a smaller patterned rug over a larger jute or flatweave one, or replace a rug that is too small. In living rooms, at least the front legs of seating should sit on the rug.

Bring in Plants and Fresh Flowers

Living greenery makes any room feel cared for. One large plant usually has more impact than several small ones, so if you buy one thing, make it a floor plant about 4 to 6 feet tall in a simple, heavy pot.

Improve the Lighting in Minutes

Lighting changes how every other decor choice looks. A few no-tool fixes:

  • Replace cool bulbs with warm white, around 2700K, in living areas and bedrooms.
  • Add at least one table or floor lamp to every room so light comes from more than one height.
  • Use smart bulbs or plug-in dimmers to lower light in the evening.
  • Place candles in groups of three on a tray for an instant focal point at dinner.
  • Put a small lamp on a bookshelf or kitchen counter for a soft glow.

Lean, don't hang

Leaning a large mirror against a wall or setting framed art on a console or dresser is the simplest way to fill a bare wall. Overlap two frames of different sizes for a relaxed, layered look, and anchor tall leaning pieces so they cannot tip. When you are ready to hang art, see our guide on how to hang wall art.

Renter note: Leaning art, freestanding plants, rugs, lamps and textiles are all fully renter friendly. If you want to go further with adhesive hooks or removable wallpaper, check your lease first and test any adhesive in a hidden spot.

Small Changes With Big Payoff

A handful of tiny swaps add polish that people notice without being able to name:

  • Decant hand soap and dish soap into matching glass or ceramic bottles.
  • Fold towels in thirds and hang them neatly in one consistent color.
  • Put a small dish by the door for keys so they stop landing on every surface.
  • Set a bowl of lemons, green apples or seasonal fruit on the kitchen counter.
  • Store remotes in a lidded box or basket.
  • Add a scented candle or diffuser by the entry. A pleasant smell makes a space feel cared for.

Putting It All Together

Pick one room and work through these easy home decor ideas in order: 15-minute fixes first, then rearrange, restyle surfaces, swap textiles, add plants and fix the lighting. By the end of a weekend, the room will look intentional and calm, and you will likely have spent very little.
