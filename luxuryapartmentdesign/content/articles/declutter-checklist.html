A declutter checklist turns a vague, overwhelming job into a series of small decisions you can finish. Instead of standing in the middle of a crowded apartment wondering where to begin, you work one room and one category at a time, tick items off, and see progress after every session. This room by room declutter checklist is built for apartments, where a single overstuffed closet affects how the whole home feels.

Below you will find a simple sorting method, a realistic schedule, and a separate checklist for every room: entry, living room, kitchen, bathroom, bedroom, closets, home office, and the hidden spots people forget. Tick items as you go; the checklist works on screen or as a decluttering checklist printable straight from your browser.

The goal is not an empty, sterile apartment. It is a home where everything you own has a place, and everything in that place is something you use or love.

How to Declutter Your Apartment: The Method

Every effective decluttering system comes down to making a decision about each object once. Set up four labeled bags or boxes before you start:

  • Keep: you use it regularly or genuinely love it, and it has a home.
  • Donate or sell: it is in good condition but no longer serves you.
  • Recycle or trash: broken, expired, stained, or missing parts.
  • Relocate: it belongs in another room. Do not walk it there now; you will get distracted. Deliver the whole box at the end of the session.

Use these questions when you hesitate:

  • Have I used this in the last 12 months? (For seasonal items, the last full season.)
  • Would I buy this again today at full price?
  • Do I own a duplicate that does the same job better?
  • Am I keeping it out of guilt, or "just in case"?
  • If I let it go, could I replace it for under $20 in under 20 minutes?

What about sentimental items?

Leave letters, photos, inherited pieces, and keepsakes until the very end, after the easy rooms have built your decision-making confidence. Give yourself one memory box per person and keep what fits. Photograph bulky items, such as a child's artwork or an old team jersey, before letting them go; the photo often holds the memory as well as the object did. If a decision feels too hard, put the item in a dated box and revisit it in six months rather than stalling the whole project.

Designer tip: Work from the doorway inward and from top to bottom. Clearing high shelves first means dust and items fall onto areas you have not cleaned yet, not onto finished ones.

How Long Does Decluttering an Apartment Take?

Time depends on how much you own and how quickly you make decisions. These are typical ranges for a first full pass in a lived-in apartment, working steadily:

AreaTypical first passDifficultyGood session length
Entry and hallway30–60 minutesEasyOne session
Bathroom1–2 hoursEasyOne session
Living room2–4 hoursMediumTwo sessions
Kitchen3–6 hoursMediumSplit by zone: pantry, cabinets, drawers
Bedroom2–3 hoursMediumTwo sessions
Clothes closet3–5 hoursHardSplit by category
Home office and paperwork2–6 hoursHardSeveral short sessions
Sentimental items and photosVaries widelyHardestSave for last

A one-bedroom apartment typically takes two to four weekends for a thorough first pass. Sessions of 60 to 90 minutes are more sustainable than one exhausting marathon.

Before your first session

  • Block time on your calendar for each session.
  • Gather bags, boxes, labels, a marker, and cleaning supplies.
  • Look up local donation drop-offs, textile recycling, and e-waste collection.
  • Check your building's rules for bulk trash pickup.
  • Start with the easiest room to build momentum.
  • Set a timer and put on music or a podcast.

Entry and Hallway Declutter Checklist

The entry is small but sets the tone for the whole apartment, and it is the fastest win.

  • Remove shoes you have not worn this season; keep only the pairs in daily rotation by the door.
  • Clear coats down to one or two per person on hooks; store the rest.
  • Empty the key bowl or tray of receipts, coins, and dead batteries.
  • Sort and shred any mail older than a week.
  • Remove tote bags beyond the three or four you actually use.
  • Check umbrellas and return or donate extras.
  • Wipe down surfaces and the inside of the shoe cabinet.

Living Room Declutter Checklist

  • Clear every surface: coffee table, side tables, console, windowsills.
  • Edit decor objects; keep your favorites and store or donate the rest.
  • Sort books; donate those you will not reread or reference.
  • Recycle old magazines and catalogs.
  • Gather all cables and chargers, label the ones you keep, and recycle unidentified ones.
  • Donate DVDs, CDs, and games you no longer play.
  • Reduce throw pillows and blankets to what the sofa comfortably holds plus one spare.
  • Remove dying or struggling plants.
  • Empty the media console drawers and baskets.

Kitchen Declutter Checklist

Kitchens hold the most duplicates. Work zone by zone: pantry, fridge, cabinets, drawers, counters.

  • Toss expired food, spices, and condiments.
  • Combine duplicate open packages.
  • Clear the fridge and freezer of old leftovers and freezer-burned items.
  • Match every food container to a lid; recycle the orphans.
  • Reduce mugs and glasses to what fits on one shelf.
  • Donate single-purpose gadgets you have not used in a year.
  • Keep one set of measuring cups, one can opener, one peeler.
  • Pare down excess takeout cutlery, napkins, and sauce packets.
  • Remove worn or stained dish towels and sponges.
  • Clear counters down to items used daily.
  • Edit the under-sink area and toss empty cleaning products.

Bathroom Declutter Checklist

  • Toss expired medications through a pharmacy take-back program, not the trash or toilet.
  • Discard makeup past its use-by date and dried-out products.
  • Recycle empty or nearly empty bottles.
  • Remove hotel toiletries and samples you will not use; donate unopened ones.
  • Replace old toothbrushes and razors.
  • Reduce towels to two sets per person plus a guest set.
  • Clear the counter to daily essentials only.
  • Wipe drawers and cabinets before putting items back.

For small bathrooms, bathroom drawer organizers help keep the edited products tidy under the sink.

Renter note: Many buildings restrict what goes down the trash chute or into bulk pickup, especially electronics, batteries, paint, and furniture. Check with your building manager before a big clear-out so you do not get a fine or a note from the super.

Bedroom and Closet Declutter Checklist

Bedroom

  • Clear nightstands to a lamp, a book, and one small dish or tray.
  • Pull everything out from under the bed and decide on each item.
  • Reduce bedding to two sets per bed.
  • Remove anything that does not belong in a bedroom: work papers, exercise gear, laundry piles.
  • Empty and edit dresser drawers one at a time.
  • Donate or discard worn pillows.

Clothes closet

  • Take everything out of one category at a time: tops, bottoms, dresses, outerwear, shoes.
  • Try on anything you are unsure about.
  • Donate items that do not fit, are uncomfortable, or have not been worn in a year.
  • Recycle stained, torn, or stretched items through textile recycling.
  • Pair socks and toss singles.
  • Reduce hangers to one style and return wire hangers to the dry cleaner.
  • Store off-season clothing in labeled bins or under the bed.
  • Edit bags, belts, scarves, and jewelry.

A useful tip: turn all hangers backward. When you wear something, hang it back the right way. After six months, the backward hangers show you what you do not wear.

Home Office and Paperwork Declutter Checklist

  • Shred old bank statements and bills you no longer need to keep (follow your tax adviser's guidance on retention).
  • Scan important documents and store the originals in one labeled folder.
  • Test pens and markers and toss the dead ones.
  • Recycle old electronics through an e-waste program after wiping data.
  • Clear your desk surface down to what you use every day.
  • Unsubscribe from catalogs and junk mail to stop the inflow.
  • Organize digital files, downloads, and the desktop while you are at it.

Hidden Spots Checklist

  • Hall or linen closet: towels, sheets, cleaning supplies.
  • Storage unit or building storage cage, if you have one.
  • Balcony: broken pots, old furniture cushions, empty planters.
  • Car trunk and glove box.
  • Junk drawer: keep only what has a clear purpose.
  • Gift wrap and craft supplies.
  • Luggage: use suitcases to store off-season items.

How to Keep Your Apartment Clutter-Free

Decluttering once is the hard part; staying decluttered is about small habits.

  • One in, one out: when something new comes home, something similar leaves.
  • A donation bag lives in your closet so letting go is effortless.
  • Ten-minute evening reset: return everything to its home before bed.
  • Handle mail daily: recycle, act, or file at the door.
  • A quick seasonal pass: repeat this checklist lightly every three to six months.
