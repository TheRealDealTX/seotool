Every new year arrives with a long list of shoulds. Lose weight. Save money. Read more. Scroll less. By February, most of those resolutions are quietly packed away with the holiday decorations.

A few years ago, I tried something different. Instead of a list, I chose a single word. Just one. And I let it decide what I wanted more of—and what I was ready to have less of.

It changed my year more than any resolution ever had.

If you’re looking for word of the year ideas, or wondering whether one little word can really make a difference, let’s explore it together. It starts with a simple question: More of what? Less of what?

What Is a Word of the Year?

A word of the year is a single word you choose to guide your intentions, decisions, and attention for the next twelve months. Instead of setting a dozen goals, you pick one word—like calm, brave, or enough—and let it act as a compass whenever you’re deciding how to spend your time and energy.

It’s different from a resolution. A resolution is something you do or don’t do: “Go to the gym three times a week.” Miss a week, and it feels broken. A word is something you return to. You can’t fail at gentle. You can only come back to it.

That’s what makes it powerful. It’s forgiving, flexible, and surprisingly sticky.

The “More of This, Less of That” Approach

Here’s the part most people miss. A good word of the year works in two directions at once.

It tells you what to welcome more of. And it quietly tells you what to have less of.

If your word is peace, you’ll want more quiet mornings, more honest conversations, more walks. And less drama, less overcommitting, less doom-scrolling at midnight.

If your word is courage, you’ll want more speaking up, more trying new things, more asking for what you want. And less hiding, less waiting for permission, less people-pleasing.

The word does the sorting for you. Every invitation, habit, and choice can be held up against it: Does this give me more of my word, or less?

Word of the Year Ideas to Get You Started

There’s no perfect list, but browsing helps you notice which words make something in you light up—or flinch. Here are some word of the year ideas grouped by what you might be craving.

If you want more calm

Peace. Ease. Gentle. Still. Breathe. Slow. Rest. Simple. Soften.

If you want more courage

Brave. Bold. Rise. Leap. Voice. Try. Begin. Fearless. Dare.

If you want more joy

Play. Delight. Light. Savor. Laugh. Wonder. Yes. Bloom.

If you want more focus

Focus. Clarity. Intentional. Commit. Finish. Discipline. Essential. Less.

If you want more connection

Connect. Open. Love. Together. Kindness. Present. Listen. Home.

If you want more growth

Grow. Learn. Stretch. Evolve. Becoming. Curious. Build. Expand.

Words for Particular Seasons of Life

Sometimes the best word of the year ideas come from the season you’re in rather than the goals you have. Life doesn’t always hand us a fresh start on January first. Here are a few words that fit specific chapters.

  • A year of grief or loss: Tender. Gentle. Remember. Breathe. These words give you permission to go slowly and not expect too much of yourself.
  • A new baby or a big caregiving role: Patience. Enough. Grace. Lean. Words that soften the pressure to do it all perfectly.
  • A career change or new venture: Begin. Build. Bold. Learn. Words that welcome being a beginner again.
  • A year of healing: Restore. Mend. Rest. Nourish. Words that put recovery first.
  • An empty nest or retirement: Explore. Rediscover. Savor. Play. Words that turn open space into possibility.
  • A year that feels stuck: Shift. Move. Unstick. Open. Words that invite small changes rather than a total overhaul.

If none of these fit, that’s a good sign too. It means you know your season well enough to know what it needs. Trust that.

One more tip: your word doesn’t have to be a classic “positive” word. Some people choose no for a year of boundaries, or less for a year of simplifying. Others choose something playful, like sparkle or wild. The best word of the year ideas are the ones that feel personal, even a little surprising.

How to Choose Your Word

Choosing the word is half the magic. Rush it, and you might pick something that sounds nice but doesn’t mean much. Give it a little time, and the right word tends to find you.

1. Look back before you look ahead

Spend fifteen minutes reflecting on the year behind you. What drained you? What lit you up? When did you feel most like yourself? Write freely.

2. Make two lists

Title one “More of this” and the other “Less of that.” Fill them in without editing. More sleep, more friends, more creative time. Less rushing, less worry, less saying yes when you mean no.

3. Find the thread

Read your lists and ask: what single word would move me toward the first list and away from the second? Often a few candidates appear. Write them down.

4. Live with it for a few days

Try each candidate on. Say it out loud. Notice how it feels in your body. The right word usually brings a small sense of relief, or a nervous little thrill.

5. Choose and commit

Pick one. Don’t overthink it. There’s no wrong word—only one you’re willing to come back to again and again.

How to Live Your Word All Year

Choosing a word is easy. Remembering it in April is the real test. A few simple practices help keep it alive.

  • Put it where you’ll see it. A sticky note on the mirror. Your phone’s lock screen. A small card in your wallet.
  • Use it as a decision filter. Before saying yes to something, ask: Will this give me more of my word?
  • Check in monthly. On the first of each month, jot down one way you lived your word and one way you drifted.
  • Let it evolve. A word often reveals new layers as the year goes on. Strong might begin as physical strength and end up meaning emotional resilience.
  • Share it. Tell a friend or family member. Saying it out loud makes it real—and gives someone permission to gently remind you.

A word can also help protect your energy. If your days feel stretched thin, it can act as a boundary, helping you decide where your attention goes. I wrote more about that in how to manage your personal bandwidth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Choosing a word you think you should want. If productive makes you feel tired just reading it, it’s probably not your word. Choose what you genuinely long for, not what sounds impressive.

Treating it like a resolution. Your word isn’t a test you can fail. It’s a direction. Drifting is normal; returning is the practice.

Picking too many. Three words quickly becomes a list again. One word is easier to remember and easier to live.

Forgetting the “less of that.” Adding good things is fun. Letting go of what crowds them out is where the real change happens.

Giving up after a hard month. Some months your word will feel far away. That’s okay. Pick it back up on any ordinary Tuesday.

What a Word Can Do in a Year

One word won’t transform your life overnight. But here’s what often happens slowly.

You start noticing your word everywhere—in books, songs, conversations. You make small choices differently. You decline the extra commitment because your word is rest. You send the brave email because your word is courage. You put down the phone at dinner because your word is present.

Each choice is small. Together, they shift the shape of your year.

And when December comes, you can look back and see a thread running through it all. Not a list of things you failed to do, but a story of who you were becoming. If you enjoy this practice, you might also like the reflections in things that connect.

More of this. Less of that. And a word to help you know the difference.
