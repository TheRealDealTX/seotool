“Well, shake it up, baby, now.” If you can read that line without hearing “Twist and Shout” in your head, you’re doing better than I am.

There’s a reason that song still gets people off their chairs. It’s pure energy. Movement. A little bit of joyful chaos. And sometimes, that’s exactly what a life needs.

Sometimes the best thing you can do is shake things up. Because routines are wonderful—until they aren’t. The same commute, the same meals, the same conversations, the same thoughts on repeat. At some point, the days start to blur. You’re not unhappy, exactly. Just a little flat. A little dusty.

That’s when it’s time to shake things up. Not by blowing your life apart, but by giving it a good, intentional shake—so the stale stuff falls away and something fresh can come in.

What Does It Mean to Shake Things Up?

To shake things up means to deliberately change something familiar in order to bring in new energy, perspective, or possibility. It can be as small as taking a different route home or as big as rethinking how you spend your weekends. The point isn’t change for its own sake. It’s breaking autopilot so you can feel awake in your own life again.

Think of shaking out a rug. You’re not replacing it. You’re just releasing what’s built up so you can see the pattern again.

Why Routines Go Stale

Routines exist for good reasons. They save energy. They make life predictable. They free your mind from having to decide everything from scratch.

But the same efficiency that makes routines useful can also make them numbing. When every day looks the same, your brain stops paying close attention. It files the day under “already seen.” Weeks start to compress. Months disappear.

I wrote about this effect in life is fleeting—how novelty creates the memory markers that make time feel fuller. When nothing stands out, time seems to slip through your fingers faster.

A stale routine can also quietly shape who you think you are. “I’m not creative.” “I’m not a morning person.” “That’s just not me.” Sometimes those stories are true. Sometimes they’re just habits wearing the costume of identity.

Signs It’s Time to Shake Things Up

You don’t need a crisis to justify a change. Here are a few quieter signals:

  • You can’t remember anything specific about last week.
  • You feel restless but can’t say why.
  • Your conversations keep circling the same topics.
  • You’ve stopped being curious about anything new.
  • You look forward to very little in a typical week.
  • Your space feels cluttered, heavy, or stuck in an old version of you.

If several of these ring true, it might be more than a rut. It might be the early edges of complacency—something I break down in 11 signs you’re stuck in complacency.

Start With Your Space: Shake It Clean

One of the simplest places to start is also the most physical. Change your environment.

Our surroundings hold a lot of stuck energy. Piles of paper. Drawers that don’t close. A room arranged the same way for years. When you shake your space clean, you often find your mind follows.

Try a quick, ten-minute reset:

  • Clear one surface completely. A desk, a counter, a nightstand. Wipe it down. Put back only what you truly use.
  • Shake out the soft things. Rugs, throws, pillows. Open a window. Let fresh air in.
  • Move one thing. Rearrange a chair, swap a picture, turn your desk to face a window.
  • Remove three items that no longer belong in your life. Donate, recycle, or toss them.

It sounds small. It is small. But walking into a room that feels different sends a signal: something has changed here. That signal is often the spark for bigger shifts.

Small Ways to Shake Things Up This Week

You don’t have to overhaul your life. In fact, small experiments are often more powerful because you’ll actually do them.

Pick one. Just one. Try it for a week and notice how it feels.

Bigger Ways to Break the Pattern

Sometimes small changes reveal that a bigger shift is calling. If that’s where you are, here are a few deeper ways to break the pattern.

Learn Something That Makes You a Beginner

There’s something humbling and energizing about being bad at something new. A language. An instrument. Pottery. Rock climbing. Being a beginner pulls you out of autopilot and back into curiosity.

Travel—Even Locally

New places wake up the senses. You don’t need a passport. Explore a neighborhood you’ve never walked through. Visit a museum in your own city. Take a day trip somewhere you’ve always driven past.

Change Who You Spend Time With

The people around us shape our thinking. Joining a class, a club, or a volunteer group brings new perspectives into your life—and often new versions of yourself.

Rethink One Big Default

What’s one assumption about your life you’ve never really questioned? How you spend weekends. Where you live. What success looks like. You don’t have to change it. Just look at it with fresh eyes.

Wander Without a Plan

Leave a free afternoon open. No itinerary. No goal. Follow whatever catches your interest. I’ve found that some of the best ideas show up when I stop aiming so hard—something I explore in the wonder of the wander.

Why a Little Disruption Is Good for You

We tend to think of disruption as something to avoid. Plans falling through. Schedules thrown off. Surprises we didn’t ask for.

But chosen disruption is different. When you decide to change something on purpose, you get the benefits of novelty without the stress of chaos. You stay in the driver’s seat.

Here’s what a deliberate shake-up can give you:

  • Fresh attention. New experiences make you notice again—colors, sounds, conversations you’d normally tune out.
  • Better memories. Days with something different in them tend to stand out when you look back.
  • Unexpected ideas. A change of scene or routine can loosen thinking that has gotten stuck.
  • A clearer sense of what matters. When you step outside your habits, you often see which ones you truly value and which you were just repeating.
  • Confidence. Every small change you survive—and enjoy—reminds you that you’re more adaptable than you thought.

None of this requires a big budget or a dramatic leap. It just requires a willingness to do one thing a little differently than you did yesterday.

Shake Up Your Thinking, Too

Not every rut is external. Some of the stalest patterns live in our heads.

Try noticing the thoughts you repeat most often. “I don’t have time.” “It’s too late for that.” “That’s not for people like me.” Then ask, gently: Is this actually true? Or just familiar?

A few ways to loosen mental routines:

  • Read outside your usual lane. A genre, author, or viewpoint you’d normally skip.
  • Ask better questions. Swap “Why does this always happen?” for “What could I try differently?”
  • Write by hand for ten minutes. No editing. Just let whatever is there come out.
  • Argue the other side. Pick a belief you hold firmly and try to make the best case against it.

When your thinking shifts, your choices tend to follow.

Common Mistakes When Changing Your Routine

Changing everything at once. A total overhaul feels exciting on day one and exhausting by day ten. Small, steady shake-ups last longer.

Running from something instead of toward something. Change can be a way to avoid a feeling or a problem. Before a big shake-up, ask what you’re hoping to find, not just what you’re hoping to escape.

Throwing out the good with the stale. Some routines are anchors. Keep the ones that nourish you—sleep, movement, meaningful rituals—and shake up the rest.

Waiting for motivation. You won’t always feel like changing. Often, the energy comes after you start moving, not before.

Reflection Prompts

Take a few minutes with a notebook and answer:

  1. Which part of my life feels most on autopilot right now?
  2. When did I last feel truly curious or excited?
  3. What’s one small change I could make this week?
  4. What’s one bigger change I keep thinking about but never act on?
  5. Which of my routines do I want to protect, no matter what?

Give It a Good Shake

You don’t need a dramatic reinvention. You don’t need to quit, move, or start over.

Sometimes you just need to open a window, move a chair, take a different road, or try something you’ve never done. Little shakes. Fresh air.

Life has a way of settling. Dust collects. Patterns harden. That’s normal.

So every now and then, shake things up. Twist a little. Shout a little, if you need to.

And see what comes loose.
