Most underpaid claims aren’t denials. They’re estimates that stop short: two roof slopes instead of four, a basement floor patched when the planks are discontinued, drywall replaced with no allowance for the knockdown texture. The process

From written demand to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process. Here is the route Utah Insurance Appraiser takes once an amount dispute is confirmed.

  1. 1

    Document and supplement

    Gather the carrier’s full estimate, your own estimate, photos and correspondence. Submit a written supplement request explaining what’s missing. Many disputes close here.

  2. 2

    Make the written demand

    If the gap remains, either party can demand appraisal in writing under the policy’s appraisal condition, naming or promising to name an appraiser.

  3. 3

    Each side names an appraiser

    You and the insurer each select a competent, impartial appraiser, usually within a set number of days stated in the policy.

  4. 4

    The appraisers pick an umpire

    The two appraisers agree on a neutral umpire. If they can’t, many policies allow a court in the county where the property sits to appoint one.

  5. 5

    Appraisers work the differences

    The appraisers inspect, compare measurements and pricing, and agree on as many items as they can. Many files end with an agreed award.

  6. 6

    Umpire decides what’s left

    Remaining items go to the umpire, who reviews both positions and the evidence, then joins one appraiser on each disputed item.

Utah storm record

Events that shaped Utah claims

Utah’s biggest property losses rarely come from one kind of storm. Floods, tornadoes, windstorms, wildfire, earthquakes and record snow have all tested local policies. Utah Insurance Appraiser keeps that history in mind when reading damage and policy language.

  1. 1983

    Spring snowmelt floods

    A record snowpack melted fast, turning State Street in Salt Lake City into a sandbagged channel and flooding communities statewide.

  2. 1999

    Salt Lake City tornado

    On August 11, a tornado crossed downtown, the Avenues and Capitol Hill, damaging hundreds of buildings.

  3. 2005

    Santa Clara River flood

    In January, flooding on the Santa Clara and Virgin rivers destroyed and damaged homes in Washington County.

  4. 2011

    Davis County windstorm

    On December 1, downslope gusts over 100 mph tore through Centerville, Farmington and neighboring towns.

  5. 2017

    Brian Head Fire

    A summer wildfire in Iron and Garfield counties burned for weeks and destroyed homes and cabins.

  6. 2020

    Magna earthquake & windstorm

    A magnitude 5.7 quake in March, then a September downslope windstorm that toppled thousands of trees.

  7. 2023

    Record snowpack winter

    Months of heavy snow led to roof and carport failures, ice dams and spring flood worries across northern Utah.

Right tool, right time

Is appraisal the right move for your claim?

Appraisal solves one problem well. A good Utah Insurance Appraiser will tell you when your claim needs something else.

Appraisal usually fits

  • Coverage accepted, cost disputed
  • Repair versus replace disagreements
  • Matching and discontinued materials
  • Missing trades, code items or overhead and profit

Look elsewhere first

  • Outright denials and coverage disputes
  • Claims-handling or bad-faith concerns
  • Gaps too small to justify fees
  • A suit deadline that’s close

FAQ

Questions people ask a Utah Insurance Appraiser

Plain answers about the process, the cost and when appraisal is the wrong move.

All FAQs Glossary
What does a Utah Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your selected appraiser, we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster presents and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When does it make sense to call a Utah Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage has been accepted, you have the insurer’s estimate, and the gap between it and a contractor’s number is meaningful. As a rough guide, more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is the appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, generally yes, once any two of the three panel members sign. It doesn’t decide coverage.

What does appraisal cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work starts. The first conversation is free.

Do you work outside the Wasatch Front?

Yes. We cover all 29 Utah counties, including Cache Valley, the mountain resort areas and southern Utah.
