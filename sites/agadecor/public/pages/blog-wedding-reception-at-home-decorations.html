Wedding reception at home: decoration ideas for backyards & living rooms

AGA Décor JournalUpdated October 20266 min read

In this article

  2. Tent or no tent?
  3. Lighting does most of the work

  5. Decorating a reception inside the house

A wedding at home is personal in a way no venue can match — the garden your parents planted, the porch where you got engaged. It also means building a venue from scratch. Here’s how to approach wedding home decoration so the space feels like an event, not a party that got out of hand.

Start with zones, not décor

Walk the property and mark out five zones: arrival, ceremony, cocktails, dinner and dancing. Even in a small backyard, giving each moment its own spot creates the sense of a journey. Then decorate each zone with one clear idea instead of scattering décor everywhere.

Tent or no tent?

If you’re in Chicago between May and October, plan for a tent even if you hope not to use it. A clear-top tent keeps the sky in view; a white sailcloth tent glows at night. Inside, the decoration plan becomes simple: draping or greenery on the poles, a statement fixture overhead, and string lights from peak to edge.

Lighting does most of the work

At home, lighting is the difference between “backyard” and “wedding.” Layer it:

  • Overhead: café lights zig-zagged across the dining area or tent.
  • Table: pillar candles in hurricanes and votives in mercury glass (hurricanes keep flames safe outdoors).
  • Landscape: up-lights on trees and the house façade, lanterns along paths.

Check power early — a generator or dedicated circuits may be needed for lighting, catering and the band.

Home wedding decoration ideas

  • Long family-style tables under the trees with garland runners and mismatched candlesticks.
  • A ceremony backdrop — a draped arch, a flower wall, or simply the best tree on the property dressed with florals.
  • Lounge vignettes with rugs, loveseats and side tables so older guests have somewhere comfortable to sit.
  • Signage to guide guests from the street, to the bar and to the restrooms.
  • A dressed front door — a floral garland or a pair of urns sets the tone the moment guests arrive.

Decorating a reception inside the house

For an intimate living-room or dining-room reception, clear out as much furniture as you can and rent the rest. Swap your dining chairs for Chiavari chairs, cover the table in a floor-length linen, and concentrate florals on the mantel, the dining table and the staircase. Candles on every surface (and a fire extinguisher nearby) finish it.

What to rent

A typical at-home wedding needs: tables and chairs, linens, glassware and china, a dance floor, lighting, a tent and sidewall, restroom trailers for larger guest lists, and décor pieces like vases, candle holders and backdrops. Ask for delivery, set-up and tear-down to be included — you don’t want to be stacking chairs the morning after. See our décor rental collection for the pieces we bring.

Day-of checklist

  1. Confirm neighbours know, and check local noise and parking rules.
  2. Mow and water the lawn two days out, not the morning of.
  3. Set up the tent and power the day before.
  4. Florals and décor install the morning of — keep flowers in the shade.
  5. Have a rain plan for the ceremony that doesn’t involve the dining tent.
  6. Assign one person (not you) to handle vendors on the day.
