Corporate gift etiquette: the complete guide

Budgets, gift policies, branding, timing and cultural customs — everything you need to send business gifts with confidence.

Corporate gifts can strengthen relationships for years — or create awkward moments if they break a policy or feel like a sales pitch. These are the etiquette rules we recommend to every business that wants its gifts to land as genuine thanks.

Rule 1: Know the gift policy first

Many organizations limit what employees can accept. Common limits sit between $25 and $100 per year, and government employees, healthcare professionals and those in financial services are often subject to stricter rules or outright bans.

  • Ask your contact (or their assistant) discreetly if there's a policy.
  • When unsure, send something consumable and shareable for the whole team.
  • A charitable donation in their name is always policy-safe.

Rule 2: Set tiered budgets

Not every relationship warrants the same spend. A tiered approach keeps things fair and controllable:

1Top clients, key partners$150–$500Experiences, premium wine, personalized leather
2Regular clients$75–$150Executive gift box, crystal barware
3Wider list, prospects$30–$75Chocolate tasting box, coffee, candles
TeamYour employees$50–$200Time off, experiences, wellness

Rule 3: Understand the tax position

In the US, the deduction for business gifts is generally capped at $25 per recipient per year. Incidental costs like engraving, packaging and shipping usually don't count toward the cap, and some items (such as branded promotional goods under a small value) may be treated differently. Tax rules change and vary by country — always confirm with your accountant.

Rule 4: Keep branding subtle

A corporate gift should feel like gratitude, not advertising. Put your logo on the card, the tissue sticker or the outer box — not stamped across the gift. Better-made, unbranded items get used and kept; logo-heavy items get donated.

Rule 5: Time it to stand out

December is crowded. Consider instead:

  • Thanksgiving week or mid-January "thank you for a great year" gifts.
  • The anniversary of when you started working together.
  • Immediately after a successful launch, closing or renewal.
  • Their company's milestone or their personal promotion.

Rule 6: Respect cultural customs

  • Alcohol may be inappropriate for some recipients — choose gourmet foods or tea instead.
  • Numbers and colors carry meaning in many cultures (for example, four is considered unlucky in parts of East Asia, and white can symbolize mourning).
  • Presentation matters: in Japan, for instance, gifts are often presented and received with both hands, and may not be opened in front of the giver.

Rule 7: Write a real card

A printed "Season's greetings from all of us" is forgettable. A two-line handwritten note from the person who manages the relationship is not. Mention something specific from your year together. Our gift message generator has a client-appreciation mode with formal and warm options.

Corporate gifting checklist

  1. List recipients and assign tiers.
  2. Check gift policies for each organization.
  3. Set budget per tier and in total.
  4. Choose gifts and order personalization early.
  5. Confirm delivery addresses (home vs office).
  6. Write cards by hand.
  7. Track who received what for next year.

Frequently asked questions

Is it appropriate to give a gift to my boss?

Gifting up the chain can be awkward individually. A group gift from the team, of modest value, is the most appropriate approach.

Should I send corporate gifts to a client's home or office?

Office is the default and safer choice. Only send to a home address if the client has shared it and the relationship is personal enough.

Can I give cash or gift cards to clients?

Cash is generally inappropriate and gift cards are often treated as cash equivalents under gift policies. Choose a physical gift or experience instead.
