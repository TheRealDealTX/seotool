Holiday gift planning timeline: a stress-free checklist

A week-by-week plan from October to Christmas Eve — so every gift is thoughtful, on budget and on time.

Planning3 min readUpdated 2026-09-10
In this guide
  1. Early October: set your budget and list
  2. Late October: order anything personalized
  3. November: buy the main gifts
  4. Early December: wrap and write
  5. Mid-December: watch shipping deadlines
  6. Last week: the VIP last-minute playbook
  7. After the holidays

Most holiday gifting stress comes from doing everything in the last two weeks. Spread it out and you'll spend less, choose better and actually enjoy the season. Here's our timeline.

Early October: set your budget and list

  • Write down everyone you'll buy for — family, friends, clients, teachers, hosts.
  • Set a total budget, then divide it. Our gift budget planner can auto-split it by relationship.
  • Start a running note of hints people drop.

Late October: order anything personalized

  • Engraving, monograms, custom art and photo books can take two to four weeks — more in December.
  • Book experiences that sell out: shows, restaurant reservations, spa days.
  • Order corporate gifts in bulk now for early-December delivery.

November: buy the main gifts

  • Use early sales for planned purchases — not impulse buys.
  • Track deliveries and check items as they arrive.
  • Buy wrapping supplies: heavy paper, real ribbon, tissue and cards.

Early December: wrap and write

  • Set up a wrapping station and do it in one or two sessions. See how to wrap like a boutique.
  • Write cards by hand — use our gift message generator for inspiration.
  • Ship anything going long-distance.

Mid-December: watch shipping deadlines

Carriers publish holiday shipping cut-offs each year, typically falling in the week before Christmas for standard services. Check the current dates for your carrier and aim to ship several days earlier to be safe.

Last week: the VIP last-minute playbook

  • Give an experience: book it online and present the confirmation in a beautiful card.
  • Shop local boutiques — they often wrap beautifully for free.
  • Choose consumable luxuries: chocolate, wine, flowers, coffee.

After the holidays

  • Note what worked and what people loved for next year.
  • Send thank-you notes for gifts you received.
  • Consider mid-January client gifts — they stand out when the rush is over.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start holiday gift shopping?

Start planning in early October and order personalized items by late October. Buy most gifts in November and use December for wrapping and shipping.

How do I set a holiday gift budget?

Decide a total you're comfortable with, list everyone, then allocate more to closer relationships. A budget planner makes it easy to see what's left.
