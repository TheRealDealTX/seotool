Easy Paper Shamrock Garland for St. Patrick’s Day

Cut, fold and string a cheerful paper shamrock garland in about an hour, with layered and 3D versions for extra charm.

A paper shamrock garland is one of the quickest ways to make a room feel ready for St. Patrick’s Day. With a few sheets of green cardstock, a homemade template and some twine, you can make a six-foot strand that drapes across a mantel, a window or a doorway in an afternoon.

This tutorial walks you through drawing a clean shamrock template from three hearts, cutting a stack of shapes efficiently, and stringing them so they hang straight instead of flipping around. You’ll also find a layered version and a folded 3D version if you want more depth.

It’s a great project to share with kids: older children can cut, and younger ones can sort colors and help thread the twine.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. 1

    Draw a shamrock template

    Fold a small square of scrap paper in half and draw half of a heart about 2 in. tall along the fold; cut it out to get a symmetrical heart. Trace that heart three times on cardboard with the points meeting in the center, then add a curved stem about 1 in. long. Cut out the full shamrock, which should end up roughly 4 to 4.5 in. across.

  2. 2

    Trace and stack-cut

    Trace the template onto the back of your cardstock, fitting about four shamrocks per sheet. To save time, staple two sheets together outside the traced lines and cut both layers at once. For a 6 ft garland spaced 3 in. apart, plan on about 16 to 18 shamrocks.

  3. 3

    Smooth the edges

    Go back around each shape with small scissors to clean up the inner dips between leaves, which is where most cutting looks rough. Erase any pencil marks that show on the front. Thin stems tear easily, so cut them a little thicker than you think you need, about 1/4 in. wide.

  4. 4

    Add accents

    For a layered look, cut a second shamrock about 3/4 in. smaller from a different green and glue it centered on top. A small gold circle or a white dot in the middle also reads nicely from across the room. Let the glue set for a few minutes so the layers don’t shift.

  5. 5

    Punch hanging holes

    Punch two holes near the top of each shamrock, one in each upper leaf, about 1/2 in. from the edge. Two holes keep the shamrocks facing forward instead of spinning on the string. Keep the hole positions consistent by marking them on your template first.

  6. 6

    String the garland

    Leave about 12 in. of twine free at one end for tying. Thread the twine from back to front through one hole and front to back through the other, then slide the shamrock into place. Alternate shades of green and space them about 3 in. apart, finishing with another 12 in. tail.

  7. 7

    Hang it up

    Tie the tails to small command hooks, cabinet knobs or nails, letting the garland swag slightly in the middle. If shamrocks slide toward the center, add a tiny dab of glue or a piece of tape behind each hole. Mini clothespins are a good alternative if you want to rearrange later.

Tips for success

  • Heavier cardstock (65 to 110 lb) hangs flatter and survives a few seasons of storage much better than construction paper.
  • Glitter cardstock dulls scissors quickly; keep an old pair just for it.
  • If kids are helping, pre-trace the shapes with a thick marker so they have a clear line to follow and use child-safe scissors.
  • Store the finished garland wrapped around a piece of cardboard to prevent tangles.

Make it your own

Folded 3D shamrocks

Cut three identical shamrocks per unit, fold each in half along the stem, and glue the back halves together so the leaves fan out. String them through the center fold with a needle and thread for a dimensional garland.

Patterned paper mix

Swap in scrapbook papers with plaid, gingham or tiny dots in green tones. Mixing solids with patterns looks intentional and uses up leftover scraps.

Mini shamrock string

Shrink the template to about 2 in. and use a shamrock paper punch if you have one. Strung tightly, these look lovely draped on a bookshelf or wrapped around a jar.

Frequently asked questions

How many shamrocks do I need for a garland?

Plan on roughly four shamrocks per foot when they are about 4 in. wide and spaced 3 in. apart. A 6 ft strand uses 16 to 18, and you can space them wider if you’re short on paper.

Can I hang a paper garland outdoors?

Paper will curl and fade in damp weather. For a covered porch, you can laminate the shapes or cut them from craft foam or felt instead, which holds up much better.

What age is this craft good for?

Kids around 6 and up can trace and cut with supervision, and younger children can help glue accents and thread pre-punched shapes onto the twine.
