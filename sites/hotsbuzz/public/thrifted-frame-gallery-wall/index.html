Thrifted Frame Gallery Wall With Painted Frames

Mismatched secondhand frames become a cohesive thrifted frame gallery wall with a coat of paint, paper templates and a little planning.

By the HotsBuzz team · September 15, 2026

  • Skill levelModerate
  • TimeHalf a day plus drying
  • Budget$30-$60

A thrifted frame gallery wall is a budget-friendly way to fill a big empty wall with personality. Secondhand stores and yard sales are full of frames in every shape and finish, and a single coat of paint pulls the whole mismatched collection together.

The secret is to vary the shapes and sizes while keeping the color consistent. Ornate gold frames, plain wooden ones and chunky modern styles all look intentional when they share one finish, whether that’s matte black, warm white or a bold color.

This guide covers how to choose frames, prep them for paint, plan your layout with paper templates and hang everything level. Plan on a weekend: one day for painting and drying, and a few hours for hanging. Frames at secondhand shops often cost a few dollars each, so the total usually lands between $30 and $60 including paint and hardware.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. 1

    Pick your frames

    Look for solid frames with tight corners and interesting profiles; the glass and art don’t matter. Mix at least one large anchor frame, several medium frames and a couple of small or oval pieces. Skip frames with loose joints or chipped glass that could cut you.

  2. 2

    Disassemble and clean

    Remove backs, glass and old art, setting the glass aside somewhere safe and wearing gloves if edges are rough. Pull out leftover staples with pliers. Scrub each frame with soapy water or degreaser to remove dust and grime, then let it dry completely. Fill any small dings with wood filler and sand it smooth once dry.

  3. 3

    Scuff the surface

    Lightly sand glossy or lacquered frames with a fine sanding sponge so the paint can grip. You’re not removing the finish, just dulling the shine. Wipe away the dust with a damp cloth.

  4. 4

    Paint the frames

    Spray paint outdoors or in a well-ventilated space, wearing a respirator and glasses. Hold the can about 10 inches away and spray light, sweeping coats, turning the frame to reach every curve and edge. Two or three thin coats look better than one heavy one; let them dry for a full day before reassembling.

  5. 5

    Make paper templates

    Trace each frame on kraft paper, cut it out and mark where the hanging hardware sits. Tape the templates to the wall and rearrange until the layout feels balanced. Keep 2 to 3 inches between frames and center the group about 57 to 60 inches from the floor.

  6. 6

    Fill and reassemble

    Place your new art inside each frame and replace the glass and backing. Use small brads or flexible points to hold the backing, and add sawtooth hangers to any frame that lacks hardware.

  7. 7

    Hang the gallery wall

    Drive the nail or hook right through the mark on each paper template, then tear the paper away. Hang the anchor frame first and work outward. Check each frame with a level and add felt bumpers to the bottom corners to keep them from shifting.

Tips for success

  • Lay out the frames on the floor first and take a photo so you remember your favorite arrangement.
  • Choose a paint finish to suit the room: matte reads modern, satin is easier to wipe clean.
  • For heavy frames, use two hooks spaced apart so the frame stays level and secure.
  • Mix art types like botanical prints, family photos and small mirrors to keep the wall interesting.
  • Test your paint color on the back of one frame first; some old finishes react with spray paint and wrinkle, which a primer coat prevents.

Make it your own

Ombre colors

Paint frames in shades of the same color, from light to dark, and arrange them in a gentle gradient across the wall.

Empty frames

Leave a few frames empty to show off a painted or wallpapered wall behind them.

Fabric-backed frames

Wrap the backing boards in patterned fabric scraps or wallpaper samples instead of using prints.

Leaning ledge display

If you rent or don’t want many nail holes, install one or two picture ledges and lean the painted frames in overlapping layers. You can swap the arrangement with the seasons in minutes.

Frequently asked questions

How many frames do I need for a thrifted frame gallery wall?

Seven to eleven frames fill a typical wall above a sofa or bed. Odd numbers tend to look more balanced, but the total width matters more than the count.

Do I need to prime thrifted frames?

Use a spray paint with built-in primer or a separate bonding primer on very glossy frames. A light scuff sanding also helps paint stick.

Can I paint the frames with the glass in?

It’s possible if you tape the glass carefully, but removing the glass gives cleaner edges and is safer when handling and sanding.
