DIY Teacup Candles with Scented Soy Wax

Thrifted teacups and simple soy wax become a charming, practical gift. Here’s how to melt, scent and pour them safely.

By the HotsBuzz team · September 24, 2026

  • Skill levelModerate
  • Time1.5 hours plus 1-2 weeks curing
  • Budget$25-$40 for 4-6 candles

Teacup candles are a lovely way to give a mismatched thrift-store cup a second life. Fill it with softly scented soy wax and you have a gift that looks vintage, smells wonderful and costs far less than a boutique candle.

Soy wax is a friendly starting point: it melts at a low temperature, cleans up with hot soapy water, and gives a creamy, smooth top. The same method works for small mason jars, ramekins and other heat-safe containers if teacups aren’t your style.

Because you’re working with hot wax and, later, an open flame, this is a project for adults or teens with close supervision. Take your time with the temperatures and wick choice; those two details matter most.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. 1

    Prep the cups and wicks

    Wash and fully dry each teacup. Measure the inside diameter at the top and choose wicks rated for that width; wick packaging lists the range. Stick a wick to the center of each cup bottom and hold it upright with a centering bar or a clothespin resting across the rim.

  2. 2

    Weigh and melt the wax

    Fill each cup with dry flakes and pour them back out, then double that amount, since flakes melt down to about half their volume. Melt the wax in a double boiler over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally, until it reaches about 185°F. Never melt wax directly on a burner.

  3. 3

    Add fragrance

    Remove the pitcher from the heat. Add fragrance oil at about 6-8 percent of the wax weight (roughly 1 oz per pound) and stir gently for a full two minutes so it binds with the wax. Add dye now too if you’re using it.

  4. 4

    Cool before pouring

    Let the wax cool to around 130-140°F, or whatever pour temperature your wax label recommends. Pouring at the right temperature helps prevent rough tops and gaps along the sides.

  5. 5

    Pour slowly

    Pour wax into each cup in a slow, steady stream, stopping about 1/2 in. below the rim. Re-center the wicks if they drift. Save a little wax in the pitcher for touch-ups.

  6. 6

    Let them set

    Leave the candles undisturbed at room temperature, away from drafts, for at least 24 hours. If a sinkhole forms around the wick, gently reheat the reserved wax and pour a thin top layer.

  7. 7

    Trim and cure

    Trim each wick to 1/4 in. Let the candles cure for 1-2 weeks before burning so the fragrance fully develops; they’ll smell noticeably stronger after waiting.

  8. 8

    Test burn one first

    Burn one candle for about 2 hours and watch it. A good melt pool reaches close to the edge without a tall, flickering flame or heavy smoke. If the flame is too large, try a smaller wick in your next batch.

Tips for success

  • Hot wax can cause serious burns. Keep kids and pets out of the area while melting and pouring, and work with a lid and a box of baking soda nearby. Never use water on a wax fire.
  • Skip cracked, crazed or very thin cups, which can break from heat. Avoid anything with metallic trim near the rim since it can get very hot.
  • Only use fragrance oils labeled safe for candles; essential oils and food flavorings can behave unpredictably in wax.
  • Add a small label on the bottom with the scent and simple burn instructions: trim wick to 1/4 in., never leave unattended, and stop burning with 1/2 in. of wax left.
  • Clean pitchers while the wax is still soft by wiping with paper towels, then washing with hot soapy water. Never pour wax down the drain.

Make it your own

Mason jar candles

Use 8 oz jelly jars and add a fabric circle and twine under the lid for a rustic gift. Jars are sturdy and easy to find in sets.

Layered color candle

Pour a thin layer of tinted wax, let it set until firm but still warm, then pour the next color. Two or three layers look striking in clear glass.

Teacup and saucer set

Glue the cup to its saucer with a strong ceramic adhesive so the saucer catches drips and the gift looks finished. Let the glue cure before pouring.

Frequently asked questions

How much wax do I need for one teacup candle?

A typical 6-8 oz teacup holds about 5-6 oz of melted wax by weight. Weighing one cup’s worth of water and using that as a guide works well.

Why doesn’t my candle smell strong?

The fragrance may have been added at too low a temperature or not stirred long enough, or the candle needs more cure time. Waiting a full two weeks often makes a big difference.

Can I reuse the teacup after the candle burns down?

Yes. Pop it in the freezer for a few hours, then the leftover wax usually pops out. Remove the wick tab and wash the cup with hot soapy water.
