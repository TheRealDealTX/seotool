DIY Valentine Pop-Up Heart Card Tutorial

Open the card and a layered heart springs off the page. It looks clever, but it’s just two cuts, a few folds and some glue.

A pop-up heart card gets a reaction that store-bought cards rarely do. When the card opens, a heart lifts off the inside page and stands in 3D, then folds flat again when it closes. It feels like paper engineering, but the mechanism is just a pair of parallel cuts folded the opposite way.

This tutorial uses one sheet of letter-size cardstock for the pop-up base, a second sheet for the outer cover and a few scraps for hearts. Once you’ve made one, you can turn out a whole stack for classmates, coworkers or family in an evening.

Kids around 8 and up can do most of the steps themselves; younger crafters can decorate while an adult handles the cutting. Set aside 30-45 minutes for your first card, including drying time. The finished card measures 4.5 x 6 in. closed, so it slips into a standard envelope for mailing or goes straight into a lunchbox.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. 1

    Fold the inner page

    Cut the white cardstock to 8.5 x 5.5 in. (half a sheet) and fold it in half to 4.25 x 5.5 in. Crease it sharply with a bone folder. This is the page that holds the pop-up.

  2. 2

    Mark and cut the tab

    With the card still folded, measure 2 in. in from each side along the fold so you have a 1.25 in. section in the center. Draw two lines from the fold straight into the card, each 1.5 in. long. Cut along both lines through both layers.

  3. 3

    Pop the tab

    Fold the cut flap back and forth along a line connecting the ends of your cuts to set a crease. Open the card halfway, then push the flap through to the inside so it folds the opposite way and forms a little step. Close the card and press firmly.

  4. 4

    Make the hearts

    Fold a scrap of red cardstock in half and draw half a heart against the fold, about 3 in. tall. Cut it out for a symmetrical heart. Make a smaller pink and white heart to layer on top if you like.

  5. 5

    Attach the heart

    Glue the layered hearts together. Apply glue only to the front face of the pop-up step, then press the bottom of the heart onto it so the heart sits above the step. Check that the heart doesn’t poke out past the edge when the card closes; trim if needed.

  6. 6

    Add the cover

    Cut the red cardstock to 9 x 6 in. and fold in half. Glue the back of the inner page to the inside of the cover, taking care not to put glue on the pop-up step itself. Press under a book for a few minutes.

  7. 7

    Decorate and write

    Add a message on the inside page below the heart and decorate the front with a smaller heart, stickers or hand lettering. Let any glue dry fully before opening and closing the card repeatedly.

Tips for success

  • Craft knives are very sharp; an adult should do the tab cuts for younger kids, always cutting away from fingers on a mat.
  • Heavier cardstock (65-80 lb.) holds the pop-up shape best; printer paper tends to sag.
  • Make one test card from scrap paper first to check that your heart clears the edges when closed.
  • Score fold lines before folding for crisp edges that don’t crack.
  • Use a glue stick or tape rather than liquid glue near the pop-up step; wet glue can soak through and stick the step to the page.

Make it your own

Double pop-up

Cut two shorter tabs side by side, one 1 in. deep and one 2 in. deep, and attach a heart to each for a layered, staggered effect.

Pop-up heart bouquet

Glue three small hearts on paper stems to a single wider tab so a little bouquet springs up when the card opens.

Classroom Valentines

Scale the card down to 3 x 4 in. folded and use a single 1 in. tab. Cut the base cards in a batch so kids can focus on decorating.

Photo pop-up

Glue a small printed photo, trimmed into a heart shape, onto the pop-up step instead of a plain paper heart. It turns the card into a little keepsake for a partner or grandparent.

Frequently asked questions

Why won’t my heart pop up?

Usually the tab was pushed inward but not re-creased. Open the card halfway and pinch the tab folds firmly so they reverse direction, then close and press the card flat. Also check that no glue landed on the back of the step, which locks it to the page.

Can I mail a pop-up card?

Yes, as long as it closes flat. Use a slightly larger envelope and a sheet of cardboard if it’s thick. Embellishments like big gems or buttons make the envelope lumpy and may require extra postage or hand canceling, so keep mailed cards flat and save the 3D extras for cards you hand-deliver.

What size envelope fits this card?

The finished card is 4.5 x 6 in., which fits a standard A6 envelope (4.75 x 6.5 in.).
