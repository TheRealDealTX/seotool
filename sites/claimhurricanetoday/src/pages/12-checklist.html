How to use this checklist well

Do Phase 1 and the first three items of Phase 3 on the same day if you can; reporting does not have to wait for the evidence phase to be perfect. Phase 2 is time-sensitive in a different way: once cleanup begins, the best evidence disappears. If you are displaced or evacuated, do what you safely can and pick up the rest when you return.

Documents to have in your claim folder

  • Full policy with endorsements and the current declarations page
  • All letters and emails to and from the insurer, in date order
  • Claim number, adjuster name and license number
  • Photo and video files, with a written index of what each shows
  • Receipts for emergency repairs, supplies and living expenses
  • Contents inventory with descriptions, ages and replacement costs
  • Every estimate: the insurer's, your contractor's and any engineer's report
  • Your call log

FAQ: the hurricane claim checklist

Where is my checklist progress saved?

Only in your own web browser, using local storage on this device. It is not sent to us or anyone else. Clearing your browser data, or using a private window, removes it.

What is the single most important item on the list?

Reporting the claim and confirming it in writing. For most current losses, Florida bars a new claim unless notice is given within one year of landfall, and the insurer's own deadlines do not start until you report.

Do I need my own contractor estimate?

It is not required to file, but a detailed estimate from a licensed contractor is the easiest way to spot items the insurer's estimate missed or underpriced.

What if I already threw out damaged items?

Use whatever evidence remains: photos from before disposal, receipts for the items, bank statements and dumpster or debris-removal receipts. Describe what was discarded and why, such as health and safety.
