P-pop Explained: SB19, BINI and the Rise of Filipino Pop

From SB19's viral dance practice to BINI's summer anthem Pantropiko, here's how P-pop became the sound of a generation, plus the groups and fandoms to know.

By Cebu-Davao Editorial Team · October 7, 2026 · 4 min read

P-pop (Pinoy pop) is the Filipino idol-group scene, and its two biggest names are the boy group SB19 and the girl group BINI. SB19 debuted in 2018 and broke out with viral dance videos and hits like "Bazinga" and "Gento", while BINI debuted in 2021 and became a national phenomenon with "Pantropiko" and "Salamin, Salamin". Together they turned P-pop from a niche into a mainstream force with packed arenas and passionate fandoms.

What is P-pop?

P-pop refers to Filipino pop music made by idol-style groups: trained singers and dancers who release music, perform tight choreography, and build close relationships with fans online. The idea was influenced by K-pop's training system, but P-pop's identity is Filipino, with songs in Filipino, English and Taglish, and lyrics and visuals that draw on local culture.

The term is sometimes used broadly for any Filipino pop music, but today it most often means the idol-group wave led by SB19 and BINI.

SB19: the group that opened the door

SB19 is a five-member boy group made up of Pablo, Josh, Stell, Ken and Justin. They trained under ShowBT Philippines, a company with Korean management, before debuting in 2018 with "Tilaluha".

Their break came in 2019 when a fan-shot video of their dance practice for "Go Up" went viral. Suddenly, a group that had been performing to small crowds found itself trending online, booking TV appearances and selling out shows.

SB19's key songs

SB19 is known for members who write, produce and choreograph much of their own work, especially Pablo, the leader, who has a strong songwriting and production role. Their fandom is called A'TIN, a play on the Filipino word atin, meaning "ours".

BINI: the nation's girl group

BINI is an eight-member girl group: Aiah, Colet, Maloi, Gwen, Stacey, Mikha, Jhoanna and Sheena. They were formed through ABS-CBN's Star Hunt Academy training programme and debuted in 2021.

Their early releases built a loyal following, but the turning point was "Pantropiko", a breezy summer pop song released in 2023 that exploded on social media in 2024. Its dance became a trend everywhere, from offices and schools to barangay events. "Salamin, Salamin" followed it up the charts, and BINI went from up-and-comers to household names.

BINI's fandom is called Blooms. The name plays on the group's own: bini comes from binibini, the Filipino word for a young woman, and suggests blooming.

Other P-pop groups to know

SB19 and BINI lead the scene, but they're not alone. Other names fans follow include:

  • BGYO – a boy group from ABS-CBN's Star Hunt, BINI's label-mates.
  • ALAMAT – a boy group known for songs in multiple Philippine languages, celebrating regional identity.
  • MNL48 – a Manila-based sister group of Japan's AKB48.
  • KAIA – a girl group that emerged in the P-pop wave.

The scene changes quickly, with new groups debuting and solo careers developing. Follow official pages for the latest releases.

Why P-pop rose

Several things came together to push P-pop into the mainstream.

  1. Social media and dance challenges. Short, catchy choreography spreads fast on TikTok, Facebook and YouTube. Both "Gento" and "Pantropiko" became challenge trends.
  2. Organised fandoms. A'TIN and Blooms stream, vote, trend hashtags and fill venues. They also organise charity projects and fan events.
  3. Pride in Filipino identity. Fans enjoy seeing Filipino artists in a polished idol format, singing in their own language.
  4. K-pop as a model. K-pop's popularity in the Philippines created an audience familiar with idol culture, ready for a homegrown version.
  5. Strong songs. In the end, songs like "Gento" and "Pantropiko" are simply catchy, and they reach people outside the fandom.

P-pop and OPM

P-pop is part of the wider story of OPM (Original Pilipino Music). It sits alongside bands, solo singers and rappers, and some fans enjoy everything from 90s rock to today's idol groups. For the band side of the story, read our list of Filipino bands, and for music from the Visayas, our Bisrock bands list.

Seeing P-pop live in Cebu and Davao

Major P-pop tours usually include big venues in Metro Manila, and sometimes extend to Cebu, Davao and other cities. To avoid disappointment:

  • Follow the groups' official social media for tour announcements.
  • Buy tickets only through official or authorised sellers.
  • Be cautious of resellers and "payment first" offers on social media.
  • Check our Cebu cinemas guide and news section for concert films and event news.

Tip: Light sticks and fan merch can be expensive. Buy from official stores or verified fan-club sellers to avoid counterfeits.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does P-pop mean?

P-pop means Pinoy pop or Philippine pop. Today it usually refers to Filipino idol groups like SB19 and BINI.

When did SB19 and BINI debut?

SB19 debuted in 2018, and BINI debuted in 2021. Both had major breakouts after their debuts through viral songs and dances.

What are the SB19 and BINI fandom names?

SB19's fans are called A'TIN, and BINI's fans are called Blooms.

Is P-pop the same as K-pop?

No. P-pop was influenced by K-pop's idol and training model, but it is Filipino music by Filipino artists, often sung in Filipino or Taglish.
