National ID Philippines Guide: PhilSys Registration and ePhilID

How to register for the PhilSys National ID, what the ePhilID and digital ID are, where you can use them and what to do if your card is lost.

The National ID Philippines system, officially the Philippine Identification System (PhilSys), gives every Filipino and resident alien a single, government-issued ID. Registration is free and done in steps: you submit your information, have your biometrics taken, and then receive a physical card, a printed ePhilID or a digital version. Here is how it works and what to do if something goes wrong.

What Is PhilSys?

PhilSys was created by the Philippine Identification System Act (Republic Act No. 11055), signed in 2018. It is run by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA). The goal is to give everyone one foundational ID, making it easier to open bank accounts, access government services and prove identity without carrying multiple cards.

Each registrant receives a PhilSys Number (PSN), a permanent number that should be kept private, and a PhilSys Card Number (PCN) printed on the ID, which is the one used for everyday transactions.

National ID Philippines: How to Register

Registration steps may change, so always check the official PhilSys website for the current process. In general:

  1. Prepare your documents. Bring a primary ID or document, such as a PSA birth certificate together with a government-issued photo ID, or a passport. If you lack these, accepted secondary documents may be combined.
  2. Find a registration centre. Registration has been offered at PSA offices, local government centres, malls and mobile registration sites. Some areas also hold barangay-level registration drives.
  3. Submit your demographic information. Your name, birth date, address and other details are recorded. Check every detail carefully.
  4. Capture biometrics. Your photo, fingerprints and iris scan are taken. Children have different requirements depending on age.
  5. Keep your transaction slip. This contains your transaction reference number, which you need to follow up on your ID.

Tip: Double-check the spelling of your name and your birth date before leaving the registration centre. Corrections later can take more time.

ePhilID and the Digital National ID

Because printing and delivering millions of physical cards takes time, the PSA introduced alternatives.

ePhilID

The ePhilID is a printed, paper version of the National ID with a QR code. It has been issued at registration centres to people still waiting for their physical card. It is meant to be accepted as a valid proof of identity, like the physical card.

Digital National ID

The PSA later introduced a digital National ID that can be accessed online and through the government's eGovPH app, among other channels. It shows your details and a QR code that can be verified. Features and access steps may be updated, so follow official PSA and eGovPH announcements.

What Can You Use the National ID For?

Under the law, the PhilID is meant to be accepted as valid proof of identity in government and private transactions. Common uses include:

  • Opening a bank or e-wallet account, such as GCash or Maya
  • Applying for government benefits and social services
  • Applying for a passport as a supporting ID
  • Enrolling in schools and applying for jobs
  • Signing a lease, such as when renting a house in Davao

Some establishments may still ask for an additional ID, but the PSA has repeatedly reminded institutions to accept the National ID. Because the QR code can be verified, it helps reduce identity fraud.

How to Track Your National ID Delivery

Physical PhilID cards have been delivered mainly through the Philippine Postal Corporation (PHLPost). To check your status:

  1. Keep your transaction reference number from your registration slip.
  2. Use the official PhilSys or PHLPost tracking tools online, when available, to check delivery status.
  3. Contact your local post office or PSA office if the card is overdue.

Delivery times have varied widely by area. If you have waited a long time, ask about getting an ePhilID or using the digital National ID in the meantime.

What to Do If Your National ID Is Lost

  1. Report the loss as soon as possible. Some offices may ask for an affidavit of loss.
  2. Visit a PSA or PhilSys registration centre to request a replacement.
  3. Bring supporting documents and your transaction details, if you have them.
  4. Pay the replacement fee if required. First issuance is free, but replacements for lost or damaged cards may involve a fee. Confirm the current amount with the PSA.
  5. Use the digital National ID while waiting, if available to you.

Protect your PhilSys Number. Avoid posting photos of your ID online, since identity details can be misused.

For official information, visit the PhilSys website and the Philippine Statistics Authority. Beware of fake pages charging fees for registration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the National ID in the Philippines free?

Yes, registration and the first issuance of the PhilID are free. Replacements for lost or damaged cards may have a fee.

What is the difference between the ePhilID and the PhilID?

The PhilID is the physical plastic card, while the ePhilID is a printed paper version with a QR code issued as an interim ID. Both are meant to serve as valid proof of identity.

How can I get the digital National ID?

The PSA has made a digital National ID available online and through the eGovPH app. Check official PSA announcements for the current steps.

How do I check if my National ID has been delivered?

Use your transaction reference number with the official PhilSys or PHLPost tracking tools, or contact your local post office or PSA office.

What should I do if I lose my National ID?

Report the loss, then go to a PSA or PhilSys registration centre to request a replacement. Bring supporting documents and ask about any fee.
