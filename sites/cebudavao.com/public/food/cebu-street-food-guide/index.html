Cebu Street Food Guide: 12 Local Eats and Where to Find Them

From ngohiong to tuslob buwa, here are Cebu's must-try street eats, where locals find them and how to eat safely.

Cebu street food is cheap, filling and full of local character: think ngohiong spring rolls with spicy sauce, tuslob buwa dipping in Pasil, smoky barbecue at Larsian, and endless puso (hanging rice). Most of it costs less than a fancy coffee, and the best spots are where you see locals queueing.

Here are the dishes to try, where to find them and how to eat safely.

1. Ngohiong

Ngohiong is Cebu's take on the Chinese-Filipino spring roll. A thin wrapper is filled with ubod (heart of palm) or other vegetables, seasoned with five-spice powder, then battered and deep-fried. It is served with a sweet, sticky, chili-spiked sauce. You will find it at street stalls and carinderias (small eateries), often eaten with puso.

2. Tuslob Buwa

Tuslob buwa means "dip in the bubbles". A small pan of pork brain, liver, shrimp paste, onions and chili is cooked at your table until it bubbles, and you dip pieces of puso into the rich, salty sauce. It became famous in Pasil, a working-class area near the fish market in downtown Cebu City, and has since spread to other parts of the metro. It is hearty and very much an acquired taste.

3. Balbacua

Balbacua is a slow-cooked stew of beef skin, tendons and oxtail or bones, simmered for hours until gelatinous and rich, flavoured with ginger, lemongrass, peanuts or beans and sometimes annatto. It is a favourite late-night and early-morning meal, sold at carinderias and stalls around the city.

4. Larsian Barbecue

Larsian is a well-known barbecue area near Fuente Osmeña Circle in Cebu City. You pick raw skewers of pork, chicken, isaw (intestines), hotdogs and more from the display, hand them to the grill staff, and eat them with puso and a dipping sauce of vinegar, soy and chili. It is lively at night and a classic Cebu experience. For a homemade version, try our Pinoy barbecue recipe.

5. Puso (Hanging Rice)

No Cebu street food meal is complete without puso: rice cooked inside a pouch woven from young coconut leaves. It is portable, cheap and the perfect partner for barbecue, ngohiong and lechon. Learn more in our guide on how to make puso.

Tip: To open puso, pull the loose ends of the leaf apart and cut or tear along the weave. Locals often order "two puso" automatically with any grilled food.

6. Ginabot

Ginabot is deep-fried pork intestines (similar to chicharon bulaklak in Luzon), crunchy outside and slightly chewy inside. It is often sold by chicharon vendors and paired with spicy vinegar. Rich and salty, it is best shared.

7. Siomai sa Tisa

Cebuanos love their siomai, and Tisa, in the Labangon area of Cebu City, is famous for it. Siomai sa Tisa is pork siomai steamed and served with a punchy chili-garlic sauce, often with puso or rice. Many stalls across the city now sell "Tisa-style" siomai. Make it at home with our Cebu siomai recipe.

8. Bihon and Other Noodles

Simple stir-fried bihon (rice noodles) or pancit is a staple at carinderias and street stalls, sold by the plate or in small packs. It is often eaten with puso or bread for an inexpensive merienda (afternoon snack).

9. Budbud

Budbud is a Visayan sticky rice cake wrapped in banana leaves, similar to suman. Some versions are made with millet (budbud kabog). It is sweet, chewy and perfect with hot chocolate (sikwate) or ripe mango for breakfast. Look for it at markets and roadside vendors.

10. Masareal

Masareal is a Mandaue specialty: a dense, sweet bar made from ground peanuts and sugar. It is more of a pasalubong (gift snack) than a hot street food, but you will see it sold in shops and stalls around Metro Cebu.

11. Rosquillos

Rosquillos are crisp, ring-shaped cookies associated with the town of Liloan, north of Cebu City. Titay's is the best-known maker. They are light, not too sweet and a classic Cebu pasalubong. See our full pasalubong guide for more take-home treats.

12. Sutukil and Lechon

Not strictly street food, but no Cebu food list is complete without them. Sutukil means choosing fresh seafood and having it grilled, cooked in soup or prepared as kilaw (see our sutukil explainer), and Cebu lechon (roast pig) is sold by the kilo at markets and stalls.

Food Safety Tips for Street Food in Cebu

Most people enjoy Cebu's street food without problems, but a few habits help:

  1. Choose busy stalls. High turnover means fresher food.
  2. Eat it hot. Freshly grilled or fried food is safer than food that has been sitting out.
  3. Watch the sauces. Shared dipping sauces can be a risk; ask for your own cup if possible.
  4. Drink bottled or sealed water and be cautious with ice if you have a sensitive stomach.
  5. Wash or sanitise your hands, especially before eating with your hands (kamay).
  6. Go easy at first. Rich dishes like ginabot and tuslob buwa can be heavy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most famous street food in Cebu?

Barbecue with puso is the most common, but ngohiong, tuslob buwa and siomai sa Tisa are also iconic Cebu street foods.

What is tuslob buwa?

Tuslob buwa means "dip in the bubbles". It is a bubbling sauce of pork brain, liver and shrimp paste cooked at your table, eaten by dipping puso into it. It became famous in Pasil.

Where is Larsian in Cebu?

Larsian is a barbecue area near Fuente Osmeña Circle in Cebu City, where you choose skewers and have them grilled on the spot.

Is Cebu street food safe to eat?

Generally yes, if you choose busy stalls, eat food hot and freshly cooked, and drink bottled water. Travellers with sensitive stomachs should start slowly.
