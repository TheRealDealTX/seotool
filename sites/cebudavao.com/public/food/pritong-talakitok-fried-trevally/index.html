Talakitok Recipe: Crispy Fried Trevally and Bisaya Escabeche

Learn how to cook talakitok two ways: golden pritong talakitok with vinegar dip, and a Bisaya-style escabeche with ginger, peppers and a sweet-sour glaze.

This talakitok recipe gives you two classic ways to cook trevally: crispy pritong talakitok (fried trevally) served with spiced vinegar, and a Bisaya-style escabeche where the fried fish is coated in a sweet-sour ginger sauce. Both are weeknight-friendly and need only basic pantry ingredients. Talakitok has firm, meaty flesh that holds together well in hot oil, which is why it is a favourite for frying across Cebu and Davao.

What Is Talakitok?

Talakitok is the Tagalog name for trevally or jack, a family of silver, deep-bodied fish common in Philippine waters. In Cebuano Bisaya, larger trevallies are often called mamsa. The fish has a mild, slightly rich flavour and fewer small bones than many reef fish, which makes it family-friendly.

Fish names change from island to island, so check our guide to Cebuano, Tagalog and English fish names or try the fish name translator before you head to the market.

LanguageName
EnglishTrevally, jack
TagalogTalakitok
Cebuano BisayaMamsa (larger fish), talakitok also understood

How to Choose Fresh Talakitok

At the palengke (wet market), look for:

  • Clear, bulging eyes, not cloudy or sunken.
  • Bright red gills. Lift the gill cover to check.
  • Firm flesh that springs back when pressed.
  • A clean sea smell, never sour or ammonia-like.

A fish of about 700 g to 1 kg feeds four people. Ask the vendor to scale, gut and score it for you; most will do it at no extra cost. Prices change with season and size, so compare a few stalls.

Ingredients

For the pritong talakitok

  • 1 whole talakitok, about 800 g to 1 kg, scaled and gutted
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon calamansi juice or lemon juice
  • 3 tablespoons cornstarch or all-purpose flour (optional, for extra crispness)
  • 1 1/2 cups cooking oil for frying

For the Bisaya escabeche sauce

  • 2 tablespoons cooking oil
  • 1 thumb-sized piece ginger, cut into thin strips
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 medium onion, sliced
  • 1 small red bell pepper, cut into strips
  • 1 small carrot, cut into thin strips
  • 1/3 cup cane vinegar
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 3 tablespoons brown sugar
  • 2 tablespoons banana ketchup or tomato ketchup
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch dissolved in 2 tablespoons water
  • Salt and pepper to taste

For the dipping sauce (if serving plain fried)

  • 1/4 cup cane vinegar
  • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 chopped siling labuyo (bird's eye chilli)
  • A pinch of salt and pepper

Instructions

  1. Rinse the fish and pat it completely dry with paper towels, inside and out. A dry fish fries crisper and spits less oil.
  2. Score both sides with three or four diagonal cuts down to the bone. This helps it cook evenly.
  3. Rub the fish all over with salt, pepper and calamansi juice. Let it rest for 15 minutes, then pat dry again.
  4. Lightly dust the fish with cornstarch, shaking off the excess.
  5. Heat the oil in a wide pan over medium-high heat until a pinch of flour sizzles immediately.
  6. Carefully slide in the fish, laying it away from you. Fry without moving for 6 to 8 minutes until the underside is deep golden and releases easily.
  7. Flip gently with two spatulas and fry the other side for another 6 to 8 minutes until crisp and cooked through near the bone. Drain on a rack or paper towels.
  8. For plain pritong talakitok, mix the dipping sauce ingredients and serve the fish hot with rice.
  9. For escabeche, heat 2 tablespoons oil in a clean pan. Sauté ginger for 1 minute, then add garlic and onion and cook until soft.
  10. Add carrot and bell pepper and stir-fry for 2 minutes so they stay crunchy.
  11. Pour in vinegar and let it boil for 1 minute without stirring to mellow the sharpness.
  12. Add water, brown sugar, ketchup and soy sauce. Simmer for 2 minutes, then season with salt and pepper.
  13. Stir in the cornstarch slurry and cook until the sauce turns glossy and slightly thick.
  14. Place the fried fish on a platter and pour the sauce and vegetables over it. Serve immediately.

Talakitok Recipe Tips for Perfect Frying

Tip: Do not stir vinegar right after adding it. Filipino cooks let it boil first so the raw, sharp taste cooks off, a habit you will also see in adobo and paksiw.

  • Dry fish equals less splatter. Most oil spitting comes from surface water.
  • Don't crowd the pan. If the fish is long, cut it in half crosswise.
  • Medium-high, not smoking hot. Too hot and the skin burns before the inside cooks.
  • Test doneness by poking near the backbone: the flesh should be white and flake easily.

Escabeche Bisaya Style vs Other Versions

The escabeche you find in Cebu and Davao homes is usually sweeter and more colourful than the Spanish original, thanks to brown sugar, ketchup and bell peppers. Some families use pineapple juice or chunks for extra sweetness, while others keep it sharper with more vinegar and ginger.

Bisaya home styleSweet-sourKetchup, brown sugar, bell pepper, carrot
Ginger-forwardSour, spicyLots of ginger, less sugar

The same method works with lapu-lapu (grouper), maya-maya (snapper) and tilapia. For another grouper idea, try sinigang na lapu-lapu, or go raw with kinilaw Davao style.

What to Serve With Talakitok

  • Steamed rice or puso (hanging rice)
  • Sliced tomatoes and salted egg
  • A simple vegetable dish such as utan Bisaya
  • Fresh mango or banana for dessert

Storing Leftovers

Fried talakitok is best eaten right away. Leftover escabeche keeps in a covered container in the fridge for up to two days; reheat gently in a pan. The sauce will soften the crust, but many people like it even better the next day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is talakitok in English?

Talakitok is trevally, also called jack. In Cebuano Bisaya, larger trevallies are often called mamsa.

How do you cook talakitok so it doesn't stick?

Pat the fish completely dry, heat the oil properly before adding the fish, and do not move it for the first 6 minutes. A light coating of cornstarch also helps prevent sticking.

What is escabeche Bisaya style?

It is fried fish topped with a sweet-sour sauce made from vinegar, sugar, ginger, garlic, onion, bell peppers and often ketchup. It is sweeter and more colourful than Spanish escabeche.

Can I use fillets instead of whole fish?

Yes. Fry talakitok fillets for about 3 to 4 minutes per side depending on thickness, then top with the escabeche sauce.

Is talakitok a bony fish?

It has a central backbone and some pin bones, but fewer fine bones than many smaller reef fish, which makes it easy to eat.
