Visayan Songs and Bisrock: The Bands That Shaped Cebuano Rock

From classic love songs to Bisrock and Visayan pop, here is how Cebu and Davao built a music scene in their own language.

Visayan songs range from old Cebuano love ballads sung at fiestas to Bisrock — rock music written and sung in Bisaya — and today's wave of Visayan pop on streaming apps. Cebu has been the heart of this scene for decades, with Davao and other Mindanao cities adding their own bands, singers and audiences.

This guide walks through the classics, the Bisrock bands that made Cebuano rock cool, the Cebu acts that went national singing in English, and where to find new Bisaya songs today.

What Is Bisrock?

Bisrock is short for "Bisaya rock". The term describes rock bands from the Visayas and Mindanao who write original songs in Cebuano rather than English or Tagalog. The movement grew in Cebu in the late 1990s and 2000s, when local radio stations, gigs and independent recordings gave Cebuano-language songs a real audience.

What made Bisrock special was the language itself. Songs used everyday Bisaya — jokes, street slang and local references — so listeners heard their own lives in the lyrics. Before Bisrock, many people assumed a "serious" band had to sing in English or Tagalog. Bisrock proved them wrong.

Missing Filemon

Missing Filemon is the band most often named when people talk about the birth of Bisrock. Their witty, heartfelt Cebuano lyrics and strong live following helped convince a generation of young Cebuanos that rock in their own language could be both fun and meaningful. Many later bands cite them as an influence.

Phylum

Phylum became one of the most recognisable Bisrock names, and "Phylum songs" is still a common search among fans of 2000s Cebuano rock. Their music was a staple of local radio and campus gigs during the scene's peak.

Junior Kilat

Junior Kilat took Bisaya lyrics into reggae, a genre that fits island life perfectly. The band became a favourite at beach parties, school events and festivals, and showed that the Cebuano language could carry many different styles, not just rock.

Sheila and the Insects

Sheila and the Insects brought a female voice to a scene dominated by male frontmen. They are remembered fondly by fans of the Bisrock era.

Cebu and Davao Bands That Sing in English

Not every band from the south sings in Bisaya. Some of the biggest Cebu acts made their names nationally with English or Tagalog songs.

  • Urbandub — A Cebu band known for emotional, heavy alternative rock in English. They became one of the most respected rock acts in the country and proved that a Cebu band could compete with Manila on the national stage.
  • Cueshé — A Cebu pop-rock band that found mainstream success in the mid-2000s with radio-friendly love songs, reaching audiences far beyond the Visayas.

Davao has its own lively band scene, centred on bars, campus events and festival stages during the Kadayawan Festival. Many Davao bands mix Bisaya, Tagalog and English in a single set, which says a lot about the city's language mix.

Old Visayan Songs and Classic Love Songs

Long before Bisrock, Visayan songs were part of everyday life — sung at fiestas, weddings, wakes and family gatherings, often with a guitar. Cebu's guitar-making tradition, centred in Lapu-Lapu City, helped keep this music alive; read more in our story on Cebu guitars.

Some classic titles you will hear at gatherings and in school programmes:

  • Matud Nila — Perhaps the most famous Cebuano love song, a tender ballad still sung by romantic crooners
  • Usahay — A wistful love song ("Sometimes") known across the Visayas
  • Rosas Pandan — A lively folk song often performed with dance
  • Dandansoy — A Visayan folk song about leaving home, popular in the Western Visayas

Cebuano entertainer Max Surban is another household name, known for decades of comic and romantic Bisaya songs that are still played at parties.

Visayan Pop and New Bisaya Songs

The 2010s brought a new chapter: Visayan pop. Songwriting competitions and campaigns in Cebu encouraged young songwriters to write catchy, modern pop in Cebuano, and the best entries were played on local radio and shared widely online. This helped create a fresh generation of Bisaya love songs that feel current rather than nostalgic.

Today, new Bisaya songs and the latest releases are easiest to find on streaming platforms and video sites. Search for terms like "Bisaya song", "Visayan pop" or "Cebuano song" and you will find independent artists from Cebu, Davao, Cagayan de Oro and beyond, many of them releasing music without a major label.

Where to Catch Live Bisaya Music

In Cebu, live music happens in bars and restaurants around the city, at campus events and during festivals like the Sinulog. In Davao, look out for gigs during Kadayawan and weekend shows in the city's bar districts. Line-ups change often, so follow venues and bands on social media for current schedules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Bisrock?

Bisrock means "Bisaya rock" — original rock music written and sung in Cebuano. It grew in Cebu in the late 1990s and 2000s, with bands like Missing Filemon and Phylum among its best-known names.

What are some famous old Visayan songs?

Classic titles include Matud Nila, Usahay and Rosas Pandan. They are still sung at fiestas, weddings and school programmes across the Visayas and Mindanao.

Are Urbandub and Cueshé Bisrock bands?

Both are Cebu bands, but they are best known for songs in English, so they are usually described as Cebu rock or pop-rock bands rather than Bisrock.

Where can I find new Bisaya songs?

Streaming platforms and video sites are the easiest places. Search for "Bisaya song" or "Visayan pop" to discover independent artists from Cebu, Davao and other southern cities.
