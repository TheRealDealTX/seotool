Beaches Near Davao City: Samal, Talikud, Dahican and More

Davao City's own shoreline is better for picnics than swimming. The real beaches are a short boat ride away on Samal and Talikud, or a road trip to Dahican.

The best beaches near Davao City are on Samal Island and Talikud Island, both a short boat ride across the Davao Gulf, and at Dahican Beach in Mati, a longer road trip east. Beaches inside the city proper, like Times Beach, are fine for picnics and sunsets but not great for swimming. If you want clear water and white sand, plan to cross the water.

This guide covers what to expect at each option, how to get there and what to budget.

Beach in Davao City Proper: Times Beach and the Shoreline

People often search for a "beach resort in Davao City proper", and there are some options along the coast in areas like Matina Aplaya and Talomo. The best known is Times Beach, a public stretch of dark sand where families picnic, friends grill, and people gather for the sunset.

Be realistic about the water, though. The shoreline near the city sits close to river mouths and busy port areas, so the water is often murky and not ideal for swimming. Treat Times Beach and nearby city beaches as a place to hang out, eat and watch the sun go down, not as a swimming destination.

A few resorts along the city coast have swimming pools, cottages and function halls, which makes them popular for parties and family gatherings. These are some of the more affordable resorts in Davao if you just want a pool and a sea breeze without a boat ride. Check recent reviews and entrance fees before going, as they change.

Samal Island: The Classic Davao Beach Escape

The Island Garden City of Samal is the main reason Davao is a beach destination. It lies just across the channel from the city, and the trip is short enough for a day trip.

What to expect

  • Resorts for every budget, from simple beach cottages with entrance fees to luxury properties such as the Pearl Farm Beach Resort
  • Day-use beach parks like Paradise Island Park and Beach Resort, popular with families
  • Inland sights such as Hagimit Falls and the Monfort Bat Cave, home to a huge colony of fruit bats
  • Kaputian, on the southern part of the island, known for its long public beach

How to get to Samal

Most visitors cross by RoRo (roll-on, roll-off) ferry from the Sasa area or by passenger boat from other wharves in the city. The crossing itself is short. Once on Samal, use tricycles, habal-habal (motorcycle taxis) or a hired vehicle to reach your resort. Our full guide on how to reach Samal Island has the details.

A bridge linking Davao City and Samal has been under construction; see our Samal bridge explainer for the latest. Until it opens, boats remain the way across.

Talikud Island: Quieter Beaches and Coral

Talikud is a smaller island south-west of Samal and part of the same city. It is quieter, with fewer big resorts, clear water and good snorkelling spots close to shore. Boats to Talikud have traditionally left from Santa Ana Wharf in Davao City, and you can also reach it from Samal.

Isla Reta is one of the best-known beach resorts on Talikud; see our guide on how to get to Isla Reta Beach Resort. Bring cash, snacks and water, as facilities are simpler than on Samal.

Tip: Boat schedules to Talikud are limited and can be cancelled in rough weather. Leave early, ask about the last return trip, and check the forecast before you go.

Dahican Beach, Mati: Worth the Road Trip

If you have an extra day or two, head east to Dahican Beach in Mati City, Davao Oriental. It is a long, wide stretch of fine white sand facing the Pacific, famous for skimboarding and surfing. Local conservation groups have worked to protect sea turtles that nest along the coast, so follow any posted rules.

To get there, take a bus or van from Davao City to Mati (roughly three to four hours, depending on the route and traffic), then a tricycle to the beach. There are guesthouses and small resorts nearby, so staying overnight makes the trip far more relaxing.

Tips for Davao Beach Trips

  1. Go early. Boats and roads are less crowded, and the sea is often calmer in the morning.
  2. Bring cash. Many small resorts and boatmen do not accept cards or e-wallets.
  3. Pack reef-safe sunscreen, water and snacks, especially for Talikud.
  4. Respect the environment. Do not stand on coral, take shells or leave trash.
  5. Check the weather. Davao is outside the main typhoon belt, but heavy rain can still disrupt sea travel.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best beach near Davao City?

For most visitors, Samal Island offers the best mix of good beaches, easy access and choice of resorts. For quieter beaches and snorkelling, try Talikud; for a long white-sand beach, Dahican in Mati is worth the trip.

Is Times Beach in Davao good for swimming?

Not really. The water near the city is often murky because of nearby rivers and port activity. Times Beach is better for picnics, food and sunsets.

How do I get from Davao City to Samal Island?

Take a RoRo ferry or passenger boat across the channel; the crossing is short. On Samal, use tricycles, habal-habal or a hired vehicle to reach your resort.

Are there cheap resorts in Davao City?

Yes. Simple beach resorts on Samal and Talikud and pool resorts along the city coast charge modest entrance fees. Rates change, so check recent reviews or contact the resort before going.
