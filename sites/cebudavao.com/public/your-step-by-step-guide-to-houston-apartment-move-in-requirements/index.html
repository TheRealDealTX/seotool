Your Step-by-Step Guide to Houston Apartment Move-In Requirements

Moving into a new apartment in Houston is an exciting chapter, but it comes with a to-do list that every renter should be ready for. Whether you’re relocating from another city or simply moving across town, being prepared with the right documentation, services, and knowledge of local requirements is key. This guide walks you through the step-by-step process to help you move in smoothly.

1. Understand the Application Process

Before you can get your keys, you’ll need to complete an apartment application. Most Houston apartments require proof of income (usually 2–3 times the rent), undergo a credit and background check, and pay a non-refundable application fee, often between $25 and $75.

Be ready to provide a government-issued ID, proof of income, rental history, and references. A helpful breakdown of this process is outlined by Houston Apartments, which details each step from applying to moving in.

2. Review Rental Criteria

Each property will have its own set of rental qualifications, which may include minimum credit scores, income verification, and criminal background checks. Make sure to request this information before submitting your application to avoid wasting time and money.

You can explore a sample set of rental criteria used by Houston apartment communities on the Houston Apartment Association’s resource page.

3. Gather Necessary Documentation

To make your application process smoother, gather these essential documents ahead of time:

  • Government-issued ID (e.g., driver’s license or passport)

  • Recent pay stubs or bank statements to verify income

  • Previous landlord contact information

  • Letters of recommendation or personal references

These items are commonly required and should be submitted along with your application.

4. Secure Renter’s Insurance

Many Houston apartments require tenants to have renter’s insurance before moving in. This insurance protects your belongings from damage or theft and may also cover liability if someone is injured in your unit.

You’ll often need to name the landlord or property management company as an “additional insured.” Learn more about what a typical renter’s insurance policy should include on the Texas Department of Insurance website.

5. Set Up Utilities in Advance

Before moving in, make sure utilities are active and billed in your name. Common services include:

  • Electricity and gas (via providers like Reliant or CenterPoint)

  • Water and trash (sometimes handled by the apartment community)

  • Internet and cable

To ensure your utilities are ready on move-in day, it’s a good idea to reference the City of Houston Utility Services for guidance on providers and regulations.

6. Complete a Move-In Inspection

Don’t skip the move-in inspection. This document records the condition of your apartment and helps prevent disputes over damage when you move out. Make note of:

  • Scuffs or holes in walls

  • Appliance performance

  • Water leaks or electrical issues

  • Smoke detector function

The Redfin Move-In Checklist offers a solid reference to ensure you don’t miss any important inspection points.

7. Know Your Rights and Local Housing Laws

Houston has its own standards for multi-family housing. Properties with three or more units must be registered for habitability inspections to ensure they meet structural and safety codes. These inspections are handled by the City of Houston’s Multi-Family Habitability Program.

Additionally, it’s important to understand your rights as a renter. The City of Houston Housing and Community Development Department provides renters with valuable information on landlord responsibilities and tenant protections.

8. Use Apartment Search Services

If you’re overwhelmed by the process, working with a professional locator can simplify your search. The experts at Houston Apartments offer a comprehensive apartment-finding service that includes helping you identify your preferences, find deals, schedule tours, and guide you through the move-in steps.

They also provide access to exclusive listings, move-in specials, and help you understand different property policies—making the whole process easier and more efficient.

9. Schedule Your Move and Notify Others

Once approved and move-in is scheduled, it’s time to handle the logistics:

  • Book a moving truck or hire movers

  • Pack and label boxes by room

  • Update your mailing address with USPS

  • Notify your bank, subscriptions, and employer of your new address

Consider using a checklist like this apartment moving guide to stay organized and on track.

10. Settle In and Stay Informed

After moving in, spend some time checking that everything is functioning properly. Make sure your lease documents are accessible, and don’t hesitate to contact the property manager if any concerns arise.

You should also familiarize yourself with the apartment community’s rules, from trash pickup to quiet hours, to avoid misunderstandings. And if any maintenance issues come up, use the formal process your landlord outlines—often included in your lease or welcome packet.

Final Thoughts

Moving into a Houston apartment doesn’t have to be complicated. By following this step-by-step guide and using trusted resources like Houston Apartments and the City of Houston Housing Department, you’ll be well-prepared for a stress-free transition.

From paperwork and policies to utilities and inspections, knowing what to expect—and where to get help—can make all the difference. Happy moving!
