**Cebu guitars** are handmade acoustic and classical guitars, ukuleles and folk string instruments crafted mostly in Lapu-Lapu City on Mactan Island, especially in the barangays of Abuno and Maribago. They range from simple souvenir pieces to serious concert instruments, and a good one offers excellent value compared with factory guitars of similar build. Here is what makes them special and how to buy one wisely.

## Where Cebu Guitars Are Made

Guitar-making on Mactan is a family trade passed down over generations. Small workshops line the roads of Abuno and Maribago, a short drive from Mactan-Cebu International Airport, and many families have been building instruments for decades. Visitors can often walk into a showroom, watch craftsmen shaping necks or sanding bodies, and buy directly from the makers.

The tradition grew out of the Spanish-era love of string music. The *rondalla*, an ensemble of plucked instruments such as the *bandurria*, *octavina* and *laud*, is still taught in Philippine schools, and Mactan workshops build these alongside modern guitars.

Some of the larger shops, such as the long-established Alegre Guitar Factory in Abuno, welcome walk-in visitors, and tour itineraries around Mactan often include a stop. Smaller family workshops can be just as good, so it is worth comparing a few.

## The Woods and Craft

What gives Cebu guitars their character is the use of tropical woods. *Nangka* (jackfruit) is a favourite for backs and sides on student instruments because it is light and resonant. Mahogany and narra appear on mid-range and higher-end guitars, and many better instruments pair these with imported spruce or cedar tops for a brighter, more projecting sound.

Decorative shell inlay is a hallmark. Workshops cut small pieces of shell into rosettes, borders and fretboard markers, which is why many Cebu guitars look more ornate than plain factory models. Keep in mind that decoration does not equal quality. A plain guitar with a solid top often sounds better than a heavily inlaid one with a laminated top.

### Solid top versus laminated

- **Solid top:** made from a single solid piece of wood (usually two book-matched halves). It vibrates more freely and the sound tends to improve as it ages.
- **Laminated (plywood) top:** layers of thin wood glued together. Tougher and cheaper, but usually less rich in tone.

Ask the seller directly which you are getting, and look inside the soundhole at the edge of the top. A laminate often shows layered lines.

## What to Check Before Buying

Whether you are buying in a Mactan showroom or a city mall, go through this checklist:

1. **Neck straightness.** Sight down the neck from the headstock. It should be straight or with only a very slight curve.
2. **Action (string height).** Strings that sit too high make the guitar hard to play. Press a few notes around the 7th to 12th frets and see if it feels comfortable.
3. **Fret ends.** Run your hand along the side of the neck. Sharp, protruding fret ends suggest the wood has dried and shrunk.
4. **Tuning stability.** Tune it, strum for a few minutes and check again.
5. **Buzzing.** Play every string on several frets. A little buzz can be fixed with setup; heavy buzz all over is a red flag.
6. **Finish and glue lines.** Look for cracks, gaps around the bridge and lifting at the bridge edge.
7. **Humidity readiness.** Guitars built in humid Cebu can crack if taken straight to a very dry climate. Ask how the wood was dried and consider a humidifier if you are flying to a cold country.

> **Tip:** Bring a friend who plays, or ask the seller to play the guitar for you while you stand a few metres away. You will hear the instrument the way an audience does.

## Cebu Guitar Prices (Approximate)

Prices vary widely by shop, wood and finish, and they change over time, so treat these as rough ranges only and confirm with the seller.

| Type | Approximate price range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Souvenir or mini guitar / basic ukulele | ₱800 – ₱3,000 | Good gift, not a serious instrument |
| Student classical or acoustic (laminated) | ₱2,500 – ₱7,000 | Fine for beginners |
| Mid-range with solid top | ₱8,000 – ₱20,000 | Best value for most players |
| Handcrafted, all-solid, custom | ₱25,000 and up | Ask for build details and setup |

Haggling politely is normal at smaller workshops, especially if you are buying more than one instrument. Ask whether a padded gig bag or hard case is included, since a case adds to the cost but is essential for flying.

## How Cebu Guitars Compare Worldwide

Around the world, most affordable guitars are made in large factories in China, Indonesia and elsewhere, while premium acoustic guitars come from established makers in the United States, Europe and Japan. Classical guitar fans often look to Spain, the instrument's spiritual home.

Cebu guitars occupy an interesting niche. They are genuinely handmade, often by a single craftsman or a small team, yet they cost less than many imported factory instruments of similar specification. The trade-off is consistency: two guitars from the same workshop can sound quite different, so you should always play the exact instrument you are buying.

| Factor | Cebu handmade | Typical imported factory guitar |
|---|---|---|
| Build | Handmade, small workshops | Mass production |
| Consistency | Varies guitar to guitar | Very consistent |
| Price for a solid top | Usually lower | Usually higher |
| Character | Ornate inlay, tropical woods | Standardised looks |
| After-sales support | Direct from maker if local | Through dealers |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where can I buy guitars in Cebu?

The best-known area is Lapu-Lapu City on Mactan, especially Abuno and Maribago, where workshops sell directly to visitors. Music stores in Cebu City malls also stock both local and imported guitars.

### Are Cebu guitars good quality?

Many are very good, particularly mid-range models with solid tops, though quality varies from piece to piece. Always play the actual guitar and check the neck, action and frets before paying.

### How much does a guitar cost in Cebu?

As a rough guide, student guitars start at a few thousand pesos, solid-top models run from around ₱8,000 to ₱20,000, and custom handcrafted instruments cost more. Prices change, so confirm with the shop.

### Can I bring a Cebu guitar on a plane?

Many travellers carry guitars as cabin baggage or check them in a hard case, but airline rules differ. Check your airline's policy before you fly and use a protective case.
