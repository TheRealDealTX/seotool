Davao food is built on two things the region has in abundance: tropical fruit and fresh seafood. Come for durian, pomelo, marang and mangosteen, stay for grilled tuna jaw, *kinilaw* and *sinuglaw*, and leave with Davao-made chocolate and coffee in your bag. This guide covers what to eat, where locals go and what makes the best *pasalubong* (take-home gifts).

## Davao Food at a Glance

| Must-try | What it is | Best time |
|---|---|---|
| Durian | Creamy, pungent "king of fruits" | Peak supply usually around August to October, starting around Kadayawan |
| Pomelo | Large, sweet citrus | Available much of the year |
| Marang | Soft, fragrant fruit with sweet white pods | Seasonal; ask at fruit stalls |
| Mangosteen | Purple fruit with snowy, tangy segments | Seasonal, often mid-year |
| Tuna panga | Grilled tuna jaw | All year |
| Kinilaw / sinuglaw | Fish "cooked" in vinegar; with grilled pork | All year |
| Local chocolate and coffee | Grown in the Davao region | All year |

Fruit seasons shift with the weather each year, so ask vendors what's at its best during your visit.

## Davao's Famous Fruits

### Durian

Davao is the durian capital of the Philippines in the popular imagination, and the fruit is everywhere: in stalls, candies, ice cream, coffee and pastries. Its smell is famously strong, but the custard-like flesh wins many converts. Varieties differ in sweetness, bitterness and texture, so let a vendor help you choose and open one. Our dedicated [durian guide](/food/davao-durian-guide/) goes deeper.

> **Tip:** Many hotels, malls and vehicles ban durian because of the smell. Eat it at the stall, and check with your airline before trying to pack fresh durian.

### Pomelo, marang and mangosteen

Davao **pomelo** is sweet, juicy and easy to carry, which makes it a popular pasalubong. **Marang** is soft and fragrant, best eaten ripe on the day you buy it. **Mangosteen**, sometimes called the "queen of fruits," has a thick purple rind and tangy-sweet white segments. Fruit stalls cluster in markets and along busy roads, and during Kadayawan the fruit displays become part of the celebration. See our [Kadayawan Festival guide](/festivals/whats-so-interesting-about-the-kadayawan-festival/).

## Seafood: Tuna, Kinilaw and Sinuglaw

Davao sits on the Davao Gulf, and nearby General Santos City is the country's best-known tuna port. That means tuna is excellent and affordable here compared with many places.

### Grilled tuna panga

*Panga* is the jaw and collar of a large tuna, marinated and grilled until the fatty meat falls off the bone. It is a centrepiece at seafood restaurants and *sugba* (grill) stalls. Share one with a group alongside rice and a dipping sauce of soy, calamansi and chillies.

### Kinilaw

*Kinilaw* is raw fish cubed and "cooked" in vinegar or citrus with ginger, onion, chilli and sometimes coconut milk. In Davao, tuna and *malasugi* (marlin) are popular choices. Freshness is everything, so eat it at busy places. Try our [Davao-style kinilaw recipe](/food/kinilaw-na-malasugi-davao-style/).

### Sinuglaw

*Sinuglaw* combines *sinugba* (grilled pork belly) and *kinilaw*: smoky, fatty pork chunks tossed with vinegared fish. It's a Mindanao favourite, often ordered as *pulutan* (food to share with drinks).

## Home-Style Bisaya Dishes in Davao

Davao is largely Cebuano-speaking, and many home dishes are shared with Cebu. Two you'll meet often:

- **Pochero** — in Cebuano kitchens, a hearty beef shank and bone-marrow soup with vegetables, similar to *bulalo*. Many families make a Cebu-style pochero recipe with corn, cabbage and pechay, simmered for hours until the beef is tender.
- **Humba** — a Visayan braised pork dish, slow-cooked with soy sauce, vinegar, sugar and sometimes salted black beans until sticky and tender. It's sweeter and darker than adobo.

You'll also find *utan Bisaya* (vegetable soup) and batchoy on many tables; see our [utan Bisaya recipe](/food/utan-bisaya-davao-style/).

## Davao Chocolate and Coffee

The Davao region grows cacao and coffee, and local makers have built a following for single-origin chocolate. **Malagos Chocolate**, made from cacao grown in the Malagos area of upland Davao City, is one of the best-known Davao brands and has received international recognition. Look for Davao chocolate bars and *tablea* (pure cacao tablets for hot chocolate) in pasalubong shops and malls.

Coffee from the region's highlands, including areas around Mount Apo and Marilog, shows up in local cafés. Café culture has grown fast in the city; read about it in our [Cebu and Davao café guide](/lifestyle/coffee-shops-and-cafe-culture-cebu-davao/).

## Where to Eat in Davao

| Area | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Roxas Avenue night market | Street-food stalls with grilled skewers, seafood and snacks in the evening |
| Downtown and San Pedro Street area | Older eateries, bakeries and Chinese restaurants near the city's historic core |
| Seafood and *sugba* restaurants | Pick your fish, choose how it's cooked: grilled, soup or kinilaw |
| Fruit stalls and markets | Durian, pomelo, marang and mangosteen by the kilo or piece |
| Malls and Lanang / Matina | Cafés and restaurant chains with air-conditioning |

The night market is the easiest introduction: walk the stalls, point at what looks good and eat at the shared tables. For sit-down recommendations, see our list of [where to eat in Davao](/restaurants-2/top-ten-restaurants-in-davao/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What food is Davao famous for?

Davao is famous for durian, pomelo, marang and mangosteen, as well as fresh tuna dishes like grilled panga and kinilaw. It is also known for locally grown chocolate and coffee.

### When is durian season in Davao?

Supply usually peaks from around August to October, and durian is everywhere during Kadayawan in August. Exact timing varies with weather, so ask local vendors.

### What is sinuglaw?

Sinuglaw is a Mindanao dish that combines grilled pork belly (*sinugba*) with *kinilaw*, raw fish cured in vinegar or citrus with onions, ginger and chilli.

### What is Cebu-style pochero?

In Cebuano cooking, pochero is a rich beef shank and bone-marrow soup with vegetables such as corn and cabbage, similar to bulalo. It's popular in both Cebu and Davao.

### What is the best pasalubong from Davao?

Durian-flavoured sweets, pomelo, Davao chocolate or tablea, and local coffee are the most popular choices.
