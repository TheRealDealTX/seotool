**AyosDito** (ayosdito.ph) was a popular Philippine online classifieds website for buying and selling used items, cars, property and more. Around 2014 it was merged into OLX Philippines, together with rival Sulit.com.ph, so the old AyosDito address no longer works as a separate marketplace. Today, Filipinos mostly buy and sell secondhand items on Facebook Marketplace and Carousell, and new goods on Shopee, Lazada and TikTok Shop.

## What Was AyosDito?

The name is a play on Filipino slang: *ayos dito* roughly means "it's good here" or "all set here". The site let anyone post free ads for gadgets, furniture, vehicles, jobs and real estate, and buyers contacted sellers directly to meet up or arrange delivery.

In the early 2010s, AyosDito was one of the best-known classifieds sites in the country alongside Sulit and OLX. Many people in Cebu and Davao used it to sell secondhand phones, motorcycles and appliances, or to find rooms and apartments for rent.

## What Happened to AyosDito?

The Philippine online classifieds market consolidated in the mid-2010s. Based on public reports at the time, the timeline looked roughly like this:

| Period | What happened (approximate) |
|---|---|
| Early 2010s | AyosDito, Sulit and OLX compete as major Philippine classifieds sites |
| Around 2014 | Sulit and AyosDito are combined into the OLX Philippines brand |
| Late 2010s | OLX Philippines remains a major classifieds platform |
| Around 2019–2020 | OLX Philippines is combined with Carousell's business in the country |

Exact dates and deal details varied in reporting, so treat this as an overview rather than a precise corporate history. The practical point is simple: if you type ayosdito.com, ayosdito.ph or www ayosdito com today, you will not find the old marketplace. Be careful with any site claiming to be AyosDito, since old brand names are sometimes used by unrelated or suspicious websites.

## Where to Buy and Sell Online in the Philippines Now

The marketplace landscape has changed a lot since the AyosDito days. Social media and big e-commerce apps now dominate.

| Platform | Best for | How payments usually work |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook Marketplace | Local secondhand items, cars, rentals | Cash on meet-up, bank or e-wallet transfer |
| Carousell | Secondhand gadgets, fashion, collectibles | Meet-up or delivery; some in-app options |
| Shopee | New items, small sellers, fast delivery | In-app payment, COD, e-wallets |
| Lazada | New items, official brand stores | In-app payment, COD, cards, e-wallets |
| TikTok Shop | Trending products, live selling | In-app checkout |

### Facebook Marketplace

For local, secondhand deals, Facebook Marketplace is now the closest thing to the old AyosDito. You can search by location, so it is easy to find items in Cebu City, Mandaue, Lapu-Lapu or Davao City. There are also many local buy-and-sell Facebook groups.

### Carousell

Carousell is a dedicated marketplace app with listings for gadgets, fashion, hobbies, cars and property. It feels the most like a classic classifieds site.

### Shopee and Lazada

These are the giants of Philippine e-commerce for new products. They offer buyer protection, ratings and in-app payment, which makes them safer for beginners than direct deals.

### TikTok Shop

Live selling has become huge in the Philippines. TikTok Shop combines short videos and live streams with checkout, and many small sellers in Cebu and Davao use it alongside Facebook live selling.

## Safety Tips for Buying and Selling Online

Online scams are common everywhere. Protect yourself with these habits:

1. **Meet in public places.** Choose malls, busy coffee shops or police-station "safe zones" where available. Bring a companion for expensive items.
2. **Inspect before paying.** Test gadgets, check serial numbers and confirm the item matches the listing.
3. **Avoid paying in advance to strangers.** Many scams ask for a deposit or "reservation fee" by e-wallet and then disappear.
4. **Use in-app payments when possible.** Shopee and Lazada hold payment until delivery, giving you more protection.
5. **Check the seller.** Look at ratings, account age and reviews. Brand-new accounts with very low prices are a red flag.
6. **Never share one-time PINs (OTPs).** No legitimate buyer or seller needs your OTP, password or full card details.
7. **Beware of fake payment screenshots.** Sellers should confirm the money has actually arrived in their account before handing over items.
8. **Verify car and property documents.** For vehicles, check the OR/CR (official receipt and certificate of registration). For rentals, never pay before viewing the property.

If you are scammed, report it to the platform, your bank or e-wallet provider, and the PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group. The government's [Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center](https://cicc.gov.ph) also accepts reports.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is AyosDito still working?

No. AyosDito was folded into OLX Philippines around 2014 and no longer runs as a separate marketplace. Be wary of any site using the AyosDito name today.

### What replaced AyosDito?

OLX Philippines absorbed it, and OLX's Philippine business was later combined with Carousell. Today most local buy-and-sell activity happens on Facebook Marketplace and Carousell.

### What is the safest way to buy secondhand items online?

Meet the seller in a public place, inspect the item before paying, and avoid sending deposits to strangers. For new items, using in-app payment on Shopee or Lazada gives you more protection.

### Where can I sell used items in Cebu or Davao?

Facebook Marketplace and local buy-and-sell groups are the most active, followed by Carousell. Clear photos, honest descriptions and fair prices help items sell faster.
