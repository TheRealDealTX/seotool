To get to Simala Shrine from Cebu City, take a southbound bus from the Cebu South Bus Terminal, get off at the Simala junction in Sibonga, then ride a tricycle or *habal-habal* (motorcycle taxi) uphill to the shrine. The trip usually takes about two to three hours each way depending on traffic. Driving or hiring a car is faster and more comfortable, especially for groups.

The shrine, formally the Monastery of the Holy Eucharist in Lindogon, Simala, Sibonga, is one of Cebu's most visited pilgrimage sites. Here is everything you need for a smooth visit.

## Simala Shrine at a Glance

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Official name | Monastery of the Holy Eucharist |
| Location | Lindogon, Simala, Sibonga, Cebu |
| Distance from Cebu City | Roughly 50-60 km south |
| Travel time | About 2-3 hours by bus; less by private car with light traffic |
| Known for | Castle-like architecture, Marian devotion, prayer requests |
| Dress code | Modest clothing required; no shorts, sleeveless tops or short skirts |

## Why People Visit Simala

Simala is run by a Marian religious community, and it has drawn pilgrims for years because of devotion to the Virgin Mary and stories of answered prayers shared by visitors. Many come to pray for health, family, exams or work, and some return to give thanks.

Even if you are not religious, the building is striking. Its towers, turrets and ornate details make it look like a European castle set on a Cebu hillside, and the grounds offer views over the countryside. Inside you will find chapels, statues, devotional areas and halls displaying items and letters left by grateful pilgrims.

## How to Get to Simala by Public Transport

### Step 1: Go to the Cebu South Bus Terminal

The Cebu South Bus Terminal (CSBT) on N. Bacalso Avenue serves buses heading to southern Cebu. Take a taxi, Grab or jeepney there. Arrive early on weekends, as queues can be long.

### Step 2: Ride a southbound bus

Board a bus or van bound for southern towns that pass through Sibonga, such as those heading to Argao, Dalaguete, Oslob or beyond. Tell the conductor you are going to Simala so they can drop you at the right junction. Fares are approximate and change, but a regular bus ticket to Sibonga is usually in the range of PHP 80-150, with air-conditioned buses at the higher end.

### Step 3: Get off at the Simala junction

The drop-off point is along the national highway in Sibonga, where the road to the shrine branches inland. Conductors on this route are used to pilgrims and generally know the stop.

### Step 4: Tricycle or habal-habal to the shrine

From the junction, tricycles and habal-habal wait to take visitors uphill to the shrine. Agree on the fare before you go; it is usually a modest amount per person. Some drivers will also offer a return trip at an agreed time.

> **Tip:** Some vans and jeepneys run directly to the shrine, especially on weekends and holidays. Ask around the south terminal or nearby van stops, but confirm the destination before boarding.

## How to Get to Simala by Car

If you are driving, head south from Cebu City along the coastal highway through Talisay, Minglanilla, Naga, San Fernando and Carcar into Sibonga, then follow the signs inland to the shrine. A navigation app makes this easy. Parking is available near the shrine but fills up on weekends and holy days.

Hiring a car with a driver is popular with families and groups. Prices vary by vehicle and whether you add other stops, so get quotes and confirm what is included (fuel, tolls, driver's meals).

## Dress Code and Shrine Etiquette

Simala strictly enforces a modest dress code. Guards may turn away visitors who are not appropriately dressed.

- **Wear:** Long pants or skirts below the knee, tops with sleeves, closed or decent footwear.
- **Avoid:** Shorts, mini skirts, sleeveless tops, spaghetti straps, see-through or very tight clothing.
- **Bring a shawl or sarong** just in case. Wraps are sometimes available on site, but do not rely on it.

Inside, keep your voice low, follow posted rules on photography, and be mindful of people praying. Queues to certain devotional areas can be long, so be patient.

## Best Time to Visit Simala

- **Weekdays, early morning** are the quietest.
- **Weekends, holidays and Holy Week** bring large crowds and longer travel times.
- **Rainy season** (roughly June to November) can bring afternoon downpours; bring an umbrella. Check the [Cebu weather](/weather/) before you go.

## What to Bring

1. Modest clothes and a light shawl
2. Water and snacks (there are food stalls, but queues can be long)
3. Cash in small bills for fares, candles and donations
4. Umbrella or hat
5. A written list of prayer intentions, if you have them

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far is Simala from Cebu City?

Simala is in Sibonga, roughly 50-60 km south of Cebu City. Travel takes about two to three hours by bus, depending on traffic.

### What bus goes to Simala?

Take any southbound bus or van from the Cebu South Bus Terminal that passes through Sibonga, such as those going to Argao, Dalaguete or Oslob. Ask to be dropped at the Simala junction.

### What is the dress code at Simala Shrine?

Modest clothing is required. Avoid shorts, sleeveless tops, short skirts and revealing clothes. Long pants or skirts and tops with sleeves are safest.

### Can I visit Simala and Oslob in one day?

Yes, but it makes for a long day. Start very early, visit Oslob first in the morning and stop at Simala on the way back to Cebu City.
