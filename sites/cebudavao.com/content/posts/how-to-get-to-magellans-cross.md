Magellan's Cross stands inside a small octagonal kiosk on Magallanes Street in downtown Cebu City, right beside the Basilica Minore del Santo Niño and close to Cebu City Hall. The easiest way to get there is by taxi or Grab; budget travellers can take a jeepney heading downtown. Once there, you can walk to Fort San Pedro and Plaza Independencia in about 10 to 15 minutes.

## The History of the Cross of Magellan

In April 1521, Portuguese explorer Ferdinand Magellan, sailing for Spain, arrived in Cebu. Rajah Humabon, the local ruler, and many of his people were baptised, and a cross was planted to mark the event. A few weeks later, Magellan was killed in the Battle of Mactan by forces led by Lapu-Lapu.

Today, the original cross is said to be encased inside a protective wooden cross, partly because devotees once chipped off pieces as relics. The kiosk's ceiling features a painting depicting the planting of the cross. For Filipino Catholics, the site marks the beginning of Christianity in the Philippines and remains a place of prayer as well as a tourist stop.

> **Tip:** Candle vendors around the plaza offer prayer-dance rituals and coloured candles, each colour linked to a different intention. It's a lovely local tradition to watch; be respectful if you take photos.

## How to Get to Magellan's Cross

### By taxi or Grab

From most parts of Metro Cebu, a taxi or Grab is the simplest choice. Tell the driver "Magellan's Cross" or "Santo Niño Church", and every driver will know it. Expect heavy traffic downtown at rush hour.

| Starting point | Approximate travel time by car |
|---|---|
| Cebu IT Park / Lahug | 20-40 minutes |
| Ayala Center Cebu | 15-30 minutes |
| SM City Cebu | 15-25 minutes |
| Mactan-Cebu International Airport | 45-90 minutes depending on traffic and route |

Times are approximate. From the airport, see our guide to [Mactan airport taxis](/travel/yellow-taxis-at-mactan-international-airport/).

### By jeepney

Jeepneys are the cheapest way downtown. Look for routes heading to **Colon** or **Carbon**, two downtown landmarks within walking distance of the Basilica and the cross. Ask the driver or fellow passengers to tell you where to get off; saying *"Lugar lang, sa Santo Niño"* ("Stop here, at Santo Niño") works well. Fares are low and change from time to time; carry coins and small bills.

### On foot

If you're already downtown, at Colon Street or Carbon Market, it's a short walk. Keep your phone and wallet secure in crowded areas.

## Walking Route: Magellan's Cross to Fort San Pedro

Downtown Cebu's main heritage sites are close together, so you can see them in a half-day walk.

1. **Basilica Minore del Santo Niño** — start at the Philippines' oldest Roman Catholic church site and home of the Santo Niño image. Read our [Santo Niño de Cebu history](/culture/santo-nino-de-cebu-history/).
2. **Magellan's Cross** — next door, in its kiosk on Magallanes Street.
3. **Cebu City Hall** — directly across the area, a handsome government building.
4. **Fort San Pedro** — walk south-east toward the waterfront, around 10-15 minutes. It's a small Spanish-era triangular fort with gardens and walls you can explore.
5. **Plaza Independencia** — the park beside the fort, with shady trees and monuments.

You can continue to the Heritage of Cebu Monument and the Yap-Sandiego Ancestral House in the Parian district if you have time. For more ideas, see our list of [Cebu tourist spots](/travel/destinations/cebu-tourist-spots/) and our [3-5 day Cebu itinerary](/travel/cebu-itinerary-3-5-days/).

## Tips for Visiting

- **Go early in the morning** to avoid heat and crowds.
- **Dress modestly**, especially if visiting the Basilica (cover shoulders and knees).
- **Expect huge crowds in January** during the Sinulog and the Feast of the Santo Niño. See our [Sinulog guide](/festivals/what-makes-the-sinulog-2012-festival-the-number-one-festival-in-the-philippines/).
- **Watch your belongings** in busy downtown streets.

## What You'll See at Magellan's Cross

The cross stands at the centre of a small, open-sided octagonal pavilion with a tiled roof. Look up to see the ceiling painting showing the planting of the cross and the baptism scene of 1521, a favourite photo subject. The wooden cross itself is large, dark and protected, so you view it from a short distance rather than touching it.

Around the pavilion, candle vendors, rosary sellers and souvenir stalls keep the plaza busy. Inside the neighbouring Basilica, devotees queue to view the image of the Santo Niño, and Masses are held throughout the day. Visitors are welcome, but remember that this is an active place of worship, so keep noise down during services.

Downtown Cebu has plenty of simple eateries serving *puso*, grilled skewers and local favourites. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where is Magellan's Cross located?

It's on Magallanes Street in downtown Cebu City, beside the Basilica Minore del Santo Niño and near Cebu City Hall.

### Is the original Magellan's Cross still there?

The cross on display is said to encase the original cross planted in 1521, protecting it from devotees who once took pieces as relics.

### Is there an entrance fee to Magellan's Cross?

There was no entrance fee at last check, though donations are welcome. Confirm locally, as arrangements can change.

### How far is Magellan's Cross from Fort San Pedro?

About a 10-15 minute walk, making it easy to visit both, plus Plaza Independencia, in one trip.
