Are **inhalers allowed on airplanes**? Yes, on both domestic and international flights in the Philippines. Keep them in your carry-on so you can use them during the flight, and bring a prescription or doctor's note if you can. Below is a quick guide to what else you can and cannot pack, from liquids and power banks to lechon, durian and dried mangoes.

## Who Sets the Rules in the Philippines?

You may have heard of the TSA, but that is the US Transportation Security Administration and it only covers flights from American airports. In the Philippines, airport screening is handled by the **Office for Transportation Security (OTS)**, while each airline, such as Philippine Airlines or Cebu Pacific, sets its own rules on baggage allowance, dangerous goods and special items.

Rules follow international aviation standards, so they are broadly similar to those in other countries. But airlines can be stricter than the minimum, and screening officers have the final say. Always check your airline's website before you fly.

> **Tip:** "Baggage" in Filipino is *bagahe*. Checked baggage is often called *check-in bagahe*, and carry-on is *hand-carry*.

## Are Inhalers Allowed on Airplanes? Medicine Rules

Asthma inhalers, both metered-dose and dry powder, are generally allowed in carry-on and checked baggage. To make screening easy:

- Keep inhalers and essential medicine in your **carry-on**, not checked luggage.
- Bring medicine in its **original packaging** with a readable label.
- Carry a **prescription or doctor's note**, especially for liquid medicine, injectables or large quantities.
- Declare liquid medicine over 100 ml at screening. Necessary medicines are usually exempt from the liquid limit but may be inspected.
- If you use a nebuliser or portable oxygen, contact your airline in advance.

This article is for general information, not medical advice. If you have a respiratory condition, see your doctor before travelling.

## Carry-On vs Checked: Quick Reference

| Item | Carry-on | Checked | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asthma inhaler | Yes | Yes | Keep in carry-on for use |
| Liquids, gels, aerosols | Up to 100 ml each | Yes | In one clear resealable bag of about 1 litre |
| Prescription medicine | Yes | Yes | Bring label or prescription |
| Power bank | Yes | No | Watt-hour limits apply |
| Spare lithium batteries | Yes | No | Protect terminals |
| Lighter | Restricted | No | Check airline and OTS rules |
| Knives and sharp tools | No | Yes | Includes scissors with long blades |
| Dried mangoes, danggit | Yes | Yes | Seal well to contain smell |
| Durian | Usually no | Usually no | Most airlines ban it |

Rules can change and vary by airline. Treat this table as a general guide and confirm with your carrier.

## The 100 ml Liquid Rule

For carry-on bags, liquids, gels and aerosols must be in containers of **100 ml or less**, all fitting into one clear, resealable plastic bag of about one litre. This covers toiletries, sauces, peanut butter, soft cheese and even some *pasalubong* like bottled spreads or vinegar.

Exceptions usually include medicine and baby food or milk in reasonable quantities. Anything larger goes in your checked luggage.

## Power Banks and Electronics

Power banks and spare lithium batteries must go in your **carry-on only**. Under common airline rules:

- Up to **100 Wh** (around 27,000 mAh at 3.7V): generally allowed.
- **100 to 160 Wh**: usually needs airline approval, with a limit on quantity.
- **Over 160 Wh**: not allowed.

Many airlines now also restrict using or charging power banks during the flight. Check your airline's current policy, as these rules have been tightened in recent years.

## Lighters and Matches

Lighters and matches are restricted items. They are never allowed in checked baggage, and screening officers may confiscate them from carry-on bags. If you smoke, plan to buy a lighter at your destination.

## Food and Pasalubong

Bringing *pasalubong* (gifts from your trip) home is a Filipino tradition, and most dry food is fine.

### Dried Mangoes, Danggit and Otap

Dried mangoes, *otap*, *rosquillos* and other baked goods can go in carry-on or checked bags. Dried fish like *danggit* is allowed but smells strong, so pack it in sealed containers or vacuum packs. Our [pasalubong guide](/food/filipino-pasalubong-guide-cebu-davao/) lists the best things to buy in Cebu and Davao, and our guide to [danggit](/food/kitang-or-danggit-or-samaral-rabbitfish-or-spinefoot/) explains what it is.

### Lechon on a Plane

Cebu lechon is a classic pasalubong. Shops that sell to travellers usually pack it in sealed boxes for flights, and it is commonly carried on domestic routes. Ask the shop for travel packaging and check your airline's rules on carry-on weight and size. Read more about [Cebu lechon](/food/secret-ingredients-of-cebu-lechon-revealed/).

### Can You Bring Durian on a Flight?

Usually not. Because of its powerful smell, most airlines in the Philippines do not allow fresh durian in the cabin or in checked bags. Durian candy, jam and dried durian in sealed packaging are often accepted, but confirm with your airline. See our [Davao durian guide](/food/davao-durian-guide/).

## Popular Routes: Manila, Cebu and Davao

Flights between Manila, Cebu and Davao are some of the busiest in the country, served by carriers including Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines and others. Baggage allowances differ by fare type. Budget fares often include only a small carry-on, with checked baggage added for a fee. Booking baggage online in advance is usually cheaper than paying at the airport.

## Before You Fly: Checklist

1. Check your airline's baggage allowance and dangerous goods page.
2. Put medicine, inhalers, power banks and valuables in your carry-on.
3. Pack liquids over 100 ml in checked luggage.
4. Seal strong-smelling food.
5. Complete any required entry forms, such as eTravel, for international trips.

For official guidance, check the [Office for Transportation Security](https://www.ots.gov.ph), [Philippine Airlines](https://www.philippineairlines.com) and [Cebu Pacific](https://www.cebupacificair.com).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are inhalers allowed on airplanes in the Philippines?

Yes. Inhalers are allowed in both carry-on and checked baggage, but keep them in your carry-on so you can use them in flight. Bringing a prescription helps.

### Does an inhaler count as a liquid?

Inhalers are medical items and are generally screened separately from the 100 ml liquid rule. Declare them if asked.

### Can I bring a power bank on a domestic flight?

Yes, in your carry-on only. Power banks up to 100 Wh are generally allowed, and larger ones may need airline approval.

### Can I bring lechon on a plane?

Lechon is commonly carried on domestic flights when packed in sealed travel boxes. Check your airline's carry-on weight and size limits first.

### What is bagahe in English?

*Bagahe* is the Filipino word for baggage or luggage, borrowed from Spanish *bagaje*.
