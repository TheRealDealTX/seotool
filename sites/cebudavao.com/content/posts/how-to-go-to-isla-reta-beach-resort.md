Isla Reta is a simple, laid-back **Talikud Island beach resort** known for fine white sand, clear water and overnight camping, just off the coast of Samal and a short boat ride from Davao City. The usual route is a public boat from Santa Ana Wharf in downtown Davao, or a trip via Samal Island. Here is how to get there, what to bring and what to expect.

## Where Is Talikud Island?

Talikud is a smaller island southwest of Samal Island in the Davao Gulf. It is part of the Island Garden City of Samal and is quieter and less developed than Samal itself. People come for its beaches, snorkelling and the feeling of being far away while staying close to the city.

Isla Reta sits on Talikud's coast and is best known as a budget-friendly day trip and camping spot. Facilities are basic, which is part of the charm. For other options on the island and on Samal, see our list of [resorts in Davao and Samal](/beach/list-of-resort-destinations-in-davao/).

## How to Get to Isla Reta from Davao City

### Option 1: Boat from Santa Ana Wharf

This is the most common route for travellers already in the city.

1. **Get to Santa Ana Wharf.** It is in the downtown area near the old port district. Take a taxi or ride-hailing car, or a jeepney heading to the Santa Ana area.
2. **Take a public boat to Talikud.** Motorised outrigger boats (*pump boats*) carry passengers and cargo to Talikud. Ask at the wharf for the trip that stops closest to Isla Reta. Departures are typically in the morning, and schedules depend on demand and weather.
3. **Ride to the resort.** Depending on where the boat lands, you may walk or take a *habal-habal* (motorcycle taxi) to Isla Reta.

The boat ride usually takes around 45 minutes to an hour, depending on the boat and the sea. Return trips are usually limited in the afternoon, so confirm the last boat back.

### Option 2: Via Samal Island

If you are already on Samal, or want to combine both islands:

1. **Cross to Samal.** From Davao City, take the ferry or barge from the Sasa area to a landing point on Samal. Our guide explains [how to reach Samal Island](/travel/tips/how-to-reach-the-samal-island-in-davao/).
2. **Find a boat landing.** Ask a habal-habal or tricycle driver to bring you to a landing with trips to Talikud.
3. **Cross to Talikud.** Small boats cross the channel, and some can be hired privately to bring you directly to the resort.

### Option 3: Private Boat Hire

For groups, hiring a private boat from Davao City or Samal offers more flexibility, especially for a day trip that includes snorkelling stops. Rates depend on boat size, distance and negotiation.

> **Tip:** If you are coming straight from the airport, Santa Ana Wharf is the simpler option. Book a taxi to the wharf rather than to Samal, and travel early to catch a morning boat. Our [Davao airport taxi guide](/travel/taxi-procedure-davao-international-airport/) explains the process.

## What About the Davao Water Taxi?

Searches for a "Davao water taxi" usually mean the small boats crossing to Samal or Talikud. These are informal passenger boats rather than a city-run water taxi service with fixed routes. Services can change, so ask at the wharf or your accommodation for current options.

## What to Bring

Facilities are basic, so pack as if you are camping.

- **Cash** in small bills. There are no ATMs nearby.
- **Food and drinking water**, or buy simple meals if available.
- **Tent and sleeping mat** if you plan to camp and are not renting.
- **Flashlight or headlamp** and a power bank. Electricity may be limited.
- **Snorkel and mask** for the reef.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen**, hat and sunglasses.
- **Insect repellent** for the evening.
- **Trash bag** to take your rubbish back with you.
- **Dry bag** to protect phones and documents on the boat.

## Camping Tips at Isla Reta

1. **Arrive early** to choose a shaded tent spot.
2. **Check the tide** and do not pitch too close to the waterline.
3. **Respect quiet hours** if the resort has them.
4. **Leave no trace.** Small islands struggle with waste, so take everything back.
5. **Check the weather.** Avoid going during storms or strong winds. See the [Davao weather](/weather/) forecast.

## Is This Talikud Island Beach Resort Worth It?

If you want a five-star resort, look elsewhere. But if you love simple beach days, clear water and sleeping to the sound of waves without leaving the Davao area, Talikud is a gem. For a more luxurious stay nearby, see our guide to the [Pearl Farm resort on Samal](/beach/pearl-farm-davao/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I get to Isla Reta Beach Resort?

The easiest route is a public boat from Santa Ana Wharf in Davao City to Talikud Island, then a short walk or habal-habal ride to the resort. You can also go via Samal Island.

### How long is the boat ride to Talikud Island?

From Santa Ana Wharf, the boat ride usually takes around 45 minutes to an hour, depending on the boat and sea conditions.

### Can you camp at Isla Reta?

Yes, Isla Reta is known for camping. You can usually bring your own tent or rent one, but confirm availability and rates with the resort first.

### Is there electricity on Talikud Island?

Electricity can be limited at simple beach resorts. Bring a power bank and a flashlight, and charge your devices before you leave the city.
