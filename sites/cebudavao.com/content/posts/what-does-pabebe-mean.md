The most common way to say **childish in Tagalog** is *isip-bata* (literally "child-minded") or *parang bata* ("like a child"). Online, Filipinos often use a playful word instead: *pabebe*, which describes someone acting cute, whiny or baby-like on purpose. Here is what it means, where it came from and the other Pinoy slang you will hear alongside it.

## What Does Pabebe Mean?

*Pabebe* comes from *pa-* (a prefix meaning "pretending to be" or "acting as") and *bebe* or *baby*. So a *pabebe* person is someone "acting like a baby". It usually describes:

- Talking in a sweet, high-pitched or baby voice to get attention
- Pouting or acting overly cute in photos and videos
- Being clingy or whiny in a way that seems deliberate

It is mostly teasing, not a serious insult. Friends say *"Ang pabebe mo!"* ("You're being so pabebe!") when someone is acting coy. But it can sting if used to mock someone, so read the room.

## Where Did Pabebe Come From?

The word became a nationwide buzzword in 2015. A short video of a group of young women, later nicknamed the "Pabebe Girls", went viral for their exaggerated cute delivery, and an online argument around it spread fast. Soon after, the *pabebe wave*, a small, shy hand wave, became a pop-culture moment on noontime TV and social media.

Like much internet slang, the word outlived the viral moment. Today *pabebe* is simply part of everyday Taglish.

*Pabebe* is just one option. Here are more ways Filipinos describe childish or immature behaviour.

## Spoiled Brat in Tagalog

There is no single perfect translation for "spoiled brat", but these come close:

- **Laki sa layaw** means "raised with every whim indulged". It is the closest phrase for spoiled.
- **Spoiled** is commonly used as is in Taglish: *"Spoiled na spoiled ang anak niya."*
- **Maarte** describes someone fussy or demanding, often overlapping with "bratty".
- **Pasaway** means stubborn or disobedient, commonly used for naughty kids.

So "spoiled meaning in Tagalog" is usually *laki sa layaw*, while a naughty, stubborn child is *pasaway*.

## Selfie Meaning in Tagalog

Filipinos simply say *selfie*. There is no widely used native Tagalog word for it, and attempts at literal translations, such as describing it as a photo of oneself, never caught on in everyday speech. Filipinos are enthusiastic social media users, so the English word fits right in, along with related terms like *groufie* or *groupie* for group shots.

## Popular Pinoy Internet Slang

Filipino slang moves fast, and much of it plays with reversed syllables or shortened phrases. Here are terms you will still see in comments and group chats.

| Slang | Origin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Charot* / *char* | Gay lingo (*swardspeak*) | "Just kidding!" |
| *Lodi* | "Idol" spelled backwards | Someone you look up to |
| *Petmalu* | *Malupit* (fierce) reversed | Awesome, amazing |
| *Werpa* | "Power" reversed | Strength, "you've got this" |
| *Sana all* | "I hope everyone has it" | Mild envy, "I wish that were me too" |
| *Naol* | Short for *sana all* | Same as sana all |
| *Marites* | From "Mare, ano ang latest?" | A gossip |
| *Beshie* | From "best friend" | Bestie, close friend |
| *Jowa* | Gay lingo | Boyfriend or girlfriend |
| *Awit* | Interjection | "Ouch", "that's sad" |

### How to Use Them Naturally

1. **Charot** softens a tease: *"Ang tanda mo na, charot!"* ("You're so old, just kidding!")
2. **Sana all** reacts to someone's good news: a friend posts a beach photo and you comment *"Sana all!"*
3. **Marites** labels a gossip: *"Huwag kang marites."* ("Don't be a gossip.")
4. **Lodi** compliments: *"Lodi talaga kita!"* ("You're my idol!")

> **Tip:** Some slang ages quickly. Words like *petmalu* and *werpa* peaked a few years ago, so younger Filipinos may tease you for using them. *Sana all* and *marites* remain very common.

## Slang in Cebu and Davao

In the Visayas and Mindanao, Taglish slang mixes freely with Bisaya. You might hear *"Pabebe kaayo ka!"* ("You're so pabebe!"), with *kaayo* meaning "very". ## Why Filipino Slang Spreads So Fast

Filipinos are enthusiastic social media users, and slang travels fast through memes, noontime shows, teleseryes and group chats. Many words come from *swardspeak*, the playful lingo of the Filipino gay community, which has given the mainstream terms like *charot* and *jowa*.

Another favourite trick is reversing syllables, as in *lodi* and *petmalu*. It is a game that makes everyday words feel fresh and a little secret.

## Is It Rude to Call Someone Pabebe?

Among friends, it is usually light teasing. With strangers, elders or in professional settings, avoid it, since calling someone childish can come across as disrespectful. Filipinos value *pakikisama* (getting along) and respect for elders, so keep slang for casual chats.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is childish in Tagalog?

The most common Tagalog words are *isip-bata* (child-minded) and *parang bata* (like a child). In casual slang, *pabebe* describes someone acting cute or baby-like on purpose.

### What does pabebe mean in English?

Pabebe means acting like a baby, usually by being overly cute, coy or whiny to get attention. It is mostly used as playful teasing.

### How do you say spoiled brat in Tagalog?

The closest phrase is *laki sa layaw*, meaning someone raised with every wish granted. Filipinos also commonly use the English word "spoiled" in Taglish.

### What does sana all mean?

*Sana all* is Taglish for "I hope everyone gets that too". People use it to express friendly envy at someone's good fortune, and it is often shortened to *naol*.

### What does marites mean?

*Marites* is slang for a gossip, from the phrase "Mare, ano ang latest?" ("Friend, what's the latest?"). Calling someone a marites means they love spreading chismis.
