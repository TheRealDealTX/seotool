The **Philippine Eagle Center in Davao City** is in Malagos, in Davao City's Baguio District near Calinan, about an hour to an hour and a half's drive north-west of downtown, depending on traffic. You can reach it by private car, taxi or ride-hailing, or by jeepney or van to Calinan followed by a short tricycle or motorcycle ride. It is the best place in the country to see the Philippine eagle, the national bird, up close.

Here is how to get there, what you will see, and why the centre matters for one of the world's rarest birds of prey.

## Philippine Eagle Center at a glance

| Detail | What to know |
|---|---|
| Location | Malagos, Baguio District (near Calinan), Davao City |
| Run by | The Philippine Eagle Foundation |
| Main attraction | Philippine eagles in large aviaries, plus other native birds and animals |
| Travel time from downtown | Roughly 1 to 1.5 hours by car, longer by public transport |
| Nearby | Malagos Garden Resort, cacao farms, Calinan town |
| Good to bring | Light jacket, water, comfortable shoes, insect repellent |

Entrance fees, opening hours and visiting rules can change. Check the Philippine Eagle Foundation's official channels before you go.

## How to get to the Philippine Eagle Center by car

Driving is the easiest option, especially for families or groups.

1. From downtown Davao City, head towards Calinan. Ask your driver or check a map app, as there are several routes through the city's western districts.
2. In Calinan, follow the signs to Malagos and the Philippine Eagle Center.
3. The centre sits next to Malagos Garden Resort, so either name will get you there.

Taxis and ride-hailing cars can take you there, but they may charge for the return trip or be hard to find for the ride back. Agree on a waiting or return arrangement in advance, or book a private car or tour for the day.

> **Tip:** Combine the Eagle Center with lunch and a chocolate stop at the neighbouring Malagos Garden Resort. Read our guide on [how to get to Malagos Garden Resort](/travel/tips/how-to-get-to-malagos-garden-resort/).

## How to get there by public transport

Public transport is cheaper and very doable if you start early.

1. **Get to a Calinan-bound jeepney or van.** Vehicles to Calinan leave from terminals in the city. Ask locals or your hotel for the nearest current pick-up point, as terminals sometimes move.
2. **Ride to Calinan town.** The trip usually takes around an hour or more, depending on traffic and stops.
3. **Take a tricycle or *habal-habal* to Malagos.** From Calinan, a short ride brings you to the Eagle Center gate. Agree on the fare before you go.

Public transport fares are low, but they change with fuel prices, so carry small bills. For the return trip, do not leave too late in the afternoon, when vehicles can become less frequent.

## What you will see

The centre is a conservation facility first and a tourist attraction second. Its main purpose is breeding, rescue and education, and visitors help support that work.

- **Philippine eagles.** Several eagles live in large enclosures. Some were bred at the centre; others were rescued after being injured, often by hunters or traps, and cannot safely return to the wild.
- **Other wildlife.** Depending on the time of your visit, you may see other raptors, hornbills, owls, and native mammals and reptiles.
- **Forest trail.** The centre is set among trees and greenery, with a nature trail that keeps things cool and shady.
- **Educational displays.** Signs explain the eagle's biology, threats and the work being done to save it.

Please follow staff instructions, keep your voice down near the enclosures, and never use flash photography on the birds.

## The Philippine eagle: a conservation story

The Philippine eagle (*Pithecophaga jefferyi*) is one of the largest and most powerful eagles in the world. It is found only in the Philippines, mainly on the islands of Mindanao, Luzon, Samar and Leyte. With its shaggy crest, blue-grey eyes and massive bill, it is unforgettable once you see it.

It was once called the "monkey-eating eagle", but it eats a range of prey, including flying lemurs (*colugos*), civets, snakes and birds. In 1995 it was declared the national bird of the Philippines.

The species is listed as critically endangered. The main threats are:

- **Habitat loss** from logging and land clearing.
- **Hunting and shooting**, even though killing an eagle is illegal under Philippine law.
- **Slow breeding.** A pair raises just one chick roughly every two years, so populations recover very slowly.

The Philippine Eagle Foundation has worked for decades on captive breeding, rescue, research and community-based forest protection. A milestone came in 1992 with Pag-asa, the first Philippine eagle bred and hatched in captivity at the centre. His name means "hope" in Filipino. You can learn more on the [Philippine Eagle Foundation website](https://www.philippineeaglefoundation.org/).

## Tips for visiting

- **Go in the morning.** Birds are often more active and the air is cooler.
- **Bring a light jacket.** Calinan is higher and cooler than downtown, and rain showers are common.
- **Wear comfortable shoes.** Paths can be slippery after rain.
- **Pack water and snacks**, but follow any food rules inside the centre.
- **Plan a full day.** Pair the visit with Malagos Garden Resort or other sights. Our [Davao 3-day itinerary](/travel/davao-itinerary-3-days/) shows how to fit it in.
- **Check the weather** on our [Davao weather page](/weather/) before setting out.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where is the Philippine Eagle Center in Davao City?

It is in Malagos, Baguio District (near Calinan), Davao City, next to Malagos Garden Resort. The drive from downtown usually takes about 1 to 1.5 hours.

### How do I get to the Philippine Eagle Center by public transport?

Take a jeepney or van to Calinan, then a tricycle or motorcycle taxi to Malagos. Ask locals for the current pick-up terminals in the city.

### Can I see a Philippine eagle in the wild?

Wild Philippine eagles are very rare and hard to spot. The Eagle Center is the most reliable place to see one.

### Why is the Philippine eagle endangered?

The main threats are forest loss, hunting and very slow breeding. The species is listed as critically endangered.

### Who was Pag-asa?

Pag-asa was the first Philippine eagle bred and hatched in captivity, in 1992 at the centre. His name means "hope".
