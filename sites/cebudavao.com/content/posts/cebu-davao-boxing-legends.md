Many of the best Filipino boxers have come from the south: Cebu, the wider Visayas and Mindanao. Gabriel "Flash" Elorde was born in Bogo, Cebu; Manny Pacquiao rose from General Santos City; and Cebu's ALA Gym became a famous factory of world champions. This is a guide to the legends, the gyms and what makes boxing such a big deal in Cebu and Davao.

## Why the South Produces So Many Filipino Boxers

Boxing in the Philippines has deep roots, and the Visayas and Mindanao have always been strongholds. Gyms in Cebu City, General Santos, Davao and nearby provinces have long given young fighters a path to a living and, for a few, to global fame. Fights are hugely popular, with crowds gathering around TVs in homes, barbershops and *sari-sari* stores whenever a Filipino champion steps into the ring.

## Legends From Cebu and the Visayas

### Gabriel "Flash" Elorde

Flash Elorde, born in Bogo, Cebu, is often named the greatest Filipino boxer before the Pacquiao era. He held the world junior lightweight (super featherweight) title through much of the 1960s, and his footwork and southpaw style made him famous. The "Elorde" name lives on in boxing gyms and sports centres across the country.

### Gerry Peñalosa

Gerry Peñalosa, a product of the Cebu boxing scene, won world titles at super flyweight and bantamweight. He comes from a boxing family; his brother Dodie Boy Peñalosa was also a world champion. After retiring, Peñalosa stayed involved in boxing as a trainer and promoter.

### Nonito Donaire

Nonito "The Filipino Flash" Donaire was born in Talibon, Bohol, before his family moved to the United States. He became a four-division world champion, famous for his left hook. His nickname is a nod to Flash Elorde.

## ALA Gym: Cebu's Champion Factory

**ALA Gym** in Cebu City, founded by Antonio L. Aldeguer, became one of the most famous boxing stables in Asia. Its promotional arm, ALA Promotions, staged the long-running *Pinoy Pride* fight cards that showcased Filipino fighters on TV.

### Donnie Nietes

Donnie "Ahas" Nietes, from Negros Occidental, trained at ALA Gym and became a four-division world champion. He was known for his technical skill and long, unbeaten title reigns at the lighter weights.

### Other ALA fighters

ALA Gym also developed fighters such as Rey "Boom Boom" Bautista from Bohol, a hugely popular knockout artist, and Milan Melindo, a former IBF light flyweight champion. Many younger Cebuano boxers trained in its programmes.

> **Tip:** Some Cebu gyms welcome visitors for fitness boxing sessions. Ask in advance, and respect training time for professional fighters.

## Mindanao's Boxing Legends

### Manny Pacquiao

Manny "Pacman" Pacquiao is the most famous Filipino boxer of all time. Born in Bukidnon and raised in General Santos City, he started fighting as a teenager and went on to win world titles in eight weight divisions, a record no other boxer holds. Beyond boxing, he has served in the Philippine Senate and is a well-known figure in national life. His big fights were national events, with streets famously quiet as people gathered to watch.

### Jerwin Ancajas

Jerwin "Pretty Boy" Ancajas, from Panabo in Davao del Norte, held the IBF super flyweight title for several years and made many successful defences. He is one of Davao region's best-known modern champions.

### General Santos and Davao gyms

General Santos City, known as the tuna capital, is also a boxing hotbed thanks to Pacquiao's success and the gyms that grew around it. Davao City and nearby provinces also have active amateur and pro boxing scenes, feeding fighters into national teams and professional stables.

## Boxing Culture in Cebu and Davao Today

Boxing remains part of daily life in both cities. School and barangay amateur tournaments, local pro cards and TV broadcasts keep the sport visible. Filipinos also follow international fights closely on streaming platforms and in public viewings.

## How Filipino Boxers Train

Life in a Filipino boxing gym follows a familiar rhythm. Fighters often run early in the morning, before the heat, then return for afternoon sessions of skipping rope, shadowboxing, bag work, pad work with trainers and sparring. Many young boxers in established stables live in or near the gym, and trainers act as coaches, mentors and sometimes guardians.

The lighter weight divisions, from minimumweight up to bantamweight and featherweight, have produced many of the country's champions. Filipino fighters are often praised for their speed, stamina and willingness to take on tough opponents abroad, in the United States, Japan, Mexico and elsewhere.

### The amateur pathway

Most professionals start in the amateur ranks, in school, barangay and regional tournaments. The best amateurs may be invited to national training pools and compete at events such as the Southeast Asian Games. Boxing has also given the Philippines some of its Olympic medals, which keeps the sport popular in schools and sports programmes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who is the greatest Filipino boxer?

Manny Pacquiao is widely considered the greatest Filipino boxer, as the only boxer to win world titles in eight divisions. Flash Elorde is often named the greatest of the earlier era.

### Which famous boxers are from Cebu?

Flash Elorde was born in Bogo, Cebu, and Gerry Peñalosa and his brother Dodie Boy Peñalosa are from Cebu. Many other champions, like Donnie Nietes, trained at Cebu's ALA Gym.

### What is ALA Gym?

ALA Gym is a famous boxing gym and stable in Cebu City, founded by Antonio L. Aldeguer, which produced several world champions and ran the Pinoy Pride fight series.

### Are there boxing champions from Davao?

Yes. Jerwin Ancajas from Panabo, Davao del Norte, was a long-reigning IBF super flyweight champion, and the wider Mindanao region produced Manny Pacquiao.
