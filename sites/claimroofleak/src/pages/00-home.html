The first 72 hours after you find a leak

What you do in the first few days shapes the whole claim. Florida policies require you to protect the property from further damage, and Florida law gives insurers specific inspection and decision deadlines once you report. These four checklists keep you on the right side of both.

Stop the water

  • Move belongings and put down buckets and towels
  • Have a tarp or temporary patch installed if it’s safe
  • Turn off power to wet ceiling fixtures
  • Keep every receipt for tarps, fans and dehumidifiers

Document before cleanup

  • Date-stamped photos and video of ceilings, walls and attic
  • Photos of the roof from the ground (don’t climb in bad weather)
  • Save damaged materials or samples if you can
  • Write down the date you first saw water

Report it promptly

  • Call or file online with your insurer and get a claim number
  • Note who you spoke to and when
  • Ask for a copy of the policy if you don’t have it
  • Expect an acknowledgment within 7 days

Be careful what you sign

  • Read any roofer contract before signing
  • Policies issued since 1/1/2023 can’t be assigned
  • Don’t let a contractor “handle” your claim
  • Check licenses with the DBPR

Florida rules that shape every roof claim

These are the statutes most likely to matter for a roof leak claim on a Florida home. They set minimums; your policy may add its own conditions. Deadlines depend on your policy and the date of loss.

  1. 1 yrnotice of claim

    s. 627.70132. For current law, a new or reopened claim is barred unless notice is given within 1 year after the date of loss; a supplemental claim within 18 months. Older losses may fall under earlier, longer windows.

  2. 7 / 30days

    s. 627.70131. The insurer must acknowledge your claim communication within 7 days and, if it physically inspects, do so within 30 days after receiving proof-of-loss statements.

  3. 60days

    s. 627.70131(7). The insurer must pay or deny the claim, or a portion of it, within 60 days after notice, with a written explanation, unless factors beyond its control apply. Late payments generally carry interest.

  4. 15 yrsroof age

    s. 627.7011(5). For policies issued or renewed since July 1, 2022, an insurer can’t refuse to write or renew a home solely because a roof is under 15 years old. This is an underwriting rule, not a claim-payment rule.

  5. 25%roof rule

    s. 553.844(5). If a roof was built, repaired or replaced to the 2007 Florida Building Code or later, repairing 25% or more no longer forces the whole roof to current code – only the repaired part. How the 25% rule works.

  6. 10business days

    s. 627.70152. Before suing, a homeowner must give written notice of intent to initiate litigation through the Department of Financial Services at least 10 business days in advance.

Sudden damage vs. slow deterioration

Almost every roof leak claim dispute comes back to this line. Florida homeowners policies (most are built on standard “HO-3” style forms) insure the dwelling against direct physical loss unless an exclusion applies – and the exclusions that matter for roofs are wear and tear, deterioration, faulty workmanship, and water that leaks repeatedly over time.

The fight is usually about cause. An adjuster who sees curled, granule-worn shingles may write “age-related deterioration,” while a roofer sees creased tabs and lifted shingles from a wind event. Weather records, photographs and a qualified inspection are what settle that argument. Read more in is a roof leak covered by homeowners insurance in Florida.

Usually covered

  • Wind or hail that tears off or lifts shingles
  • A tree or limb that punctures the roof
  • Rain entering through a storm-created opening
  • Resulting drywall, insulation and flooring damage
  • Reasonable emergency repairs to stop the leak

Usually excluded

  • Wear, tear and old-age deterioration
  • Leaks that went on for weeks or months
  • Poor installation or skipped maintenance
  • Mold beyond the policy’s mold sublimit
  • Damage made worse by not protecting the home

Common questions

Do I need a lawyer to file a roof leak claim?

No. You file the claim yourself, directly with your insurance company, and many claims are paid without any dispute. Homeowners usually look for legal help when the claim is denied, paid far below repair estimates, or stalled past Florida’s 60-day pay-or-deny deadline.

How long do I have to report a roof leak in Florida?

Under s. 627.70132, Florida Statutes, notice of a new or reopened property claim generally must be given within 1 year after the date of loss, and a supplemental claim within 18 months. But your policy also requires “prompt” notice, and waiting lets the insurer argue the damage got worse because of the delay. Report as soon as you can.

Will my insurer pay to replace the whole roof?

Only if the covered damage, the policy and the building code require it. Insurers often approve a repair of the damaged slopes. Whether a full replacement is owed can depend on the extent of damage, matching, the Florida Building Code’s 25% rule, and any law and ordinance coverage in your policy.

Can my roofer handle the claim for me?

Not under current Florida law. Section 489.147 bars contractors from interpreting your policy or adjusting your claim unless they hold a public adjuster license, and policies issued on or after January 1, 2023 cannot have their benefits assigned to a contractor (s. 627.7152(13)).
