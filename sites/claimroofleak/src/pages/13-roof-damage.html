How a roof damage claim is valued

A roof damage insurance claim in Florida is usually priced with estimating software that lists each item – tear-off, underlayment, shingles or tiles, drip edge, flashing, vents, permits, disposal – with a unit price. The insurer’s adjuster prepares one estimate; your roofer or a public adjuster may prepare another. Most disputes come down to four questions:

  1. Scope: how much of the roof needs work – spot repair, one slope, or the full roof?
  2. Price: are the unit prices realistic for your area after a storm?
  3. Depreciation: is the insurer paying replacement cost, actual cash value, or a scheduled percentage?
  4. Code and matching: must undamaged areas be replaced so the roof meets code or matches?

Repair vs. replacement

Insurers commonly approve a repair of the damaged area. A full replacement may be owed when the damage is widespread, when the materials can’t be repaired without causing more damage, when replacement parts can’t reasonably match, or when the building code requires more than a patch.

Matching: s. 626.9744

Unless the policy provides otherwise, when a homeowners policy pays on a repair or replacement cost basis and replaced items don’t match in quality, color or size, s. 626.9744(2) requires the insurer to make reasonable repairs or replacement of items in adjoining areas. The insurer may weigh the cost, the uniformity achievable, and the remaining useful life of the undamaged portion. Discontinued shingles and tile profiles are a frequent source of matching disputes.

Repair damage and code betterment

The same statute says physical damage caused while making a covered repair is part of the loss, and the insured may not be required to pay for betterment required by ordinance or code, except the deductible, unless the policy specifically excludes or limits it (s. 626.9744(1)). Read that alongside your policy’s law and ordinance coverage: under s. 627.7011(1)–(2), insurers must offer this coverage at 25% or 50% of the dwelling limit, and absent a written rejection, the policy is deemed to include it at 25%. Code-driven costs connect directly to the Florida 25% roof rule.

Replacement cost, ACV and roof payment schedules

Under s. 627.7011(3)(a), a replacement-cost homeowners policy generally requires the insurer to pay at least the actual cash value (replacement cost minus depreciation) first, then the remaining replacement cost as work is performed and expenses are incurred. For a total loss, the insurer must pay full replacement cost without holding back depreciation.

Some Florida policies change that for the roof surface through an endorsement:

  • Roof surface payment schedules. These endorsements pay the roof covering at a percentage of replacement cost that drops as the roof ages, with different curves for shingle, tile and metal. Newer roofs may be paid in full; older roofs at a fraction. Insurers often pair them with a premium credit. Read the schedule table in your policy, and confirm the roof age the insurer used.
  • Actual cash value roof endorsements. These pay the roof covering on a depreciated basis instead of replacement cost.
  • Separate roof deductible. Allowed by s. 627.701(10) since 2022, capped at the lesser of 2% of Coverage A or 50% of the roof replacement cost, and not applicable to hurricane losses, total losses, tree-fall punctures of the roof deck, or repairs under 50% of the roof. Until you show you paid it, the insurer may pay the roof at ACV (s. 627.7011(3)(a)).

Check the roof age the insurer used. Under s. 627.7011(5)(d), roof age for underwriting runs from the last date 100% of the roof was built or replaced to code. Insurers often use the same date for schedules and depreciation. Permit records from your county or city can prove a newer roof date.

Roof age and keeping your policy

A frequent worry after a roof damage claim is losing coverage. For policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2022, s. 627.7011(5) bars an insurer from refusing to issue or renew solely because a roof is less than 15 years old. For older roofs, the insurer must allow an inspection by an authorized inspector (at the homeowner’s expense) before requiring replacement as a condition of coverage, and may not refuse to write or renew solely for roof age if the inspection shows at least 5 years of remaining useful life. Those rules govern underwriting; they don’t control how a specific claim is adjusted.

Working with roofers under Florida law

Since 2021, s. 489.147 has regulated how contractors interact with roof claims. Contractors may not:

  • Solicit with door hangers, flyers or emails that omit required warnings about deductibles and insurance fraud;
  • Offer gifts, cash, gift cards or deductible waivers in exchange for a roof inspection or a claim;
  • Pay or accept referral fees for services paid with insurance proceeds;
  • Interpret your policy, advise you on coverage, or adjust the claim unless licensed as a public adjuster; or
  • Give you a repair agreement without a good-faith itemized estimate.

Every roof repair or replacement contract must also include a bold notice telling you to contact your insurer to verify coverage before signing (s. 489.147(7)). And for residential policies issued on or after January 1, 2023, assigning your claim benefits to a contractor is void (s. 627.7152(13)). You stay in control of the claim.

Evidence that moves roof estimates

  • Close-up photos of creased, lifted or missing shingles, cracked tiles, and damaged flashing, with a wide shot showing where each is located.
  • A slope-by-slope diagram or report from a licensed roofer.
  • Manufacturer or supplier letters about discontinued products (for matching).
  • Your building department’s permit history and any code requirements cited by the roofer.
  • Weather data for the date of loss, such as wind gusts or hail reports near your address.
  • A comparison of the insurer’s line items against your contractor’s, highlighting what was left out.

FAQ

Will insurance pay for a new roof in Florida?

Only when the covered damage, the policy terms and applicable code require it. Many claims are paid as repairs. Matching under s. 626.9744, the 25% rule and law and ordinance coverage can support a larger scope.

Why did my insurer pay only part of my roof estimate?

Common reasons are depreciation or a roof payment schedule, a smaller repair scope, the deductible, and missing line items. Ask for the insurer’s detailed estimate and compare it line by line with your roofer’s.

Can my insurer cancel my policy after a roof claim?

Insurers can make nonrenewal decisions for lawful reasons, but for policies issued or renewed since July 1, 2022 they may not refuse to renew solely because a roof is under 15 years old, and older roofs get an inspection option under s. 627.7011(5).

Is it legal for a roofer to waive my deductible?

No. Section 489.147 prohibits contractors from offering deductible waivers or other inducements for a roof inspection or claim, and the statute’s required notices warn that waiving a deductible with intent to defraud can be a felony.
