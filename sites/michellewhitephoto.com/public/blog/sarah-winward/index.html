Sarah Winward: seasonal florals that photograph beautifully

October 20265 min readMichelle White Photo

Few floral designers have shaped the look of modern weddings quite like Sarah Winward. Working from Salt Lake City, she became known for loose, seasonal, garden-inspired arrangements that look as if they were gathered rather than constructed — and for championing flowers grown with care and used in their natural season. Her book Honest Flowers captures that philosophy for a wider audience.

From a photographer’s point of view, it’s easy to see why this style became so influential. It simply photographs beautifully.

Why seasonal, garden-style florals work on camera

  • Movement. Trailing stems, open blooms and asymmetry create lines and depth. Tight, uniform domes tend to look flat in photographs.
  • Translucency. Petals and leaves glow when they’re backlit. Airy arrangements let light pass through instead of blocking it.
  • Natural colour. Seasonal palettes tend to be harmonious, so they sit comfortably with skin tones and landscape.
  • Texture. Foliage, grasses, branches and fruit add detail that rewards close-ups.

How to photograph wedding flowers well

Whoever your florist is, a little planning ensures the flowers you invested in are captured properly:

  1. Shoot the bouquet early. Photograph it as soon as it arrives, while it is at its freshest, near a window with soft side light.
  2. Find the backlight. Turn the arrangement toward the sun for a few frames — the petals will glow.
  3. Photograph the reception before guests arrive. Ten quiet minutes with the tablescape is worth asking your planner for.
  4. Include hands. A bouquet held naturally, with rings visible, tells more of a story than a bouquet on a table.
The best floral photographs show flowers the way they were meant to be seen — in season, in motion and in good light.

Bringing it into your own wedding

Ask your florist what will be at its peak in your month, leave room for their creativity rather than copying a single reference image, and consider where the arrangements will sit in relation to the light. An arch placed so the sun is behind it during the ceremony will look wonderful in photographs. Explore our wedding portfolio for more floral moments.
