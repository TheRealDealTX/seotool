Hurricane deductibles: the rules behind the calculator

Every Florida homeowners policy that covers wind carries two deductibles: an all-other-perils (AOP) deductible, often $1,000 to $2,500, and a hurricane deductible, usually a percentage of the dwelling limit. Section 627.701 governs how insurers may offer them, and s. 627.4025 decides which one applies to a given storm.

Which storms trigger it

The hurricane period starts when the National Hurricane Center issues a hurricane warning for any part of Florida and ends 72 hours after the last hurricane watch or warning for Florida is lifted. A tropical storm that never becomes a hurricane is an AOP loss.

One per calendar year

For personal residential policies issued or renewed since May 1, 2005, the hurricane deductible applies once per calendar year across policies with the same insurer or group. If you absorbed part of it in an earlier hurricane, a later one that year costs you the greater of what remains or your AOP deductible. The insurer may require you to report below-deductible hurricane losses, or keep receipts, so they count. That is why the calculator asks what you have already absorbed.

A Destin example

A Crystal Beach house insured for $425,000 with a 2% hurricane deductible has an $8,500 deductible. Isaias wind damage of $21,000 leaves the insurer owing about $12,500 before depreciation and limits. Damage of $7,000 produces no payment, but reporting it puts $7,000 toward the year's deductible.
