The clocks in s. 627.70131

7 daysTo acknowledge a communication about your claims. 627.70131(1)(a)
7 daysTo begin investigating after proof-of-loss statementss. 627.70131(3)(a)
30 daysTo complete any physical inspection after proof of losss. 627.70131(3)(b)
60 daysTo pay or deny all or part of the claims. 627.70131(7)(a)

Each of these has an escape valve: “factors beyond the control of the insurer.” After a landfall that damages tens of thousands of buildings, regulators can also extend deadlines by order. That does not mean the clock stops for good. It means you should ask, in writing, which deadline the insurer believes applies and why.

The adjuster who inspects must give you a printed or electronic document with their name and Florida adjuster license number, and later communications about the claim must include the name and license number of whoever is communicating.

What stalling looks like

  • The document loopRequests for the same receipts, photos or proof of loss you already sent.
  • The adjuster carouselA new desk adjuster every few weeks, each starting from zero.
  • The re-inspectionAn engineer or second field inspection scheduled weeks out, pushing the decision past 60 days.
  • The partial paymentThe undisputed part is paid; the rest sits “under review” indefinitely.
  • The examination under oathA formal, recorded interview requested late in the claim. You may have to attend; get advice first.

A delay log that works

Keep a single document or spreadsheet with: date, who you spoke to (name and license number), what was asked, what you sent and how. Save every email and portal message as a PDF. When you send documents, list them in the email body so there is no argument later about what was attached. The damage log on this site keeps the damage side organized.

Escalation ladder

  1. Written status request. Ask for the claim status, the outstanding items, the deadline the insurer is applying, and its detailed estimate.
  2. Supervisor. Ask for the adjuster's supervisor by name.
  3. DFS consumer complaint. Call 1-877-693-5236 or file online with the Department of Financial Services.
  4. DFS mediation. Once a claim is eligible under s. 627.7015, a mediator can help resolve it.
  5. Legal steps. A Civil Remedy Notice under s. 624.155, which gives the insurer 60 days to cure, or the pre-suit notice under s. 627.70152 ahead of a lawsuit.

Your deadlines do not pause while theirs slide

A slow insurer does not extend your 18-month window for supplemental claims under s. 627.70132, or the lawsuit limitations period in s. 95.11. If a claim from Isaias or Sally is still stuck, get it reviewed.
