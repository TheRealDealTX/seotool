Reading Destin weather when a storm is coming

Destin sits on a narrow strip between the Gulf and Choctawhatchee Bay, so it gets both the onshore Gulf wind and, as a storm passes, wind off the bay that can push water into the harbor and low streets. Four signals on this page are worth watching:

  • The first coral bar on the gust chart. That is when tropical-storm-force gusts are forecast to begin. Bridges may close once sustained winds reach about 40 mph, and outdoor work becomes dangerous. Have shutters up and boats secured before it.
  • Pressure falling at KDTS while wind backs from east to northeast: the center is approaching from the south.
  • Rain totals over 5 inches in 72 hours. Expect street flooding in low spots. During Isaias, Destin reported flooding on Sibert Avenue; in Sally, creeks across Okaloosa and Walton overflowed.
  • Spiral bands on radar. Bands on the east side of a landfalling storm spin up short-lived tornadoes, with little warning. The NHC flagged that risk across the Panhandle during Isaias. Know your interior room.

Where these numbers come from

Observations are from the KDTS station at Destin Executive Airport. Forecast text, gusts and rainfall are the official NWS Mobile gridded forecast for Destin, pulled from api.weather.gov in your browser. Walton County is forecast by NWS Tallahassee, so conditions toward Santa Rosa Beach can differ a little. Destin Hurricane does not alter any of it.

Destin weather through hurricane season

Most summer days on the Emerald Coast follow a pattern: a sea breeze builds by late morning, thunderstorms pop inland in the afternoon, and some drift back toward the coast by evening. Those storms bring lightning and short bursts of heavy rain but rarely the sustained wind that damages roofs. A tropical system is different: wind that builds for hours from one direction, rain bands that repeat over the same streets, and water rising in the harbor and bay.

Rip currents and double red flags

Dangerous surf arrives long before a storm. Swells from a system hundreds of miles away can make the Gulf deadly on a sunny day. Okaloosa County's Beach Safety Division flies flags based on water conditions; double red flags mean the water is closed to swimmers. Check flags and the NWS surf forecast before anyone goes in.

After the storm

Watch this page in the days after landfall too. Heat and humidity return fast, and outages make them dangerous for older adults and anyone with medical conditions. Lingering rain bands can keep creeks and low streets flooding even after the wind has dropped, as Sally showed in 2020. And document conditions for your claim: the date, the wind you saw, the time water came in. That record helps when an adjuster later asks what caused what.
