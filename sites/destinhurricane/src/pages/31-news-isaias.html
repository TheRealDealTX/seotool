Thursday, October 8: warnings and orders

At 10 a.m. CDT, NHC Advisory 8 had Isaias about 380 miles south of the mouth of the Mississippi River with 85 mph winds, moving east-northeast at 10 mph and strengthening. A Hurricane Warning ran from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to the Bay/Gulf county line in Florida, which covers all of Okaloosa and Walton counties. A Storm Surge Warning ran from the mouth of the Mississippi River to the Suwannee River.

Okaloosa County, which had declared a local state of emergency the evening before, put a mandatory evacuation for coastal Zones A and B into effect at noon, including Okaloosa Island, coastal Fort Walton Beach, coastal Destin and parts of coastal Mary Esther and Niceville, and asked visitors to leave the impact area. Shelters opened at 6 p.m. at Davidson Middle School in Crestview (general population, special needs and pet-friendly) and the Raider Arena at Northwest Florida State College in Niceville.

Friday, October 9: the approach

By 4 p.m. CDT, Isaias was a Category 3 with 115 mph winds, about 90 miles south of Pensacola and moving north at 17 mph. Hurricane-force winds extended up to 35 miles from the center and tropical-storm-force winds up to 205 miles. The NHC's surge forecast for the stretch from the Alabama/Florida line to Grayton Beach was 6 to 9 feet above ground if the peak arrived at high tide, and 4 to 7 feet from Grayton Beach to Indian Pass.

The NHC's landfall update put the center near 30.4°N, 86.5°W, moving north at 19 mph with a minimum pressure estimated at 971 mb. A NOAA gauge in Panama City Beach showed water about 5.6 feet above mean higher high water, and a gust of 100 mph was reported at a WeatherSTEM station near Pensacola. By 10 p.m. Isaias was inland over the western Panhandle with 90 mph winds; it became post-tropical over Alabama early Saturday.

What Destin saw

  • The city reported flooding, downed power lines and debris across Destin; nearly all of the city lost power.
  • Video showed surge overtopping fences and beach walkways; officials urged people in low-lying and beachfront areas to stay away from the shoreline.
  • Minor flooding and debris were reported on Sibert Avenue. Okaloosa deputies directed traffic at intersections with dark signals.
  • Utility and debris crews worked along Highway 98 before sunrise Saturday. Mayor Bobby Wagner described the priorities as power, water and transportation.
  • In Fort Walton Beach and on Okaloosa Island, gale-force winds bent stop signs and thrashed trees.

Across the region, two storm-related deaths were confirmed in the Florida Panhandle: a man in Escambia County killed when a tree fell on a home, and a man who died in a garage where a generator was running. Hundreds of thousands of customers across Florida and Alabama lost power.

Where things stand

News reports describe a federal emergency declaration for Florida covering Okaloosa, Walton and other counties. Whether FEMA Individual Assistance for households follows depends on damage assessments; check DisasterAssistance.gov. Flooding from rain bands remained a concern across the Panhandle for days after landfall.

What to do this week

  1. Photograph all damage before cleanup and report your claim. Our Isaias claim guide walks through it.
  2. Check power and water status and boil-water notices.
  3. Follow debris-pickup rules in after the storm.
