Why Destin floods the way it does

The northeastern Gulf has a wide, shallow continental shelf, which lets a hurricane pile water higher than it could against a steep coast. Destin adds geography that makes the water hard to escape:

  • A narrow strip of land. Much of Destin lies between the Gulf and Choctawhatchee Bay; Okaloosa Island and Holiday Isle are lower still, with water on both sides.
  • East Pass. The inlet between Okaloosa Island and Holiday Isle connects the Gulf to the harbor and the bay. Surge pushes in through it, raising water at the docks on Harbor Boulevard and the bayou neighborhoods.
  • A long bay. Choctawhatchee Bay stretches east toward Freeport. After the center passes, winds swing around and can drive bay water against Niceville, Bluewater Bay and the north shore of Destin.
  • Coastal dune lakes. In South Walton, rare coastal dune lakes connect to the Gulf through outfalls that open and close; surge and heavy rain both raise them into nearby yards.

Three kinds of hurricane flooding

Storm surge

Seawater driven ashore over a few hours, often with waves on top. The reason evacuation zones exist.

Rain flooding

Slow storms overwhelm drainage far from the water. Sally produced more than 14 inches near Destin in 2020.

Creek and river flooding

Rivers crest days later. In Sally, Alaqua Creek in Walton County rose above record flood stage and the I-10 bridge over the Shoal River was closed.

Waves on surge

In Opal, the NHC noted that breaking waves on top of surge added roughly 10 feet at the Panama City Beach pier.

Surge in Destin's storm record

  1. 1995

    Opal

    Still-water marks inside buildings across the Panhandle reached 5 to 14 feet; marks on buildings and dune debris lines near the Gulf ran 10 to 21 feet. Structures not elevated high enough suffered major damage.

  2. 2004

    Ivan

    The NHC recorded 10 to 15 feet from Destin west to Mobile Bay, 6 to 9 feet in Okaloosa County and 8 to 10 feet in Walton County.

  3. 2005

    Dennis

    6 to 7 feet on Santa Rosa Island near landfall, overwashing the island near and west of Navarre Beach; 4 to 6 feet elsewhere in the Panhandle.

  4. 2026

    Isaias

    Forecast 6 to 9 feet from the Alabama line to Grayton Beach. Surge overtopped fences and beach walkways in Destin; the Panama City Beach gauge measured about 5.6 feet above mean higher high water.

Flood claims are a different animal

A flood claim runs under the NFIP's Standard Flood Insurance Policy or a private flood policy, not Florida's homeowners claim statutes. Key differences: NFIP building coverage tops out at $250,000 and contents at $100,000 for a home; coverage below an elevated building's lowest floor is limited; a signed proof of loss is generally due within 60 days of the loss; and a lawsuit over a written denial must generally be filed within one year (42 U.S.C. 4072). Appeals to FEMA are generally due within 60 days of the denial letter.

When the same house has surge and wind damage, two adjusters will each try to assign damage to the other policy. Photograph water lines and debris lines separately from roof, window and siding damage, and file with both insurers. For the wind side, see wind versus surge disputes.
