  • “Wear and tear”Blaming storm-damaged shingles on age or poor maintenance.
  • “It's flood, not wind”Shifting surge-zone losses to an excluded or separate flood policy.
  • Below-deductible estimatesScoping just enough damage to land under the hurricane deductible.
  • Patch, don't replacePaying for a few shingles when matching and code require a new roof section.
  • Heavy depreciationDeducting so much for age that the check can't fund the repair.
  • Delay and paperworkRepeated document requests, re-inspections and examinations under oath.

Don't miss Florida's claim deadlines

Florida shortened its property-insurance deadlines in 2022–2023. A new hurricane claim generally must be reported within one year of the date of loss, and a supplemental or reopened claim within 18 months. Lawsuit deadlines were also shortened, and Florida requires a written pre-suit notice before a property insurance lawsuit can be filed. If your claim from Hurricane Isaias, Sally or another storm is being denied or lowballed, get advice early.

FAQ

Pensacola Hurricane claim questions

My hurricane claim was denied. Can I still recover?

Often, yes. Insurers deny hurricane claims for reasons such as wear and tear, pre-existing damage, flood exclusions or late notice, and many of those denials can be challenged with the right policy analysis, inspection and evidence. An attorney can review the denial letter and your policy at no cost. More on denied claims.

How long do I have to file a hurricane claim in Florida?

Under current Florida law, a new hurricane or windstorm claim generally must be reported within one year of the date of loss, and a supplemental or reopened claim within 18 months. Lawsuit deadlines are also short, so get advice early.

What does it cost to hire The Lawgical Firm?

A Pensacola Hurricane case review is free and there are no upfront fees. The firm works on a contingency basis, so there is no attorney fee unless there is a recovery. Ask how case costs are handled under the written fee agreement.

Should I hire an attorney or a public adjuster?

A public adjuster can help prepare and negotiate a claim, but only an attorney can represent you in a lawsuit over a denied or underpaid claim, including Florida's required pre-suit notice process. The Lawgical Firm's founding attorney is also a former Florida insurance adjuster and appraiser.

I already accepted a check. Is it too late?

Not necessarily. Cashing a partial payment usually does not end a claim, and Florida allows supplemental claims within 18 months of the date of loss when additional damage or costs are found. Read anything you signed carefully and get it reviewed.
