Six Destin hurricane claim problems, and where to start on each

Most claims after a Gulf storm are handled fairly. The ones that are not tend to fail in the same few ways. Each line below links to a full guide written for Okaloosa and Walton County property owners.

  1. The denial letter

    “Wear and tear,” “flood, not wind,” “pre-existing,” “late notice.” How to test the reason the insurer gave against the policy and the evidence.

  2. The check that will not fix the house

    Patch-repair roof estimates, missing interior work, heavy depreciation and estimates that land just under a 2% hurricane deductible.

  3. The claim that never moves

    Florida gives insurers 7 days to acknowledge, 30 days to inspect after proof of loss, and 60 days to pay or deny. What to do when those dates pass.

The Florida claim clock

Dates every Destin hurricane claimant should write down

Florida rewrote its property-insurance statutes between 2021 and 2023. These are the general rules for losses today; the version that applies to you can depend on when your policy was issued and when the damage happened.

  1. Within 7 days

    The insurer acknowledges your claim

    Section 627.70131(1) gives the insurer 7 calendar days to review and acknowledge a communication about a claim.

  2. Within 30 days of proof of loss

    Any physical inspection happens

    If the insurer inspects in person, it must do so within 30 days after receiving proof-of-loss statements, and the adjuster must give you a name and license number.

  3. Within 60 days

    Pay or deny, with reasons in writing

    The insurer must pay or deny the claim, or a portion of it, within 60 days of notice unless factors beyond its control prevent it; late payments generally carry interest.

  4. Within 1 year of landfall

    Report a new or reopened claim

    Section 627.70132 bars a new or reopened claim without notice within 1 year of the date of loss, which for a hurricane is the landfall date.

  5. Within 18 months

    Supplemental claim for damage found later

    Additional damage or costs on an open or settled claim generally must be reported within 18 months of the date of loss.

  6. 10 business days before suit

    Pre-suit notice

    Section 627.70152 requires written notice of intent to sue, filed through the Department of Financial Services, at least 10 business days before a property insurance lawsuit.

General information, not legal advice. Deadlines depend on your policy and date of loss. Read the full claim guide.
