Safety comes first

Wait for officials before going back into evacuated areas. Stay out of floodwater, treat every downed line as live, and run generators outside only. Florida reported a generator-related death during Isaias.

What Isaias did on our stretch of coast

The National Hurricane Center's 4 p.m. advisory on October 9 had Isaias at 115 mph, a Category 3, about 90 miles south of Pensacola and moving north at 17 mph, with a storm-surge forecast of 6 to 9 feet above ground from the Alabama/Florida line to Grayton Beach. By landfall near Destin around 8:30 p.m. CDT, radar showed sustained winds of 105 mph. A WeatherFlow station near Santa Rosa Sound reported a 116 mph gust, and the NOAA gauge at Panama City Beach measured water about 5.6 feet above mean higher high water.

In Destin, the city reported flooding, downed lines and debris; nearly all of the city lost power, and surge washed over fences and beach walkways. Okaloosa County had ordered Zones A and B to evacuate at noon on October 8. The full timeline is in our Isaias landfall report.

Your Isaias claim checklist

  1. Photograph before you clean. Wide shots of every room and each side of the house, then close-ups: missing shingles, lifted ridge caps, broken screens, water lines, wet insulation, damaged contents with model numbers. Narrate a video with the date.
  2. Stop the damage from growing. Tarp, board, pull wet carpet and drywall, run fans and dehumidifiers. Keep every receipt and the drying company's moisture logs.
  3. Report to each insurer. Homeowners or wind carrier for wind and rain damage; flood carrier for surge or rising water. Write down claim numbers and every name.
  4. Keep samples. A cut of carpet and pad, a few shingles, a section of baseboard. Photograph anything you throw away.
  5. Be precise in recorded statements. Say what you know. If you do not know the roof's age or when a stain first appeared, say so.
  6. Ask for the adjuster's estimate in writing and get a licensed contractor's estimate to compare.
  7. Do not assign your benefits. For policies issued on or after January 1, 2023, assignments of post-loss benefits are void under s. 627.7152(13). Read any contractor paperwork carefully.

Disputes we expect from Isaias

  • Wind versus surge on Okaloosa Island, Holiday Isle, Crystal Beach and Miramar Beach Gulf-front property, where carriers may assign everything below the water line to excluded flood.
  • Condo master-policy and HO-6 overlaps in Gulf-front towers, and large special assessments. See the condo guide.
  • Roof scope. Patch repairs offered where s. 553.844 and matching rules support more.
  • Interior water from wind-driven rain, labeled long-term leakage.
  • Estimates just under the hurricane deductible. Check the math.
  • Vacation-rental losses, including lost rental income during repairs, where coverage depends on the policy form.
