The storms that shaped Destin

  1. August 3, 1995

    Erin

    Erin came ashore on Santa Rosa Island near Navarre Beach. NHC analysis put winds near 100 mph, Category 2, in a small part of the northeastern eyewall as it reached the coast near Fort Walton Beach. It was the first of two hurricanes on this coast that season.

  2. October 4, 1995

    Opal

    Opal deepened to 916 mb in the Gulf, then weakened to a marginal Category 3 before landfall at Pensacola Beach late that afternoon. The NHC estimated the strongest surface winds in a narrow swath at the eastern end of Choctawhatchee Bay, between Destin and Panama City. Hurlburt Field logged sustained winds of 73 knots with a gust to 125 knots. Surge and breaking waves did extensive damage along most of the Panhandle coast; water and debris marks near the Gulf reached 10 to 21 feet. Florida's one recorded death was from a tornado in Crestview.

  3. September 16, 2004

    Ivan

    Ivan made landfall near Gulf Shores, Alabama, as a Category 3, putting Destin in the storm's right-front quadrant. The NHC recorded 10 to 15 feet of surge from Destin west to Mobile Bay and 6 to 9 feet from Destin east to St. Marks. Niceville measured 6.55 inches of rain.

  4. July 10, 2005

    Dennis

    Ten months later, Dennis came ashore on Santa Rosa Island between Navarre Beach and Gulf Breeze at about 120 mph. A Florida Coastal Monitoring Program tower in Destin recorded sustained winds of 55 knots and a 70-knot gust; Destin's airport gusted to 64 knots. Surge reached 6 to 7 feet near landfall.

  5. October 10, 2018

    Michael

    Michael struck Mexico Beach as a Category 5, about 70 miles east. A storm surge warning ran from the Okaloosa/Walton County line eastward. Destin Executive Airport peaked at a 52-knot gust: a reminder of how fast conditions change with distance from the eyewall.

  6. September 16, 2020

    Sally

    Sally crawled ashore at Gulf Shores as a Category 2. On our coast the damage was mostly water: a CoCoRaHS observer 1.2 miles northwest of Destin measured 14.23 inches of rain, Fort Walton Beach 14.22 inches, South Walton High School 13.96 inches. Alaqua Creek rose above record flood stage and flooded homes along SR-20 west of Freeport with up to 4 feet of water, and the I-10 bridge over the Shoal River closed. The Okaloosa Island pier recorded a 61-knot gust.

  7. October 9, 2026

    Isaias

    Isaias peaked at 115 mph in the Gulf and made landfall near Destin around 8:30 p.m. CDT at 105 mph. Okaloosa ordered Zones A and B out the day before. The NHC forecast 6 to 9 feet of surge from the Alabama line to Grayton Beach; surge overtopped fences and beach walkways in Destin, nearly the whole city lost power, and a 116 mph gust was reported near Santa Rosa Sound. Four lessons from the record

West is worse

Ivan, Dennis, Erin and Opal all came ashore west of Destin and still delivered damaging wind and surge here, because Destin sat on their stronger eastern side.

Slow means wet

Sally was a Category 2 at landfall about 70 miles away, yet it brought more than 14 inches of rain to Destin and record creek flooding in Walton County.

Back-to-back years happen

Ivan and Dennis struck ten months apart, in different calendar years, so many homeowners paid two hurricane deductibles. Read how the deductible works.

October counts

Opal, Michael and Isaias all arrived in October. The season runs through November 30; keep the plan and the policy current until then.

What history means for a claim today

Insurers reviewing an Isaias claim may point at Sally, Michael or an older storm and call damage pre-existing. Photos from before the storm, repair invoices from after Sally, roof permits and the weather recorded nearby on October 9 all help answer that.
