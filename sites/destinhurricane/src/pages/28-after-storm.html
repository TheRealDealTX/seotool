Coming home safely

  • Wait until officials say your area is open. Okaloosa County may restrict access to evacuated zones for a time after a storm.
  • Avoid downed lines and flooded roads. Never drive or charge an electric vehicle that sat in saltwater; the county warns that battery fires can start days or weeks later.
  • Do not light a flame when you first enter. Check for gas smell, sagging ceilings and wet electrical panels.
  • Run generators outside, at least 20 feet from doors and windows, with a battery CO alarm inside.
  • Watch for boil-water notices. If you must treat water, Okaloosa County's guide says strain it, then boil it at a rolling boil for at least three minutes, or, if nothing else is available, add 1/8 teaspoon (about 16 drops) of plain unscented bleach per gallon and let it stand 30 minutes.
  • Points of Distribution (PODs) for water and food are announced through local media and the county's public information office when they open.

Insurance comes first

Report the claim to your homeowners or wind insurer and, separately, your flood insurer if water came in from outside. Before cleanup, photograph and video everything; keep samples; tarp and dry out; save every receipt. Our Isaias claim steps and damage log walk through it.

FEMA help for households

When a hurricane receives a major disaster declaration that includes Individual Assistance for Okaloosa or Walton County, residents can apply for help with temporary housing, basic repairs insurance does not cover, and other serious needs such as medical, child-care or essential household items. An emergency declaration alone usually funds government response, not grants to households.

Apply at DisasterAssistance.gov, in the FEMA app or at 1-800-621-3362. Have your insurance policy and claim number, Social Security number, the damaged address and bank details ready. If FEMA denies or underpays, you can generally appeal within 60 days of the decision letter. Disaster Recovery Centers often open in affected counties after major declarations.

SBA disaster loans

The U.S. Small Business Administration lends to homeowners and renters, not only businesses, for repairs and lost property after a declared disaster, and to businesses and nonprofits for physical damage and economic injury. Apply at sba.gov/disaster.

Debris at the curb

  • Separate piles: vegetation; construction debris (drywall, carpet, lumber, shingles); appliances; electronics; household hazardous waste.
  • Put piles in the right-of-way at the edge of your property, not on sidewalks, against mailboxes or hydrants, or under power lines.
  • Empty refrigerators and freezers before setting them out.
  • Pickup schedules come from your city or county: Destin, Fort Walton Beach and Niceville announce theirs, and Okaloosa and Walton counties cover unincorporated areas. Gated and private roads may need permission.

Contractors, and the people who follow storms

Out-of-area crews arrive within days of every landfall. Most do honest work. To protect yourself:

  • Verify the license at MyFloridaLicense.com.
  • Make sure the job is permitted with the City of Destin, Fort Walton Beach, Niceville, or Okaloosa or Walton County, as applies.
  • Get written, itemized estimates; never pay in full up front.
  • Florida's s. 489.147 bars contractors from offering gifts, cash or deductible waivers for a roof inspection or claim. Treat a “free roof” pitch as a warning.
  • For policies issued since January 1, 2023, assignments of post-loss insurance benefits are void (s. 627.7152(13)). Do not sign paperwork that hands a contractor control of your claim.

More help

  • 2-1-1 connects residents with food, shelter, utility help and volunteer cleanup.
  • American Red Cross runs shelters and emergency help; it sent more than 140 volunteers to the region for Isaias.
  • Disaster SNAP and Disaster Unemployment Assistance may open after major declarations.
  • Florida Department of Financial Services consumer helpline 1-877-693-5236 for insurance problems.
