Seven signs of an underpaid storm claim

  1. The roof is scoped as a repair of a few squares on a slope your roofer says must be replaced, or with no allowance for matching.
  2. Rooms are missing. Ceilings, insulation and drywall under the leak are in; the hallway, closet and cabinets beside them are not.
  3. No code-upgrade money. Policies with ordinance-or-law coverage pay for upgrades the building code requires; estimates often leave them out.
  4. Depreciation swallows the check. Actual cash value payments on a 12-year-old roof can be a fraction of replacement cost.
  5. No overhead and profit on a job that clearly needs a general contractor to coordinate trades.
  6. Other structures and contents are thin. Fences, screen enclosures, docks, pool cages and spoiled food are left out or valued low.
  7. Additional living expenses are ignored even though the house was unlivable for weeks.

Why Destin claims get underpaid

After a landfall like Isaias, carriers bring in large numbers of temporary field adjusters. Estimates are written in pricing software during a surge of claims, sometimes from the ground or from aerial images. Coastal factors make it worse: salt-air corrosion on fasteners, condominium common elements that overlap with unit-owner coverage, and Gulf-front construction requirements that make repairs more expensive than inland pricing assumes.

None of that is necessarily bad faith. It is why the first number is often not the right number.

The Florida rules that help close the gap

Supplemental claims

Section 627.70132 lets you report additional damage or costs within 18 months of the date of loss. For Isaias, that is roughly April 2028.

Matching

For replacement-cost homeowners policies, s. 626.9744(2) requires reasonable repair or replacement in adjoining areas when replaced items do not match in quality, color or size.

Code betterment

Section 626.9744(1) says the insured may not be required to pay for betterment required by ordinance or code, except the deductible, unless the policy specifically excludes or limits it.

Explaining the gap

Section 627.70131(7)(a) requires a written explanation when the insurer pays less than its own detailed estimate shows.

How to rebuild the number

  1. Ask the insurer for its full, line-item estimate and photos, not just the payment letter.
  2. Get a line-item estimate from a licensed Florida contractor using the same format, so differences show up line by line.
  3. Photograph and log anything found later, such as new ceiling stains after the next rain, cracked tiles or swollen cabinets, and report it as a supplemental claim.
  4. Keep every receipt: tarps, drying equipment, hotel, extra mileage, meals above your normal grocery bill.
  5. Do not sign a release or a direction-to-pay you do not understand. For policies issued on or after January 1, 2023, s. 627.7152(13) makes assignments of post-loss benefits void, so be wary of contractors asking you to sign over your claim.
