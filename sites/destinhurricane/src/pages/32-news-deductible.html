Yes, Isaias is a “hurricane” for deductible purposes

Florida defines the hurricane period in s. 627.4025(2)(c): it starts when the National Hurricane Center issues a hurricane warning for any part of Florida and ends 72 hours after the last hurricane watch or warning for Florida ends. Isaias was a declared hurricane, and Hurricane Warnings were in effect for the Panhandle by October 8. Wind damage from Isaias in Okaloosa and Walton counties therefore falls under the hurricane deductible on most homeowners policies, not the all-other-perils deductible.

The same definition covers ensuing interior damage: rain that came in through an opening the wind first created is part of the hurricane loss.

The calendar-year rule

Under s. 627.701(5)(a), for personal residential policies issued or renewed on or after May 1, 2005, the hurricane deductible applies on an annual basis to all covered hurricane losses in a calendar year with the same insurer or insurer group. If a second hurricane strikes in the same year, the deductible is the greater of the remaining hurricane deductible or the all-other-perils deductible. Insurers may require you to report below-deductible hurricane losses, or keep receipts, so they count toward the total.

Isaias was the first hurricane of 2026 on this coast, so most Destin homeowners start with the full deductible. A later 2026 hurricane would cost only what remains, or the AOP deductible if that is greater. A storm in January 2027 starts a new year and a new deductible, which is exactly what happened to many households with Ivan (September 2004) and Dennis (July 2005).

Commercial residential policies, such as many condominium association master policies, work differently: s. 627.701(5)(b) requires insurers to offer both an annual deductible and a per-hurricane deductible.

Three Destin examples

Damage well above the deductible

$450,000 dwelling limit, 2% deductible = $9,000. Roof and interior damage of $38,000. The insurer owes about $29,000 before depreciation, limits and exclusions.

Estimate just under

Same house, but the adjuster writes $8,700. No payment. Before accepting that, check what is missing: interior rooms, matching shingles, code upgrades, overhead and profit, screen enclosure, fence.

A second 2026 storm

If you absorbed $9,000 from Isaias and another hurricane damages the house in November, the deductible for that storm is the greater of $0 remaining or your AOP deductible, say $2,500.

Surge and flood

Flood is a separate policy with separate deductibles. A Gulf-front Isaias loss can involve a hurricane deductible on the wind claim and flood deductibles on the flood claim.

When “below deductible” is worth a second look

A first estimate that lands a few hundred dollars under the deductible is common after a big storm, and it is often incomplete. You have 18 months from the October 9, 2026 landfall to file a supplemental claim for damage found later under s. 627.70132.
