The Destin hurricane countdown

  1. Before June 1

    Insurance and the house

    Read your declarations page and write your hurricane deductible in dollars. Buy flood coverage now; NFIP policies usually take 30 days. Trim trees, check the roof, cut and label plywood, and sign up for AlertOkaloosa or Alert Walton.

  2. A system in the 7-day outlook

    Restock

    Refill prescriptions, top up water, food and batteries, and service the car. Boat owners: line up a haul-out or hurricane hole.

  3. About 5 days out

    Fuel and photos

    Fill tanks and fuel cans, get cash, and film every room, closet and the roof for your insurance file.

  4. Watch issued (~48 hours)

    Secure

    Shutters up, outdoor furniture, grills, kayaks and paddleboards inside. Documents in a waterproof pouch. Decide where you will go.

  5. Warning or evacuation order

    Go, or hunker down

    If your zone is ordered out, leave before the bridges close. If not, fill tubs, freeze water, charge everything and pick your interior room.

  6. Tropical-storm winds arrive

    Stay put

    No driving, no checking the beach. Keep a weather radio on for tornado and Extreme Wind Warnings.

Hardening a coastal home

Most of what fails in a Destin hurricane fails in a predictable order: an opening breaks, wind pressurizes the house, then the roof lifts. The upgrades that interrupt that chain:

  • Opening protection on every window, slider and the garage door: rated shutters or impact glass.
  • Roof-to-wall connections such as hurricane clips and straps. A wind-mitigation inspection documents them and can lower the wind portion of your premium.
  • A sealed roof deck (secondary water barrier) so rain stays out when shingles go.
  • Salt-air maintenance. Corroded fasteners and connectors on Gulf-front homes fail sooner; inspect them when the roof is serviced.

Florida's My Safe Florida Home program has offered free wind-mitigation inspections and matching grants for eligible homeowners when the Legislature funds it. Check the program's site for current availability.

The ten-minute video that pays for itself

Walk every room with your phone, open closets and cabinets, film appliances' model plates, the roof from the yard and the outside of the house. Upload it to the cloud. After the storm it is the best evidence of what you had and its condition. Then use our damage log.

Destin-specific prep

Boats

Destin Harbor and the bayou marinas fill fast once a storm is in the cone. Book a haul-out or a protected hurricane hole early in the season, double lines and add chafe gear, remove canvas and electronics, and photograph the boat and its documentation. Boat damage is usually a separate policy from your home.

Electric vehicles

Okaloosa County's 2026 guide warns that saltwater in an EV's high-voltage battery can cause a fire immediately or weeks later. Never park an EV in a garage in a flood-prone area, do not leave it plugged in, and park it at least 50 feet from structures and other vehicles. Never drive or charge one that has been submerged.

Sandbags

When flooding is imminent, Okaloosa County makes sandbags available at its public works yard at 1759 S. Ferdon Blvd in Crestview and at 714 Essex Rd in Fort Walton Beach. Walton County announces its sites before each storm.

Special needs

Anyone who depends on electricity for medical equipment, needs transport to evacuate, or needs help with medication should register on the Florida Special Needs Registry (snr.flhealthresponse.com) before a storm. In Okaloosa, the special-needs shelter at Davidson Middle School requires registration in advance.
