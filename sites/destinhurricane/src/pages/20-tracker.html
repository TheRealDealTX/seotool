Using a Destin hurricane tracker without fooling yourself

The cone is for the center

The forecast cone shows where the center is likely to travel; historically the center stays inside it about two-thirds of the time. Wind, surge and rain reach well outside. In 1995, Opal's center came ashore at Pensacola Beach, yet the NHC estimated its strongest winds in a narrow swath near the eastern end of Choctawhatchee Bay, between Destin and Panama City.

East of the center is the rough side

Northern Hemisphere hurricanes spin counterclockwise, so onshore winds and the highest surge usually sit to the right of a northbound storm. A landfall anywhere from Mobile Bay to Navarre puts Destin on that side, as Ivan did in 2004, when the NHC recorded 10 to 15 feet of surge from Destin west to Mobile Bay.

Speed matters as much as category

Isaias was racing north at about 19 mph when it came ashore, which limited rain at the coast. Slow storms do the opposite: Sally crawled ashore in 2020 and dropped more than 14 inches of rain near Destin and Fort Walton Beach.
