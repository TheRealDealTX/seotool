What the letters mean

Both counties use five surge-based zones. Zones are cumulative: when a higher letter is ordered out, every lower letter goes too. Walton County stresses that its zones describe storm-surge threat, not wind or storm category.

ZoneWho is in itRecent orders
AThe most surge-exposed land. In Walton County that includes Gulf-front stretches of Miramar Beach and the 30A beaches; in Okaloosa County, Zone A is a set of small, low pockets along the shoreline, bayous and soundOkaloosa ordered A out for Isaias (Oct. 8, 2026)
BLow coastal ground. On Okaloosa County's map, addresses we tested on Okaloosa Island, Holiday Isle and Destin's Gulf side fall in Zone BOkaloosa ordered B out for Isaias
CThe next ring inland, still within reach of a strong surgeWalton ordered A–C out for Michael (2018)
DHigher ground that floods only in severe surge scenariosStorm-specific
EThe outer edge of the worst-case surgeOnly for the most extreme forecasts

Orders depend on each storm's size, track and forecast surge. Okaloosa County notes that zones can change from year to year, so look yours up each season.

Leaving Destin: routes and choke points

Destin, Okaloosa Island and South Walton share one problem: every route out crosses water or funnels into a few highways. Plan which one you will use before you need it.

Okaloosa County's hurricane guide says that once sustained winds reach 40 mph, the Florida Department of Transportation and local law enforcement decide whether to close a bridge. After that, nobody drives out and emergency crews may not be able to reach you. Check live closures on FL511 and see our shelters and bridges page.

Visitors and vacation renters

Okaloosa County asked out-of-town visitors to leave the forecast impact area before Isaias. If you are renting on the coast when a storm threatens, follow your rental company's instructions and the county's orders, and do not wait for a zone letter you may not know. Owners of rentals should give guests the zone, the routes and the county alert links before arrival.

Run from water, hide from wind

Zones exist because surge kills more people than wind. If you are outside the zones in a sound, well-built home, staying put usually keeps the roads clear for people who must go. Okaloosa County recommends going to friends or family who live outside the evacuation zone rather than a public shelter when you can.

Special needs, pets and rides

  • Special needs: register on the Florida Special Needs Registry (snr.flhealthresponse.com) before hurricane season. Okaloosa's special-needs shelter at Davidson Middle School in Crestview requires registration in advance; Walton's primary shelter at Freeport High School serves registered special-needs clients.
  • Pets: Okaloosa has pet-friendly shelters where the Panhandle Animal Welfare Society checks pets into a separate area; exotic pets are not allowed. Bring crates, food and vaccination records.
  • No car: in Okaloosa, schedule a ride to a shelter before the storm with EC Rider at 850-833-9168.
