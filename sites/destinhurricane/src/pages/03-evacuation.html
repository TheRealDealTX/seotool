Evacuation basics for Pensacola

“Run from water, hide from wind”

Zones are drawn around storm-surge risk, because water causes most hurricane deaths. If you are not in an evacuation zone and live in a well-built home, sheltering in place is usually the safest choice — it keeps the roads open for the people who must leave. You only need to travel tens of miles, not hundreds: inland Escambia and Santa Rosa (north of I-10) is outside the surge zone.

Leave early from the islands

The Pensacola Bay Bridge, the Bob Sikes Bridge, the Theo Baars Bridge to Perdido Key and the Navarre Beach bridge are typically closed once sustained winds reach about 40 mph. After that, no one leaves the island and emergency crews cannot reach you. If you are in Zone A, leave as soon as the order is issued.

Primary evacuation routes

  • From Pensacola Beach and Gulf Breeze: US-98 west across the Pensacola Bay Bridge, then north on I-110 or US-29; or US-98 east to SR-87 north through Navarre and Milton.
  • From Perdido Key and the Southwest: SR-292/Sorrento Rd. or Blue Angel Parkway (SR-173) north to US-90/I-10, then US-29 north.
  • From Navarre: SR-87 north to I-10 and Milton, or US-98 east/west.
  • From Milton, Pace and inland Santa Rosa: SR-87 or SR-89 north toward Jay and Alabama.

Check live road and bridge status on our shelters & roads page or FL511 before you go.

Special needs, pets and transportation

Escambia and Santa Rosa counties each run a special-needs shelter registry for residents who need medical support or help getting to a shelter. Register before a storm through your county emergency management office. Many public shelters accept pets in designated areas — bring a crate, food and vaccination records.
