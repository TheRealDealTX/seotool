Where the water comes from

Surge reaches Destin two ways. Along the Gulf beaches from Holiday Isle east past Crystal Beach, it rides in over the dunes. Through East Pass, the inlet between Okaloosa Island and Holiday Isle, it fills Destin Harbor and the bay, raising water along Harbor Boulevard's docks and the low streets behind them. After the center passes, winds turning to the north can push bay water back against Destin's north shore.

In Isaias, surge overtopped fences and beach walkways, the city reported flooding (including on Sibert Avenue), and nearly all of Destin lost power. In Ivan in 2004, the National Hurricane Center measured 10 to 15 feet of surge from Destin westward; in Sally in 2020, the problem was rain, with more than 14 inches measured just northwest of the city.

Getting out

  • North: SR-293 over the Mid-Bay Bridge to Niceville, then SR-85 toward Crestview and I-10.
  • West: US-98 over the Marler Bridge to Okaloosa Island and Fort Walton Beach.
  • East: US-98 into Walton County toward US-331.

Okaloosa County's guide says bridge closures are considered when sustained winds reach 40 mph. From Destin, every route north crosses a bridge, so leaving early matters more here than almost anywhere in the county.

Destin homes and the claims they produce

Vacation rentals

A large share of Destin housing is rented by the week. Lost rental income, rental-use exclusions and owner-occupancy questions come up in these claims.

Harbor and bayfront

Docks, seawalls and boat lifts are often limited or excluded under homeowners policies. Check other-structures limits.

Condos

Holiday Isle and Gulf-front towers split coverage between the master policy and HO-6. See the condo guide.

Wind versus surge

Lower floors near the harbor can have both. File with both insurers and keep evidence separate. Surge guide.
