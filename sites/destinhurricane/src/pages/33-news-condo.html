Two policies, one building

The association's master policy

Covers the building and common elements: roof, exterior walls, windows and sliders in many buildings, elevators, pools, parking structures. Bought and claimed by the association. Its deductible is often a large percentage of the building's value.

Your HO-6 unit-owner policy

Covers your personal property, the interior items your declaration makes the owner's responsibility (often flooring, cabinets, fixtures and finishes), improvements you added, loss of use, and loss assessments.

The dividing line is set by your condominium's declaration and Florida's Condominium Act, not by the adjusters. Ask the association manager for the declaration's insurance and maintenance sections and the master policy's declarations page. They decide whether a blown-out slider or soaked drywall is the association's claim or yours.

Loss assessment coverage

When the master policy does not pay everything (most often because of its hurricane deductible), the association can levy a special assessment on owners. Florida requires HO-6 policies to include some coverage for this. Under s. 627.714(1), for policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2010, a unit owner's policy must include at least $2,000 of property loss assessment coverage for assessments from the same direct loss, with a deductible of no more than $250 per direct property loss. Many owners buy more; check your limit.

The deadline is different

Section 627.70132(4) sets a special notice rule for loss assessment claims: notice may not come later than 3 years after the date of loss, and must be given by the later of 1 year after the date of loss or 90 days after the association's board votes to levy the assessment. Keep the board minutes and the assessment notice.

What goes wrong in condo claims

  • Finger-pointing. The association says interior damage is the owner's; the HO-6 carrier says it began with a common-element failure.
  • Delay at the master level. Big buildings mean engineers, re-inspections and long negotiations. Your own HO-6 claim deadlines still run from the October 9, 2026 landfall.
  • Short-term rental units. Rental use can change what the HO-6 covers and whether lost rental income is insured. Check the endorsements.
  • Wind versus surge on the ground floors. Lobbies, garages and ground-floor units on Okaloosa Island and Holiday Isle face the same wind-or-water argument as single-family homes, with flood coverage on the master side often written separately.

An owner's to-do list after Isaias

  1. Photograph your unit and contents before the association's contractors start work.
  2. Report to your HO-6 carrier promptly, even if you think the association's policy will pay.
  3. Ask the association, in writing, for its claim number, adjuster contact and timeline.
  4. Keep receipts for hotel stays and moving costs (loss of use).
  5. When an assessment is voted, file a loss assessment claim within the s. 627.70132(4) window.
