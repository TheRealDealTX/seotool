How to read a hurricane forecast for Pensacola

The cone is about the center, not the impacts

The NHC forecast cone shows where the center of the storm is likely to go — historically, the center stays inside the cone about two-thirds of the time. Hurricane-force winds, surge and rain routinely extend well outside it. Hurricane Ivan's center came ashore near Gulf Shores, Alabama, about 30 miles west of Pensacola, yet the worst surge in Escambia County history followed because Pensacola sat in the storm's right-front quadrant.

The right side of the storm matters most

In the Northern Hemisphere, winds circle counter-clockwise, so the strongest winds and highest surge are usually on the right side of the track (east of the center for a northbound storm). A landfall anywhere from Mississippi to Alabama puts Pensacola on that dangerous side.
