Pensacola Hurricane History and Storm Archive

More than 450 years of hurricanes have shaped Pensacola — its forts, its bridges, its building codes and the way residents read a forecast cone. This archive covers every major storm, past and present.

Major Pensacola hurricanes, newest to oldest

  1. 2020

    Hurricane Sally Cat 2

    Landfall: September 16, 2020, near Gulf Shores, Alabama, with 105 mph winds.

    Sally is the storm that rewrote Pensacola's understanding of risk. It slowed to roughly 2–3 mph as it approached the coast, parking its eastern eyewall and rain bands over Escambia and Santa Rosa counties for hours. Rainfall totals reached roughly 20 to 30 inches in parts of the area, and a storm surge of around 5–6 feet flooded downtown Pensacola, Pensacola Beach and the bay shoreline.

    Construction barges working on the new Pensacola Bay Bridge (the “Three Mile Bridge”) broke loose and struck the span, closing it until 2021 and cutting the most direct link between Pensacola and Gulf Breeze. Tens of thousands of homes were damaged, and roof and water-intrusion claims filled the courts for years afterwards. Sally showed that a Category 2 with slow forward speed can be as damaging as a stronger, faster storm.

  2. 2020

    Hurricane Zeta Cat 3

    Six weeks after Sally, Zeta made landfall in southeastern Louisiana on October 28 and raced northeast. Pensacola saw gusty winds, power outages and surge on the bays while Sally debris still lined the streets — a reminder that late-season storms are common on the northern Gulf.

  3. 2018

    Hurricane Michael Cat 5

    Michael struck Mexico Beach, about 130 miles east of Pensacola, on October 10, 2018, as a Category 5 — one of only a handful of Category 5 U.S. landfalls on record. Pensacola escaped with tropical-storm conditions, but the city became a staging area for linemen and relief crews, and many Panhandle evacuees sheltered in Escambia and Santa Rosa counties.

  4. 2017

    Hurricane Nate Cat 1

    Nate moved unusually fast and made landfall near the mouth of the Mississippi River and then near Biloxi on October 7–8, 2017. Pensacola received surge flooding along the beaches and bays and scattered outages, but was spared a direct hit.

  5. 2012

    Hurricane Isaac Cat 1

    Isaac crawled into Louisiana in late August 2012. Its wide wind field and slow motion pushed surge into Pensacola Beach and Perdido Key and dropped heavy rain on the western Panhandle.

  6. 2005

    Hurricane Katrina Cat 3

    Katrina's catastrophic Mississippi and Louisiana landfall on August 29, 2005 sent surge into Pensacola Beach and Navarre Beach, re-flooding areas still recovering from Ivan and Dennis.

  7. 2005

    Hurricane Dennis Cat 3

    Landfall: July 10, 2005, on Santa Rosa Island between Pensacola Beach and Navarre Beach, with about 120 mph winds.

    Dennis arrived just ten months after Ivan, while thousands of Pensacola homes still had blue tarps on their roofs and families were living in FEMA trailers. It was a smaller, faster storm than Ivan, so surge was lower, but its compact core produced intense winds in Navarre, Gulf Breeze and eastern Pensacola. Dennis pushed many residents to rebuild to higher standards and sped up the adoption of stronger wind codes.

  8. 2004

    Hurricane Ivan Cat 3

    Landfall: September 16, 2004, just west of Gulf Shores, Alabama, with about 120 mph winds.

    Ivan is the defining Pensacola hurricane of the modern era. It crossed the Gulf as a Category 4 and, although it weakened slightly before landfall, its huge wind field and the fact that Pensacola sat on its dangerous eastern side produced a storm surge of roughly 10 to 15 feet in Pensacola Bay and Escambia Bay, topped by large waves.

    • More than a quarter-mile of the I-10 Escambia Bay Bridge collapsed when surge and waves lifted spans off their piers, cutting the interstate for weeks.
    • Pensacola Beach and Perdido Key were overwashed; homes, condos, roads and dunes were destroyed, and Fort Pickens Road was buried in sand.
    • Tens of thousands of homes in Escambia and Santa Rosa counties were damaged or destroyed, and power was out for weeks in some areas.
    • Ivan's total U.S. damage was estimated in the tens of billions of dollars, at the time one of the costliest hurricanes in U.S. history.

    Ivan led to a new I-10 bridge built higher, stronger building requirements across the region, and the modern evacuation zone system.

  9. 1998

    Hurricane Georges Cat 2

    Georges made landfall near Biloxi on September 28, 1998, then stalled. The western Panhandle received extreme rainfall — more than two feet in parts of Santa Rosa and Escambia counties — causing major river flooding on the Blackwater, Yellow and Escambia rivers.

  10. 1997

    Hurricane Danny Cat 1

    Danny stalled over Mobile Bay in July 1997, dropping more than 30 inches of rain on parts of coastal Alabama and soaking western Escambia County for days.

  11. 1995

    Hurricane Opal Cat 3

    Landfall: October 4, 1995, near Pensacola Beach, with about 115 mph winds after rapidly weakening from Category 4 in the central Gulf.

    Opal's surge and battering waves, estimated in the range of 10 to 15 feet along parts of Santa Rosa Island and Okaloosa County, destroyed dunes, roads and hundreds of beachfront buildings on Pensacola Beach and Navarre Beach. Evacuation traffic clogged roads as the storm intensified overnight — one reason Pensacola-area officials now order evacuations earlier.

  12. 1995

    Hurricane Erin Cat 2

    Two months before Opal, Erin crossed the Florida peninsula and made a second landfall near Navarre Beach on August 3, 1995. Wind damage was widespread in Pensacola, Gulf Breeze and Navarre, softening up roofs and dunes ahead of Opal.

  13. 1979

    Hurricane Frederic Cat 3

    Frederic made landfall near Dauphin Island, Alabama, on September 12, 1979. Pensacola, on the eastern side, suffered heavy wind and surge damage, and Perdido Key and Pensacola Beach were overwashed. Frederic was among the costliest U.S. hurricanes of its era.

  14. 1975

    Hurricane Eloise Cat 3

    Eloise struck near Panama City Beach in September 1975. Pensacola was on the weaker western side, but its surge damage along the Panhandle coast helped prompt Florida's stricter coastal construction rules.

  15. 1926

    The 1926 Pensacola hurricane Major

    After devastating Miami, the Great Miami Hurricane crossed the Gulf and slowed near the Alabama–Florida line on September 20, 1926. Pensacola endured roughly a full day of hurricane conditions; the surge wrecked the waterfront, wharves and much of the fishing fleet.

  16. 1906

    The 1906 Pensacola hurricane Major

    On September 27, 1906, a major hurricane came ashore between Mobile and Pensacola. Surge reportedly reached around 10 feet in Pensacola, destroying much of the waterfront and dozens of ships in the harbor, and the storm killed well over 100 people along the Gulf Coast.

  17. 1752

    Santa Rosa Island presidio destroyed

    A hurricane in 1752 destroyed the Spanish settlement of Presidio Santa Rosa Punta de Sigüenza on Santa Rosa Island. The Spanish moved the town to the mainland — the site of today's historic downtown Pensacola.

  18. 1559

    The Luna expedition hurricane

    In August 1559, Spanish explorer Don Tristán de Luna y Arellano founded a colony on Pensacola Bay with about 1,500 settlers and soldiers. A hurricane struck in September, sinking most of the fleet and supplies at anchor; the colony struggled and was abandoned in 1561. Archaeologists have since found Luna shipwrecks in the bay and the settlement site on land — physical proof of the oldest recorded Pensacola hurricane.

Wind speeds are approximate peak sustained winds at landfall from National Hurricane Center records. Surge and rainfall figures are rounded ranges from NHC tropical cyclone reports and NWS Mobile summaries.

Lessons from Pensacola's hurricane history

  • Distance from the eye is not safety. Ivan, Frederic and the 1906 and 1926 storms all made landfall to the west and still devastated Pensacola.
  • Slow storms are rain storms. Sally, Georges and Danny caused much of their damage through rainfall rather than wind.
  • Back-to-back seasons happen. Erin and Opal (1995), Ivan and Dennis (2004–05), and Sally and Zeta (2020) all came in pairs.
  • Claims take time. After Ivan and Sally, many homeowners spent months or years resolving roof, water and flood claims. Documentation made the difference — see the claims center.
