Pensacola Beach hurricane guide

Pensacola Beach sits on Santa Rosa Island, a narrow barrier island between the Gulf and Santa Rosa Sound. Almost the entire community is in Evacuation Zone A and FEMA flood zones VE or AE, which means it is the first place ordered to evacuate when a Pensacola hurricane threatens.

Hurricanes that hit Pensacola Beach

  • Hurricane Opal (1995) overwashed the island, flattening dunes and destroying beachfront homes and businesses.
  • Hurricane Ivan (2004) pushed surge and waves completely across the island in places, burying Via de Luna and Fort Pickens Road in sand and destroying hundreds of homes and condos.
  • Hurricane Dennis (2005) made landfall on Santa Rosa Island just east of Pensacola Beach ten months later.
  • Hurricane Sally (2020) flooded the island and its loose construction barges damaged the Pensacola Bay Bridge, cutting the beach's main link to downtown for months.

See the full Pensacola hurricane history for details on each storm.

Evacuation in Pensacola Beach

  • Leave as soon as an evacuation order is issued — the Bob Sikes Bridge to Gulf Breeze closes when sustained winds reach roughly 40 mph.
  • From Gulf Breeze, go west over the Pensacola Bay Bridge to I-110/US-29, or east on US-98 to SR-87 north.
  • After the storm, re-entry is controlled at the toll plaza; residents and business owners may need re-entry documentation, so keep proof of address with you.

Local preparation tip

Condo owners should know how their association's master policy and their own HO-6 policy split coverage, and that units on the ground floor need flood insurance even if the building has a master flood policy.
