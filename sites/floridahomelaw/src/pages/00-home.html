Florida home law is a lot of things, but when a storm, a leak or a fire hits, the part that matters most is the law that governs your homeowners insurance claim. This free guide explains it in plain English: the deadlines you must meet, the deadlines your insurer must meet, what the 2022–2023 reforms changed, and where to turn when a claim goes sideways.

Know your rights

Ten Florida homeowner insurance rights worth knowing before you file

Tap any right to see what it means in practice and where it comes from. These summaries apply to most personal residential policies; your policy wording and date of loss can change the answer.

Your claim must be acknowledged within 7 days

Once you report a claim, the insurer generally has 7 calendar days to acknowledge it and send claim forms, instructions and a phone number. A dated phone acknowledgment counts if it is noted in the claim file.

Florida Statutes s. 627.70131(1) and (2)

You get a plain-language Bill of Rights within 14 days

For personal residential policies, the insurer must send the Homeowner Claims Bill of Rights within 14 days of your first communication about the claim. Read what it promises.

s. 627.7142

Inspection within 30 days of your proof of loss

If the insurer wants to physically inspect, it must generally do so within 30 days after it receives your proof-of-loss statement, and the adjuster must give you their name and state license number.

s. 627.70131(3)(b)

A copy of the adjuster’s detailed estimate within 7 days

When the insurer’s adjuster prepares a detailed estimate of the loss, you are generally entitled to a copy within 7 days after it is generated. Ask for it in writing if it does not arrive.

s. 627.70131(3)(e)

Payment or a written denial within 60 days

For residential claims, the insurer must pay or deny, in whole or in part, within 60 days after notice of an initial, reopened or supplemental claim, and explain its decision in writing. Limited exceptions apply, such as a declared state of emergency or a policyholder who does not cooperate.

s. 627.70131(5) and (7)

Interest if payment comes late

If the insurer pays after the deadline, the payment generally carries statutory interest from the date the insurer received notice of the claim. The policy cannot waive this.

s. 627.70131(7)(a)

Free state mediation of a disputed claim

The Florida Department of Financial Services runs a mediation program for residential property claim disputes, and the insurer pays the mediator. You can back out of a mediated settlement within 3 business days unless you have already cashed the check. How mediation works.

s. 627.7015

Replacement cost, paid in stages

With a replacement cost dwelling policy, the insurer must pay at least the actual cash value (minus the deductible) first and the rest as repairs are done. For a total loss it must pay replacement cost without holding back depreciation.

s. 627.7011(3)(a); s. 627.702

Your roof’s age alone cannot cost you coverage if it is under 15

For policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2022, an insurer may not refuse to write or renew a home policy solely because the roof is less than 15 years old. Older roofs can earn coverage with an inspection showing at least 5 years of useful life. Roof and hurricane rules.

s. 627.7011(5)

One hurricane deductible per calendar year

A hurricane deductible applies once per calendar year to all hurricane losses under policies from the same insurer group. After the first storm, later hurricane claims that year use the greater of the remaining hurricane deductible or your regular deductible.

s. 627.701(5)

Deadlines

The claim clock under Florida home law

Most claim problems we hear about start with a missed date. Here are the main ones in order, for residential claims with recent dates of loss.

  1. Day 7Insurer acknowledges your claim and sends formsInsurer
  2. Day 14Homeowner Claims Bill of Rights arrivesInsurer
  3. Day 30*Physical inspection done (*after proof of loss)Insurer
  4. Day 60Claim paid or denied in writing, with reasonsInsurer
  5. 1 yearLast day to give notice of a new or reopened claimYou
  6. 18 monthsLast day to give notice of a supplemental claimYou
  7. 10 business daysPre-suit notice must precede any lawsuitYou / your lawyer
  8. 5 yearsSuit for breach of the policy, counted from the date of lossYou / your lawyer

For a hurricane or other weather event, the date of loss is the landfall date or the date NOAA verifies the event (s. 627.70132(3)).

A rough guide only. The 1-year and 18-month windows apply to losses under the post-December 2022 version of s. 627.70132; older losses had longer windows. Your policy may impose its own conditions, so have a lawyer confirm any deadline you are close to.

Getting help

When Florida home law says it is time to talk to a lawyer

Plenty of claims are paid fairly without anyone hiring a lawyer. These are the moments when a second opinion tends to be worth it:

  • The insurer denied the claim, or paid only for “wear and tear” or “pre-existing” damage.
  • The offer is far below your contractor’s estimate, or the adjuster’s estimate leaves out whole rooms or the roof.
  • Sixty days have passed with no payment or written decision.
  • You are asked for an examination under oath, a recorded statement or a stack of documents with a short deadline.
  • The insurer demands appraisal, or you are weighing whether to invoke it.
  • You are close to the 1-year or 18-month notice deadline.

Questions homeowners ask about Florida home law

What is the Florida Homeowner Claims Bill of Rights?

It is a short notice, required by section 627.7142 of the Florida Statutes, that a personal residential insurer must send within 14 days after you first contact it about a claim. It summarizes your rights to acknowledgment, estimate copies, a 60-day decision, interest on late payment, and free mediation.

How long do I have to report a homeowners insurance claim in Florida?

For most current losses, notice of a new or reopened claim must be given within 1 year after the date of loss, and notice of a supplemental claim within 18 months, under section 627.70132. Older losses fell under longer windows, so the date of loss matters.

Does my insurer still pay my attorney fees if I win?

Generally no, for newer policies. The 2022 special session (SB 2-A) ended one-way attorney fees in property insurance suits and HB 837 repealed the old fee statute in 2023. Which rule applies can depend on when your policy was issued, so ask a lawyer about your specific policy.

Is mediation required before I can sue my insurer?

No. State mediation under section 627.7015 is optional for the homeowner. What is required before most property insurance lawsuits is a written pre-suit notice, submitted on a Department of Financial Services form at least 10 business days before suit, under section 627.70152.

Does this site cover HOA or landlord-tenant law?

No. This site covers Florida homeowner law only as it relates to property insurance claims. For HOA, landlord-tenant or real estate questions, contact the Florida Bar Lawyer Referral Service.
