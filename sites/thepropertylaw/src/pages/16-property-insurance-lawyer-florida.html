The role of a property insurance lawyer in Florida

A property insurance lawyer in Florida represents policyholders in disputes with their own insurance company over damage to a home, condominium or commercial building. Most claims are paid without one. A lawyer becomes useful when the insurer denies a claim, pays well below what repairs cost, delays a decision, or starts asking for sworn statements and documents in a way that suggests the claim is being investigated rather than adjusted.

The work is mostly about three things: reading the complete policy against the facts, building evidence of cause and cost, and using the procedures Florida law provides at the right time. Because the 2021–2023 reforms added several mandatory steps and shortened deadlines, timing has become a large part of the job.

When to call a lawyer

  • You received a denial letter citing wear and tear, pre-existing damage, late notice, an exclusion, or failure to cooperate.
  • The insurer's estimate is far below a licensed contractor's estimate, or omits whole areas of damage.
  • Sixty days have passed since you reported a residential claim and there is no pay-or-deny decision.
  • The insurer has requested an examination under oath, a recorded statement or extensive financial records.
  • The insurer invoked appraisal, or you are considering it.
  • You are approaching a deadline: 1 year from the date of loss for a new claim, 18 months for a supplemental claim, or 2 years from discovery for a sinkhole claim.
  • You are being asked to sign a release or a proof of loss you disagree with.
  • A commercial claim involves business interruption, coinsurance or multiple buildings.

What the work looks like, stage by stage

1. Review

The lawyer obtains a certified copy of the full policy, the claim file materials the insurer has provided (estimates, photos, engineering reports, letters), and your documentation. The question is whether the insurer's position matches the policy wording and Florida law, and what the realistic value of the claim is.

2. Evidence

Where cause or scope is disputed, the lawyer may retain independent professionals such as engineers, general contractors, estimators, geologists for sinkhole claims, or forensic accountants for business income. The goal is a documented estimate the insurer has to respond to line by line.

3. Communication and negotiation

Once a policyholder is represented, communications go through counsel. Under s. 627.70131(1)(c), some of the insurer's acknowledgment duties change when the claimant is represented. The lawyer presents the supporting documentation and negotiates, including through DFS mediation for residential claims where it fits.

4. Pre-suit notice

Before any suit, s. 627.70152 requires a written notice of intent to initiate litigation filed with DFS at least 10 business days before filing, with specific content: the alleged acts or omissions and, for most disputes, an itemized settlement demand and disputed amount. A defective notice can result in dismissal, so this step is handled carefully.

5. Appraisal or litigation

If the insurer requires appraisal in response to the notice, or the policy's appraisal clause is invoked, the lawyer advises on appraiser selection and the process. If the dispute goes to court, the lawyer handles pleadings, discovery, depositions, testimony from engineers and estimators and trial or settlement.

6. Bad faith, where justified

In some cases a civil remedy notice under s. 624.155 is filed to preserve a bad faith claim. Against a property insurer, that claim generally cannot proceed until a court has ruled the insurer breached the policy (s. 624.1551).

How fees work after the reforms

For decades, Florida law required an insurer that lost a coverage lawsuit to pay the policyholder's attorney fees. The December 2022 special session (SB 2-A) eliminated that one-way fee shifting for suits under residential and commercial property policies, and the 2023 tort reform law (HB 837) repealed the underlying statutes. For current policies, each side generally pays its own lawyer, subject to other rules such as offers of judgment under s. 768.79.

As a result, most property insurance lawyers who represent policyholders work on a contingency basis, with the fee coming out of what is recovered. {{FIRM}}'s terms are simple to state: no upfront fees, and the firm does not get paid unless you do. Whatever lawyer you consult, ask for the fee agreement in writing and ask:

  • What percentage applies, and does it change if the case is litigated?
  • How are case costs (engineers, appraisers, filing fees) handled, and who pays them if there is no recovery?
  • Who will actually work on the file and how will you be updated?

Florida Bar rules require contingency fee agreements to be in writing and signed, and in some kinds of cases require the client to receive a written statement of client's rights.

What to bring to a consultation

  • The declarations page and, if you have it, the full policy
  • The claim number and the date of loss
  • Every letter or email from the insurer, especially denials and payment explanations
  • The insurer's estimate and any estimates you obtained
  • Photos and video of the damage, before and after mitigation
  • Receipts for emergency repairs and living expenses

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a lawyer or a public adjuster?

A licensed public adjuster can help prepare and negotiate a claim for a fee. A lawyer can do that and also give legal advice, send the statutory pre-suit notice, handle appraisal disputes and file suit. If a claim has been denied or legal deadlines are near, legal advice is usually the priority.

Can I still hire a lawyer if I already cashed a check?

Usually yes, unless you signed a release. Partial payments do not end the claim, and supplemental claims can be filed within 18 months of the date of loss. Do not sign a release of all claims without advice.

Will hiring a lawyer slow down my claim?

Not necessarily. The statutory timelines in s. 627.70131 still apply, and the pre-suit process has its own short response periods. A lawyer can often move a stalled claim by putting the dispute on a defined track.

How much does a property insurance lawyer cost in Florida?

Many work on contingency, so there is no upfront fee and the fee is a percentage of the recovery. Terms vary by firm and case, so get the agreement in writing and ask how costs are handled.
