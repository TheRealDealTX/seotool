“Do I need a lawyer for a denied insurance claim?” is the right question to ask before you respond to a Florida property insurer. The answer depends on why the claim was denied, how much is at stake, how close you are to a deadline, and whether the insurer has asked you for a sworn statement or an examination under oath. This chapter gives you a way to decide.

Start with the denial letter

Florida law requires the insurer to give you a reasonable written explanation of the basis in the policy for a denial or partial denial (s. 627.70131(7)(a)). Read it closely and sort the reason into one of three buckets:

  1. Paperwork. A missing document, an unsigned form, or an unanswered request. These can often be fixed by supplying what was asked for and asking the insurer to reconsider in writing.
  2. Amount. The insurer accepts the loss but pays less than the repair cost, or says the damage is below your deductible. These are valuation disputes.
  3. Coverage. The insurer says the policy does not cover the loss: wear and tear, a long-term leak, an exclusion, a late-notice defense, or a breach of a policy condition. These turn on legal interpretation and causation evidence.

Bucket one is often manageable on your own. Buckets two and three are where legal help most often changes the outcome.

When you may be able to handle it yourself

  • The denial cites a specific missing document and you can supply it.
  • The amount in dispute is small relative to the cost of hiring help.
  • You have plenty of time before the deadlines in s. 627.70132 and s. 95.11 (see Florida claim deadlines).
  • The insurer is communicating, acknowledging your letters and explaining its reasons.

Florida also offers free help. The Department of Financial Services' Division of Consumer Services assists policyholders with insurer disputes at no cost (helpline 1-877-693-5236), and DFS runs a free mediation program for most residential claims under s. 627.7015, where the insurer pays the mediator. You can attend mediation without a lawyer.

Signs you should talk to a lawyer

About the claim

  • A full denial based on an exclusion, wear and tear, or causation.
  • A large gap between the insurer's estimate and your contractor's.
  • A denial for late notice or failure to cooperate.
  • Mixed causes of loss, such as wind and water after a hurricane.
  • A commercial property claim, which DFS mediation generally does not cover.

About the process

  • The insurer requests an examination under oath or a recorded statement.
  • The insurer demands appraisal, or you are considering it.
  • You are within a few months of a notice or suit deadline.
  • No decision long after the 60-day period, or no written reasons.
  • You are being asked to sign a release or accept a final payment.

What a lawyer changes

A lawyer cannot make a covered loss out of an excluded one. What counsel can do is make sure the insurer's position is tested against the policy's exact language and against real evidence, and that every procedural step is taken correctly and on time. In Florida that procedure has become technical:

  • A lawsuit requires a pre-suit notice under s. 627.70152 with specific contents, filed at the right time; a suit filed without it must be dismissed.
  • Appraisal is binding on the amount of loss, so how it is invoked and who serves as your appraiser matter. See insurance appraisal in Florida.
  • A bad-faith claim needs a properly specific Civil Remedy Notice and usually must wait until the underlying claim is resolved. See Florida insurance bad faith law.
  • Offers of judgment now carry fee risk for policyholders under ss. 624.1552 and 768.79. See attorney fees after HB 837.

What to have ready before you call

  • Your policy declarations page and, if you have it, the full policy with endorsements.
  • The denial or payment letter and the insurer's estimate.
  • Photos and video of the damage, dated if possible.
  • Any contractor, roofer or plumber estimates and invoices for emergency repairs.
  • The date of loss and the date you first reported the claim.
  • Your claim number and the adjuster's name and license number.

Do not throw away damaged materials or finish permanent repairs without documenting the damage first, and do not give a recorded statement or sign a release until you understand what it covers.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a lawyer for a denied insurance claim in Florida?

Not always. Paperwork denials can often be fixed directly, and DFS offers free help and mediation. A lawyer is most useful for coverage denials, large valuation gaps, examinations under oath, appraisal, and any claim near a deadline.

Can I go to DFS mediation without a lawyer?

Yes. DFS mediation under s. 627.7015 is designed to be accessible to policyholders, and the insurer generally pays the mediator. You may rescind a signed settlement within 3 business days if you have not cashed the check.

Is it too late to get help if my claim was denied months ago?

Possibly not. A suit for breach of a property insurance contract may be filed within five years of the date of loss under s. 95.11(2)(e), provided notice of the claim was timely. Get advice promptly.

Should I hire a public adjuster or a lawyer?

Public adjusters help prepare and negotiate claims; lawyers give legal advice, handle pre-suit notice and litigation, and evaluate bad faith. For a coverage denial or a dispute heading toward suit, legal advice is usually the better fit.
