Reference

Property Insurance Claim Glossary

44 terms from policies, estimates and appraisal awards — including Massachusetts-specific ones — in plain English.

ACDFHILMNOPRSTUWX
Actual Cash Value (ACV)
The replacement cost of damaged property minus depreciation. Many policies pay this amount first.
Additional Living Expense (ALE)
The extra cost of living elsewhere while your home is unlivable after a covered loss. It usually falls under loss of use coverage.
Appraisal award
The written decision that sets the amount of loss, signed by at least two of the three panel members.
Appraisal clause
The policy provision that lets either side demand a panel to decide a disputed amount of loss.
Chapter 176D
The Massachusetts law on unfair methods of competition and unfair claim settlement practices in insurance. Appraisal does not decide claims under it.
Chapter 93A
The Massachusetts consumer-protection law. Whether it applies to an insurance dispute is a legal question for an attorney.
Code upgrade / Ordinance or law
Coverage for added costs to bring repaired work up to current building codes. It is often subject to a separate limit.
Coverage A
Dwelling coverage for the house itself and attached structures.
Coverage B
Other structures coverage for detached buildings such as garages, sheds and fences.
Coverage C
Personal property coverage for your belongings.
Coverage D
Loss of use coverage, including additional living expense and fair rental value.
Declarations page
The summary page of a policy listing the insured, property, limits, deductibles and policy period.
Deductible
The portion of a covered loss you pay before the insurer pays.
Depreciation
The reduction in value for age, wear and condition. It is subtracted from replacement cost to reach actual cash value.
Disinterested appraiser
An appraiser with no financial or personal stake in the outcome, as most appraisal clauses require.
Division of Insurance
The Massachusetts state agency that regulates insurers and handles consumer complaints. It is part of the Office of Consumer Affairs and Business Regulation.
Fair rental value
The rent you lose when a covered loss makes a rented part of your property unlivable.
HO-3
A common homeowner policy form covering the dwelling on an open-perils basis and belongings on a named-perils basis.
HO-6
A condo unit owner policy covering interior improvements, belongings, loss of use and some liability.
Holdback
Recoverable depreciation the insurer withholds until repairs are completed.
Ice dam
A ridge of ice at the roof edge that blocks melting snow, which can back water up under shingles and into the house.
Line item
A single entry in an estimate, such as one material or task with a quantity and unit price.
Loss of use
Coverage for added living costs or lost rent while a home cannot be lived in after a covered loss.
Master policy
The insurance an association carries on a condominium building and common areas.
Matching
The question of whether undamaged materials must be replaced so repairs look uniform with the rest of the surface.
Mitigation
Steps taken to prevent further damage after a loss, such as tarping or water extraction. Most policies require reasonable mitigation.
MPIUA / FAIR Plan
The Massachusetts Property Insurance Underwriting Association, the state’s insurer of last resort for property coverage. It insures many coastal homes.
Named-storm deductible
A separate deductible, often a percentage of the dwelling limit, that applies to damage from a named storm or hurricane.
Nor’easter
A coastal storm with strong northeast winds, common in New England from fall through spring, that can bring wind, snow, surge and flooding.
Overhead and profit (O&P)
Charges for a general contractor’s business costs and margin, often around 10% each when a job needs coordination of several trades.
Pitch
The steepness of a roof, written as rise over a 12-inch run, such as 8/12. Steeper roofs often cost more to repair.
Proof of loss
A sworn statement of the amount claimed that some policies require within a set time after a loss or a request.
Recoverable depreciation
Depreciation that a replacement cost policy pays back once repairs are completed.
Reference (standard fire policy)
The procedure in the Massachusetts standard fire policy for disinterested referees to determine a disputed amount of loss. Many forms call it appraisal.
Replacement Cost Value (RCV)
The cost to repair or replace damaged property with like kind and quality, without subtracting depreciation.
Scope
The list of damaged items and the work needed to repair them. Many disputes are about scope rather than price.
Snow load
The weight of snow and ice on a roof. Heavy loads can crack rafters or cause collapse.
Square
A roofing unit equal to 100 square feet of roof surface.
Sub-limit
A cap inside a broader coverage that limits payment for a specific type of property or loss.
Supplement
A request to add items or costs to an estimate after the first one, often because hidden damage was found.
Triple-decker
A three-family wood-frame house with one unit per floor, common in Worcester, Dorchester, Lowell and other Massachusetts cities.
Umpire
The neutral third panel member who decides items the two appraisers cannot agree on.
Waste factor
Extra material added to an estimate to account for cutting and fitting, especially on roofs and flooring.
Xactimate
Estimating software widely used by insurers, adjusters and contractors to price property repairs line by line.
