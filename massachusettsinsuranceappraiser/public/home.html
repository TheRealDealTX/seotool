Covered isn’t the same as paid

What a Massachusetts Insurance Appraiser actually does

Most short claims aren’t denials. They’re estimates that skip the ice and water shield, price a 12/12 Cape roof as if it were walkable, or patch one wall of cedar shingles that will never match. A Massachusetts Insurance Appraiser closes that distance with a valuation built from our own inspection — then defends it in front of the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, an umpire.

  • Independent scope. We measure and photograph before we read anyone else’s numbers.
  • Local pricing. Labor and materials priced for where the property actually is — Back Bay is not Berkshire County.
  • Straight answers. If your file needs an attorney or a simple supplement instead, we say so on the first call.

Storm almanac

Massachusetts weather that fills claim files

Hurricanes, nor’easters, ice storms and the occasional tornado. Every storm leaves a different fingerprint on a roof — and in an estimate. A Massachusetts Insurance Appraiser has to read all of them.

1938

The Great New England Hurricane

A fast-moving hurricane brought destructive surge to Buzzards Bay and wind damage far inland. It still shapes coastal building rules and hurricane barrier design.

1953

Worcester tornado

On June 9, an F4 tornado tore through Worcester County — one of the deadliest tornadoes in U.S. history and proof that inland Massachusetts sees violent wind.

1978

The Blizzard of ’78

A February nor’easter buried eastern Massachusetts and pushed record tides into coastal towns. Homes were lost along the South Shore.

1991

Hurricane Bob & the Halloween nor’easter

Bob raked Cape Cod and the South Coast in August; weeks later the “Perfect Storm” battered the same shoreline with surf and surge.

2008

December ice storm

Ice-laden trees and lines collapsed across Worcester County and north-central Massachusetts, leaving many homes without power for days.

2011

Springfield tornado & Irene

An EF3 tornado crossed the Pioneer Valley on June 1. Tropical Storm Irene flooded western river towns that August.

2015

Record-setting snow

Boston logged about 110 inches for the season. Roof collapses and ice dam leaks filled claim queues from Worcester to the Cape.

2018

Back-to-back March nor’easters

Coastal flooding swamped Boston Harbor streets, Quincy, Scituate and Marshfield, followed by heavy wet snow and fallen trees.

The roadmap

How a Massachusetts Insurance Appraiser moves a stalled claim

Appraisal turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. These are the six moves that matter most.

Read the full roadmap
  1. 1

    Pin down the disagreement

    List exactly which items, quantities and prices you and the insurer see differently. A clear written gap is the foundation for everything that follows.

  2. 2

    Send the written demand

    Invoke the appraisal or reference provision in writing, naming the claim and stating that the amount of loss is disputed. Keep proof of delivery.

  3. 3

    Name your appraiser

    Each side selects its own competent, disinterested appraiser within the period your policy sets. Your appraiser then studies the file and the property.

  4. 4

    Pick the umpire

    The two appraisers try to agree on a neutral umpire early. If they cannot, the policy usually lets either side ask a court to appoint one.

  5. 5

    Inspect and exchange estimates

    The appraisers walk the property, compare measurements and trade line-item estimates. Photos, invoices and contractor bids are shared at this point.

  6. 6

    Narrow the gap

    The appraisers work through the differences one item at a time. Many files settle here with both appraisers signing the same figures.

Questions

What people ask a Massachusetts Insurance Appraiser

Short, plain answers. There are more on the questions & answers page and in the insurance terms glossary.

What does a Massachusetts Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As the appraiser you appoint, we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is appraisal the same as the “reference” in the Massachusetts standard policy?

They are closely related. The Massachusetts standard fire policy provides for disinterested referees to decide the amount of loss, and many homeowner forms call that process appraisal. Your own policy wording controls.

When is it worth calling a Massachusetts Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s itemized estimate, and the gap to your contractor’s number is meaningful — often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Does appraisal decide coverage or bad-faith claims?

No. Appraisal decides the amount of loss. Coverage questions and claims under Massachusetts consumer-protection laws are matters for an attorney.

How are fees handled?

Each side pays its own appraiser, and the umpire’s fee is usually split evenly. We put our fee in writing before any work begins, and the first conversation is free.

Do you work outside Greater Boston?

Yes. We work in all 14 counties, from Pittsfield and Springfield to Worcester, the South Coast, Cape Cod and the Islands.
