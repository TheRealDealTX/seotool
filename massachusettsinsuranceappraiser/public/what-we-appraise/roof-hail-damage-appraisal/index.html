Roof Damage Insurance Appraisal in Massachusetts

Roof disputes are rarely about whether a roof exists. They are about how many squares, which slopes, what pitch and whether repairs can match. We measure and document so the panel can see the real number.

Hail in Massachusetts is real, just uneven

Most roof damage insurance appraisal requests we see after summer storms come from Central Massachusetts and the Pioneer Valley, where thunderstorms can drop hail across a narrow path. Towns a few miles apart may have very different damage. Along the coast, wind and nor’easters do more roof damage than hail. Either way, the dispute usually comes down to measurement and repairability.

Checklist: what a solid roof file includes

  • Total area by slope, with waste factor stated.
  • Pitch for each slope, since steep-charge labor changes the price.
  • Test squares or marked hits per slope, photographed with scale.
  • Soft metals: gutters, vents, flashing, drip edge, skylight frames.
  • Number of existing layers and whether a tear-off is required.
  • Ice and water shield requirements under current building code.

Our roof area and pitch converter turns plan measurements into sloped area, which is often where the first disagreement lives.

Slate, cedar and matching

Older homes in Brookline, Newton, Concord and many Berkshire towns have slate roofs that can outlast several asphalt roofs. Cedar shingle roofs are common on Cape Cod and the Islands. When a few slates or shingles are damaged, the carrier may propose spot repair. Whether a repair can reasonably match, and what the policy says about matching, are often the real questions.

Repair or replace

Spot repairs on brittle slate can cause more breakage. Faded cedar may not blend with new shingles. We document condition and get repair quotes from roofers who actually work with those materials, then let the panel weigh it. We do not promise a full replacement.

Wear versus storm damageCarriers often cite age, granule loss or prior repairs. Appraisal can value the storm damage, but if the insurer says there is no covered storm damage at all, that is a coverage dispute, and you may need legal advice.

Timing after a storm

Hail marks on asphalt can be hard to see after a few months of weather, and soft-metal dents may be the clearest evidence left. Photograph the roof, gutters, downspouts, window wraps and any outdoor equipment soon after the storm, and note the date. Local news reports and neighbors’ claims can also help place the storm, although they do not prove damage to your roof. If a roofer has already made emergency repairs, keep their photos and invoice, because the panel will want to see what the roof looked like before it was patched.

Common questions

Does hail really damage roofs in Massachusetts?

Yes, though it is less frequent than in some regions. Summer storms in Central and Western Massachusetts can produce hail that damages shingles, siding and soft metals.

Can appraisal force a full roof replacement?

Appraisal sets the amount of loss. If the panel finds repairs cannot reasonably restore the roof, the award may reflect replacement, but no outcome is guaranteed.

What if the insurer says the damage is wear and tear?

That is often a coverage question rather than an amount question. Appraisal may not resolve it, and a Massachusetts attorney can explain your options.
