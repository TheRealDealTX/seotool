Wind Damage Claim Appraisal in Massachusetts

Big old maples and oaks, older framing and sudden storms make wind one of the most common sources of disputed claims here. We help the panel see the whole path of the damage.

Wind damage claim appraisal covers a wide range of Massachusetts losses: shingles peeled off by a nor’easter, a red oak through a Wellesley roof, siding torn away by a summer downburst, or rare tornado damage. Massachusetts has a real tornado history, including the 1953 Worcester tornado and the June 1, 2011 tornado that tracked from Westfield through Springfield, Wilbraham, Monson and Brimfield. The October 2017 and October 2019 wind storms brought down huge numbers of trees across eastern Massachusetts.

Tree-fall losses

A tree on a house often causes more damage than the first photos show. The roof hole is obvious. Cracked rafters, shifted ridge boards, racked walls and broken plaster in rooms below are not. Carriers sometimes estimate the visible hole and stop there.

Debris removal and the tree itself

Policies usually have specific, often limited, terms for tree removal and debris. The panel can value removal costs, but what the policy pays is set by its wording. We list removal separately so it is easy to apply the right limit.

What gets missed after a windstorm

  1. Creased shingles that look fine from the ground but have lost their seal.
  2. Sheathing nails pulled up under intact-looking shingles.
  3. Gutter and fascia damage along the windward side.
  4. Window seals and storm windows on older homes.
  5. Fence, shed and detached garage damage under other structures coverage.
  6. Interior water staining that appears days or weeks later.
Document before cleanupTake photos and video before trees are cut up and hauled away, if it is safe. Keep receipts for emergency tarping and tree work. Safety comes first; never climb onto a damaged roof.

Wind versus other causes

Along the coast, insurers may argue that water damage came from surge or flooding rather than wind-driven rain, which can change coverage entirely. Inland, the argument is more often wind versus age. When cause of loss is the real dispute, appraisal may be limited. Our page on hurricane and nor’easter appraisal covers the coastal side.

Regional patterns

Wind losses look different across the state. Western and Central Massachusetts see more summer thunderstorm and downburst damage, and the 2008 ice storm showed how ice-laden limbs can wreck roofs and power lines across Worcester County. Eastern Massachusetts gets more nor’easter damage, with long hours of sustained wind loosening shingles and siding a little at a time. Neighborhoods with mature street trees, from Newton to Longmeadow, see more tree-fall claims after any strong gust.

How we approach a wind file

We walk the full property, not just the obvious damage, and trace the storm’s direction across the roof and siding. Measurements go into a line-item estimate priced for your region, whether that is Hampden County or the North Shore. We then meet the other appraiser to narrow differences before anything goes to an umpire.

Track dates and correspondence with the claim calendar builder. We serve Springfield, Worcester and towns statewide.

Common questions

Does homeowners insurance cover a neighbor’s tree that fell on my house?

Generally, damage to your house from a falling tree is handled under your own policy regardless of whose tree it was. Debris removal and liability questions depend on policy wording and circumstances.

Can you appraise damage from a tornado?

Yes. Tornado damage is typically a wind loss under homeowner policies, and appraisal can value it when the amount is disputed.

What if damage shows up weeks after the storm?

Report it to your insurer promptly and document it. Policies commonly require prompt notice, so check your policy and ask an attorney if a delay is raised.
