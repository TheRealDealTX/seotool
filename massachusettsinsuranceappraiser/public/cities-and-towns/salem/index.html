Historic homes on the harbor

Salem, in Essex County, has one of the most concentrated collections of historic homes in the country. The McIntire Historic District along Chestnut Street and Federal Street has Federal-era mansions, many with original woodwork, plaster and windows. Derby Street runs along the harbor near the waterfront, with older homes and businesses. Salem Willows sits on a point at the edge of the harbor, with cottages and homes exposed to wind and water.

Other neighborhoods, including the Point, South Salem and the area near Gallows Hill, have two-families, triple-deckers and Victorians. Condo conversions are common in older buildings across the city.

Storms on the North Shore

The Blizzard of February 1978 brought heavy snow, high winds and coastal flooding to the North Shore, and it remains a benchmark for coastal storms here. Nor’easters still bring wind, rain and flooding to Salem’s waterfront neighborhoods. Flood from storm surge is excluded under standard homeowner policies, while wind damage is usually covered.

Our hurricane and nor’easter appraisal service focuses on the wind side of these claims. For water damage from burst pipes or ice dams, see water damage claim appraisal.

Historic repairs cost moreOriginal woodwork, plaster and windows in Salem’s historic homes often cost more to repair than estimates allow. Historic district rules may also require specific materials.

Common Salem claim disputes

  • Historic woodwork and plaster priced as modern materials.
  • Wind versus surge on waterfront homes.
  • Ice dam interior damage scoped too narrowly.
  • Fire and smoke damage in closely spaced older homes.
  • Condo water losses involving both the master policy and an HO-6.

Getting started

If your claim is stuck, our guide to the Massachusetts reference and appraisal clause explains how the process usually works. In short, each side picks a disinterested appraiser, the appraisers try to agree on the amount of loss, and an umpire or referee resolves what they cannot. The process sets a number; it does not decide coverage, and it is not a lawsuit.

Documenting an older Salem home

For a historic house, the best evidence is often what the home looked like before the loss: photos of moldings, doors, mantels and windows, notes on original materials, and any past restoration invoices. That record helps show why a like-kind repair costs more than a modern replacement. If a historic commission review is required for exterior work, keep copies of those requirements too, since they can affect both cost and schedule.

Salem’s waterfront neighborhoods also face salt exposure year-round, which can shorten the life of roofing, flashing and paint. Insurers sometimes point to that wear when they limit a claim, so separating storm damage from age-related wear is an important part of any estimate review.

Salem questions

Do historic district rules affect my Salem claim?

They can. Historic district rules may require specific materials or methods, which can raise repair costs. Ordinance-or-law coverage in your policy may apply.

My Salem Willows home flooded in a nor’easter. Is that covered?

Storm surge flooding is generally excluded from homeowner policies and covered under flood insurance, if you have it.

Can you help with a condo in a converted Salem building?

Yes. We review condo claims, including disputes involving master policies and HO-6 coverage.
