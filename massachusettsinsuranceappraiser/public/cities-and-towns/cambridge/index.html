Claim risks in Cambridge

  • Ice dams

Dense, old and mostly wood

Cambridge packs a lot of housing into a small area. Around Harvard Square and Mid-Cambridge, large Victorians and mansard-roof homes sit on small lots. Cambridgeport and the area near Central Square have rows of two-families and triple-deckers. East Cambridge mixes older worker housing with newer condo buildings near Kendall. Porter Square and North Cambridge have more two-families, many of them converted into condominiums.

Wood-frame buildings this close together carry real fire exposure, and a fire or smoke loss next door can damage your home too. Many of these houses also have plaster walls, original trim and balloon framing, which change how a repair is priced. Our fire and smoke damage appraisal work focuses on getting those details into the scope.

Winter is the big season

In the winter of 2015, repeated storms buried Greater Boston, and Cambridge saw ice dams, roof leaks and interior water damage across older homes with little attic insulation. Ice dam claims are a familiar pattern: the policy may treat the dam as maintenance but cover the resulting interior damage, and the dispute is over how much of the ceiling, insulation, framing and paint actually has to be replaced.

Frozen and burst pipes are the other common winter loss, especially in older buildings where pipes run through exterior walls or unheated porches. Large old trees along Cambridge streets also bring limbs down during wind storms and heavy wet snow.

Snow load on flat and low-slope roofsMany Cambridge two-families and triple-deckers have low-slope roofs where snow and ice can build up. Our roof snow load estimator gives a rough sense of the weight involved.

Condo conversions and shared policies

A Cambridge two-family split into two condos often has a small association with its own master policy, plus an HO-6 policy for each owner. Water from an upstairs unit, a roof leak, or a fire can involve all three. Who repairs what depends on the master deed and the policies. We help owners understand where the lines fall before valuing the loss; see our condo and HOA appraisal page for more.

Common Cambridge disputes

  • Ice dam interiors scoped as a patch-and-paint job.
  • Plaster and period trim priced as drywall and stock molding.
  • Unit-to-unit water losses with unclear master-policy and HO-6 responsibility.
  • Smoke damage in a neighboring unit downplayed or left out.
  • Small association policies with high deductibles and confusion over who pays.

Cambridge questions

Our two-unit condo in Cambridge has a small association. Does it need its own policy?

Most associations carry a master policy for the building, and each owner carries an HO-6. The master deed and bylaws usually explain who insures what. Check your documents and ask your agent.

Is ice dam damage covered in Massachusetts?

Policies often cover resulting interior water damage from an ice dam but not the ice dam removal or maintenance itself. Wording varies, so check your policy.

Can appraisal help if my insurer and the association’s insurer disagree?

Appraisal sets the amount of loss under the policy that invokes it. It does not decide which policy is responsible. Coverage disputes may need advice from a Massachusetts attorney.
