A large town with a long coast

Plymouth covers more land than any other municipality in Massachusetts, stretching along Cape Cod Bay from North Plymouth to the Cape Cod Canal area. Its coastal neighborhoods include Manomet, White Horse Beach, Priscilla Beach and Ellisville, many of them with older beach cottages and homes on bluffs or close to the water. Inland, Cedarville, South Pond and the pine woods have newer subdivisions, ranches and Colonials. Downtown Plymouth has older homes near the waterfront.

That mix means claims range from wind-driven damage on exposed oceanfront homes to tree-fall losses in wooded neighborhoods.

Hurricanes and nor’easters

Hurricane Bob in August 1991 brought strong winds to southeastern Massachusetts, including Plymouth, with widespread tree damage and power outages. Nor’easters are more frequent and can bring high winds, coastal flooding and erosion to bluffs and beaches, especially in places like Manomet and White Horse Beach.

Coastal homes may have separate wind or named-storm deductibles, often a percentage of the dwelling limit. Flood from storm surge is excluded from standard homeowner policies. Our hurricane and nor’easter appraisal service focuses on documenting the wind side of these losses.

Know your deductibleA percentage deductible can be much larger than the flat deductible you expect. Use our hurricane deductible calculator to understand what applies before a storm.

Wooded neighborhoods and winter

Much of inland Plymouth is wooded with pitch pine and oak. Wind storms and heavy wet snow bring trees down on roofs, decks and fences. In winter, ice dams on Capes and frozen pipes in seasonal or lightly used homes are common claims. Seasonal homes left unheated can have pipes freeze and burst, and some policies have conditions about maintaining heat or draining systems.

Common Plymouth claim disputes

  • Wind versus surge arguments on coastal homes.
  • Percentage hurricane deductibles applied after named storms.
  • Tree-fall claims with removal limits.
  • Frozen pipe claims on seasonal homes.
  • Cedar shingle siding repairs without matching.

Appraisal may help when the carrier’s estimate is far below the real repair cost. It is not the right tool for every problem, though. If the insurer denies coverage outright, for example by calling the whole loss flood, appraisal may not resolve that question, and a Massachusetts attorney can explain your options.

Seasonal and second homes

Plymouth has many homes that sit empty for part of the year. Damage there can go unnoticed for weeks, and insurers sometimes argue that a slow leak or long-standing moisture is not a sudden loss. Regular checks, a working low-temperature alarm, and dated photos of the home’s condition can help if a dispute arises later.

Plymouth questions

My Manomet home has a hurricane deductible. How does that work?

Many coastal policies use a percentage deductible for named storms or wind, based on your dwelling limit. Check your declarations page for the exact terms.

I have a seasonal cottage in Plymouth and the pipes froze. Is that covered?

It can be, but many policies require you to maintain heat or drain the plumbing in unoccupied homes. Check your policy conditions.

Does Plymouth have flood zones?

Yes, many coastal areas are in FEMA flood zones. Flood is excluded from homeowner policies and needs separate flood insurance.
