The 2011 tornado and its lessons

On June 1, 2011, an EF3 tornado tracked from Westfield across the Connecticut River and into Springfield, then continued east through Wilbraham, Monson and Brimfield. In the city it damaged downtown, the South End, Forest Park, East Forest Park and Sixteen Acres, tearing roofs off brick apartment blocks, toppling old trees and leaving many homes partly standing but badly racked.

Claims after events like that show how hard it is to value partial damage. A house that looks repairable from the street can have shifted framing, broken rafters or a foundation crack that only shows up once work starts. Estimates written quickly after a disaster often miss that hidden damage, and supplements become a long negotiation. Our wind and tornado claim appraisal service is built around that kind of loss.

Housing across Springfield

Springfield’s housing stock is old and varied. Forest Park and the McKnight district have large Victorians and Queen Annes with slate roofs, wraparound porches and detailed trim. The South End and neighborhoods near downtown include brick multi-family buildings and row houses. Sixteen Acres and East Forest Park lean toward Capes, splits and ranches on bigger lots with mature trees. Each of those requires a different approach to pricing, and template estimates often miss slate, copper flashing, or the labor of matching period details.

Slate and older roofsSlate is often repairable, but only by trades who know it. When an estimate swaps slate for asphalt or limits repairs to a few tiles on a brittle roof, the scope deserves a closer look.

Other Pioneer Valley storms

The same year as the tornado, an unusual late-October 2011 snowstorm fell on trees still in leaf, bringing down limbs across the region and causing long outages. The valley also gets summer thunderstorms with hail and damaging wind, and the Connecticut and Mill rivers can flood low areas in heavy rain. Flood from rising water is excluded under standard homeowner policies, but wind, hail and tree damage from the same storm may still be covered.

Common Springfield claim issues

  • Racked framing and hidden structural damage left out of the first estimate.
  • Tree removal limits applied to large trees that came down on the house.
  • Slate and historic trim priced as modern materials.
  • Code upgrades required by the city but excluded from the scope.
  • Hail claims denied as old or cosmetic.

If the numbers are far apart, the appraisal award gap estimator can help you see whether the difference is worth pursuing through appraisal.

Springfield questions

My Springfield house was damaged by the 2011 tornado and I found more damage later. Can I still reopen it?

Time limits in your policy and in Massachusetts law may apply, and many policies limit suits to about two years. Check your policy and talk to a Massachusetts attorney about your situation.

Does my homeowners policy cover flooding from the Connecticut River?

Generally no. Flood from rising water is excluded and usually requires an NFIP or private flood policy. Wind and rain entering through a damaged roof are handled differently.

Can you help with a brick multi-family in the South End?

Yes. We review residential and smaller commercial or multi-family claims, including masonry repairs and shared roofs.
