Old buildings, tight lots and shared walls

Much of Boston’s housing was built before modern codes, and that shapes almost every claim in Suffolk County. In Dorchester and parts of Jamaica Plain, wood-frame triple-deckers stack three households under one roof, so a single burst pipe or kitchen fire can touch three units, two policies and a condo association at once. Back Bay and the South End add brick and brownstone row houses with slate or copper details, ornate plaster and narrow access that drives up labor and staging costs. Charlestown and South Boston have dense blocks of attached row houses where a fire or water loss next door becomes your loss too.

When an estimate prices plaster as drywall, ignores the cost of working on a one-way street with no driveway, or leaves out code upgrades the city requires once walls are opened, the gap between the carrier’s figure and a real contractor bid can be wide. Our claim estimate review compares the two line by line before anyone invokes appraisal.

The harbor, the snow and the wind

East Boston, the Seaport, the waterfront near Long Wharf and low-lying parts of Dorchester sit close to sea level. The January 2018 nor’easter pushed harbor water into streets around the waterfront, and the March 2018 storms that followed added more coastal flooding around the region. Those events are a reminder that flood and wind are covered differently: standard homeowner policies exclude flood, so many disputes turn on what the wind did versus what the water did.

Winter brings its own pattern. Boston received roughly 110 inches of snow in the winter of 2015, and ice dams and roof leaks followed across West Roxbury, Roslindale and Hyde Park. Interior damage from an ice dam is often covered while the dam itself is treated as maintenance, which is why our ice dam and snow load appraisal work focuses on documenting the full interior scope.

Condo owners: two policies, one lossMany Boston units sit in buildings with an association master policy. Your HO-6 may only respond to what the master policy does not. Our guide to condo master policy vs. HO-6 claims explains how the pieces usually fit.

Common Boston claim disagreements

  • Triple-decker losses where the scope stops at one unit even though damage runs through shared framing.
  • Historic or architectural trim priced as stock material.
  • Wind-versus-flood arguments after coastal storms in East Boston or the Seaport.
  • Ice dam claims that cover the ceiling stain but not insulation, framing or electrical repairs.
  • Smoke damage in attached homes that the estimate limits to the unit where the fire started.

If your carrier’s number does not match the repair, appraisal under your policy may let a neutral process set the amount of loss. Start with our appraisal roadmap to see how the steps usually unfold.

Boston questions

Does appraisal work for a Boston triple-decker owned as three condos?

It can. Each unit owner and the association may each have a policy, and appraisal generally sets the amount of loss under the policy that invokes it. Sorting out which policy covers which part of the building usually comes first.

My basement in East Boston flooded during a nor’easter. Will my homeowners policy pay?

Standard homeowner policies exclude flood from surface water and storm surge, so that part typically falls to an NFIP or private flood policy. Wind or roof damage from the same storm may still be covered under your homeowners policy. Check your policy wording.

Can appraisal decide whether my Boston claim was handled unfairly?

No. Appraisal is about the dollar amount of loss. Questions about claim handling or consumer-protection laws are legal matters; consult a Massachusetts attorney.
