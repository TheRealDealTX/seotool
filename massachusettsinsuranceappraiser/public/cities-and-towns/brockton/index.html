A former shoe city with older housing

Brockton, in Plymouth County, grew as a major shoe-manufacturing center, and its housing reflects that history. Campello in the south and Montello in the north have dense blocks of triple-deckers and two-families built for factory workers. Other parts of the city, including the west side near D.W. Field Park, have Capes, ranches and Colonials on larger lots with mature trees.

Older multi-family homes bring stacked porches, low-slope roofs and layered siding that are easy to underestimate. Capes and ranches bring ice dams and attic leaks in hard winters. Our home insurance claim appraisal service covers both.

Wind and trees

In late October 2017, a powerful wind storm swept across southeastern Massachusetts, bringing down trees and knocking out power for many households. Another strong storm in October 2019 did similar damage. In Brockton, where large oaks and maples shade many streets, these storms often mean tree-fall claims with damage to roofs, gutters, siding, fences and cars.

Tree claims can get complicated. Tree removal coverage often has its own limits, and damage to the house may be split from removal costs. A careful scope covers framing, roofing, interior water damage and debris removal together.

Roof measurements matterMany tree and wind disputes come down to how much roof needs replacing. Our roof area and pitch converter helps check the measurements in an estimate.

Winter claims

Brockton gets the same heavy snow and cold snaps as the rest of the South Shore. Ice dams on Capes, frozen pipes in older multi-families and snow load on low-slope roofs are all familiar. In multi-family buildings, a frozen pipe in one unit can damage others, which raises questions about which policy responds.

Common Brockton disputes

  • Tree-fall claims with low removal limits or incomplete roof repairs.
  • Triple-decker water losses that cross units.
  • Ice dam interior damage scoped too narrowly.
  • Fire and smoke damage in closely spaced homes.
  • Siding repairs without matching on older homes.

If the carrier’s number does not cover the repair, our claim estimate review is a good first step before deciding on appraisal.

What to gather

Photos of the damage before cleanup, the carrier’s estimate and any supplements, contractor bids, receipts for emergency work such as tree removal or tarping, and every letter from the insurer. Many Massachusetts policies limit lawsuits to about two years after a loss, so check your policy and talk to an attorney if timing is a concern. Our claim calendar builder can help you keep track of dates.

Brockton questions

A tree fell on my Brockton home in a wind storm. What does my policy cover?

Most homeowner policies cover damage to the house from a falling tree. Tree removal often has its own sublimit. Check your policy for details.

My Campello triple-decker had a frozen pipe that damaged two units. Is that one claim or two?

If you own the building, it may be one claim under your policy. Tenants’ belongings are usually covered by their own renters policies. The wording of your policy matters.

Do you work with Brockton condo associations?

Yes. We review condo and association claims, including disputes involving master policies.
