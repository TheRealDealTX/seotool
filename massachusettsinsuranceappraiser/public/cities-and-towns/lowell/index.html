From mills to lofts

Lowell grew around textile mills powered by the Merrimack River and its canals, and many of those brick mill buildings downtown are now loft condos and apartments. These buildings bring their own claim issues: heavy timber framing, large windows, shared roofs and mechanical systems, and a condo association master policy that sits on top of each owner’s HO-6. A water loss on an upper floor of a mill building can run through several units and common areas before anyone finds the source.

Outside downtown, the Acre, Centralville, Pawtucketville, Belvidere and the Highlands are full of triple-deckers, two-families and Victorian homes. Belvidere has some of the city’s larger older homes, while the Acre and Centralville have dense multi-family blocks close together.

Mill-loft condosIn a converted mill, the master deed often defines what the association insures and what belongs to each unit owner. Read it alongside our guide on condo master policy vs. HO-6 claims before you assume a loss is all yours or all theirs.

The river and the weather

The Merrimack and Concord rivers have flooded Lowell more than once. In May 2006, heavy rain brought serious flooding to the Merrimack Valley, and low-lying parts of the region took water. Flood from rising water is excluded under standard homeowner policies, so flood losses usually go through an NFIP or private flood policy, while wind and rain damage through a roof or window may be covered by the homeowners policy. When both happen in one storm, the split can be contested.

Winter brings ice dams, frozen pipes and snow load on older roofs. Many Lowell triple-deckers have low-slope roofs and stacked porches, both of which collect snow and ice. Our water damage claim appraisal service covers burst pipes, ice dam leaks and sudden plumbing failures.

Common Lowell claim disputes

  • Loft water losses with unclear responsibility between the association and unit owners.
  • Fire and smoke losses in dense triple-decker blocks.
  • Flood versus wind or sewer backup in the same event.
  • Frozen pipes in vacant or partially rented units.
  • Older plaster, trim and windows priced as modern materials.

Lowell questions

My loft in a Lowell mill building had water damage from a unit above. Who pays?

It depends on the master deed, the association’s master policy and your HO-6. Often the association covers common elements and original structure, while your HO-6 covers your improvements and belongings.

Does my homeowners insurance cover Merrimack River flooding?

Usually not. Flood from rising water is excluded from standard homeowner policies and typically needs separate flood insurance.

Can appraisal be used for a triple-decker I rent out?

Often yes, if the policy includes an appraisal or reference provision. Rental properties may be on a dwelling or commercial form, so the wording matters.
