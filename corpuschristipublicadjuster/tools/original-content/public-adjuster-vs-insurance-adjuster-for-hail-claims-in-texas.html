What Is an Insurance Adjuster?

An insurance adjuster is assigned by your insurance company to evaluate your claim. They inspect. They estimate. They report. But they represent the insurer.

Their Role Includes

  • Inspecting damage
  • Taking photos
  • Writing an estimate
  • Applying policy coverage
  • Recommending settlement

Types of Insurance Adjusters

  • Staff adjusters (company employees)
  • Independent adjusters (contracted by insurers)

Both serve the same purpose to assess the claim within company guidelines.

The Key Limitation

They are not your advocate. They are evaluating what the insurance company should pay not necessarily what your full damage justifies.

What Is a Public Adjuster?

A public adjuster works for you. They manage the claim from inspection to negotiation, with one goal maximize your settlement.

What They Do Differently

  • Conduct detailed inspections
  • Document all damage thoroughly
  • Interpret policy language
  • Build comprehensive estimates
  • Negotiate directly with insurers

How They Get Paid

  • Typically 10% of the final claim
  • No upfront fees
  • Paid only when you get paid

Their incentive aligns with your outcome.

FAQs

What is the main difference between a public adjuster and an insurance adjuster?

A public adjuster represents the homeowner, while an insurance adjuster represents the insurance company.

Do I have to pay an insurance adjuster?

No, insurance adjusters are paid by the insurance company and do not charge homeowners directly.

How do public adjusters get paid?

They typically charge a percentage of the final settlement, usually between 10%.

Can hiring a public adjuster really increase my claim payout?

Yes, public adjusters often identify additional damage and negotiate higher settlements.

When should I hire a public adjuster for a hail claim?

You should consider hiring one if your claim is large, complex, denied, or underpaid.

Can a contractor handle my insurance claim instead of a public adjuster?

No, contractors can estimate repairs but typically cannot legally negotiate insurance claims.

What is a supplement in a hail damage claim?

A supplement is a request to increase your claim amount after additional damage is discovered.

Is it okay to accept the first insurance estimate?

Not always initial estimates are often incomplete, so reviewing them carefully is important.

Are public adjusters licensed in Texas?

Yes, public adjusters must be licensed and regulated by the state of Texas.

Does location like Corpus Christi affect hail damage claims?

Yes, environmental factors in places like Corpus Christi can impact damage severity and repair costs.
