What Is a Public Adjuster?

A public adjuster is a licensed professional who represents you, the policyholder — not the insurance company.

That distinction matters.

When damage occurs, your insurance company assigns an adjuster to evaluate your loss. That adjuster works for the carrier. A public adjuster works for you.

Here’s a simple comparison:

Public adjusters handle documentation, valuation, and negotiation. Attorneys handle lawsuits. Different lanes.

If your claim hasn’t turned into litigation, a public adjuster may be the practical first step.

What Does a Public Adjuster Actually Do?

This is one of the most common questions about public adjusters — and it deserves a clear answer.

A good public adjuster does four primary things:

1. Conducts a Thorough Damage Inspection

They don’t just look at obvious roof shingles missing. They check:

  • Flashing and underlayment
  • Hidden moisture in walls
  • Structural framing
  • HVAC damage from wind-driven rain
  • Saltwater corrosion on coastal properties

Missed damage equals missed money. It’s that simple.

2. Reviews Your Insurance Policy Line by Line

Policies are contracts. Detailed ones.

A public adjuster identifies:

  • Replacement cost provisions
  • Actual cash value clauses
  • Additional living expense coverage
  • Code upgrade endorsements
  • Windstorm vs flood distinctions

Many homeowners don’t realize what they’re entitled to. A policy review changes that.

3. Prepares Documentation and Estimates

Insurance companies rely heavily on documentation. So should you.

Public adjusters prepare:

  • Detailed repair estimates
  • Loss inventories
  • Photo and video evidence
  • Contractor comparisons
  • Depreciation analysis

Strong documentation shifts negotiation leverage.

4. Negotiates the Settlement

They communicate directly with the carrier.
They dispute low valuations.
And they push for reconsideration when necessary.

And they understand the tactics insurers use to control payouts.

Common Questions About Public Adjusters (And Straight Answers)

Let’s tackle the most frequent questions about public adjusters head-on.

How Do Public Adjusters Get Paid?

Most work on contingency. That means:

  • They take a percentage of the settlement.
  • No large upfront fee.
  • If you don’t recover money, they typically don’t get paid.

In Texas, fee caps may apply after declared disasters.

Are Public Adjusters Worth the Cost?

It depends.

For small claims? Maybe not.
For large or disputed claims? Often yes.

If a public adjuster increases your settlement significantly beyond the insurer’s initial offer, their fee can be justified financially.

Are Public Adjusters Licensed in Texas?

Yes. They must be licensed through the Texas Department of Insurance. Always verify credentials before signing anything.

Can I Hire One After Filing a Claim?

Absolutely. Many people contact a public adjuster after receiving a disappointing initial offer.

Can a Claim Be Reopened?

Sometimes. If new damage is discovered or documentation was incomplete, reopening may be possible — depending on policy terms and timing.

Will Hiring One Upset My Insurance Company?

Insurance companies deal with public adjusters regularly. Professional representation is legal and common. You are exercising your rights under your policy.

Do Public Adjusters Handle Flood Claims?

Some do. Flood insurance, especially under federal programs, follows specific rules. Make sure your adjuster has experience in this area.

Corpus Christi-Specific Claim Challenges

Living on the coast isn’t the same as filing a claim inland.

In Corpus Christi, we see:

  • Hurricane deductibles based on property value
  • Windstorm exclusions requiring separate policies
  • Saltwater corrosion damage
  • Repeated storm exposure weakening structures over time

Local experience matters. An adjuster unfamiliar with coastal construction issues may overlook key claim elements.

If you decide to move forward, here’s what typically happens:

  1. Initial Consultation
    Review of your damage and policy.
  2. Contract Signing
    Clear explanation of fees and scope.
  3. Property Inspection
    Comprehensive documentation.
  4. Policy Analysis
    Identification of applicable coverages.
  5. Claim Presentation
    Submission of organized, evidence-backed demand.
  6. Negotiation
    Back-and-forth with the insurance company.
  7. Settlement Agreement
    Final approval and payment.

The process is structured. Not chaotic.

Situations Where You Might Not Need a Public Adjuster

Let’s be balanced.

You may not need one if:

  • The claim is small and straightforward.
  • The insurance company promptly pays a fair amount.
  • You’re comfortable reviewing estimates and negotiating yourself.

Representation is strategic. Not automatic.

Red Flags to Watch Out For

Unfortunately, after storms, questionable operators show up.

Be cautious of:

  • Door-to-door contracts pushed aggressively
  • Promises of “guaranteed huge payouts”
  • Pressure to sign immediately
  • Lack of license verification
  • No physical office or local presence

Professional adjusters do not rush you. They explain. They document. And they earn trust.

Questions You Should Ask Before Hiring

If you’re interviewing a public adjuster in Corpus Christi, ask:

  • How many hurricane claims have you handled locally?
  • What percentage do you charge?
  • Can you provide references?
  • How often will you communicate updates?
  • What happens if the claim is denied?

Clear answers indicate professionalism.

A coastal homeowner receives an initial insurance estimate of $42,000 for roof and interior repairs after a hurricane. A contractor says repairs will cost closer to $75,000.
