Why the metro matters

A roof tarp is specified for the weather it has to survive, and Texas weather is regional to an unusual degree.

  • The Gulf coast — Houston, Corpus Christi, Beaumont — takes tropical systems that load a roof for hours with the wind direction reversing as the centre passes. Anchoring has to hold from every direction, and coastal salt corrodes ordinary fastenings.
  • Hail alley — DFW, the I-35 corridor, Waco — sees narrow, intense hail swaths. Damage is often invisible from the ground and concentrated on a street or two.
  • The High Plains and Permian Basin — Lubbock, Midland — is flat and open, so wind arrives unobstructed. Standard anchor spacing that works elsewhere does not hold here.
  • The desert — El Paso — has flat roofs, violent monsoon bursts and the harshest UV in the state, which shortens tarp life dramatically.
  • The Piney Woods — The Woodlands, Huntsville — is tree-fall country. Damage is concentrated point impact, often with the tree still in place.

If your town is not listed, call anyway. These are the metros we are asked for most often, not the limit of where we work.
