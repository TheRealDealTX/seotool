Downtown El Paso Christmas Lights: A Winter Photo Session Guide

For a few weeks every year, the center of the city trades its usual sun-bleached glow for something warmer. The downtown El Paso Christmas lights turn ordinary blocks into a backdrop that feels nothing like the desert portraits most people picture when they think of the Borderland, and that contrast is exactly why we love it for engagement sessions, senior pictures and family photos. This guide covers where to go, when to shoot, what to wear when a mild afternoon turns into a cold desert night, and how to keep a holiday session calm when half the city had the same idea.

Why winter in El Paso is a photographer's season

People who have never spent a winter in El Paso are often surprised by it. Days tend to be clear and dry, and the low winter sun stays soft for longer than the hard summer light does. Then the temperature drops quickly once the sun goes behind the mountains. That combination matters for photos: you get long, warm golden light in the late afternoon, a clean blue sky at dusk, and very little haze to flatten the scene.

The holiday season adds the one thing the desert usually lacks after dark, which is light. Strings and displays downtown give you a reason to keep shooting after sunset instead of packing up, and they let you plan a session that moves from natural light into evening without changing locations.

Where to find the downtown El Paso Christmas lights

In recent years the city's holiday celebration downtown has centered on San Jacinto Plaza, with lights, decorations and seasonal events spreading out into the surrounding blocks. Exactly what goes up, and where, changes from year to year, so treat the list below as a starting point and walk the area before your session if you can.

  • The plaza itself. It has the highest concentration of lights and the most atmosphere. It also draws the biggest crowds, especially on weekends and event nights.
  • Side streets around the plaza. A block or two away you will often find decorated storefronts and trees with far fewer people walking through the frame.
  • Historic building facades. Downtown has wonderful early twentieth century architecture. Brick, carved stone and old theater signage read beautifully at blue hour, even without holiday decorations in the frame.
  • High vantage points. On a clear evening the lit star on the Franklin Mountains is visible from many points around the city, and a wide shot with the mountain and city lights behind a couple is a very El Paso image.
Scout at the same hour: Walk your route at the time of day you will actually shoot. A corner that looks dull at noon can be the best spot in town once the lights come on, and the reverse is just as common.

Timing a session under the downtown El Paso Christmas lights

The single biggest mistake with a lights session is starting it after dark. When the sky is fully black, the lights become bright points against nothing, faces fall into shadow, and the camera has to work hard to keep up. The better plan is to start before sunset and let the light change around you.

  1. 30 to 45 minutes before sunset: Start with natural light. Warm, low sun on faces, relaxed portraits, the easy shots that ease everyone into the session.
  2. Sunset to about 20 minutes after: Move toward the lights. As the sky softens, the displays start to show up in the frame.
  3. Blue hour: This is the window you are really planning around. The sky is a deep blue, the lights glow without blowing out, and faces can still be lit by the ambient light around them.
  4. After dark: Use the last few minutes for close, cozy images where the lights become soft background bokeh.

Winter sunsets in El Paso come early, so a session that starts around 4:30 in the afternoon is common in December. If you want a precise plan for your date, our golden hour photography guide explains how to work out golden and blue hour for any day.

What to wear in desert winter

An afternoon in the sixties can turn into an evening near freezing, and that shift happens right in the middle of your session. Dress for the end of the shoot, not the start.

  • Coats are part of the outfit. A camel wool coat, a deep green or burgundy layer, or a classic denim jacket with a warm sweater all photograph well against holiday lights.
  • Choose texture over pattern. Knits, wool, corduroy and suede catch the warm light nicely. Busy prints compete with the lights in the background.
  • Go richer in color. Cream, rust, forest green, navy and wine tones look intentional at night. Neon and very pale pastels can look washed out.
  • Plan for hands. Gloves come off for photos, so bring a pair to wear between setups. Cold hands look red and stiff on camera.
  • Comfortable shoes. Downtown sessions involve walking. Bring the nice shoes for the close-ups and wear something sensible between spots.

Senior pictures in El Paso TX with a holiday twist

Most senior sessions in the region lean on mountains, desert trails and golden fields, which is lovely, but a lot of portfolios start to look the same. A winter lights session gives a senior something different for graduation announcements and gives the family images that feel tied to a specific time of year. It also pairs well with a daytime session elsewhere, so the final gallery has range.

A few ideas that work especially well for seniors downtown:

  • A letterman jacket or school colors layered under a winter coat.
  • A walking shot down a lit block with the senior looking back toward the camera.
  • Seated portraits on a bench or steps with lights behind, so the background melts away.
  • A few frames with a best friend or sibling, which often produce the most natural smiles of the night.

Handling crowds and public spaces

Holiday lights are a shared community event, and that is part of the charm. It also means you will be working around families, strollers and other photographers. A little planning goes a long way.

  • Check the city calendar and avoid big event nights unless you want the crowd energy in your photos.
  • Choose a weeknight over a weekend if you can.
  • Keep gear light: no stands or bags spread across a walkway.
  • Have two or three backup spots within a short walk.
  • Agree on a meeting point in case you get separated from family members who came along.
  • Bring a small blanket or towel to sit on, and a thermos of something warm.

Be considerate of other people's moments. A family taking their own photo under the main display deserves the same patience you would want. Waiting thirty seconds for a clear frame usually costs nothing.

A note on technique for the photographer in the family

If you are shooting this yourself, the basics are simple. Use a lens with a wide aperture so the lights turn into soft circles behind your subjects, and put some distance between the couple and the lights so the background can blur. Watch skin tones: holiday bulbs are often very warm, and a white balance that looks natural on faces may push the lights orange. A small, dim continuous light held off to one side can lift faces after dark without killing the mood, but test it first and do not aim it at passersby. Most of all, keep moving. Light changes fast at blue hour, and the best frames tend to come from walking, not posing.

Book early: Holiday weekends fill up fast for local photographers. If you know you want a lights session, reach out in early fall and ask what evenings they have open.

Winter in El Paso is brief, and the lights only stay up for a few weeks. If you have been thinking about engagement photos, senior portraits or a family session, this is one of the most memorable times of year to do it. When you are ready to plan your own, browse more ideas on our portraits page.

Questions, answered

When are the downtown El Paso Christmas lights usually up?

In recent years the city's holiday lighting downtown has typically gone up around Thanksgiving and stayed into early January. Dates and hours change, so check the City of El Paso events calendar before you book a session.

Do I need a permit to take photos downtown in El Paso?

Casual portrait sessions on public sidewalks generally do not need one, but rules can differ for plazas, private properties and commercial shoots with lighting rigs. Ask the city or the property owner before you plan anything elaborate.

What time should a holiday lights session start?

Start about 30 to 45 minutes before sunset. You get warm natural light first, then the blue hour when the lights and the sky are balanced, which is usually the most flattering window.

Is this a good idea for senior pictures in El Paso TX?

Yes. A lights session gives seniors a festive, different look from the usual desert and mountain portraits, and it pairs well with a daytime session somewhere like the Franklin Mountains.
