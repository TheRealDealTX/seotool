The Senior Portrait Session Checklist: From Booking to Photo Day

Senior pictures are one of the few portrait sessions that are entirely about one person at a turning point. They end up on graduation announcements, yearbook pages, a grandparent's fridge and, years later, a slideshow at a reunion. A little planning goes a long way, so we put together a senior portrait checklist that walks seniors and parents through every step, from choosing a date to the drive home.

Use it as a starting point, then adjust for your school, your photographer and your own style. The checklists below are tickable, so you can work through them as you go.

Step one: timing and booking

The biggest scheduling mistake is waiting too long. Yearbook deadlines, graduation announcement printing and the busy fall season can sneak up quickly.

  • Find your school's yearbook photo deadline and any format requirements
  • Decide when you want announcements printed and work backward
  • Book your photographer two to four months ahead if possible
  • Pick a date with a backup in case of weather
  • Avoid scheduling right after a haircut, sports tournament or big trip
  • Look up sunset for your date and plan around the light

Many seniors book in the summer before senior year or early fall. Summer offers long evenings and green landscapes; fall brings cooler temperatures and, in many places, warmer colors. In the desert Southwest, fall and spring tend to be more comfortable than midsummer, when you will want to shoot right at sunrise or the last hour before sunset. Our golden hour photography guide explains why that last hour matters so much.

Step two: choosing a location

The best senior session locations say something about the person in the photos. Do you love the city, the outdoors, music, a sport or a particular place in your hometown? Some seniors choose one location that feels scenic and one that feels personal: a downtown street and the ball field, or a desert trail and a favorite coffee shop.

Not sure where to start? Our guides to senior pictures in Tucson and downtown Los Angeles senior photos show how pairing an urban spot with a natural one creates variety.

Check permissions: schools, stadiums, private businesses and some parks require permission or permits for photos. Ask well ahead, and confirm any fees or hours directly.

Step three: planning outfits

Outfits are where most of the pre-session energy goes, and for good reason. Here are senior pictures tips that work for almost everyone.

Plan two to four looks

  • Everyday: what you would wear on a great day at school, but a notch dressier.
  • Dressy: a dress, suit, blazer or something you would wear to a nice dinner.
  • Personal: a jersey, a band uniform, a letterman jacket, work boots, a favorite vintage find.
  • Optional: cap and gown, if you want graduation-style photos.

Choose colors that photograph well

Solid colors and soft textures photograph better than busy patterns or large logos. Earthy, muted shades tend to suit outdoor sessions, while richer colors pop against neutral city backdrops. Our guide to what to wear for engagement photos was written for couples, but the color and fabric advice applies just as well to senior sessions.

Check the fit

Try everything on a few days before. Sit, walk and lift your arms. Make sure nothing gaps, pulls or needs constant adjusting. Steam or iron every piece the night before.

Step four: hair, makeup and grooming

Look like yourself on your best day, not like someone else. That might mean professional hair and makeup or simply a fresh shave and clean nails.

  • Get haircuts about one to two weeks ahead, not the day before
  • Try any new makeup look in advance, ideally with a test photo
  • Keep skin care routine consistent; avoid new products the week of
  • Clean and shape nails, since hands appear in more photos than you think
  • Bring lip balm, a comb or brush, and blotting papers
  • Take out hair ties from wrists before each set of photos

Step five: props and personal touches

Props should be meaningful, not filler. A guitar, a camera, a skateboard, a basketball, a horse, a favorite book or a college sweatshirt can say more than any pose. A few ideas:

  • Sports gear or a team jersey
  • A musical instrument
  • A college acceptance shirt or pennant
  • A pet (confirm with your photographer and bring a handler)
  • A car or truck you have restored or love
  • Your class year in balloons or numbers

Limit props to one or two so the session stays focused on you.

Step six: the night before

  • Lay out each outfit with shoes and accessories on a hanger or in a bag
  • Charge your phone and download directions to the location
  • Pack a session bag: water, snacks, lint roller, safety pins, mirror
  • Confirm the time and meeting spot with your photographer
  • Get a good night's sleep and drink plenty of water

Step seven: session day

Arrive a few minutes early so you are not rushing. Wear the first outfit, and bring everything else. Expect the first 10 or 15 minutes to feel a little awkward; that is normal, and it fades. A good photographer will guide your posing and keep you moving.

For parents

Parents can be a huge help with outfit changes, holding bags and keeping track of time. But many seniors loosen up when parents are not watching every frame. Consider stepping back during portraits, maybe a short walk or a coffee nearby, and let your senior and the photographer work. Talk about this together before the session so nobody is surprised.

Step eight: after the session

Ask your photographer when to expect your gallery and how to submit yearbook photos in the required format. Plan your announcement order early, since printing can take a couple of weeks. Back up your gallery somewhere safe, and print a few favorites. Digital files are wonderful, but a printed photo is the one that actually gets looked at.

The quick senior portrait checklist

Here is the short version, for printing or pinning:

  1. Check yearbook deadlines and requirements
  2. Book a photographer and date, with a backup
  3. Choose one or two meaningful locations
  4. Plan two to four outfits that fit well
  5. Schedule hair and grooming a week or two ahead
  6. Pick one or two meaningful props
  7. Pack your session bag the night before
  8. Arrive early, relax and have fun

Senior pictures are a chance to capture who you are right now, before everything changes. Prepare the practical parts with this senior portrait checklist, and then let yourself enjoy the session. If you are planning portraits for your family as well, explore our portraits section for more ideas.

Questions, answered

When should seniors take their senior pictures?

Many seniors schedule sessions in the summer before or the early fall of senior year, which leaves time for yearbook deadlines and graduation announcements. Check your school's yearbook submission date and work backward from it.

How many outfits should a senior bring?

Two to four outfits is typical, depending on session length. A good mix is one casual look, one dressier look, and one that shows a sport, instrument or hobby.

Should parents come to senior portrait sessions?

Often, yes, especially for help with outfit changes and logistics. Many seniors relax more if parents step back during the session itself, so agree beforehand on how involved everyone will be.

What if my school has specific yearbook photo requirements?

Many yearbooks require a particular background, crop or format. Ask the yearbook advisor for the requirements early and share them with your photographer before the session.
