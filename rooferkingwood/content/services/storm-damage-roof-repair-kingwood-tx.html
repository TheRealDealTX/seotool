What Storms Do to Kingwood Roofs

Hail

Hailstones bruise asphalt shingles, cracking the fiberglass mat and knocking granules loose. The damage often looks like dark spots or shiny patches and can be hard to see from the yard. Soft metals tell the story too: dented roof vents, gutters, downspouts and AC fins are clues that hail hit the roof. Our post on how to spot hail damage shows what to look for.

Wind

High gusts break the adhesive strips that hold shingles down. Shingles may crease, lift and settle back, looking fine from the street while the seal is gone. Ridge caps, hip shingles and rake edges are the first to go. Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 left many Kingwood roofs with this kind of hidden damage.

Wind-driven rain and debris

Tropical systems push rain sideways into vents, flashing and window heads. Under Kingwood's canopy, storms also throw branches, pine cones and needles across the roof, scraping shingles and clogging valleys right when they need to drain. If a limb or a whole tree came down, our tree damage roof repair page covers that situation.

Right After the Storm

  1. Stay safe. Keep away from downed power lines and do not climb onto a wet, debris-covered roof.
  2. Take photos. Capture damage in the yard, on the house and inside, with dates.
  3. Limit further damage. Move belongings, catch drips and call for tarping if the roof is open.
  4. Get an inspection. A roofer can see what is not visible from the ground.
  5. Review your policy. Note deadlines and your wind and hail deductible.

Storm Damage Roof Repair in Kingwood: Our Process

We start with a free roof inspection. We walk each slope, check ridges, valleys, flashing and vents, look at soft metals, and go into the attic when we can. Every finding is photographed and dated so you have a clear record.

If the roof is open to the weather, we tarp it first. A properly secured tarp, fastened to battens and lapped over the ridge, keeps water out until the permanent work is done. Loose tarps held down with a few bricks tend to blow off in the next band of rain.

We also check the parts of the house that roofs drain into. Hail-dented gutters, crushed downspout elbows and split fascia boards are easy to overlook, and they belong in the same record as the shingles. So do skylights, satellite dish mounts and attic vents that took a hit.

Then we recommend a repair or a replacement based on what we found, not on what a claim might pay. Scattered wind damage on a younger roof can often be repaired. Hail bruising across multiple slopes usually calls for a full replacement, which our roof replacement page explains.

Rebuilding Stronger

A storm repair is a chance to improve the roof. Options worth considering:

  • Class 4 impact-resistant shingles, which some insurers recognize with premium discounts. Ask your agent.
  • High-wind nailing and sealed starter strips at eaves and rakes.
  • Ice-and-water barrier in valleys and around penetrations to resist wind-driven rain.
  • Trimming overhanging limbs before the next hurricane season.

If your village association requires approval for a new shingle color, start that paperwork early so it does not hold up repairs.
