Signs It Is Time for a New Roof

Age alone does not decide it. A 15-year-old roof under full sun can be in worse shape than a 20-year-old roof that faces north. These are the signs we look for during an inspection:

  • Brittle or cupped shingles across several slopes, not just one patch.
  • Granule loss that leaves dark, smooth spots and sand-like grit in the downspouts.
  • Repeated leaks in different rooms after each heavy storm.
  • Soft spots when walking the roof, a sign the decking is breaking down.
  • Heavy algae and moss on shaded slopes that has started lifting shingle edges.

What Older Kingwood Homes Hide Under the Shingles

Tear-offs on homes in Kingwood Forest, Trailwood or Woodland Hills sometimes turn up surprises. Some houses still have skip sheathing left from an original cedar shake roof, with gaps between boards that will not hold modern shingle nails properly. Others have plywood that has delaminated from decades of attic heat and humidity, or edges softened by gutters that overflowed with pine needles for years.

We inspect every sheet once the old roof is off. Damaged boards are replaced, and if a section needs to be re-sheathed we photograph it and explain the cost before we proceed. That way you know your new shingles are fastened to something solid.

Village Approval and Material Choices

Most Kingwood villages operate under deed restrictions, and a re-roof often needs architectural control committee approval of shingle color and style. Some associations keep an approved color list; others review samples. Rules differ from Bear Branch to Kings Mill, so check with your village association before you sign anything. We give you the manufacturer, product line and color name you need for the submittal, and we can wait to order materials until approval comes back.

How We Handle a Roof Replacement in Kingwood TX

  1. Inspection and measurements. We photograph every slope, check the attic and measure the roof.
  2. Written estimate. Material, underlayment, flashing, ventilation and a per-sheet decking price are spelled out.
  3. Approval and scheduling. Once your village signs off, we schedule around the weather forecast.
  4. Protecting the property. Tarps go over landscaping, AC units and pool equipment. Under a dense canopy we plan where debris and materials can land.
  5. Tear-off and dry-in. Old roofing comes off, decking is repaired, and synthetic underlayment goes down the same day.
  6. Installation. Drip edge, valley protection, new flashings, pipe boots and shingles are installed to the manufacturer's instructions.
  7. Cleanup. We clear gutters of tear-off debris and run magnetic sweeps across the lawn, beds and driveway.
Ventilation matters here: Many older Kingwood attics rely on a few turtle vents and soffits clogged with paint or insulation. A re-roof is the right time to balance intake and exhaust, which helps shingles last longer in Texas heat.

Building a Roof for Pines and Storms

Shade and debris shape how a roof ages in the Livable Forest. We pay extra attention to valleys, where pine needles pack in and hold moisture, and we recommend algae-resistant shingles on shaded homes to slow black streaking. Where branches overhang the roof, we suggest trimming them back before installation so limbs do not scuff new shingles in the wind. For hurricane season, we use the nailing pattern the manufacturer specifies for high-wind installation and seal the starter course at eaves and rakes.
