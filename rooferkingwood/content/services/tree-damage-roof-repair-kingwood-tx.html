How Trees Damage Kingwood Roofs

Hurricane Ike in 2008 and Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 both left trees on houses across Kingwood's villages. Tree damage generally falls into a few categories:

  • Whole-tree strikes. A pine snaps or uproots in saturated soil and lands across the house, often breaking rafters or trusses.
  • Falling limbs. Heavy branches dropped from 60 feet or more can punch through shingles and decking.
  • Abrasion. Branches resting on or rubbing against the roof wear away granules and shingles over months.
  • Debris loading. Needles, cones and leaves clog valleys and gutters, pushing water under the roof edge.
  • Critter access. Overhanging limbs give squirrels an easy path to vents and roof edges.

What to Do When a Tree Hits Your House

  1. Get everyone out of affected rooms. A damaged roof can shift, and ceilings can come down.
  2. Watch for power lines. If lines are involved, stay clear and call the utility.
  3. Photograph everything before anything is moved: the tree, the roof, the attic and interior damage.
  4. Call a tree service to remove the weight safely. Roofers should not cut trees off a loaded roof.
  5. Call us to tarp and assess as soon as the tree is cleared.
  6. Notify your insurance company and keep receipts for emergency work.
Do not climb up there: A roof with a tree on it may have broken framing you cannot see. Pine needles and wet bark also make the surface dangerously slick. Leave roof access to professionals.

Tree Damage Roof Repair in Kingwood, Step by Step

Secure the opening

Once the tree is off, we cover holes and broken sections with tarps fastened to battens so they survive the next band of rain. If the opening is large, we may frame a temporary support or cover first.

Assess the structure

Older homes in villages such as Kingwood Forest, Woodland Hills and Elm Grove were often framed with dimensional lumber rafters rather than engineered trusses, and a hard strike can crack one without any obvious sag from outside. That is why the attic check is never skipped. We go into the attic to look for cracked or split rafters, broken trusses, damaged ceiling joists and wet insulation. Structural damage may need a framing contractor or an engineer's review, and we will tell you plainly when that is the case. We photograph everything for your records and your insurer.

Rebuild the roof

After framing is repaired, we replace damaged decking, underlayment, flashing and shingles. On older roofs, matching shingles can be difficult, so we will discuss whether a section repair will look acceptable or whether a full slope or full roof replacement makes more sense. If your village association requires ACC approval for a different shingle color, we provide the product details for your submittal. Insurance and Tree Damage

Many Texas homeowner policies cover a tree that falls on the house during a storm, including roof repair. Tree removal coverage often has its own limits, and a tree that was dead or decayed before the storm may be treated differently. Policies vary, so read yours and talk to your agent. We document the damage and can meet your adjuster on site, but we are not public adjusters and we never waive deductibles. Our insurance claim help page explains how we support you.

Preventing the Next Strike

You cannot remove every tree, and in many villages you would not want to. Some simple steps lower the risk:

  • Have an arborist look at large trees near the house, especially ones leaning toward it or showing dead tops.
  • Keep limbs trimmed back several feet from the roof surface.
  • Clean valleys and gutters at least twice a year.
  • Check with your village association before removing large trees, since some have rules about tree removal.
