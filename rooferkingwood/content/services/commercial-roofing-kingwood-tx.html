Commercial Roof Types We Service

Low-slope and flat roofs

Most retail and office buildings use a low-slope membrane. Common systems include:

  • TPO single-ply membrane, a white, heat-welded sheet that reflects sunlight and is widely used across the Houston area.
  • Modified bitumen, a multi-layer asphalt-based system found on many older buildings.
  • Coated roofs, where a liquid-applied coating has been used to extend the life of an existing membrane.

Steep-slope commercial roofs

Many smaller offices, churches, daycares and clubhouses in Kingwood have shingle or metal roofs like a home. We handle those as well, including metal roofing for buildings that want a longer-lasting option.

Why Flat Roofs Behave Differently

A pitched residential roof sheds water in seconds. A low-slope roof moves it slowly toward a handful of drains, scuppers or gutters, so a single clogged outlet can leave thousands of gallons sitting on the membrane. Small defects that would never leak on a sloped roof can let water in when it stands for days. That is why commercial roof care is mostly about drainage, seams and penetrations, and why a building under a pine canopy needs more frequent attention than one in an open parking lot near US-59.

Common Commercial Roofing Problems in Kingwood

The issues we see most often on area buildings include:

  • Clogged drains and scuppers. Pine needles and leaves wash to the low points of a flat roof and block drainage. Water then ponds, adding weight and finding every weak seam.
  • Ponding water. Areas that hold water for days after rain wear out membranes and coatings faster.
  • Open seams and flashing. Heat cycling loosens seams and the flashing at parapets, curbs and walls.
  • Rooftop equipment. HVAC units, vents and conduit penetrations are frequent leak points, especially after service technicians walk the roof.
  • Storm damage. Hail can puncture or bruise membranes, and high winds can peel back edge metal. See our storm damage roof repair page for how we respond.
Check the drains first: After any storm, a quick look at roof drains and scuppers can prevent a ponding problem from becoming a ceiling leak in a tenant space.

Coatings

When the existing roof is dry and sound, a coating can add years of service at a lower cost than replacement. We will tell you honestly when a roof is a good coating candidate and when it is not.

Replacement

When a roof has reached the end of its life, we plan a replacement around tenants, customers and weather. That includes removal of the old system where needed, insulation, new membrane, flashing and edge details.

Maintenance plans
