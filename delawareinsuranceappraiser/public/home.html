Covered isn’t the same as paid

What a Delaware Insurance Appraiser actually does

Most disputed claims in the First State aren’t denials. They’re short: a roof approved for repair when the shingles can’t be matched, a water loss scoped for one room when the floor runs through three, a coastal home where wind damage was written off as flood. A Delaware Insurance Appraiser exists for the gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

When either side invokes the appraisal clause, Delaware Insurance Appraiser can be the appraiser you name. We inspect the property ourselves, price the repair for your town — Wilmington labor isn’t Seaford labor — and work line by line with the insurer’s appraiser until the numbers meet or an umpire decides.

  • A fresh scope, not an edited one. Our own measurements and photos come first.
  • Numbers built to hold up. An inflated estimate loses the umpire; a supported one moves the award.
  • Straight answers. If your claim needs an attorney or a supplement instead, you’ll hear it on the first call.

The appraisal roadmap

From short check to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here’s how Delaware Insurance Appraiser moves a file from the first call to the award.

Read the full roadmap
  1. 1

    Pin down the disagreement

    Coverage is accepted, but the amount isn’t. Gather the carrier’s full estimate, your contractor’s estimate, photos and the policy.

  2. 2

    Send the written demand

    Either side can invoke appraisal in writing, as the policy describes. Name your appraiser and keep proof of delivery.

  3. 3

    Both sides appoint appraisers

    Each party chooses a competent, impartial appraiser, usually within a window the policy sets (often 20 days).

  4. 4

    Appraisers select an umpire

    The two appraisers agree on a neutral umpire. If they can’t, a court can usually be asked to appoint one.

  5. 5

    Inspection and valuation

    Each appraiser inspects and builds or reviews a line-item estimate of the loss.

  6. 6

    Appraisers work the differences

    The appraisers meet, compare line items and agree on as much as they can.

Storm record

The storms behind Delaware property claims

Direct hurricane landfalls are rare here. Nor’easters, tropical remnants and summer storms are not. A Delaware Insurance Appraiser has to know how each one shows up on a roof, a wall and an estimate.

  1. 1962

    Ash Wednesday Storm

    A slow nor’easter lingered for several tide cycles in March, battering Rehoboth, Bethany and Fenwick and reshaping the coastline.

  2. 1985

    Hurricane Gloria

    Gloria raced up the coast in September, bringing wind damage and power outages across the state.

  3. 2003

    Hurricane Isabel

    Isabel’s surge and wind affected the Delaware Bay shore and coastal communities in September.

  4. 2010

    Back-to-back snowstorms

    February storms brought record seasonal snow to northern Delaware, with roof and ice-dam claims.

  5. 2012

    Superstorm Sandy

    Sandy flooded bayfront and inland-bay communities and damaged dunes, roads and beachfront property.

  6. 2020

    Tropical Storm Isaias

    Isaias produced tornadoes in Delaware in August, including a long-track twister through Kent and New Castle counties.

  7. 2021

    Remnants of Ida

    Ida’s rain pushed the Brandywine to record levels in Wilmington in September, flooding homes and businesses.

FAQ

Questions people ask a Delaware Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All questions Claim terms
What does a Delaware Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

How is an appraiser different from a public adjuster?

A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question — the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When is it worth calling a Delaware Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the insurer’s estimate, and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful — generally more than several thousand dollars or about 10%.

Is the appraisal award binding?

Yes, on the amount of loss, once any two of the three panel members sign. That’s why your documentation should be solid before appraisal is invoked.

What does appraisal cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work begins, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my part of Delaware?

Yes — all three counties. We regularly work in Wilmington, Newark, Bear, Middletown, Dover, Smyrna, Milford, Georgetown, Seaford, Lewes, Rehoboth Beach and Bethany Beach.
