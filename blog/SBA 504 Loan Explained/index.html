What is an SBA 504 loan? The 504 program explained

A 504 loan is the SBA's fixed-rate program for buying buildings, land and heavy equipment. It splits a project three ways and locks the SBA's share for up to 25 years.

How a 504 loan is structured

Every 504 project has three parts. A bank or credit union makes a first-lien loan for 50% of the project. A Certified Development Company, a nonprofit licensed by the SBA, funds 40% through a debenture the SBA guarantees. You put in 10%. The bank sets its own rate and term on its half; the CDC portion carries a fixed rate set at a monthly bond sale.

What a 504 loan can pay for

  • Buying land and an existing building
  • Constructing or renovating a building
  • Long-life machinery and equipment, including a car wash tunnel or a CNC line
  • Refinancing existing commercial real-estate debt, in some cases with cash out for business expenses

It can't pay for working capital, inventory, goodwill or a business purchase. Those go on a 7(a) loan, and many borrowers use both.

Today's 504 rates

The October 2026 pricing set the 25-year and 20-year effective rates at 6.97% and the 10-year at 6.59%, with manufacturers at 6.74% on 25 years. These include the SBA and CDC fees. Full history and how the blended rate works: SBA 504 loan rates.

Who qualifies

For-profit businesses with a tangible net worth under $20 million and average net income under $6.5 million after taxes for the past two years. The business must occupy at least 51% of an existing building it buys, or 60% of a new build, within a year. Owners of 20% or more guarantee the loan personally.

Questions

What is an SBA 504 loan?

A 504 loan is long-term, fixed-rate financing for buildings, land and heavy equipment. A bank lends 50%, a nonprofit Certified Development Company lends 40% backed by the SBA, and the borrower puts in 10%. The SBA portion is fixed for 20 or 25 years.

What is the current SBA 504 interest rate?

The SBA-backed portion priced in October 2026 at 6.97% effective for 25-year and 20-year debentures and 6.59% for 10-year, including fees. Manufacturers got 6.74% on 25 years. The bank sets its own rate on its half.

What is the difference between a 504 and a 7(a) loan?

A 504 is for fixed assets only and comes in two pieces with a fixed SBA rate. A 7(a) is one loan from one lender that can also cover working capital, inventory and business purchases, usually at a variable rate tied to prime.

How much can I borrow with a 504 loan?

The SBA portion is capped at $5 million, or $5.5 million for manufacturers and energy projects. With the bank's 50% on top, total projects of $12 million or more are common.

What is the down payment on a 504 loan?

10% for most borrowers. It rises to 15% for a start-up or a special-purpose property such as a hotel, car wash or gas station, and to 20% when both apply.
