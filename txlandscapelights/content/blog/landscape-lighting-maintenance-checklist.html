A good LED system can run for many years with very little attention, but "very little" is not zero. Texas yards grow fast, sprinklers leave mineral deposits, storms knock fixtures out of alignment and timers drift with the seasons. Regular landscape lighting maintenance keeps every fixture bright, balanced and aimed where the designer intended.

Below is a simple routine: a monthly five-minute check, a season-by-season checklist built around the Texas calendar, and clear signs that it is time to call a pro.

Monthly quick checks

Once a month, take a slow walk around the yard after dark. It takes about five minutes and catches small problems before they become bigger ones.

  • Every fixture turns on at the scheduled time
  • No fixtures are dark, dim, flickering or noticeably different in color
  • Nothing has been knocked over by mowers, pets, kids or contractors
  • Plants are not blocking path lights or covering uplights
  • No glare is shining into windows, the street or neighbors' yards
  • The transformer cover is closed and the outlet's in-use cover is latched

Seasonal landscape lighting maintenance for Texas

Texas seasons do not line up neatly with the rest of the country. Spring brings storms and explosive growth, summer brings heat and heavy irrigation, fall brings the time change and leaf drop, and winter brings the occasional hard freeze. Here is what to focus on in each.

Cleaning lenses: hard water and mineral deposits

In much of Texas, municipal and well water is hard. Every time a sprinkler hits a fixture, it leaves a thin film of calcium and other minerals. Over months, that film turns into a cloudy crust that can noticeably dim the light and scatter it into glare.

  1. Turn it off

    Switch off the system and give fixtures a few minutes to cool.

  2. Wipe the surface

    Use a soft cloth to remove dust, cobwebs and loose dirt from lenses and housings.

  3. Dissolve the deposits

    Apply a mild solution of white vinegar and water, let it sit briefly, then wipe gently. Avoid abrasive pads that can scratch glass or remove finishes.

  4. Rinse and dry

    Rinse with clean water and dry the lens so new spots do not form.

  5. Fix the cause

    Adjust or replace sprinkler heads that spray fixtures directly. This step does more than any cleaning product.

Tip: In-ground well lights collect leaves, mulch and grass clippings. Clear the top lens and the area around the fixture so heat can escape and water can drain.

Trimming plants around fixtures

Texas landscapes grow fast. A path light that was perfectly placed in October can be buried by spring. Trim shrubs and groundcovers so path lights stand clear, and keep foliage from pressing against uplight lenses. Remember that the goal is to light the plants, not hide the fixtures inside them. For trees, keep the canopy you are uplighting reasonably open so light reaches into the branches instead of stopping at the lowest leaves.

Re-aiming fixtures

Mowers, edgers, pets and storms all nudge fixtures out of position. A light that has tilted a few degrees can go from grazing a stone column to shining straight into a bedroom window. After dark, check each uplight and wall washer against the surface it is meant to light, and look for any glare from the street or your porch. Plants also change shape as they grow, so a tree that needed two uplights at installation may need them repositioned a few years later. Checking connections and wiring

Connections are the most common source of problems in an aging system. Moisture that reaches copper causes corrosion, which raises resistance and dims or kills fixtures downstream.

  • Look for exposed wire where mulch has washed away or beds were reworked
  • Watch for fixtures that flicker when you tap the stake, a sign of a loose connection
  • Note any section of a run that went dark all at once, which often means a cut or failed splice
  • Check the transformer terminals for discoloration, corrosion or loose screws
  • Make sure wire entering the transformer has a drip loop so water cannot run inside

Transformer timers after the DST change

Daylight saving time starts on the second Sunday in March and ends on the first Sunday in November. Mechanical timers and basic digital timers do not know that, so your lights may come on an hour early or late, and sunset itself shifts across the year. In much of Texas, sunset moves from around 5:30 PM in early winter to roughly 8:30 PM in late June.

The simplest fix is to replace an old timer with an astronomical timer, a photocell or a smart controller that adjusts automatically. If you keep a manual timer, add timer checks to your March and November routine. Transformer care

The transformer rarely needs attention, but a quick look twice a year is worth it. Open the cover with the power off and check for wasp nests, ant colonies and debris, all common in Texas. Make sure vents are clear and nothing is stacked against the enclosure. If the unit feels very hot during a summer evening or hums louder than it used to, it may be overloaded or failing. Adding fixtures over the years without checking capacity is a common cause, which our guide to transformer sizing explains.

Keep a simple maintenance log

A short log makes landscape lighting maintenance faster every year. Note the date of each seasonal check, any fixtures you re-aimed or replaced, lamp types and wattages, and the transformer tap used for each run. Take a few nighttime photos of the yard when everything is aimed just right. Next season, compare against those photos and you will spot drift immediately. The same records are useful after a storm or when you decide to expand the system.

When to call a pro

Most maintenance is easy DIY work. These signs point to something bigger:

Wiring

Whole runs go dark

Several fixtures failing together usually means a cut wire, failed splice or problem at the transformer.

Power

GFCI keeps tripping

Repeated trips point to moisture or a fault. Leave it off and have it checked. Outlet work needs a licensed electrician.

Voltage

Uneven brightness

Dim fixtures at the end of runs often need re-tapping, heavier wire or a hub layout.
