Lighting for Plano's established neighborhoods

Brick, stone and traditional elevations

Plano neighborhoods are known for masonry. Much of the housing stock from the 1980s and 1990s is red or tan brick with traditional, French country or Tudor-inspired details, steep roof pitches and arched windows. Willow Bend and parts of West Plano have larger custom homes in stone and stucco, while newer infill and Legacy-area homes lean toward transitional and modern farmhouse designs with painted brick, board and batten, and black window frames.

Brick and stone look best with fixtures placed close to the wall to graze the texture. Painted brick and lighter stucco need wider, softer washes to avoid hot spots. Modern farmhouse homes often look sharp with simple, symmetrical lighting on gables and porch posts.

Mature trees and North Texas landscaping

Established Plano yards often have live oaks, cedar elms, red oaks, bur oaks and pecans, plus plenty of crape myrtles along drives and walks. Bradford pears were planted widely in older subdivisions. Beds usually include boxwood, Indian hawthorn, dwarf yaupon, nandina and seasonal color, and many homeowners have added native and drought-tolerant plants in recent years.

Mature trees make the biggest difference at night. An oak with a strong trunk and wide canopy can be uplit from two sides for depth, and crape myrtles look great with close uplights that show off the bark. See our tree uplighting guide for the approaches we use most.

Hail

Spring storms

Collin County sees frequent hail. Solid metal fixtures and tempered glass lenses keep damage to a minimum.

Ice

Winter freezes

Ice-laden limbs can fall and knock lights out of aim. We place fixtures with likely limb drop in mind.

Soil

Expansive clay

Blackland clay shrinks and swells. Sturdy stakes and buried cable with slack keep the system steady.

Upgrading older systems

A lot of Plano homes came with landscape lighting from the original builder or a past owner. Those systems often use halogen lamps, undersized transformers and connectors that have corroded underground. We can test what is there, keep good wiring in place, and convert fixtures to LED. For a sense of the power difference, read our post on switching from halogen to LED landscape lighting.

HOAs and community guidelines

Many Plano neighborhoods have HOAs, and some, including gated sections of Willow Bend and Deerfield, have architectural guidelines covering exterior changes. Low-voltage landscape lighting is usually an easy approval when it is needed at all. We keep fixtures discreet, aim them onto your property, and use warm 2700K to 3000K color temperatures that complement brick and fit neighborhood standards. Deerfield is famous for its holiday lights, and a permanent system with the right zones can make seasonal decorating easier too.

Safety and everyday use

For many Plano families, lighting is as much about function as looks. Well-lit front walks and steps help guests and kids get to the door safely, a lit driveway makes late arrivals easier, and light along side yards and back gates discourages anyone looking for a dark spot. Good security lighting does not need to be bright. Even, low-glare light that removes deep shadows works better than a single harsh floodlight.

Plano sunset times

Sunset in Plano runs from roughly 5:20 pm in early December to about 8:40 pm in late June. That swing is too big for a basic timer. Use our sunset timer planner to see suggested on and off times, and consider a smart controller that tracks sunset and lets you change schedules from your phone.

A common setup in Plano is facade and tree lighting from dusk until about 11 pm, with path and entry lights left on until dawn. That keeps the home safe and welcoming without lighting the whole yard all night.
