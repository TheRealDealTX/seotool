Fort Worth character, lit the right way

Fort Worth neighborhoods have a feel all their own: tree-lined streets near the Trinity River, historic districts full of bungalows, and big stone homes on rolling lots west of downtown. Landscape lighting here should feel natural and warm, showing off the house and trees without turning the yard into a stage.

Craftsman porches to Austin stone

Fairmount, Ryan Place and Mistletoe Heights have some of the best collections of Craftsman bungalows and Prairie-style homes in Texas, with wide porches, tapered columns and wood siding. Rivercrest and Westover Hills are known for large estates in brick and limestone, many with traditional, Tudor or Mediterranean details. Arlington Heights and Tanglewood mix brick traditionals and ranch homes, and newer builds often use the cream Austin stone that is popular across North Texas.

For bungalows, we keep fixtures small and focus on the porch, columns and roofline. For stone and brick estates, we use grazing light to bring out texture and balanced washes to frame windows and entries.

Post oaks, pecans and North Texas trees

Fort Worth sits where the Cross Timbers meets the prairie, so post oaks and blackjack oaks are native here, along with live oaks, cedar elms, bur oaks and pecans. Older neighborhoods have big, mature canopies, while Rivercrest and Westover Hills have sweeping lawns that frame specimen trees. Crape myrtles, yaupon holly, nandina and Texas sage are common in beds.

Large oaks and pecans are perfect for uplighting at the trunk and moonlighting from above. Our tree uplighting guide explains how we choose beam spread and placement for big trees.

Built for North Texas storms

Tarrant County gets some of the strongest hail in the state, and ice storms can bring down heavy limbs. A good system expects both. We choose fixtures that can take a hit, keep transformers protected and accessible, and plan canopy lights where they can be checked safely. See our checklist for protecting landscape lighting from Texas storms.

Large lots, long drives and ranch-style homes

West Fort Worth and the areas around Westover Hills and Rivercrest include some of the largest residential lots in the Metroplex. Long driveways, circular drives and deep front lawns need a different plan than a city bungalow. We focus on guiding guests safely from the street to the front door, lighting the drive edges and entry, and picking out two or three specimen trees so the property reads well from the road without lighting every square foot.

Stone ranch homes with long, low rooflines look best when the light follows the horizontal lines of the house: soft washes on stone sections, gentle accents on columns, and warm light at the entry. On bigger properties, we also plan transformer locations carefully so long cable runs keep even brightness from the first fixture to the last. Choosing the right transformer size and wire gauge up front avoids dim fixtures at the far end of the drive.

Backyards on these lots often include pools, outdoor kitchens and patios with views of mature trees. We light them in layers so the space feels comfortable for a long evening outside.

Historic districts and neighborhood associations

Fairmount, Ryan Place and several other areas are historic districts, and many Fort Worth neighborhoods have active associations. Low-voltage landscape lighting is generally a landscape feature rather than a change to the house, but it is wise to check local rules first. We keep fixtures discreet, aim them onto your property, and use warm 2700K light that suits historic homes.

Sunset times in Fort Worth

Sunset in Fort Worth runs from roughly 5:25 pm in early December to about 8:40 pm in late June, a few minutes later than Dallas. Use our sunset timer planner to see suggested schedules, and let a smart controller follow sunset automatically.
