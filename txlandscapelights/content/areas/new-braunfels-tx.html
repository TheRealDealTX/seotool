Hill Country lighting for New Braunfels

New Braunfels sits right where the Texas Hill Country meets the plains, with the spring-fed Comal River running through town and the Guadalupe winding past Gruene. Homes range from historic German-era stone houses to new master-planned neighborhoods and acreage lots with long views. Landscape lighting here should feel at home in that setting: warm, natural and easy on the night sky.

Limestone, metal roofs and German heritage

New Braunfels has a strong building tradition. Historic homes near downtown and in Gruene include German-era limestone and fachwerk-influenced buildings, along with Victorian and folk cottages. Newer homes in Vintage Oaks, River Chase and Veramendi lean toward Hill Country style: cream and tan limestone, standing-seam metal roofs, deep porches and big windows facing the view. Modern farmhouse designs are common in newer subdivisions too.

Rough-cut limestone is one of the best surfaces to light. Grazing light close to the wall shows every ledge and color. Deep porches look best with soft light on columns and the entry, and metal roofs reflect light, so we aim carefully to avoid glare.

Live oaks, cedar and native landscapes

The New Braunfels landscape is shaped by live oaks, cedar elms, Texas red oaks, pecans along the rivers, and Ashe juniper (cedar) on the hills. Bald cypress line the Comal and Guadalupe. Gardens often feature Texas mountain laurel, Texas sage, agave, yucca, prickly pear and native grasses, partly because deer browse many traditional shrubs.

Big live oaks with wide, low canopies are ideal for moonlighting. Sculptural natives look striking with a single low accent. Oak wilt is present in Comal County, so we plan trenching and canopy mounting to avoid wounding oaks during the spring risk season. Our tree uplighting guide explains how we light oaks safely.

Water

Flash flooding

The Comal and Guadalupe can rise fast. Equipment stays above likely flood levels and away from drainage paths.

Rock

Limestone and caliche

Shallow rock limits trenching. We use conduit and surface mounts on stone.

Wildlife

Deer

Sturdy fixtures, buried cable and protected placement keep deer from knocking lights out of aim.

Heat

Long summers

Solid metal LED fixtures hold up to months of heat far better than plastic.

River country and flood risk

New Braunfels sits in one of the most flash-flood prone regions in the country, and the floods of 1998 and 2002 are still remembered locally. If your home is near the Comal, the Guadalupe or a creek, we look closely at the property's history and drainage before placing transformers or splices. Read our guide to protecting landscape lighting from Texas storms for what to check after heavy rain.

Dark skies and acreage lots

One of the best parts of living in the Hill Country is the night sky. On larger lots in Vintage Oaks and the surrounding county, lighting should guide guests and highlight a few features, not light up the hillside. We use shielded fixtures, warm 2700K light, downward aiming where possible and schedules that turn most lights off late at night. On acreage, we focus on the entry, drive approach, home and a few key trees.

Historic homes in Gruene and downtown

Historic stone homes near downtown and in Gruene deserve lighting that respects their age. We keep fixtures small and hidden in beds, use warm 2700K light that flatters limestone and old wood, and highlight a few details such as a stone chimney, porch or arched doorway rather than washing the whole facade. Low-voltage lighting sits in the landscape, so it adds no permanent changes to the house itself, though homes in historic areas may want to check local review rules before installation.

HOAs and community guidelines

Veramendi, Vintage Oaks, River Chase and many other New Braunfels communities have HOAs or property owners associations with architectural guidelines. Landscape lighting is usually welcome, and we provide a layout and fixture details for any review.

New Braunfels sunset times

Sunset in New Braunfels runs from roughly 5:35 pm in early December to about 8:35 pm in late June. Our sunset timer planner shows suggested schedules, and a smart controller can follow sunset automatically.
