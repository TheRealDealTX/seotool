Dallas homes deserve lighting that matches the architecture

Dallas has some of the most carefully kept residential streets in Texas. Big front lawns, mature trees and strong architecture make it a natural place for landscape lighting, and a well-designed system brings all of that forward after dark instead of leaving the house to a single porch light.

From Park Cities Georgians to M Streets Tudors

Dallas architecture rewards thoughtful light. The Park Cities and Preston Hollow are known for Georgian, French and Mediterranean estates in brick, limestone and stucco, plus a steady stream of large modern rebuilds. The M Streets are lined with 1920s and 1930s Tudor cottages with steep gables, arched doorways and decorative brick. Lakewood mixes Tudor, Spanish eclectic and traditional homes on rolling lots near White Rock Lake, and Kessler Park has hillside homes with stone and wood.

For Tudors, we often light the chimney, gable and entry rather than washing the whole facade. Formal Georgian homes look best with balanced, symmetrical lighting on columns and windows. Modern homes call for clean, crisp lines, often with integrated step lights and fewer, better-placed fixtures.

Live oaks, cedar elms and North Texas plantings

Dallas neighborhoods are known for their canopy. Live oaks, cedar elms, red oaks, bur oaks and pecans shade many older streets, and crape myrtles are everywhere. Yaupon holly, boxwood hedges, Indian hawthorn and Japanese maples fill out foundation beds, especially in the Park Cities and Preston Hollow.

Big shade trees are perfect for uplighting and for downlighting from the canopy, which casts natural moonlight patterns across the lawn. Crape myrtles have striking bark that looks great with a close uplight in winter after the leaves drop. Our landscape lighting techniques guide walks through the options.

Storm season in North Texas

Spring storms in Dallas can bring large hail, and the February 2021 winter storm reminded everyone how much damage ice can do to trees. Good lighting design plans for both. We keep transformers protected, choose fixtures that can take a hit, and place canopy lights where we can safely reach them for adjustment. Our post on protecting landscape lighting from Texas storms has a simple checklist for after a storm passes.

Upgrading older systems in established neighborhoods

Many homes in Preston Hollow, Lake Highlands and the Park Cities already have a landscape lighting system that was installed years ago, often with halogen lamps, tired transformers and connections that have corroded in the ground. Rather than starting over, we can test the existing wiring, keep what is still sound, and convert fixtures to LED. That usually cuts power use sharply and brings back consistent brightness across the yard.

HOAs, conservation districts and neighbors

Parts of Dallas, including several areas around the M Streets and Lakewood, are covered by conservation districts, and many neighborhoods have active homeowner or neighborhood associations. Landscape lighting usually does not require approval, but it should respect neighbors. We aim fixtures onto your property, avoid glare into windows and the street, and favor warm 2700K to 3000K light that complements brick and stone.

Timing your lights to Dallas sunsets

Sunset in Dallas ranges from roughly 5:20 pm in early December to about 8:40 pm in late June. That is more than three hours of seasonal change, so a fixed timer drifts quickly. Use our sunset timer planner to see the pattern, then let an astronomical timer or app-based controller handle it for you.

Many Dallas homeowners like the front facade lit from dusk until late evening, with a few path and entry fixtures left on overnight for security. Zoning makes that easy and keeps energy use low.
