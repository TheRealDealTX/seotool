San Antonio homes look their best after dark

San Antonio has a style of its own. Spanish Colonial and Mission influences, historic Victorian streets, and Hill Country limestone on the north side all give homes here real character. Thoughtful landscape lighting brings that character out at night and makes long evenings on the patio more comfortable.

Texas Landscape Lights designs and installs custom 12V LED systems for homeowners across Bexar County. We serve Alamo Heights, Olmos Park, Terrell Hills, Monte Vista, King William, Shavano Park, Stone Oak, The Dominion and surrounding neighborhoods. Each project starts with a free consultation and night demo, then a written proposal.

Stucco, tile and limestone

Alamo Heights, Olmos Park and Terrell Hills are full of Spanish Colonial Revival and Mediterranean homes with stucco walls, clay tile roofs, arched entries and wrought iron. Monte Vista has Tudor, Prairie and Craftsman homes alongside grand Mission Revival estates. King William is known for Victorian and Greek Revival homes near the river. North of Loop 1604, Stone Oak and The Dominion feature Hill Country and Tuscan styles built in limestone and stucco.

Stucco looks best with soft, even washes that avoid harsh hot spots. Arches, courtyards and ironwork come alive with small accent lights and gentle shadows. Limestone on north side homes takes grazing light beautifully, showing off the texture of each block.

Live oaks, mesquite and South Texas plantings

San Antonio's yards are shaped by live oaks, cedar elms, Texas red oaks, pecans and mesquite. Mesquite in particular has a twisting, lacy form that makes beautiful shadows on walls and lawns. Texas mountain laurel, Texas sage, agave, prickly pear, yucca and palms fill out many gardens, especially in xeriscaped yards.

Sculptural plants like agave and yucca look dramatic with a single low uplight. Big oaks are ideal for moonlighting from the canopy. Learn more in our guide to landscape lighting techniques.

Soil

Caliche and limestone

Rocky ground on the north side limits trenching. We route cable carefully and use conduit and surface mounts where needed.

Weather

Hail and flash floods

Spring storms can bring hail and fast-rising water. Durable metal fixtures and elevated transformers handle both.

Wildlife

Deer and heat

Deer roam many north side neighborhoods. Sturdy fixtures, buried cable and protected placement keep the system intact.

Heat and long outdoor seasons

San Antonio summers are long and hot, which makes evenings the best time to be outside. That also means fixtures sit in extreme heat for months. We use solid brass or heavy aluminum LED fixtures that hold up far better than plastic and use a fraction of the power of old halogen systems. Read the best landscape lighting for Texas heat for more on materials.

Courtyards, fountains and outdoor living

Spanish-influenced homes in San Antonio often turn inward, with courtyards, loggias, fountains and covered patios that become the heart of the house on warm evenings. These spaces need soft, layered light rather than bright floods. We use small uplights on columns and arches, gentle downlights from eaves or pergolas, and submersible or close accent lights on fountains and water walls.

Outdoor kitchens and seating areas get practical task lighting that is easy on the eyes, and steps or level changes get low step lights so guests can move around safely. On the north side, many backyards open to greenbelt or Hill Country views. In those cases we keep the light focused near the house so the view stays visible and the night sky stays dark.

For pools, we balance underwater light with soft lighting on surrounding plants and walls, so the pool feels like part of the garden rather than a bright blue rectangle. Pergolas and shade structures are also good places to mount small downlights that light a table without glare.

HOAs, gated communities and historic districts

The Dominion, many Stone Oak subdivisions and other gated communities have HOAs with architectural guidelines. Low-voltage lighting is usually simple to approve, and we can provide a layout and fixture details for your submission. Historic districts such as King William and Monte Vista may have additional review, so it is worth checking first. In every neighborhood, we aim lights onto your property and avoid glare toward neighbors.

Sunset times in San Antonio

Sunset in San Antonio runs from roughly 5:35 pm in early December to about 8:40 pm in late June, a little later than Houston or Dallas in winter. Our sunset timer planner shows suggested schedules by month, and a smart controller can follow sunset automatically.
