Lighting a home in the forest

The Woodlands was planned around its trees. Homes sit back among tall pines and hardwoods, streets curve through greenbelts, and the forest is part of every yard. Landscape lighting here should feel like moonlight through the trees, not a bright yard carved out of the woods.

Texas Landscape Lights designs and installs custom 12V LED systems for homeowners across The Woodlands, which lies mostly in Montgomery County with the Village of Creekside Park in Harris County. We serve Grogan's Mill, Panther Creek, Cochran's Crossing, Indian Springs, Alden Bridge, Sterling Ridge, Creekside Park, College Park and Harper's Landing, as well as gated communities like Carlton Woods. Every project begins with a free consultation and night demo, followed by a written proposal.

Homes set among the trees

Older villages like Grogan's Mill and Panther Creek have many 1970s and 1980s homes with wood siding, cedar shingles and contemporary rooflines tucked into the forest. Later villages such as Alden Bridge, Sterling Ridge and Creekside Park feature brick and stone traditional homes, often with Mediterranean or French country touches. Carlton Woods and other luxury sections have large estates in stone, stucco and brick.

Wooded lots change how we light a house. Facades are often partly hidden by trees, so we light the entry, a few strong architectural features, and the trees that frame the home, creating depth from the street rather than a flat wall of light.

Pines, oaks and shade gardens

Loblolly pines define The Woodlands skyline, mixed with water oaks, live oaks, sweetgums, magnolias, American hollies, yaupon and dogwoods. Under that canopy, gardens tend toward shade plants: azaleas, ferns, hydrangeas, Asian jasmine and native understory shrubs.

Moonlighting is the signature technique here. Fixtures mounted high in hardwoods shine down through branches, casting soft, shifting shadows on lawns, paths and driveways. Narrow-beam uplights can trace the straight trunks of pines, and small accents bring out azaleas and ferns along walks. Our tree uplighting guide explains both approaches.

Storms

Tropical weather

Hurricanes and tropical storms bring wind, falling limbs and heavy rain. We plan for easy re-aim and keep transformers high.

Debris

Needles and leaves

Pine needles and leaves pile on fixtures. Shrouded and raised fixtures stay clearer and are easier to clean.

Moisture

Humidity and shade

Shady, damp ground invites corrosion. Sealed LED fixtures and waterproof connections are a must.

Community standards and deed restrictions

The Woodlands is known for its covenants and design standards, which cover many exterior changes and are administered through a community design review process. The rules are there to protect the forest character, and good landscape lighting fits that goal. Requirements vary, so we recommend checking with your village or HOA before work begins. We can supply a lighting plan, fixture details and color temperature so any review goes smoothly.

Our design approach already lines up with the spirit of the community: warm 2700K light, shielded fixtures, downward aiming where possible, and nothing that glares into the street, a neighbor's home or the greenbelt. Many homeowners here prefer a lower light level overall, which suits the setting and keeps wildlife undisturbed.

Wooded drives and arrivals

Many homes in The Woodlands sit at the end of curving drives with the house partly hidden behind trees. After dark, that can make the address, driveway edges and front walk hard to see. We start with practical lighting for safe arrival: discreet path lights or low bollards along the drive, a soft glow at the entry, and light on the address or a mailbox area if needed. Then we layer in tree lighting so the home reads clearly from the street without breaking the forest feel.

Storms, drainage and protecting the system

The Woodlands sits in the path of Gulf storms, and Hurricane Ike in 2008 and Hurricane Beryl in 2024 both brought down trees across the area. Heavy rain also tests drainage near creeks like Spring Creek and Panther Branch. We mount transformers above likely standing water, avoid running cable through low drainage swales, and use fixtures that can be re-aimed easily after limbs fall. See our guide to protecting landscape lighting from Texas storms.

When it gets dark here

Sunset in The Woodlands runs from roughly 5:20 pm in early December to about 8:25 pm in late June. Under a thick canopy, though, yards can feel dark well before the official sunset. A smart controller lets you start a little earlier and still follow the season. Our sunset timer planner helps you choose a schedule.
