Landscape lighting for Round Rock homes

Round Rock has grown into one of the most popular family cities in Central Texas, with established neighborhoods along Brushy Creek, newer master-planned communities on the east side, and a historic downtown with limestone buildings. Landscape lighting helps homes here feel welcoming and safe, and it turns backyards into usable space on long warm evenings.

Texas Landscape Lights designs and installs custom 12V LED systems for homeowners across Round Rock, which sits mostly in Williamson County with a small part in Travis County. We serve Teravista, Forest Creek, Behrens Ranch, Paloma Lake, Vizcaya, Round Rock Ranch, the Brushy Creek area and older homes near Old Town. Each project begins with a free consultation and night demo, then a written proposal.

Limestone, brick and Hill Country style

Round Rock homes reflect its place between the Hill Country and the Blackland Prairie. Many homes in Forest Creek and Behrens Ranch are brick and stone traditionals from the 1990s and 2000s. Teravista, Paloma Lake and Vizcaya feature newer builder homes, often with cream limestone, stucco and Hill Country or Craftsman details. Near downtown, older homes and cottages sit alongside the historic limestone storefronts that give Old Town its character.

Limestone is a fantastic surface for grazing light, which brings out every edge and color shift. Stucco needs softer washes, and Craftsman porches look best with focused light on columns and entries.

Oaks, cedar and native gardens

Round Rock yards often feature live oaks, cedar elms, Texas red oaks, pecans and Ashe juniper, with Texas redbud, mountain laurel and crape myrtles as smaller accents. Many homeowners have shifted toward native and drought-tolerant beds with salvia, lantana, agave, yucca and ornamental grasses.

Live oaks are perfect for moonlighting and uplighting. Sculptural natives look dramatic with a single low accent light. Oak wilt is a concern across Central Texas, and many arborists advise avoiding wounding oaks from roughly February through June, so we plan trenching near roots and canopy mounting carefully. Central Texas weather

Round Rock gets the full range of Central Texas weather. Spring storms can bring large hail, Brushy Creek and its tributaries can rise fast during heavy rain, and summers bring weeks of 100-degree heat. Quality fixtures, sealed connections and smart placement make a big difference. Our guide to the best landscape lighting for Texas heat explains which materials last.

Established neighborhoods along Brushy Creek

Some of Round Rock's older subdivisions, including homes along Brushy Creek and in Forest Creek, now have mature trees, established beds and, in many cases, an aging landscape lighting system installed by a builder or previous owner. Those systems often use halogen lamps and older transformers that waste power and leave uneven light. We can test the existing wiring, keep what is still in good shape, and convert fixtures to LED. Homes that back onto the Brushy Creek trail or greenbelts also benefit from careful aiming so light stays on your property.

In newer east side communities, where trees are still young, we focus first on the facade, entry and paths, and use adjustable fixtures on trees so the design can grow as the canopy fills in over the next several years.

HOAs and community guidelines

Teravista, Paloma Lake, Vizcaya, Behrens Ranch and many other Round Rock neighborhoods have HOAs with architectural guidelines. Landscape lighting is generally welcome, but some associations want a quick review. We provide a plan and fixture details, use warm 2700K to 3000K light, and aim fixtures to avoid glare toward neighbors and the street. On golf course lots in Teravista, we also keep light from spilling onto the course.

Sunset times in Round Rock

Sunset in Round Rock runs from roughly 5:30 pm in early December to about 8:35 pm in late June. A smart controller follows the change and lets you adjust zones from your phone. Use our sunset timer planner to see suggested schedules.

Many families here run front lighting from dusk until late evening, with path and entry lights on until dawn. That keeps arrivals safe and the house welcoming without lighting the whole yard all night.
