Planning & design

Where should I start with landscape lighting?

Start with how you use the property at night: arriving home, entertaining, or simply enjoying the view from inside. A good plan usually covers the front facade, the walk to the door and one or two focal trees, then expands from there. Our process begins with a free consultation for exactly this reason.

How many fixtures does a typical home need?

It depends on the size of the facade, the number of trees and the length of walks and drives. A modest front yard might use 10 to 20 fixtures, while larger properties use many more. Our fixture estimator gives a rough planning count.

What color temperature looks best?

Most homeowners prefer warm white around 2700K, which flatters brick, stone and wood. Slightly cooler tones like 3000K can suit modern homes or bring out green foliage. See our color temperature guide and the warm vs. cool white comparison.

Can I see the design before I commit?

Yes. We bring sample fixtures for a night demo at your home so you see real effects on your own house and trees. You can also experiment with our lighting visualizer beforehand.

Will the lights bother my neighbors?

They should not. We aim fixtures carefully, use shielding and glare guards, and keep brightness at a level that highlights your property without spilling into windows next door.

How much electricity does LED landscape lighting use?

Very little. A typical LED fixture draws a few watts, so even a full system often uses about as much power as a couple of old incandescent bulbs. Try the energy cost calculator with your own numbers.

Is it worth switching from halogen to LED?

Usually, yes. LED uses a fraction of the energy, runs cooler and lasts far longer between replacements. The LED savings calculator and our halogen to LED guide explain the math.

Fixtures & technology

What is low-voltage landscape lighting?

It is a 12V system: a transformer plugs into a standard outdoor outlet and steps household power down to a safe low voltage for the fixtures. Low voltage is safer around people, pets and irrigation and easy to adjust as plants grow.

Are solar landscape lights a good alternative?

Solar lights are inexpensive but tend to be dim, inconsistent and short-lived, especially after cloudy days. For reliable, even light that highlights architecture and trees, a wired low-voltage LED system performs far better.

What fixture materials hold up best?

Solid brass and copper fixtures resist corrosion and develop a natural patina, which makes them a strong choice in humid and coastal areas. Quality powder-coated aluminum can work well in drier regions. Plastic fixtures generally do not last in Texas sun.

Can I control the lights from my phone?

Yes. Smart transformers and controllers let you set schedules, create zones, dim fixtures and switch lights on from an app. Learn more about smart lighting controls.

How do I know what size transformer I need?

Add up the total wattage of your fixtures and leave headroom, commonly around 20 percent, for voltage loss and future additions. Our transformer sizing calculator and sizing guide walk through it.

Texas weather & durability

Can landscape lighting handle Texas summer heat?

Quality LED fixtures and UV-resistant components are built for it. Heat is one reason we avoid cheap plastic housings and choose sealed metal fixtures. Read more in best landscape lighting for Texas heat.

What happens during hail, floods or freezes?

Well-built fixtures and waterproof connections handle most storms, though direct hail strikes or debris can damage lenses. We place fixtures thoughtfully and use sealed connectors to reduce the risk. See our tips on protecting lighting from Texas storms.

Does coastal salt air affect fixtures?

Yes. Salt and humidity accelerate corrosion on lower-grade metals and connections. Near the Gulf Coast we recommend brass, copper or marine-grade fixtures and fully sealed wire connections.

Will clay soil or limestone cause problems?

Expansive clay can shift fixtures and stress wire, and shallow limestone makes trenching harder. We adjust stake types, mounting methods and wire routing to suit your soil, which is common in North and Central Texas.

Installation

How long does installation take?

Most residential installations take one to three days, depending on size and site conditions. Our process page outlines a typical timeline from first call to final tuning.

Do I need an electrician?

Only if you need a new outdoor outlet or circuit. That line-voltage work is handled by a licensed electrician, while the 12V lighting system itself is our work.

Will installation damage my yard?

We use narrow trenches, route wire around roots and irrigation, and restore turf and mulch as we go. Most yards look undisturbed within a few days.

Does my HOA need to approve landscape lighting?

Some HOAs require approval for exterior changes, including lighting. Check your rules early, and we can provide a plan and fixture details to support your application.

Can lighting be added in phases?

Yes. We can size the transformer and wiring for future zones so you can start with the front yard and add the backyard, pool or trees later without starting over.

Repairs & maintenance

Why are some of my landscape lights out?

Common causes include a cut or corroded wire, a failed connection, a tripped outlet, a timer issue or a failed lamp. Our landscape lighting repair service diagnoses and fixes existing systems, including ones we did not install.

How often should landscape lighting be maintained?

A check once or twice a year keeps a system looking its best: cleaning lenses, re-aiming fixtures, trimming plants and checking connections. Use our maintenance checklist between visits.

Can you upgrade an older system instead of replacing it?

Often. If the wiring and transformer are sound, we can swap in LED fixtures or lamps, add controls and fill gaps in the design. We will tell you honestly when replacement makes more sense.

Do my timer settings need to change with the seasons?

A basic timer drifts as sunset times shift through the year. Astronomical timers and smart controllers adjust automatically, or you can use our sunset timer planner to set a schedule.
