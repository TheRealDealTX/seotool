HOA entrances and monument signs

A neighborhood entrance is a promise about what is inside. When the sign is dark, half the letters are lost in shadow, or one floodlight has failed and the other glares into traffic, residents notice. Prospective buyers do too.

We light monument signs so the letters read clearly from a moving car without hot spots or reflections. For raised or cut letters, two narrow-beam fixtures from each side usually give even coverage. Flat panels and engraved stone often look better with a wider wash from a low angle. Entry walls in limestone or brick get grazed to show texture, and the trees and palms around the entry are uplit to give the whole scene depth.

In communities around Frisco, The Woodlands and other master-planned areas, entrance lighting also has to match established design standards. We can document fixture finishes, color temperature and placement for board approval.

Wayfinding and safety

On commercial property, lighting is also a safety tool. Clear paths from parking to entries, lit steps and ramps and visible building numbers all help visitors and residents move confidently after dark.

Good wayfinding does not need to be bright. It needs to be consistent. Path lights with even spacing, step lights at every grade change and soft light on the entry door guide people naturally, while shielded fixtures prevent the glare that makes it hard to see.

How a commercial project comes together

Most commercial landscape lighting in Texas starts with a conversation about what is not working: a sign residents complain about, a dark walkway, or a property that looks tired next to newer competition down the road. From there we walk the site in daylight to map power, irrigation, plant material and traffic patterns, then return after dark to test fixtures on the features that matter most.

The written proposal lists fixture types, finishes, color temperature and locations, with a phased option when budgets require it. Phasing is common for HOAs: the entrance and monument sign in the first phase, interior medians and common-area trees later. Early conversations use typical planning ranges, and the written proposal firms up the numbers so a board has something concrete to vote on.

Scheduling around residents, tenants and business hours matters too. We plan trenching and fixture placement to keep walkways open, avoid disruption during peak traffic and coordinate with landscape crews so new beds and lighting go in together.

Durability in harsh conditions

Medians and entrance islands get the worst of Texas weather: full sun, reflected heat from pavement, irrigation overspray and the occasional vehicle. We use solid brass or heavy powder-coated aluminum fixtures with tempered glass lenses, set in drained gravel where flash flooding is a risk. On Gulf Coast properties, brass and sealed connections hold up best against salt air. In North Texas, recessed lenses and shrouds help fixtures survive hail.

Energy use and run time

Many commercial properties keep lighting on from dusk to dawn. LED fixtures make that affordable. A typical HOA entrance with a dozen or more fixtures may draw only a few hundred watts total. The energy cost calculator helps estimate what a given layout costs to run each month at typical Texas rates.

Dark-sky and light trespass

Several Texas cities and many HOAs have guidelines on glare and light trespass, and the Hill Country in particular has a strong dark-sky tradition. Shielded fixtures, warm 2700K or 3000K color and aiming light at surfaces rather than the sky keep properties within those expectations while still looking great.

Maintenance agreements

On a residential property, the owner usually notices a dead light within a day. On a commercial property, nobody may notice for weeks, and a dark monument sign or a failed walkway light becomes a liability. That is why we offer maintenance agreements for commercial clients.

  1. Scheduled night checks

    We inspect the system after dark on an agreed schedule, so failures and aim problems are caught early.

  2. Cleaning and re-aiming

    Lenses are cleaned, fixtures are re-aimed after landscaping work or growth and plantings that block light are flagged for the landscape crew.

  3. Controls and seasonal changes

    Timers and controllers are checked for correct schedules, especially after outages and daylight saving time changes.

  4. Written reporting

    Property managers get a summary of what was checked, what was fixed and any recommended upgrades.

Tip: Coordinate lighting maintenance with your landscape contractor. Many wire breaks and knocked-over fixtures on commercial sites happen during bed renovations, irrigation repairs or tree planting. Marking wire routes and sharing a simple lighting map avoids repeat repairs.

Retrofitting existing commercial lighting

Many older properties still have halogen or metal halide landscape lighting that draws far more power and fails more often. Converting to LED usually reduces energy use substantially and cuts down on service calls, which makes it one of the most practical commercial landscape lighting upgrades a Texas property can make. Our landscape lighting repair team can assess an existing system and recommend whether to retrofit or replace. The halogen to LED guide covers what to expect.

Line-voltage work, such as adding power at a monument sign or a new circuit for a transformer, is handled by a licensed electrician. The landscape lighting system itself runs at 12V, which simplifies installation around public walkways and water features.
