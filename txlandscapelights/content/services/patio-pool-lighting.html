A Texas backyard does most of its living after sunset, once the heat breaks. That makes patio and pool lighting in Texas less of a luxury and more of a basic need. The goal is simple: enough light to cook, swim and walk safely, soft enough to relax in and warm enough that the space feels like an extension of the house rather than a parking lot.

Texas Landscape Lights designs 12V LED lighting for outdoor living spaces as one connected system. Your patio, pool deck, trees and paths share a transformer and a timer, but each area gets its own zone so you can set the mood for a quiet evening or a full backyard party.

Think in layers, not fixtures

Most poorly lit patios have one bright floodlight mounted on the house. It flattens everything, blinds anyone facing it and leaves the edges of the yard pitch black. A better approach stacks three layers of light.

  1. Task light

    Focused light where hands are busy: grill tops, prep counters, bar tops and steps. Typically small downlights at 3 to 5 watts, aimed tightly.

  2. Ambient light

    Soft, general light that lets people see each other: bistro strings, pergola downlights, wall washes and moonlighting from nearby trees.

  3. Accent light

    Light that adds depth: uplit trees at the edge of the yard, a grazed stone wall, a bubbling water feature or a planter bed beyond the pool.

Lighting the far edges of the yard is what makes a patio feel larger at night. Without it, the eye stops at the edge of the deck and the space feels boxed in. Our tree and garden lighting service covers that outer layer.

Outdoor kitchens, pergolas and covered patios

Outdoor kitchens

Grill and prep areas need real task light. We often use 3000K here, slightly crisper than the 2700K elsewhere, so you can tell whether the chicken is done. Under-counter strips or hooded deck lights mounted on the face of an island light the floor and footing without shining into anyone's eyes. If the kitchen needs new outlets or circuits for appliances, a licensed electrician handles that portion.

Pergolas and patio covers

Pergolas are excellent lighting frames. Small downlights mounted on beams create pools of light on the table. On cedar or stained pine structures we use brass or copper fixtures that blend in, and on aluminum or steel pergolas a powder-coated finish matched to the frame. Uplighting the posts from below adds height and makes the structure visible from inside the house.

String and bistro lighting

Bistro strings are popular for good reason, they make a patio feel festive instantly. The difference between a string that lasts and one that sags and fails is support. We run strings on a stainless steel guide wire anchored to posts or the house, use commercial-grade sockets and LED lamps, and put them on their own zone so they can dim independently.

Pool decks and water features

Pool lighting is mostly about the space around the water. We light coping edges and steps for safety, graze stone or tile walls, uplight palms or trees around the deck and add accent lights to waterfalls, scuppers, sheer descents and bubbler fountains. Light on moving water creates sparkle that still water cannot match.

In-pool fixtures are typically installed by the pool builder or a licensed electrician. We coordinate color temperature with those lights so the water and the landscape look like one design rather than competing.

Fire pits and seating areas

A fire pit is its own light source, so the surrounding lighting should step back. We keep output low around fire features, often 1 to 2 watts per fixture, and use a very warm color so the flames stay the center of attention. Path lights on the approach and a single step light are often all that is needed. Fixtures stay a safe distance from the fire and out of the zone where people move chairs.

Seat walls, steps and hardscape

Seat walls, retaining walls and steps built from limestone, flagstone or pavers are ideal for integrated hardscape lights. These low-profile fixtures tuck under a wall cap and wash light across the face of the stone and the patio surface, so there is no visible fixture at all. They are easiest to install while the wall is being built, but many can be added later by lifting a cap stone. We typically use 1 to 3 watt hardscape lights at 2700K, spaced evenly, often 4 to 6 feet apart along a seat wall, so the line of light looks intentional rather than random.

Tip: Bugs are a real part of Texas evenings. Insects are attracted most to blue-rich light, so warm 2700K fixtures, aimed downward and shielded, draw fewer moths and beetles to the table. Placing a few accent lights out at the edge of the yard can also pull insects away from where you sit.

Patio and pool lighting in Texas, built for the weather

Outdoor living spaces face chlorine, salt water pools, sprinkler overspray, 100-degree concrete and Gulf humidity. We specify fixtures and connections accordingly.

  • Solid brass or copper near salt water pools and on Gulf Coast properties
  • Sealed, gasketed fixtures rated for wet locations around decks and water features
  • Transformers mounted away from splash zones, with clearances from the water
  • Wire routed through conduit or sleeves under pavers and decking before they are set
  • Drainage gravel under in-grade fixtures to handle flash flood rain events

If you are building a new pool or patio, bring us in before the pavers go down, since the best patio and pool lighting is planned alongside the hardscape. Running sleeves and conduit under hardscape during construction costs far less than cutting into finished stone later.

Controls that match how you use the space

Outdoor living spaces work best with zones. A common setup puts the kitchen task lights, the bistro string, the pool deck and the surrounding trees on separate channels. With smart lighting controls, you can create a dinner scene, a late-night swim scene and an all-off scene, then trigger them from your phone. Because LED fixtures use so little power, the energy cost calculator usually shows that running the whole backyard on a schedule costs only a few dollars a month at typical Texas rates.
