Study garden design

Study garden design for a calm place to work

Study garden design for garden offices, writing rooms and home studies: calm planting, framed views and quiet paths that make working from home a pleasure.

Discuss your garden

More of us now work from home, and many do it in a garden room, a converted outbuilding or a study looking onto the garden. Study garden design is about creating the right view and the right walk to work: a garden that helps you concentrate and gives you somewhere to think.

The view from the desk

The view from a study window is looked at for hours every day. It should be calm rather than busy: a framed picture of foliage, a tree that moves in the wind, a small area of still water. We plan these views on the drawing board from the position of the desk itself.

  • Use evergreen structure so the view is good in winter, when most work is done.
  • Choose plants with movement, such as grasses and multi-stemmed birch, for gentle interest.
  • Keep bright colour and busy areas out of the main sightline.

The walk to work

A garden office gives you a commute of twenty metres. Make it count. A stepping-stone path through planting, past a scented shrub by the door (sarcococca in winter, philadelphus in summer), helps separate home from work.

Twenty metres of garden can be the best commute in London.

Siting a garden room

If you are planning a new garden office, where it goes changes the whole garden. We look at light, privacy, the view back to the house and the route for power and data. A well-placed garden room becomes a focal point; a badly placed one dominates the garden. We work with garden-room suppliers and architects to get it right.

Quiet and screening

Planting absorbs sound and hides neighbours. A layered boundary of trees, shrubs and climbers is more effective, and far more attractive, than a tall fence. For a study garden this matters as much as anything else.

Questions

Study garden FAQs

Where is the best place for a garden office?

Usually at the end of the garden facing back towards the house, with good light and a view over planting. We assess light, privacy and services before recommending a location.

Which plants are best to look at from a study window?

Calm, evergreen structure with some movement: multi-stemmed trees, grasses such as hakonechloa, ferns and clipped shapes, with seasonal highlights from bulbs and hellebores.

Do I need planning permission for a garden room?

Many garden rooms fall under permitted development, but rules on size, height and use apply. We always recommend checking with your local authority or architect.
