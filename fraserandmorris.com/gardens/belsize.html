Town garden design

Town garden design for terraced and period houses

Town garden design for terraced and period London houses. We plan narrow, overlooked gardens that work as outdoor rooms, with planting that looks good from the window all year.

Discuss your garden

The long, narrow garden behind a terraced house is one of London's great design challenges. It is overlooked from both sides, often shaded at one end and seen in full from the upstairs windows. Town garden design is about making that shape work for the way you live.

Breaking up the long, thin plot

Left as a lawn with borders down each side, a town garden looks like a corridor. Dividing it into a sequence of spaces makes it feel wider and gives a reason to walk to the end. A terrace by the house for eating, a planted middle section, and a quiet seat or studio at the far end is a pattern that works for many homes.

  • Turn the path or the paving at an angle to widen the view.
  • Use hedges, pleached trees or a screen of planting to hide part of the garden from the house so it can be discovered.
  • Place the best seat where the evening sun lands, which is often not next to the back door.

Privacy without losing light

Neighbours' windows are the most common complaint in a town garden. Tall fences make the space darker and smaller. Instead we use the canopies of small trees, such as multi-stemmed amelanchier, field maple or crab apple, to screen sightlines from above while leaving the light at ground level. Pergolas planted with climbers do the same job over a seating area.

Planting for a view from the window

Town gardens are looked at far more than they are used, especially in winter. We plan the planting as a picture from the main windows: evergreen structure in clipped shapes, grasses that hold their form into winter, bulbs for early spring and a long run of perennials from May to October.

Design the garden for the view from the kitchen in February, and summer will look after itself.

Working with period houses

Victorian and Edwardian houses have their own language of brick, stone and iron. We choose materials that sit comfortably alongside them: reclaimed London stock, York stone, clay pavers, or a crisp contemporary contrast where the architecture invites it, as with a modern rear extension.

Front gardens

A front garden is the first thing visitors see. Even a tiny one can hold a pair of clipped evergreens, a scented climber by the door and room for bins and bikes hidden from view.

Questions

Town garden FAQs

How do you make a narrow garden look wider?

Divide it into rooms, run paving or paths on a diagonal, use horizontal lines in fencing and paving, and plant boundaries so the edges disappear behind foliage.

Can a small town garden still have a lawn?

It can, but in a shaded or heavily used town garden a lawn often struggles. Gravel, planting and generous paving are frequently a better use of the space, but we design around what you will actually use.

Do you design front gardens too?

Yes. Front gardens are often part of a whole-house project, with planting, paths, storage for bins and bikes, and lighting.
