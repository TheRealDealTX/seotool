Do you need a Wyoming Insurance Appraiser, or someone else?

Three common situations, three different first calls. Picking the right one saves time and fees.

A

Covered, but paid short

The carrier agrees the damage is covered. The argument is about slopes, siding, matching, prices or what’s missing.

Call a Wyoming Insurance Appraiser — this is exactly what appraisal decides.

B

Not sure what’s missing

You have the carrier’s estimate and a contractor’s bid, but you can’t tell where they differ or by how much.

Start with an estimate review.

C

Denied outright

The carrier says the loss isn’t covered, raised late notice, or a deadline to sue is close.

Talk to a Wyoming-licensed attorney first. Appraisal doesn’t decide coverage.

How it works

From first call to a signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into five defined steps. Here is how a Wyoming Insurance Appraiser moves a file through them.

  1. 1

    Free first look

    We read your policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos, then tell you whether a Wyoming Insurance Appraiser can actually help — or whether you need someone else.

  2. 2

    Our own scope

    We measure and photograph the property ourselves: every roof facet, every elevation, every room. No edited copies of the carrier’s numbers.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and Wyoming Insurance Appraiser is named as your appraiser inside the policy’s window.

  4. 4

    Panel work

    We meet the carrier’s appraiser, ideally on site, and settle every line item the evidence supports.

  5. 5

    Signed award

    Anything left goes to a neutral umpire. Two of three signatures set the amount of loss, and payment follows.

Storm history

The Wyoming storms that still shape claims

Long memories matter in claims work. Carriers price risk on this history, and a Wyoming Insurance Appraiser has to understand how each kind of event shows up in a building.

  1. 1949

    The Blizzard of ’49

    A January blizzard buried southeast Wyoming and the plains for weeks, stranding ranches and trains and killing livestock across the region.

  2. 1979

    Cheyenne tornado

    On July 16 a tornado crossed north Cheyenne near the airport, damaging hundreds of homes and businesses.

  3. 1985

    Cheyenne flash flood & hail

    On August 1 a stalled storm dropped heavy rain and hail on the city; the flood that followed killed twelve people.

  4. 1987

    Teton–Yellowstone tornado

    A violent tornado crossed the Teton Wilderness above 10,000 feet, flattening forest over miles of high terrain.

  5. 2005

    Wright tornado

    An August tornado struck the town of Wright in Campbell County, killing two people and destroying homes.

  6. 2020

    Mullen Fire

    The Mullen Fire burned more than 150,000 acres in the Medicine Bow National Forest and destroyed dozens of cabins and structures.

Answers

Questions people ask a Wyoming Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it is the wrong move.

All answers Claim terms
What does a Wyoming Insurance Appraiser do?

A Wyoming Insurance Appraiser values property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As the appraiser you name, we inspect, write an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present the leftover items to a neutral umpire.

When is it worth calling a Wyoming Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage has been accepted, you have the carrier’s estimate, and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful — as a rough guide, more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is an appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, yes, once any two of the three panel members sign. It does not decide coverage, interest or bad-faith questions.

How is appraisal different from hiring a public adjuster?

A public adjuster negotiates the whole claim, usually for a share of the recovery. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half the umpire. We put our fee in writing before work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Does Wyoming Insurance Appraiser cover my town?

Yes. We work in all 23 Wyoming counties, with frequent claims in Cheyenne, Casper, Laramie, Gillette, Rock Springs, Sheridan and Jackson.
