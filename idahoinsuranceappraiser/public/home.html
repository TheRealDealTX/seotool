Why owners call us

What an Idaho Insurance Appraiser does that an adjuster won’t

Most short claims aren’t denials. They’re estimates missing a roof plane, a matching wall of siding or the code items a rebuild actually needs. Idaho Insurance Appraiser exists for that space between “covered” and “paid in full.”

The adjuster works for the insurer. When you retain Idaho Insurance Appraiser, we start from our own inspection and our own measurements, and we price the repair your property needs in your part of the state, whether that’s a Boise bungalow, a Rexburg apartment block or a barn outside Jerome.

  • A fresh valuation, not an edit. Our own scope, never a marked-up copy of the insurer’s estimate.
  • Numbers built to hold. Priced so they survive an umpire’s scrutiny.
  • Honest triage. If you need an attorney or a simple supplement instead, we say so on the first call.

The process

From first call to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here is how Idaho Insurance Appraiser moves a file through it.

  1. 1

    Free claim review

    We read the policy, the insurer’s estimate and your photos, then tell you plainly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 2

    Independent scope

    On-site inspection, measurements and a line-item estimate priced for your part of Idaho.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is invoked in writing and Idaho Insurance Appraiser is named as your appraiser within the policy’s deadline.

  4. 4

    Panel work

    We work through the gap line by line with the insurer’s appraiser. Most disputes narrow here.

  5. 5

    Signed award

    Anything left goes to a neutral umpire. Two signatures set the amount of loss.

Right tool, right job

When appraisal fits, and when it doesn’t

Idaho Insurance Appraiser will point you elsewhere when appraisal isn’t the right lever. Here’s the short version.

Good fit

  • Coverage accepted, cost disputed
  • Repair-versus-replace disagreements
  • Matching and discontinued materials
  • Missing trades, code items or overhead and profit
  • Snow-load or hail scope disputes

Poor fit

  • Outright coverage denials
  • Bad-faith or other legal claims
  • Gaps too small to justify fees
  • A suit deadline that needs an attorney now
  • Disputes over what the policy means

FAQ

Questions people ask an Idaho Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All questions Claim terms defined
What does an Idaho Insurance Appraiser do?

An Idaho Insurance Appraiser values property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on the cost. As your named appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster manages and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When should I call an Idaho Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the insurer’s estimate, and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful, often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is the appraisal award binding?

Generally yes, on the amount of loss, once two of the three panel members sign it. That’s why documentation matters before you invoke appraisal.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee in writing before work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my part of Idaho?

Yes. We work in all 44 counties, from Coeur d’Alene and Lewiston to Boise, Twin Falls, Ketchum, Pocatello and Idaho Falls.
