Tour bus and sightseeing business loans

Scenic and Sightseeing Transportation, Land · NAICS 487110 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

Sightseeing companies borrow to buy vehicles and to buy other operators. That might mean a double-decker bus, replica trolleys, open-air vans, or a whole city tour business with its booking site and permits. Revenue depends on tourism, so lenders study seasonality and visitor trends in the market as closely as the company's own numbers. They also look at online booking channels, cruise and hotel partnerships, and whether the city permits that let you operate will carry over to a new owner.

Who qualifies

Operators of land-based sightseeing tours by bus, trolley, van, jeep and similar vehicles, including hop-on hop-off services, wine and food tours, and ghost or history tours using vehicles. Lenders check passenger carrier operating authority where required, local tour permits, driver licensing and insurance. Experience in tourism or passenger transport is important. Charter-only bus companies are classed differently, but are financed in much the same way.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for vehicles and acquisitions

Covers buses, trolleys and vans, or the purchase of an existing tour company including its brand, website, permits and vehicles, on a 10-year term. A seller note is common.

Express for a single vehicle

SBA Express loans under $500,000 fit adding one trolley or van, or refreshing vehicle wraps and audio systems, with a faster approval. Rates run slightly higher than standard 7(a).

Lines for the off-season

A working capital line carries payroll, insurance and maintenance through slow months until the peak season brings cash back in. Lenders usually require the line to be paid down each year.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Tour buses, replica trolleys and open-air vehicles
  • Buying an established sightseeing company and its permits
  • Vehicle refurbishment, wraps and onboard audio systems
  • Online booking software and ticket kiosks
  • Working capital for insurance and the slow season

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Single-vehicle purchases often range from $100,000 to $500,000, and buying an established tour operator can run $500,000 to $3 million. Vehicle loans usually run 7 to 10 years, and a garage or yard up to 25. Expect at least 10% equity, more if the market is highly seasonal or the business is new.

What lenders look at for a tour bus or sightseeing company

Seasonality comes first. Lenders map monthly revenue to see how many months must cover the full year's payments, and how the business handled past downturns in tourism. They look at booking channels: direct sales, online travel agencies, cruise lines and hotel desks, and the commission each takes. City permits, curb loading rights and route approvals can be valuable and must transfer in a sale. Vehicle age and specialty builds like trolleys, which can be hard to resell, also affect terms.

Questions about tour bus & sightseeing companies loans

Can I get an SBA loan to start a tour company?

Yes, though start-ups need tourism or passenger transport experience, a market study, permit approvals and usually 15% to 20% equity. Many first-time owners begin with one or two vans and expand. Buying an existing operator with booking history is often easier to finance.

Can SBA loans finance trolleys or double-decker buses?

Yes. Specialty tour vehicles are eligible 7(a) uses. Because trolleys and double-deckers have a narrow resale market, lenders may want more equity, a professional appraisal for used units and a strong record of seasonal ridership.

How do lenders handle a seasonal tour business?

They underwrite to annual cash flow and look at the off-season closely. Some lenders offer seasonal payment schedules or pair the loan with a working capital line. Off-season revenue from private charters, events or holiday tours strengthens the application.

Can I buy an existing sightseeing company with an SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can fund the vehicles, brand, website, booking contracts and goodwill. Lenders verify that city permits and operating authority can transfer, review three years of financials and check the fleet's condition. A seller transition period is common.

What are SBA rates for a tour bus company loan?

With prime at 7.00%, 7(a) loans over $350,000 are capped at 10.00% variable, and $250,001 to $350,000 at 11.50%. Smaller loans for one vehicle can be priced up to 13.00% for $50,001 to $250,000. Your rate depends on credit and collateral.
