Legal services business loans: SBA rates and eligibility

All Other Legal Services · NAICS 541199 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

NAICS 541199 covers legal businesses that are not law offices or title and settlement firms. That includes process servers, independent paralegal services, legal document preparers, patent agents and similar legal support companies. These are service businesses with few hard assets, so loans are usually modest 7(a) or Express loans for buying a firm, opening an office or adding staff. Lenders focus on recurring clients, mostly law firms and courts, and steady cash flow.

Who qualifies

Owners with experience in the work and any credential the state requires, such as a process server license or registration as a legal document assistant. Patent agents need registration with the US Patent and Trademark Office. Lenders like firms with repeat law firm or corporate clients. Law practices themselves fall under a different code but can also get SBA loans. Businesses that offer legal advice without a license are a non-starter for any lender.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for buying a firm

A 7(a) loan can fund the purchase of a process serving or paralegal company, including goodwill and working capital, on a 10-year term. Most of the value is goodwill, so the seller's client relationships matter.

SBA Express for small needs

Loans under $500,000 for office build-out, software, vehicles for servers or a new hire's salary ramp move quickly through Express. The rate is slightly higher than standard 7(a), but the paperwork is lighter.

Microloans for start-ups

A new process server or document preparation business that needs under $50,000 can approach a nonprofit SBA microlender. Many microlenders also offer coaching on pricing, bookkeeping and finding law firm clients.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Buying an established process serving or paralegal firm
  • Case management and e-filing software
  • Office lease build-out
  • Working capital while new clients ramp up
  • Vehicles for process servers

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Loans to legal support firms are usually $25,000 to $500,000, with business acquisitions at the higher end. Buyers and start-ups need at least 10% equity, and a seller note can help on a purchase. Because there's little collateral, the lender may take a lien on the owner's home if one is available. Terms run up to 10 years.

What lenders look at for a legal services business

With little equipment to secure the loan, lenders lean on cash flow and the owner's personal credit. They check client concentration closely, since losing one large law firm can cut revenue sharply. On an acquisition they want to know whether clients will stay after the seller leaves, and they may ask for a transition period or an earnout. Licensing, errors and omissions insurance and any complaints or regulatory actions also get reviewed.

Questions about legal support services loans

What businesses are in All Other Legal Services?

NAICS 541199 covers legal services other than law offices and title or settlement offices. Common examples are process servers, independent paralegal and legal document preparation services, patent agents who are not attorneys, and other legal support firms that work for lawyers, courts and the public.

Can a process server get an SBA loan?

Yes. Process serving companies are eligible for 7(a), Express and microloans. Most borrow to buy an existing firm, expand into new counties, or fund vehicles, software and working capital. Lenders want to see repeat clients and steady monthly revenue.

Can I buy a paralegal or legal document business with an SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can finance most of the purchase price on a 10-year term, with at least 10% equity from you. The lender will review client lists, revenue trends and how much of the business depends on the seller personally.

Can a law firm get an SBA loan?

Yes. Law offices are a separate code but are eligible for SBA loans. Attorneys use 7(a) loans to buy practices, buy out partners or fund office space. Lenders do not lend against expected contingency fees from pending cases.

What rate do small legal services loans carry?

With prime at 7.00%, SBA caps variable rates at 13.50% for loans of $50,000 or less and 13.00% for $50,001 to $250,000. Small service business loans are often priced close to those caps. Larger acquisition loans fall into lower tiers.
