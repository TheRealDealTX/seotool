Frozen food distributor loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Packaged Frozen Food Merchant Wholesalers · NAICS 424420 · Program that usually fits: 7(a) and 504

Frozen food wholesalers borrow for three things: cold storage, refrigerated trucks and working capital. A distributor buying frozen meals, seafood, desserts or vegetables from manufacturers and selling to restaurants, schools and grocers ties up a lot of cash in inventory and receivables. Most deals combine a 504 or 7(a) loan for a freezer warehouse with a working capital line. Lenders focus on customer concentration, gross margin, inventory turnover and the cost of running freezers and trucks.

Who qualifies

Merchant wholesalers and distributors of packaged frozen food that are for-profit and meet the SBA size standard for wholesalers, which is based on employees rather than revenue. Lenders want distribution or food-industry experience, any required food safety registrations, and supplier agreements in place. Brokers that never take title to goods are a different business and are viewed differently. Start-ups need at least 10% equity and are usually asked for more given the working capital needs.

Which SBA program fits

504 for a freezer warehouse

A 504 loan funds a cold storage building, blast freezers and racking at a long fixed rate, usually with about 10% down. The business must occupy at least 51% of an existing building it buys.

7(a) for trucks and acquisitions

A 7(a) loan finances refrigerated trucks, forklifts and warehouse equipment, or buying an existing distribution business with its routes and customer list, typically over up to 10 years for non-real estate assets.

CAPLines for working capital

SBA CAPLines and 7(a) working capital loans help carry inventory and receivables from restaurants, schools and grocers. Borrowing limits are usually tied to eligible receivables and inventory on hand.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Freezer and cold storage warehouse space
  • Refrigerated trucks and trailers
  • Racking, forklifts and blast freezers
  • Inventory and receivables financing
  • Buying an existing frozen food distributor

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Frozen food wholesaler loans typically run from $500,000 to $3 million, and owner-occupied warehouse projects using 504 can be larger. Equity is at least 10% for start-ups and changes of ownership, and 504 projects usually need 10%. Warehouses get up to 25 years. Trucks and equipment get up to 10 years. Working capital lines are reviewed yearly or set on shorter terms.

What lenders look at for a frozen food wholesaler

Lenders look at customer concentration first, since losing one school district or grocery chain can hurt a distributor badly. They review gross margins, inventory turns and spoilage, plus how energy and fuel costs have moved. Receivables aging matters because food service customers often pay slowly. Lenders check the cold chain: backup generators, temperature monitoring and food safety audit results. Supplier terms and any exclusive distribution rights also affect how much they will lend.

Questions about frozen food wholesalers loans

Can a food distributor get an SBA loan?

Yes. Merchant wholesalers that buy and resell frozen food are eligible for 7(a), 504 and CAPLines working capital loans. Common uses are cold storage, refrigerated trucks, inventory and acquisitions. Lenders want distribution experience and steady customer relationships.

Can an SBA loan pay for a cold storage warehouse?

Yes. A 504 loan is often the best fit for buying or building a freezer warehouse the business will occupy, with a long fixed rate on the CDC portion. A 7(a) loan can also finance real estate for up to 25 years.

What is the SBA size standard for a food wholesaler?

Wholesalers are measured by number of employees, not revenue. Most frozen food distributors that qualify as small businesses are well under the limit, but larger regional distributors should check the current SBA table for NAICS 424420.

Can SBA loans finance refrigerated trucks?

Yes. Reefer trucks and trailers can be financed with a 7(a) loan, usually over a term matched to the vehicle's useful life. Some borrowers bundle trucks with warehouse equipment in one loan. Lenders check the truck's age and mileage when setting terms.

Is there an SBA line of credit for wholesalers?

Yes. SBA CAPLines include working capital lines that advance against inventory and receivables. SBA Express can also be structured as a revolving line up to $500,000. Lenders set borrowing limits based on eligible receivables and inventory.
