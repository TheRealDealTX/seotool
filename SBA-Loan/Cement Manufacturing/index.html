Cement manufacturing loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Cement Manufacturing · NAICS 327310 · Program that usually fits: 504 and 7(a)

Cement manufacturing means producing portland, masonry and other hydraulic cements, usually by heating limestone and other materials in a kiln and grinding the result. It is one of the most capital-intensive industries there is, and most plants belong to large companies that exceed SBA size limits. Small borrowers in this code tend to be specialty or blended cement producers and grinding operations. For them, the SBA 504 program is the main tool, with lenders focused on permits, raw material supply and energy costs.

Who qualifies

Cement producers that meet the SBA size standard for this code, which is based on employee count including affiliates. Subsidiaries of large building materials groups do not qualify. Lenders want experienced plant management, environmental and air permits in good standing, and secure raw material sources. Ready-mix concrete producers and concrete block makers are different codes, even though they buy cement.

Which SBA program fits

504 for plant and equipment

Manufacturers can receive up to $5.5 million in SBA debenture per project, alongside a bank first lien. That suits kiln upgrades, grinding mills, storage silos and plant buildings.

7(a) for acquisitions and working capital

Up to $5 million for buying an existing producer, working capital or mixed equipment needs. Real estate portions can run 25 years. A 7(a) loan is also more flexible than 504 when working capital or goodwill is involved.

Energy project financing

504 projects that cut energy use, such as more efficient kilns or waste heat recovery, may qualify for the larger manufacturer and energy debenture limit.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Grinding mills, kilns and dust collection
  • Storage silos and loading systems
  • Plant buildings and land
  • Buying an existing specialty cement producer
  • Energy efficiency and emissions upgrades

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Cement projects that fit SBA programs often run $1 million to well above $5 million, combining a bank loan, an SBA 504 debenture and borrower equity. Expect at least 10% equity, and 15% to 20% for start-ups or special-purpose plants. Real estate and heavy equipment can run 20 to 25 years.

What lenders look at for a cement manufacturer

Lenders start with eligibility, since affiliation with a larger company can disqualify the business. Then they focus on environmental permits, emissions compliance and any pending regulatory changes that could require costly upgrades. Energy and fuel costs drive margins, so they review several years of financials and supply contracts. Raw material reserves or supply agreements, plant age, freight distances to customers and dependence on local construction activity all factor into the credit decision.

Questions about cement manufacturers loans

Can a cement manufacturer get an SBA loan?

Yes, if the company and its affiliates meet the SBA size standard for cement manufacturing. Most large cement producers exceed it. Smaller specialty, blended or grinding operations that qualify can use 504 and 7(a) loans for equipment, plants and acquisitions.

What is the largest SBA loan for a manufacturer?

A 504 project can include up to $5.5 million in SBA debenture for manufacturers, plus a bank first lien and borrower equity, so total project size can be much larger. A 7(a) loan is capped at $5 million.

Is a ready-mix concrete plant the same as cement manufacturing?

No. Ready-mix concrete and concrete block producers have their own NAICS codes. They're eligible for SBA loans too, and the underwriting is similar, focused on equipment, trucks, permits and local construction demand. Size standards differ by code.

What is the 504 rate for a manufacturing plant?

The 25-year all-in 504 debenture rate was about 6.97% in October 2026. It's fixed for the life of the loan on the SBA's portion, roughly 40% of the project. The bank's 50% share is priced separately.

How much equity does a cement project need?

A 504 project normally needs about 10% equity, rising to 15% for start-ups or special-purpose property, and 20% when both apply. Lenders may ask for more on a capital-intensive plant with permit or market risk.
