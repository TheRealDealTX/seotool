Ambulance service loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Ambulance Services · NAICS 621910 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

Most ambulance company loans are 7(a) loans to buy vehicles, stock them with equipment, or purchase an existing EMS operator. A modern ambulance with a stretcher system, monitor and radio is expensive, and the fleet wears out fast. Lenders focus less on the vehicles and more on who pays the bills: the mix of Medicare, Medicaid, private insurance and contract revenue, how quickly claims get paid, and whether the company holds service contracts with counties, hospitals or nursing homes.

Who qualifies

Private emergency and non-emergency ambulance operators that hold a state EMS license and the required vehicle permits. Lenders want owners or managers with real EMS operations and billing experience, plus Medicare and Medicaid enrollment in good standing. Volunteer and municipal squads organized as nonprofits or government units are not eligible for 7(a). Wheelchair van and non-emergency medical transport companies usually fall under a different code, but they finance the same way.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for fleet and acquisitions

The standard choice for buying an ambulance company, adding units, or refinancing expensive vehicle debt. Vehicles and equipment usually go on a 10-year term, which keeps payments manageable while receivables catch up.

SBA Express for one or two units

Loans under $500,000 can fund a replacement ambulance, a power stretcher or cardiac monitors with a faster lender decision. Express loans carry a slightly higher rate than standard 7(a).

504 for a station or garage

If you're buying or building a base with vehicle bays, crew quarters and a supply room, a 504 loan offers a long fixed rate with about 10% down.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Buying new or remounted ambulances
  • Cardiac monitors, power stretchers and loading systems
  • Buying an existing ambulance company and its contracts
  • Working capital to carry slow insurance receivables
  • Purchasing or building a station with vehicle bays

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Ambulance company loans commonly run $150,000 to $1.5 million, depending on fleet size. Start-ups and buyers of an existing operator need at least 10% equity, and lenders often want more for a new service with no billing history. Vehicle and equipment loans run up to 10 years, and real estate up to 25 years.

What lenders look at for an ambulance service

Expect the lender to dig into your payer mix and days sales outstanding, since a company that waits months for Medicare or Medicaid payments can run short on cash even when busy. They will ask for your state license, Medicare enrollment, billing compliance history and any audits or repayment demands. Contracts with counties or hospitals help a lot, but lenders check how long they run and whether they can be cancelled. Fleet age, maintenance records and crew turnover also come up.

Questions about ambulance services loans

Can I get an SBA loan for an ambulance?

Yes. A licensed private ambulance company can use a 7(a) or SBA Express loan to buy new or remounted ambulances and the equipment inside them. The vehicle usually serves as collateral, and the lender will judge the loan mostly on whether your billing revenue covers the payment.

How do I finance buying an existing ambulance company?

A 7(a) loan can cover the purchase price, the fleet, equipment and working capital in one loan on a 10-year term. You need at least 10% equity. The lender will review the seller's payer mix, contracts, licenses and Medicare billing history, and may want the contracts to transfer cleanly to you.

Can a non-emergency medical transport company get an SBA loan?

Yes. Wheelchair van and stretcher transport companies are eligible for 7(a), Express and microloans. Lenders focus on contracts with brokers, nursing homes or Medicaid managed care plans, because those agreements drive most of the revenue and can change on short notice.

What interest rate will an ambulance company pay?

With prime at 7.00%, the SBA caps variable 7(a) rates at 13.00% for loans from $50,001 to $250,000, 11.50% from $250,001 to $350,000, and 10.00% above $350,000. A multi-vehicle loan often lands in the lowest tier.

Can a volunteer ambulance squad get an SBA loan?

No. Volunteer squads organized as nonprofits and municipal EMS departments are not eligible for SBA 7(a) or 504 loans. They usually look to USDA community facilities programs, municipal bonds, grants or local bank loans instead.
