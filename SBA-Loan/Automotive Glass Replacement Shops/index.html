Auto glass shop loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Automotive Glass Replacement Shops · NAICS 811122 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

Auto glass shop loans are usually small and practical. They pay for mobile service vans, ADAS camera calibration equipment, glass inventory, or buying an existing shop with its insurance referral relationships. Most are 7(a) or SBA Express loans. Lenders focus on how much revenue comes through insurance networks and third-party administrators, how fast those claims pay, and whether the shop can keep up with calibration requirements on newer vehicles.

Who qualifies

Owners of windshield and auto glass replacement and repair shops, including mobile-only operations. Lenders want technicians or owners with glass installation experience, and many favor shops with recognized installer certifications. A lease that runs at least as long as the loan is expected for a fixed location. Start-ups can qualify with a plan, at least 10% equity and evidence of insurance network access or commercial fleet accounts.

Which SBA program fits

SBA Express for vans and tools

Express loans finance mobile service vans, glass racks, urethane tools and calibration targets with fast decisions. For an established shop adding a van or calibration bay, Express is usually the simplest route.

7(a) for buying a shop

A 7(a) loan covers the purchase price, glass inventory, vehicles and working capital when buying an established auto glass business. Lenders will ask whether the seller's insurance network approvals carry over to the new owner.

Microloans for start-ups

A single-technician mobile start-up may fit an SBA microloan under $50,000 for a van and tools. Microlenders are nonprofits and often accept newer businesses with limited credit history.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Mobile service vans and glass racks
  • ADAS camera and sensor calibration equipment
  • Glass inventory and supplies
  • Buying an existing auto glass shop
  • Working capital while insurance claims pay

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Auto glass loans usually range from $50,000 for a van and tools to $750,000 for a multi-van shop purchase. Equity injection is at least 10% for start-ups and changes of ownership. Equipment and vehicles are financed up to 10 years, but lenders may match shorter terms to vehicle life. Smaller loans fall into the higher rate-cap tiers.

What lenders look at for an auto glass shop

Lenders want a revenue breakdown between insurance work, cash customers, dealers and fleets, since insurance network pricing sets much of the margin. They check receivables aging to see how long claims take to pay. Calibration capability is a growing focus because many newer vehicles need cameras reset after a windshield change, and shops that can't do it lose work. Lenders also look at technician retention, warranty claims and how many vans are needed to hit projected volume.

Questions about auto glass shops loans

Can I get an SBA loan for an auto glass business?

Yes. Auto glass replacement shops, including mobile-only businesses, are eligible for 7(a), Express and microloans. Common uses are vans, calibration equipment, inventory and buying an existing shop. Lenders want installation experience and a clear source of jobs.

Can an SBA loan finance ADAS calibration equipment?

Yes. Calibration targets, scan tools and the space needed to perform static calibrations are eligible equipment and improvements. Lenders like to see that the investment will bring in calibration revenue or keep insurance network work.

Can I finance a mobile glass van with an SBA loan?

Yes. Service vans and glass racks can be financed with an SBA Express or 7(a) loan. Some owners compare the SBA rate with dealer or captive auto financing, which can be cheaper for vehicles alone.

How much does it cost to buy an auto glass shop?

It depends on revenue, the number of vans and technicians, and insurance relationships. Small mobile operations may sell for under $200,000, while established multi-van shops cost more. Lenders base the loan on cash flow and an independent valuation.

What is the rate on a $150,000 SBA loan?

With prime at 7.00%, a 7(a) loan between $50,001 and $250,000 has a maximum variable rate of 13.00%. Lenders can charge less, and borrowers with strong credit and collateral often do better than the cap.
