Home and building inspection business loans

Building Inspection Services · NAICS 541350 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

Inspection companies need little capital, so loans here are small. Most are 7(a), Express or microloans to start an inspection business, buy a franchise, add inspectors and vehicles, or acquire an established company with realtor referrals. Equipment like thermal cameras, moisture meters, drones and radon monitors adds up but rarely tops six figures. Lenders focus on the owner's licensing and referral base, and on how sensitive revenue is to home sales volume.

Who qualifies

Home and commercial building inspectors with the license or certification their state requires, plus errors and omissions and general liability insurance. Lenders like owners with construction, engineering or prior inspection experience. Franchise buyers can qualify if the brand is listed in the SBA Franchise Directory. Government building code inspectors are public employees, so this code mainly covers private firms that inspect for buyers, lenders and insurers.

Which SBA program fits

Microloans and Express for start-ups

A new inspector buying tools, software, a vehicle and marketing often needs under $100,000. Microlenders cover up to $50,000, and Express covers larger amounts quickly.

7(a) for acquisitions and franchises

Buying a multi-inspector company or a territory franchise fits a standard 7(a) loan on a 10-year term, including working capital. The lender will review the franchise agreement or the seller's referral sources before approving.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Starting a home inspection business
  • Buying an inspection franchise territory
  • Thermal cameras, drones and radon or mold testing equipment
  • Vehicles for added inspectors
  • Buying an established multi-inspector company

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Most inspection business loans run $25,000 to $300,000. A franchise start-up often sits under $150,000, while buying an established multi-inspector firm can reach several hundred thousand. Start-ups and acquisitions need at least 10% equity. Terms run up to 10 years, and loans under $50,000 carry the highest SBA rate cap.

What lenders look at for a building inspection business

Revenue follows home sales, so lenders ask how the business held up when mortgage rates rose and sales slowed. They want to see where jobs come from, whether from a handful of real estate agents or a broad mix that includes commercial, insurance and new construction inspections. Ancillary services like radon, sewer scope and mold testing raise revenue per job and help the application. Claims history and E&O coverage are checked, since one lawsuit can hurt a small firm.

Questions about building inspection services loans

Can I get an SBA loan to start a home inspection business?

Yes. A licensed inspector can use an SBA microloan or Express loan for equipment, software, insurance, a vehicle and marketing. You'll need a business plan, your license or certification, and at least 10% of the project cost as equity.

How much does it cost to start a home inspection business?

Many independent inspectors start for well under $50,000, covering training, licensing, tools, software, insurance and marketing. Franchises cost more because of fees and required territory investments. The lender will want to see your own budget, not an industry average.

Can I finance a home inspection franchise with an SBA loan?

Yes, if the franchise brand is on the SBA Franchise Directory. A 7(a) or Express loan can cover the franchise fee, equipment, vehicle and early working capital. The lender will review the franchise agreement and the brand's track record.

Do I need collateral for an inspection business loan?

Loans of $50,000 or less need no collateral under SBA rules. For larger loans the lender takes business assets and may ask for a lien on your home if equity is available, but a loan is not declined only for lack of collateral.

Is a commercial property inspection firm eligible?

Yes. Firms doing property condition assessments, construction draw inspections or insurance inspections are eligible. These businesses often have steadier revenue than residential inspectors because lenders and insurers order work year-round. Lenders still ask about licensing and E&O coverage.
