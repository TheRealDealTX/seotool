PR agency loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Public Relations Agencies · NAICS 541820 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

Public relations agencies sell staff time and relationships, so there's little equipment to finance. SBA loans in this industry mostly fund agency acquisitions, partner buyouts, hiring ahead of new accounts, or an office move. Most are 7(a) or Express loans. Lenders focus on retainer revenue versus project work, how long top clients have stayed, and whether the client relationships belong to the agency or to one or two people.

Who qualifies

For-profit PR, media relations and communications firms with owners experienced in agency work. Lenders like agencies with a few years of tax returns, retainer clients and a team beyond the founder. A senior PR professional starting an agency can qualify if they bring signed clients. Political consulting firms can qualify as businesses, but loan proceeds can't fund political activity, and lobbying-only firms get extra scrutiny.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for buying an agency

Most of an agency's value is goodwill. A 7(a) loan can finance the purchase or a partner buyout on a 10-year term, often paired with a seller note.

SBA Express for growth

Express loans under $500,000 cover hiring ahead of a new account, office build-out or software with a quick decision. The rate is slightly higher than standard 7(a), and lenders still want to see retainer revenue behind the request.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Buying an established PR agency
  • Buying out a retiring partner
  • Hiring staff ahead of new retainers
  • Office build-out or relocation
  • Media monitoring and analytics software

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

PR agency loans usually range from $50,000 to $1 million, with acquisitions at the top end. Buyers need at least 10% equity, and seller notes are common in agency deals. With little hard collateral, lenders may ask for a lien on personal real estate. Terms run up to 10 years.

What lenders look at for a PR agency

Lenders want a client list with revenue by account and contract type. Retainers with long-tenured clients look far better than project work that must be replaced each year. They check how much revenue comes from the largest client and whether it's in a volatile sector. On an acquisition, the key question is whether clients will follow the seller out the door, so lenders favor transition agreements and non-competes. Staff costs and utilization also get reviewed.

Questions about pr agencies loans

Can a PR agency get an SBA loan?

Yes. Public relations agencies are eligible for 7(a), Express and microloans. The most common uses are buying an agency, partner buyouts, hiring ahead of new accounts and office build-outs. Lenders underwrite mostly on cash flow and client stability.

Can I buy a public relations firm with an SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can finance most of the purchase price, including goodwill, on a 10-year term. You'll need at least 10% equity. Lenders review client contracts, retention history and the seller's role in client relationships.

Can an SBA loan fund a partner buyout at an agency?

Yes. Partner buyouts are an eligible 7(a) use. The remaining owners must show the agency's cash flow covers the new debt, and the lender reviews whether the departing partner's clients will stay. A seller note may also help.

How much down payment does an agency acquisition need?

Current SBA rules require at least 10% equity for a change of ownership. A seller note can sometimes count toward part of that if it meets SBA standby conditions. Many lenders want more equity when clients are concentrated.

Can a new PR firm get startup financing?

Yes, but lenders want proof of demand, such as signed retainers or letters of intent from clients. An experienced founder with committed clients can often get an Express or small 7(a) loan for early salaries and setup costs.
