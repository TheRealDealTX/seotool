Buffet and cafeteria loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Cafeterias, Grill Buffets, and Buffets · NAICS 722514 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

Buffet and cafeteria loans usually finance a restaurant purchase, a remodel, or kitchen and serving-line equipment. Many of these businesses are franchises, and buffets need large dining rooms and steam tables, so start-up costs run higher than a typical restaurant. Most deals use 7(a). Lenders focus on food cost and waste control, labor, and traffic, because a buffet's margin depends on volume. They are cautious with the segment and want proof that the location draws steady crowds.

Who qualifies

Owners and buyers of buffet restaurants, cafeterias and grill buffets that are for-profit and meet SBA size standards. Lenders want restaurant management experience, ideally in high-volume food service, plus health permits and a lease that covers the loan term. Franchisees need an approved franchise agreement the lender has reviewed. Start-ups can qualify but lenders usually want more than the 10% minimum equity, and a well-supported sales projection.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for purchase or build-out

A 7(a) loan covers buying an existing buffet, leasehold improvements, kitchen equipment, franchise fees and working capital, typically on a 10-year term. It is the main program for restaurant deals of almost any size.

504 for owning the building

Buffets in freestanding buildings can use a 504 loan to buy the property with a long fixed rate. Restaurant buildings may be treated as special-purpose property, which raises the equity requirement to about 15%.

Express for remodels and equipment

SBA Express suits steam tables, sneeze guards, hood systems or a dining room refresh under $500,000. It helps an operating buffet update without the longer process of a standard 7(a).

What borrowers use the money for

  • Buying an existing buffet or cafeteria
  • Franchise fees and build-out for a new location
  • Steam tables, hot wells and kitchen equipment
  • Hood, fire suppression and walk-in coolers
  • Dining room remodel and working capital

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Buffet and cafeteria loans typically run from $250,000 to $2 million. A franchise build-out sits at the high end, and buying an independent buffet sits lower. Equity injection is at least 10%, and lenders commonly ask 20% or more for new restaurants. Terms run 10 years for equipment and acquisitions and up to 25 years when real estate is included.

What lenders look at for a buffet

Lenders look hard at food cost as a percentage of sales, since waste on a buffet line eats margin quickly. They review covers per day, average check, and labor cost trends. For an existing restaurant, they want three years of tax returns and monthly sales to see whether traffic is growing or slipping. For franchises, they check the brand's unit performance and closure history. Restaurant equipment has low resale value, so many lenders also take a lien on the owner's home when equity allows.

Questions about buffets loans

Can I get an SBA loan to open a buffet?

Yes. Buffets and cafeterias are eligible for 7(a) loans. A new buffet needs a detailed business plan, restaurant experience and equity of at least 10%, though lenders often want 20% to 30% for a restaurant start-up. Franchise buffets are easier to finance when the brand has strong unit results.

Are buffets considered risky by SBA lenders?

Restaurants in general are viewed as higher risk, and buffets carry extra exposure to food cost and labor. Lenders still finance them, but they want strong operating history, conservative projections and enough collateral or equity to offset the risk.

Can an SBA loan pay for a buffet franchise?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can cover the franchise fee, build-out, equipment, opening inventory and working capital. The lender reviews the franchise agreement to confirm the franchisee keeps enough control to meet SBA eligibility rules. Expect the lender to look at the brand's record too.

How much does it cost to open a buffet restaurant?

Costs depend on size, location and whether you lease or build. A buffet needs a large dining room, serving lines and a full commercial kitchen, so total project costs often run well into six or seven figures. Lenders size the loan to projected cash flow.

What is the interest rate on a buffet loan?

With prime at 7.00%, a 7(a) loan over $350,000 is capped at 10.00% variable, and a loan from $250,001 to $350,000 at 11.50%. Restaurant loans are often priced at or near the cap. A strong borrower with good collateral may negotiate a lower spread.
