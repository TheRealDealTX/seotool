Barber shop loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Barber Shops · NAICS 812111 · Program that usually fits: 7(a), Express and microloans

Barber shop loans are usually small. Most are microloans, Express or small 7(a) loans to open a shop, buy chairs and build out the space, or buy an established shop from a retiring owner. The big question for lenders is how the shop makes money: barbers on commission, booth or chair rental, or a mix. They look at chair count, how full the book is, and whether the clients follow the owner or the location.

Who qualifies

Licensed barbers and shop owners. Most states require both a barber license for the owner or manager and a shop license for the location. Lenders want hands-on barbering or shop management experience and a lease that runs at least as long as the loan. A shop run purely on booth rental can still qualify, but lenders review the rental agreements and turnover closely. Start-ups qualify with a plan and equity.

Which SBA program fits

Microloans for new shops

Opening a two or three-chair shop often costs under $50,000. Nonprofit SBA microlenders fund these when a bank won't, and many add business coaching. A plan and a license are required.

SBA Express for build-out

Express loans under $500,000 cover chairs, stations, plumbing and buildout for a larger shop with a fast lender decision and a slightly higher rate. It suits owners adding chairs.

7(a) for buying a shop

Buying an established shop, or a building with the shop in it, fits a standard 7(a). Real estate can run up to 25 years. Equipment and goodwill run up to 10 years.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Barber chairs, stations and mirrors
  • Plumbing, electrical and build-out for a new location
  • Buying an existing barber shop
  • Booking software and point-of-sale systems
  • Working capital while a new shop builds clientele

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Most barber shop loans fall between $25,000 and $200,000. Start-ups and buyers need at least 10% equity, and lenders often ask for 15% to 20% for a brand-new shop. Equipment and build-out loans run up to 10 years. Because many of these loans are $50,000 or less, they sit in the SBA's highest rate tier.

What lenders look at for a barber shop

Lenders want to understand the revenue model. Commission shops show payroll and product sales, while booth-rent shops show steady rent with less upside, and the cash flow looks different. They ask how many chairs are filled, how long the barbers have stayed, and whether a few top barbers bring most of the clients. On a purchase, they want proof the clients will stay after the seller leaves. Lease terms, location visibility and parking also come up.

Questions about barber shops loans

Can I get an SBA loan to open a barber shop?

Yes. Licensed barbers can use SBA microloans, Express or 7(a) loans to open a shop. You'll need a business plan, your license, a lease, and at least 10% of the project cost in equity. Lenders like to see an existing client base you'll bring along.

How much does it cost to open a barber shop?

It depends heavily on the space. A small shop in a former salon may need only chairs, stations and minor updates, while a raw space needs plumbing, electrical and finishes. Get contractor bids and include several months of working capital in the loan request.

Can I buy an existing barber shop with an SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can finance the purchase price, equipment and working capital on a 10-year term. The lender will review the seller's revenue, how the barbers are paid, and whether key barbers plan to stay.

Do booth-rental barber shops qualify for SBA loans?

Yes. The shop owner's income is the booth rent, and lenders underwrite that rent like a lease income stream. They'll review rental agreements, vacancy history and how quickly empty chairs get filled. Signed agreements help.

What is the rate on a small barber shop loan?

With prime at 7.00%, the SBA caps variable rates at 13.50% for loans of $50,000 or less and 13.00% for $50,001 to $250,000. Many small barber shop loans price at or near those caps. A larger loan drops into a lower tier.
