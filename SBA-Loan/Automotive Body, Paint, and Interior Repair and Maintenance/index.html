Auto body shop loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Automotive Body, Paint, and Interior Repair and Maintenance · NAICS 811121 · Program that usually fits: 7(a) and 504

Collision repair is equipment and real estate heavy. A modern shop needs a paint booth, frame machine, welders, lifts and diagnostic and calibration tools for driver-assist systems. Many auto body loans finance buying an existing shop along with its building, which makes 7(a) or 504 a natural fit. Lenders focus on insurance company relationships, especially direct repair program agreements, along with technician staffing and environmental condition of the property.

Who qualifies

Collision, paint and auto interior repair shops with owners or managers who know the trade and the insurance side of the business. Lenders like I-CAR or OEM certifications and direct repair program agreements with insurers. The shop needs any required state or local repair facility registration and air permits for the paint booth. Start-ups qualify with experience and equity, though buying an established shop is far more common.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for buying a shop

Finances the business, equipment, working capital and the building in one loan. A blended term can stretch to 25 years when real estate is most of the deal.

504 for buildings and booths

Buying or building a shop, or adding a large paint booth, fits a 504 loan with a long fixed rate and about 10% down. Expect an environmental review first.

SBA Express for equipment

A frame machine, ADAS calibration system or new lifts under $500,000 can go through Express with a faster decision. The rate is slightly higher than standard 7(a), but approval comes faster for equipment-only requests.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Paint booths and mixing rooms
  • Frame machines, welders and lifts
  • ADAS calibration and diagnostic equipment
  • Buying an existing collision shop and its building
  • Working capital while insurer payments clear

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Auto body loans usually range from $150,000 to $2 million, with real estate deals at the top end. Buyers need at least 10% equity, and 504 projects usually need 10% down or 15% for start-ups and special-purpose buildings. Equipment runs up to 10 years and real estate up to 25 years.

What lenders look at for an auto body shop

Lenders ask what share of work comes through insurer direct repair programs and how dependent the shop is on one carrier. They look at cycle time, labor rates and technician headcount, since a shortage of skilled techs caps revenue. On real estate, expect a Phase I environmental review and sometimes more, because paint, solvents and old fuel tanks raise contamination concerns. Equipment age and whether the shop can repair newer vehicles with advanced safety systems also matter.

Questions about auto body shops loans

Can I get an SBA loan for an auto body shop?

Yes. Collision and paint shops are eligible for 7(a), 504 and Express loans. Common uses are buying an existing shop, purchasing the building, paint booths, frame machines and calibration equipment for newer vehicles. Lenders also review insurer relationships.

Can I finance a paint booth with an SBA loan?

Yes. A paint booth can be financed through a 7(a) or Express loan, or through a 504 loan when it's part of a building project. Installed booths are often treated as fixtures with longer terms.

Can I buy a collision shop and its building in one SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can combine the business purchase, equipment, working capital and real estate. When real estate is more than half the project, the blended term can stretch to 25 years, lowering the monthly payment.

Do auto body shops need an environmental report for an SBA loan?

Usually. SBA rules require environmental review for commercial real estate, and auto body shops are flagged as higher-risk uses. Expect at least a Phase I environmental site assessment, and possibly further testing. Budget time for it before closing.

What rate will an auto body shop loan carry?

With prime at 7.00%, 7(a) loans over $350,000 are capped at 10.00% variable. A 504 loan's SBA portion had a 25-year rate around 6.97% in October 2026, with the bank's share priced separately. Smaller loans carry higher caps.
