Bowling alley loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Bowling Centers · NAICS 713950 · Program that usually fits: 7(a) and 504

Bowling center loans usually finance the purchase of an existing center with its building, or a major upgrade like new pinsetters, lanes and scoring. Many centers have added food, bars, arcades and event space to grow revenue beyond leagues. Because the building is a big part of the value, these deals often use 7(a) with a long blended term or a 504 loan. Lenders focus on league participation, food and beverage sales, and the age and condition of the equipment.

Who qualifies

Owners and buyers of for-profit bowling centers, including family entertainment centers built around bowling. Lenders want hospitality or entertainment experience and, when alcohol is served, a liquor license that transfers or can be obtained. If the center earns money from legal gambling, such as gaming machines, that income must stay under one third of gross revenue. Bowling centers are special-purpose property, so 504 deals usually need 15% equity, or 20% for a start-up.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for buying a center

A 7(a) loan up to $5 million can include the building, lanes, pinsetters, goodwill and working capital. When the building is a big share of the price, real estate stretches the blended term.

504 for real estate and renovation

A 504 loan finances a building purchase, an expansion or a full remodel at a long fixed rate. Because bowling centers are special-purpose property, plan on about 15% equity.

Express for equipment

SBA Express covers scoring systems, string pinsetter conversions, arcade games and kitchen equipment under $500,000. It suits an operating center that wants to upgrade without a full refinance.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Buying a bowling center and its building
  • Pinsetters, lanes and scoring systems
  • Bar, kitchen and party-room build-outs
  • Arcade, laser tag or other attractions
  • Roof, HVAC and parking lot repairs

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Bowling center loans usually run from $750,000 to $4 million, depending on lane count and whether real estate is included. Equity injection is at least 10%, and 15% to 20% is common for special-purpose property. Real estate-heavy deals get up to 25 years, while equipment upgrades get 10 years or less.

What lenders look at for a bowling center

Lenders look at revenue by source: league bowling, open play, parties, food and beverage, and amusements. Strong league numbers give predictable income, while food and bar sales often drive profit. They check the age of pinsetters and lanes, since replacing them is a major cost. An appraisal values the building, but bowling centers often appraise below replacement cost because few buyers want them, so cash flow must cover the loan. Deferred maintenance on roofs and HVAC is a common issue.

Questions about bowling alleys loans

Can I get an SBA loan to buy a bowling alley?

Yes. Bowling centers are eligible for SBA 7(a) and 504 loans. Buyers often finance the building, equipment, goodwill and working capital together. Lenders want entertainment or hospitality experience and at least 10% equity, often more for special-purpose property.

How much does it cost to buy a bowling alley?

Prices vary with lane count, location, food and beverage sales and whether the building is included. Smaller centers in rural areas can sell for under $1 million, while modern entertainment centers cost much more. Lenders base the loan on cash flow and appraisals.

Can an SBA loan pay for new pinsetters?

Yes. Pinsetters, including string pinsetter conversions, scoring systems and lane resurfacing, are eligible equipment. Smaller projects fit SBA Express, while larger renovations can be part of a 7(a) or 504 loan. Lenders like to see how the upgrade cuts maintenance costs.

Do bowling alleys with a bar qualify for SBA loans?

Yes. Serving alcohol doesn't disqualify a bowling center. The lender will confirm the liquor license and check that food and beverage operations are profitable and properly insured. Gambling income above one third of revenue would make the business ineligible.

What is the interest rate on a bowling alley loan?

With prime at 7.00%, a 7(a) loan over $350,000 is capped at 10.00% variable. A 504 loan's debenture portion was about 6.97% fixed for 25 years in October 2026. Actual rates depend on the lender and your deal.
