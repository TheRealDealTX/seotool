Bakery business loans

Retail Bakeries · NAICS 311811 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

SBA loans for bakeries are usually small to mid-size 7(a) or Express loans. The money goes to deck and convection ovens, mixers, display cases, a retail build-out, or buying an established bakery from its owner. Margins are thin and the work is labor-heavy, so lenders look closely at food and labor cost as a share of sales, wholesale accounts that add steady volume, and whether the lease and location support enough foot traffic to cover the payment.

Who qualifies

Retail bakeries that bake and sell on site, including cake shops, bread bakeries, donut shops and cupcake stores. Lenders want baking or food-service management experience, a clean health inspection history and a lease that runs at least as long as the loan. Home bakers moving to a storefront can qualify, but cottage-food revenue alone rarely proves cash flow. Franchise bakeries are eligible when the brand meets SBA franchise rules.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for buying or building a bakery

Covers the purchase of an existing bakery, a full build-out with hood and grease trap, equipment and opening working capital on a 10-year term. Real estate can be added on a longer term.

Express for an oven or case

Loans under $500,000 close faster through SBA Express. Good for a new deck oven, a walk-in cooler or a second display case. Rates run slightly higher than standard 7(a), and the paperwork is lighter.

Microloans for small start-ups

Nonprofit SBA intermediaries lend up to $50,000, often with coaching, to new bakers who need a first commercial oven and a modest storefront. Some also fund farmers market and cottage bakers moving up.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Deck, rack and convection ovens, proofers and spiral mixers
  • Retail build-out with hood, plumbing and display cases
  • Buying an established bakery and its recipes and accounts
  • Walk-in coolers and freezers for wholesale production
  • Working capital for flour, butter and packaging inventory

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Most bakery loans fall between $75,000 and $500,000. An equipment upgrade might be $50,000 to $150,000, while a full build-out or the purchase of a busy shop often runs $250,000 to $750,000. Terms run up to 10 years for equipment and acquisitions, and up to 25 years if you buy the building. Plan on at least 10% equity, and more for a start-up.

What lenders look at for a bakery

Lenders compare food cost and labor to sales, and a bakery with combined prime costs well above 60% of revenue is a harder sell. They want to know how much comes from wholesale accounts like cafes and grocers versus walk-in retail, and how concentrated those accounts are. Build-out costs get scrutiny because hoods and grease traps are expensive and stay with the landlord. Expect questions on owner hours and staffing, since many bakeries depend on one head baker starting at 3 a.m.

Questions about bakeries loans

Can I get an SBA loan to open a bakery?

Yes. Start-up bakeries qualify for 7(a) loans and microloans, but lenders want a detailed business plan, real baking or food-service experience, and usually 15% to 20% equity. A signed lease, contractor bids for the build-out and equipment quotes make the application much stronger.

How much does it cost to open a bakery, and how much can I borrow?

A small storefront bakery commonly needs $150,000 to $500,000 for build-out, ovens, cases and opening cash. Lenders size the loan to projected cash flow and your equity. Many first-time bakery owners borrow $100,000 to $350,000 through 7(a).

What credit score do I need for a bakery loan?

The SBA does not set a minimum score, but most lenders want personal credit around 650 to 680 or higher for a 7(a) loan. Microlenders are more flexible. Recent late payments, collections or tax liens matter more than the score itself.

Can I use an SBA loan to buy an existing bakery?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can cover the price, equipment, recipes and goodwill, plus working capital. Lenders review three years of the seller's tax returns, the lease assignment terms and whether wholesale accounts will stay after the sale.

Can I finance bakery equipment without real estate collateral?

Often. The equipment itself secures the loan, and loans of $50,000 or less require no collateral under SBA rules. For larger loans the lender takes available business assets and may ask for a lien on personal real estate if there is a shortfall.
