Medical lab loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Medical Laboratories · NAICS 621511 · Program that usually fits: 7(a) and 504

Medical laboratory loans usually fund expensive analyzers, a lab build-out or the purchase of an existing clinical lab. Most are 7(a) loans, with 504 used when the lab buys its building. Lenders focus on where the specimens come from, how the lab gets paid, and how exposed it is to cuts in Medicare and insurer reimbursement. A lab with steady physician and nursing-home clients and clean billing gets much better terms than one built on a single test or referral source.

Who qualifies

Independent clinical, reference, toxicology, pathology and specialty testing labs that are for-profit and meet SBA size standards. The lab needs CLIA certification, any state lab licenses, and a qualified laboratory director. Lenders want owners with lab operations or healthcare business experience. Nonprofit and hospital-owned labs can't use 7(a). Lenders also screen for compliance problems, since past billing investigations or exclusion from Medicare can stop a loan.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for equipment and acquisitions

A 7(a) loan funds analyzers, molecular platforms, laboratory information systems and working capital, or the purchase of an established lab with its client base, typically over 10 years.

504 for a lab building

If the lab buys or builds its own space and occupies at least 51% of it, a 504 loan offers a long fixed rate on the CDC portion with about 10% down. It suits labs that have outgrown leased space.

Working capital lines

Labs often wait weeks or months for insurer and Medicare payments. A 7(a) working capital loan or an SBA CAPLine can bridge receivables while test volume grows, so payroll and reagent bills stay current.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Chemistry, hematology and molecular analyzers
  • Buying an existing clinical or reference lab
  • Lab build-out, ventilation and biosafety upgrades
  • Laboratory information and billing systems
  • Working capital to carry insurance receivables

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Medical laboratory loans typically run from $250,000 for equipment to $2 million or more for an acquisition or new facility. Equity injection is at least 10% for start-ups and changes of ownership, and lenders often want more for new labs. Equipment and acquisitions get up to 10 years, and owner-occupied real estate up to 25 years.

What lenders look at for a medical lab

Lenders want a breakdown of revenue by payer, test type and referral source, because a lab that depends on one large client or one high-margin test can lose half its income overnight. They review collections, denial rates and days in receivables, and they look for exposure to Medicare fee schedule changes. Compliance matters: lenders ask about billing audits, marketing arrangements with physicians and CLIA survey results. Equipment leases and reagent rental contracts are also checked, since they affect cash flow.

Questions about medical labs loans

Can I get an SBA loan to buy a medical laboratory?

Yes. A for-profit clinical or reference lab is eligible for a 7(a) loan to buy the business, including equipment, goodwill and working capital. The buyer needs at least 10% equity, a CLIA-certified operation and a qualified lab director, and lenders review the seller's payer mix and billing history closely.

Can an SBA loan finance lab equipment?

Yes. Analyzers, PCR and sequencing systems, centrifuges and lab software can be financed with a 7(a) or SBA Express loan, generally over up to 10 years. Some labs compare this with vendor reagent-rental deals, which bundle equipment into the cost of supplies.

Do I need CLIA certification before applying?

Lenders will want to see that the lab holds or is getting the right CLIA certificate and any state license before funds are released. For a start-up, the lender may approve the loan with certification as a condition, but it has to be in place before testing revenue starts.

How much can a medical lab borrow with an SBA loan?

A 7(a) loan can go up to $5 million, and a 504 project can be larger when real estate is involved. Most lab loans are sized to cash flow, so the real limit is how much debt the lab's collections can comfortably support.

What rate will a lab pay on an SBA loan?

With prime at 7.00%, the maximum variable 7(a) rate is 10.00% for loans over $350,000 and 11.50% for loans from $250,001 to $350,000. Strong labs with good collateral often get a lower spread than the cap.
