Pharmacy loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Pharmacies · NAICS 456110 (formerly 446110) · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

An independent pharmacy loan is a mainstream SBA product: a handful of national lenders specialize in pharmacy purchases and will finance the full price, including goodwill and inventory, for a licensed pharmacist. The lender's main questions are about reimbursement mix, PBM contracts and whether the store's prescription count is holding up.

Who qualifies

Pharmacists buying an existing store, usually from a retiring owner, are the core borrower. Start-ups are financed too, but less often. Lenders want your license, a few years of experience, the seller's prescription data and tax returns, and an inventory count at closing.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for a pharmacy purchase

Covers the purchase price, inventory, working capital and any remodel, up to $5 million, usually on a 10-year term.

7(a) for a start-up pharmacy

Funds build-out, dispensing equipment, opening inventory and 6 to 12 months of cash while the script count builds.

504 for the building

If the pharmacy owns its building, a 504 loan finances it at a fixed rate alongside the business 7(a).

What borrowers use the money for

  • Buying an existing pharmacy, including goodwill and prescription files
  • Opening inventory and working capital to cover reimbursement lag
  • Automation, compounding or packaging equipment
  • Remodeling or adding a drive-through
  • Buying the building

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Pharmacy purchases commonly run $500,000 to $3 million. Several lenders finance 100% of the price for qualified pharmacists; others ask for 10%. Terms are 10 years for the business and 25 for real estate. Lenders will review your PBM contracts and third-party payer mix before closing.

Questions about pharmacy loans

Can a pharmacist get an SBA loan to buy a pharmacy?

Yes. Pharmacy purchases are a well-established 7(a) use, and some lenders offer 100% financing to licensed pharmacists. The seller's prescription volume and reimbursement history drive the loan amount.

How much working capital does a pharmacy loan include?

Usually enough to cover 60 to 90 days of reimbursement lag plus opening inventory. For a store filling 200 scripts a day, that's often $150,000 to $300,000 on top of the purchase price.

What is the interest rate on a pharmacy SBA loan?

Loans over $350,000 are capped at prime plus 3, or 10.00% today. Pharmacy specialists often price lower because defaults in the sector are rare.

Can I finance the inventory with an SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can fund opening inventory and ongoing working capital. The lender will usually require an independent inventory count at closing to set the final amount.

Do I need to own the building to get a pharmacy loan?

No. Most pharmacy loans are for leased locations. The lender will require a lease, including renewal options, that runs at least as long as the loan term.
