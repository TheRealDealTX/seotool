Specialty auto repair loans: SBA rates and eligibility

All Other Automotive Repair and Maintenance · NAICS 811198 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

This category covers auto service shops that don't fit the main repair codes. Think rustproofing and undercoating, tire repair shops that don't retread, emissions testing stations and similar specialty services. Loans are usually small 7(a) or SBA Express loans for lifts, equipment and working capital, or to buy an existing shop. Lenders look at how steady the customer base is, whether the shop has fleet or dealer contracts, and environmental issues at the property.

Who qualifies

Owners of specialty automotive service shops that are for-profit and meet SBA size standards. Lenders want hands-on automotive experience, any state or local permits the work requires, such as emissions inspection station certification, and a lease that runs at least as long as the loan. Start-ups can qualify with a solid plan and at least 10% equity. General repair, body, glass, oil change and car wash shops have their own NAICS codes but get similar treatment.

Which SBA program fits

SBA Express for equipment

Express loans under $500,000 cover lifts, tire machines, diagnostic tools and spray equipment with a faster approval. They are a good fit for an established shop adding a service line or replacing worn equipment.

7(a) for buying a shop

A 7(a) loan finances the purchase of an established shop, including equipment, goodwill, parts inventory and working capital, usually over 10 years. Lenders often want the seller to stay on briefly to introduce key customers.

504 for the property

If you buy the building, a 504 loan gives a long fixed rate with about 10% down. Lenders will order an environmental review because of oils, solvents and chemicals used in the work.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Buying an existing specialty service shop
  • Lifts, tire and alignment equipment
  • Emissions testing and diagnostic equipment
  • Shop build-out and ventilation
  • Working capital and parts inventory

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Most loans in this category run from $75,000 to $500,000, and a shop purchase with real estate can reach $1 million or more. Equity injection is at least 10% for start-ups and changes of ownership. Equipment and acquisition loans run up to 10 years. Small loans of $50,000 or less carry the highest rate cap.

What lenders look at for a specialty auto shop

Lenders review tax returns and sales by service line to see whether revenue is concentrated in one contract or one seasonal service, like winter undercoating. They want to know how changes in vehicle technology or state inspection rules could affect demand. For any property with lifts, chemicals or underground tanks, lenders order an environmental screen and may require a Phase I report. Equipment is valued at resale, not cost, so it rarely covers the full loan as collateral.

Questions about specialty auto repair loans

What businesses fall under All Other Automotive Repair and Maintenance?

NAICS 811198 covers auto services not classified elsewhere, such as rustproofing and undercoating shops, tire repair shops that don't retread, and emissions testing. General mechanical repair, body shops, glass shops, oil change shops and car washes each have their own codes.

Can I get an SBA loan for an auto service shop?

Yes. Auto repair and service shops are eligible for 7(a), Express and 504 loans. Common uses are buying an existing shop, financing lifts and diagnostic equipment, and working capital. Lenders want automotive experience and a reasonable plan for repaying the loan from shop cash flow.

Will an SBA loan cover shop equipment?

Yes. Lifts, tire changers, balancers, alignment racks, emissions analyzers and compressors are all eligible. An SBA Express loan is often the quickest route for equipment under $500,000, with terms up to 10 years. Used equipment can be financed too if it has a reasonable remaining life.

Do auto shops need an environmental review for an SBA loan?

If real estate is part of the loan, the lender must review environmental risk, and auto service properties are higher risk because of oils, solvents and possible underground tanks. Expect at least an environmental questionnaire and often a Phase I environmental site assessment.

What rate will I pay on a small auto shop loan?

With prime at 7.00%, a 7(a) loan of $50,000 or less is capped at 13.50% and a loan from $50,001 to $250,000 at 13.00%. Larger loans have lower caps, falling to 10.00% above $350,000. Lenders can charge less than these caps for strong borrowers.
