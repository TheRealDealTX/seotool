Business support services loans: SBA rates and eligibility

All Other Business Support Services · NAICS 561499 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

NAICS 561499 is a catch-all for office support companies that don't fit document preparation, call centers, collection agencies, credit bureaus, repossession or court reporting. It includes firms that do real-time closed captioning, mail presorting and address bar coding, bar code imprinting and other back-office services for clients. These are service businesses with modest equipment, so loans are usually 7(a) or Express loans for acquisitions, equipment and working capital. Lenders focus on contracts and client concentration.

Who qualifies

For-profit support service firms with owners experienced in the specific service and the systems behind it. Lenders like businesses with multi-year contracts or repeat corporate clients. Start-ups qualify with a plan and equity, but lenders prefer founders who bring clients from a prior role. Firms that subcontract for government agencies are eligible, and lenders will review contract terms and renewal dates.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for acquisitions

Buying a support services company is often mostly goodwill. A 7(a) loan can finance that purchase, plus equipment and working capital, on a 10-year term.

SBA Express for equipment and software

Mail sorting machines, captioning software, servers and office build-out under $500,000 fit an Express loan with a faster decision. The rate is slightly higher than standard 7(a), but the process is simpler.

CAPLines for contract work

Firms that carry receivables on large contracts can use an SBA working capital line tied to those receivables. The SBA calls these CAPLines. Borrowing rises and falls with billings, which suits seasonal or project work.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Buying an established support services firm
  • Mail sorting, printing or bar code equipment
  • Captioning, transcription or workflow software
  • Working capital to carry contract receivables
  • Office or production space build-out

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Loans in this category typically range from $50,000 to $750,000. Acquisitions sit at the higher end. Buyers and start-ups need at least 10% equity, and seller financing is common on purchases. Because collateral is thin, lenders may take a lien on the owner's home. Terms run up to 10 years.

What lenders look at for a business support services firm

Lenders want to know how revenue is contracted: multi-year agreements, month-to-month work or one-off projects. They check how much revenue comes from the top three clients and when those contracts renew. For technology-driven services like captioning, they ask whether software or automation could undercut pricing. Staffing costs and employee turnover matter, since labor is usually the biggest expense. On an acquisition, client transition from the seller gets close review.

Questions about business support services loans

What businesses are in All Other Business Support Services?

NAICS 561499 covers office and business support services not listed elsewhere. Examples include real-time closed captioning, mail presorting and address bar coding, and bar code imprinting services. Call centers, collection agencies, credit bureaus, repossession and court reporting each have their own codes.

Can a business services company get an SBA loan?

Yes. Support service firms are eligible for 7(a), Express and microloans. The most common uses are buying an existing firm, equipment and software, and working capital to cover payroll while waiting on client payments. Lenders want tax returns and contracts.

Can I finance a business with mostly goodwill using an SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can finance goodwill in a business acquisition on a 10-year term. Because goodwill isn't strong collateral, lenders rely on cash flow, your equity injection, and any personal real estate you can pledge.

Can I use an SBA loan for receivables-based working capital?

Yes. The SBA's CAPLines program offers revolving lines of credit backed by receivables and contracts. It suits firms with large clients who pay on 30 to 90-day terms. Lenders will ask for a receivables aging report and copies of major client contracts before setting the line.

What rate does a $200,000 SBA loan carry?

With prime at 7.00%, a 7(a) loan from $50,001 to $250,000 is capped at prime plus 6.0, or 13.00% variable. Strong borrowers often negotiate below the cap. Loans above $250,000 fall into lower tiers, down to 10.00% over $350,000.
