Bus and transit company loans

Bus and Other Motor Vehicle Transit Systems · NAICS 485113 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

Private bus and transit operators use SBA loans mainly to buy vehicles, a maintenance yard, or another operator. Many of these companies run routes or paratransit service under contract with a city, county or transit authority, so lenders read the contract as closely as the financial statements. The key questions are how long the contract runs, whether it can be cancelled, how quickly the agency pays, and whether the fleet can be kept running and replaced on schedule.

Who qualifies

Privately owned companies that run fixed-route bus, shuttle, commuter, paratransit or demand-response service, often as contractors to public agencies. Lenders look for FMCSA operating authority where required, DOT safety records, drug and alcohol testing programs and insurance in force. Experience running a fleet matters more than general business experience. A company that relies on a single agency contract should show its renewal history.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for vehicles and acquisitions

A 7(a) loan finances buses, cutaways and paratransit vans, or the purchase of another operator and its contracts, usually on a 10-year term or the vehicles' useful life.

504 for a bus yard or garage

A 504 loan funds a maintenance garage, fueling area or parking yard with a long fixed rate, usually with 10% down. The yard must be at least 51% occupied by your own operation, and the debenture portion is fixed near 6.57%.

Working capital lines

SBA CAPLines and Express lines help bridge slow agency payments, startup costs on a new contract and payroll during ramp-up. Lenders often size them to eligible agency receivables and renew them each year.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Transit buses, cutaways and wheelchair-accessible vans
  • Buying a garage, maintenance shop or bus yard
  • Acquiring another operator and its service contracts
  • Working capital to mobilize a new agency contract
  • Fleet tracking, fare and dispatch systems

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Loan size follows the fleet. A paratransit operator adding vans may borrow $150,000 to $500,000, while a contractor buying full-size buses or another operator can reach $1 million to $5 million. Vehicle loans usually run 7 to 10 years, and a garage or yard up to 25 years. Expect 10% to 20% equity depending on contract strength.

What lenders look at for a bus or transit operator

The contract is the collateral that matters most. Lenders check the term, renewal options, termination for convenience clauses, and how many days the agency takes to pay. They review the fleet list with mileage and age, the maintenance program and the cost of replacing buses before the loan is repaid. Safety ratings, accident history and insurance costs all affect cash flow. Driver recruiting and wage pressure are a frequent concern, since a missed run can trigger contract penalties.

Questions about bus & transit companies loans

Can I get an SBA loan to buy buses?

Yes. Buses, cutaways and accessible vans are eligible 7(a) uses. Lenders usually match the term to the vehicle's useful life, often 7 to 10 years for new units and less for used ones. They will want quotes, the fleet plan and evidence of the work the vehicles will serve.

Will a lender count a transit contract as collateral?

Not as collateral in the strict sense, but the contract drives the decision. A multi-year agency contract with steady payment history supports a larger loan. Lenders discount contracts that can be terminated on short notice or that expire well before the loan is repaid.

How do I finance slow payments from a transit agency?

An SBA CAPLine or a 7(a) working capital line can carry payroll and fuel while you wait on agency invoices. Lenders size the line to receivables and may require payments to flow into an account they control.

What are SBA loan rates for a transit company?

With prime at 7.00%, 7(a) loans over $350,000 are capped at 10.00% variable, and loans from $250,001 to $350,000 at 11.50%. Many lenders price fleet loans below the cap when the contract is strong. 504 loans for a garage carry a fixed debenture rate near 6.57%.

Can I buy an existing bus company with an SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can fund the price, the fleet and working capital. Lenders check that contracts can be assigned to the new owner, review safety and maintenance records, and usually want at least 10% equity plus a transition period with the seller.
