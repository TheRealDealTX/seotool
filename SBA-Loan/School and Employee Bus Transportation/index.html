School bus company loans

School and Employee Bus Transportation · NAICS 485410 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

School bus contractors borrow mostly to buy buses, buy a bus yard, or buy out another contractor. Revenue comes from district and employer contracts, so the loan decision rests on those contracts: how many years remain, whether pricing adjusts for fuel and wages, and whether the district can walk away. Lenders like the steady, predictable payments that district work brings, but they watch fleet age closely, because a contractor that must replace many buses at once can run short of cash fast.

Who qualifies

Private contractors that transport students for school districts, charter and private schools, and companies running employee shuttles. Drivers must hold commercial licenses with passenger and school bus endorsements, and buses must pass state inspections, so lenders look for clean inspection and safety records. Lenders want fleet management experience and insurance with adequate limits. A family business passing to the next generation or a manager can also use SBA financing for the buyout.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for buses and buyouts

Covers new and used buses, the purchase of another contractor and its contracts, and working capital before the school year starts, usually on a 10-year term. Lenders often time disbursement so new buses arrive before the first day of school.

504 for a bus yard and shop

Funds a parking yard, maintenance garage or fuel island with a long fixed rate and typically 10% equity. The debenture portion is fixed near 6.97% for 25 years, which keeps yard payments steady.

Lines for summer and start-up gaps

CAPLines or Express lines carry payroll and repairs over summer and through the slow first payments of a new contract. Lenders usually size them to district receivables.

What borrowers use the money for

  • New and used school buses, activity buses and vans
  • Buying out another contractor and its district routes
  • Purchasing a bus yard, maintenance garage or fuel island
  • Camera, GPS and routing technology for the fleet
  • Working capital for summer maintenance and fall start-up

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

A small contractor adding buses may borrow $200,000 to $600,000, while acquisitions of route portfolios commonly run $1 million to $5 million. Bus loans usually run 7 to 10 years depending on age, and a yard or garage up to 25 years. Expect at least 10% equity, and lenders may want more if contracts expire early in the loan.

What lenders look at for a school bus contractor

Contract terms come first: length, renewal options, termination clauses, and whether rates adjust for fuel and driver wages. Lenders review the fleet list for age, mileage and state retirement rules, then model the cost of replacing buses during the loan. Driver shortages are a major concern, so they ask about recruiting, wages and how many routes went unfilled. Safety records, insurance premiums and the summer gap, when revenue drops but payments continue, all affect how much they will lend.

Questions about school bus companies loans

Can I get an SBA loan to buy school buses?

Yes. School buses are an eligible 7(a) use. Lenders usually match the term to the bus's remaining useful life and any state age limits, often 7 to 10 years for new units. A signed district contract that covers the buses makes approval much easier.

Can I buy an existing school bus company with an SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can fund the purchase of the business, its fleet, yard equipment and working capital. Lenders confirm that district contracts can transfer to the new owner and review three years of financials, safety records and the fleet replacement schedule.

How do lenders handle the summer when schools are closed?

They plan for it. Many lenders set up payment schedules that reflect seasonal cash flow, or pair the term loan with a working capital line. Some contractors fill summer with camps, charters and employee shuttles, which strengthens the application.

What are SBA rates for a school bus company loan?

With prime at 7.00%, 7(a) loans over $350,000 are capped at 10.00% variable, and $250,001 to $350,000 at 11.50%. A 504 loan for a bus yard has a fixed debenture rate near 6.97%, with the bank first lien priced separately.

Do I need a school district contract to qualify?

Not strictly, but it helps a great deal. Lenders size the loan to reliable cash flow, and multi-year district or employer contracts provide it. A start-up without contracts will need strong experience, letters of intent from districts and more equity.
