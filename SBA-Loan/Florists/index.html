Florist loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Florists · NAICS 459310 (formerly 453110) · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

A florist loan is almost always a small 7(a) or SBA Express loan: flower shops are retail businesses with modest fixed costs, so the typical ask is a shop purchase, a cooler and van, or working capital to carry inventory through Valentine's Day and Mother's Day. Lenders will want to see that the shop's wire-service and walk-in revenue is stable.

Who qualifies

Owners buying an established shop, and existing florists expanding into events or a second location. Lenders look for retail or floral experience, a lease that outlasts the loan, and seasonal cash-flow projections. Start-ups can qualify with a solid plan and 15% to 20% equity.

Which SBA program fits

SBA Express for small needs

Loans under $500,000 for a van, coolers or working capital close quickly through Express, at a slightly higher rate.

7(a) for buying a shop

Covers the business purchase, inventory, build-out and working capital on a 10-year term.

Microloans under $50,000

Nonprofit SBA microlenders fund very small florist start-ups when a bank won't.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Buying an existing flower shop
  • Walk-in coolers, display cases and a delivery van
  • Working capital for holiday inventory
  • Build-out of a retail space or design studio
  • Point-of-sale and online ordering systems

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Most florist loans fall between $50,000 and $350,000. Equity injection runs 10% to 20%. The SBA caps rates on loans of $50,000 or less at prime plus 6.5, so a florist borrowing a small amount pays a higher rate than a bigger business would.

Questions about florist loans

Can a florist get an SBA loan?

Yes. Flower shops are eligible for 7(a), Express and microloans. The most common uses are buying an existing shop, equipment like coolers and a delivery van, and working capital for the holiday rush.

How much can a flower shop borrow?

Most florist loans are $50,000 to $350,000. The SBA allows up to $5 million, but lenders size the loan to the shop's cash flow, and a single-location florist rarely supports more than a few hundred thousand dollars.

What is the interest rate on a florist loan?

With prime at 7.00%, a loan of $50,000 or less is capped at 13.50%, and a loan from $50,001 to $250,000 at 13.00%. Lenders often price small loans near the cap.

Do I need collateral for a flower shop loan?

Not necessarily. The SBA doesn't require full collateral, and loans under $50,000 need none. For larger loans the lender takes what's available, including equipment and sometimes a lien on your home.

Can I get an SBA loan to start a florist from scratch?

Yes, though start-ups need a detailed plan, floral or retail experience and 15% to 20% equity. A microloan from an SBA intermediary is often the realistic first step for a new florist.
