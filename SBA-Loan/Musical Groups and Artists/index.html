Loans for musicians and bands: SBA rates and eligibility

Musical Groups and Artists · NAICS 711130 · Program that usually fits: 7(a), Express and microloans

Music groups and artists earn money from live shows, recordings, licensing and teaching, and that income is lumpy. SBA loans for this code are usually small: microloans and Express loans for instruments, sound gear, a touring van or trailer, or a rehearsal and recording space. Lenders focus on whether the act runs as a real business, with tax returns, a booking history and income that comes from more than one source.

Who qualifies

For-profit bands, ensembles, wedding and event bands, and solo artists operating as a business with a tax ID and filed returns. Lenders want at least a couple of years of performance income and a credit history to match. Nonprofit orchestras, choirs and community ensembles are not eligible for 7(a). Loans can't be used to fund a speculative album or tour with no established revenue behind it.

Which SBA program fits

Microloans up to $50,000

Nonprofit SBA microlenders are often the best fit for a working band that needs gear, a van or recording costs and has limited collateral. Many also offer business coaching.

SBA Express for established acts

An event band or touring group with steady bookings can use Express for vehicles, equipment or a rehearsal space build-out, with a faster decision. Lenders expect two or more years of steady income.

7(a) for a studio or venue business

If the act also runs a recording studio or teaching operation, a 7(a) loan can fund build-out and equipment on a 10-year term. Lesson and studio income tends to be steadier than touring income.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Instruments, amplifiers and PA systems
  • A touring van or trailer
  • Rehearsal or recording space build-out
  • Recording and production costs for an established act
  • Working capital between touring seasons

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Most loans to musicians and bands are $10,000 to $150,000. Microloans top out at $50,000. Start-ups need at least 10% equity, and lenders usually ask for more when income is irregular. Equipment and vehicle loans run 5 to 10 years. Loans of $50,000 or less carry the highest SBA rate cap.

What lenders look at for a band or musical artist

Lenders want two to three years of tax returns showing performance income, plus booking calendars, contracts and any recurring work like residencies, weddings or corporate events. Income from several sources, such as gigs, streaming, licensing and lessons, reads as more stable than touring alone. Personal credit carries a lot of weight. For a group, lenders ask who owns the gear and the name, and every 20% or larger owner signs a personal guarantee.

Questions about musicians and bands loans

Can a band get an SBA loan?

Yes, if it operates as a for-profit business with a tax ID and income history. Most bands qualify for microloans or small Express loans for gear, vans and studio costs. Lenders want tax returns, booking records and personal credit from the members who own the business.

Can I get a loan for music equipment?

Yes. Instruments, PA systems, lighting and recording gear are eligible SBA loan uses. Small gear loans often come from SBA microlenders or as Express loans, with the equipment serving as part of the collateral. Lenders still look first at whether your income covers the payment.

Can an SBA loan pay for recording an album?

Sometimes, as part of a loan to an established act that can show the income to repay it. Lenders won't fund a speculative project whose repayment depends on the album succeeding. The loan is judged on your existing cash flow.

Can an orchestra or nonprofit ensemble get an SBA loan?

Nonprofit orchestras, choirs and community ensembles are not eligible for 7(a) or 504 loans. They usually rely on donors, grants, arts council funding or conventional bank lines of credit instead. A for-profit company that books or manages musicians is a different case and can apply.

What credit score do musicians need for an SBA loan?

The SBA sets no minimum score, but most bank lenders look for scores around the high 600s or better. Microlenders are more flexible and focus on your plan, income history and willingness to work with an adviser.
