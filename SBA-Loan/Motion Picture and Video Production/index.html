Video production business loans: SBA rates and eligibility

Motion Picture and Video Production · NAICS 512110 · Program that usually fits: 7(a)

SBA loans in this code go to production companies, not to individual film budgets. Typical borrowers make commercials, corporate and training videos, event and wedding films, or content for brands and streaming platforms. Loans fund cameras, lenses, lighting, editing suites, a studio build-out or the purchase of an existing company. Lenders focus on repeat clients and recurring revenue, because a production company that depends on landing the next big project is hard to underwrite.

Who qualifies

For-profit production companies with a few years of revenue history and owners who have run shoots and managed budgets. Lenders like client rosters with agencies, corporations or networks that order work repeatedly. Financing a single independent film whose repayment depends on future sales is generally speculative and not a fit. Businesses that produce content of a prurient sexual nature are ineligible for SBA loans.

Which SBA program fits

7(a) for equipment and acquisitions

Funds camera packages, post-production systems, studio build-out and working capital, or buying an established production company, on a 10-year term. It's the most common SBA loan for production companies with steady clients.

SBA Express for gear upgrades

Loans under $500,000 for cameras, lenses, drones and edit bays close faster through Express, at a slightly higher rate. Lenders often keep gear terms shorter than the 10-year maximum.

504 for a studio building

Buying a building for a sound stage or studio fits a 504 loan with a long fixed rate and about 10% down. A portion of the space can be rented out if the business occupies most of it.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Cinema cameras, lenses and lighting
  • Editing suites and color grading systems
  • Studio or sound stage build-out
  • Buying an established production company
  • Working capital between client payments

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

Loans to production companies usually range from $50,000 to $750,000, with studio purchases running higher. Start-ups and acquisitions need at least 10% equity. Gear depreciates quickly, so lenders often keep equipment loans to 5 to 7 years even though 7(a) allows up to 10. Real estate can run 25 years.

What lenders look at for a video production company

Lenders want revenue by client for the last two or three years, and they look for repeat business rather than one-off projects. They check how quickly clients pay, since agencies often pay 60 to 90 days out. Gear loses value fast, so lenders discount equipment as collateral and may want a lien on personal real estate. Expect questions about how much work the owner does personally and whether freelancers or staff could carry the business without them.

Questions about video production companies loans

Can I use an SBA loan to finance a movie?

Generally no. Funding a single film whose repayment depends on future distribution is considered speculative, and lenders won't underwrite it. SBA loans fit production companies with existing revenue that need equipment, studio space or working capital.

Can a video production company get an SBA loan?

Yes. Companies producing commercials, corporate videos, events or branded content are eligible for 7(a), Express and microloans. Lenders want a few years of tax returns and a base of repeat clients. Wedding and event videographers qualify too.

Can I finance camera equipment with an SBA loan?

Yes. Cameras, lenses, lighting and editing systems are eligible uses. Because this gear loses value quickly, lenders often set shorter terms and look mostly at your cash flow rather than the equipment's resale value. Microloans fit small kits.

Can I buy a production company with an SBA loan?

Yes. A 7(a) loan can finance the purchase price, equipment and working capital. You'll need at least 10% equity. Lenders review client contracts and whether key clients depend on the seller personally. Seller transition periods help.

What rate do video production loans carry?

With prime at 7.00%, SBA caps variable 7(a) rates at 13.00% for loans from $50,001 to $250,000 and 11.50% from $250,001 to $350,000. Loans above $350,000 are capped at 10.00%. Strong borrowers can negotiate lower.
