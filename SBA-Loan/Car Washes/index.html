Car wash financing with an SBA loan

Car Washes · NAICS 811192 · Program that usually fits: 7(a) or 504

Car wash financing through the SBA usually means a 504 loan, because a tunnel wash is a real-estate-heavy project with expensive fixed equipment. The SBA counts the wash equipment as long-life fixed assets, so it can go in the 504 at the same fixed rate as the building. Express and self-serve washes, which cost less, more often use a 7(a).

Who qualifies

Developers building a new express tunnel, and operators buying an existing wash, are the typical borrowers. Lenders want a site study with traffic counts, a signed equipment contract and, for a purchase, three years of car counts and revenue. Experience in the industry or a management agreement with an experienced operator helps.

Which SBA program fits

504 for a new tunnel wash

Funds the land, building and tunnel equipment at a fixed rate, with the bank taking a first lien on 50% and the SBA-backed CDC taking 40%. You put in 10%, or 15% for a start-up.

7(a) for a purchase or a smaller wash

A 7(a) loan up to $5 million covers the purchase of an operating wash, including goodwill, on a 25-year term when real estate dominates.

7(a) for equipment refresh

Replacing tunnel equipment or adding a membership system can be financed on a 10-year 7(a) loan.

What borrowers use the money for

  • Land and construction of a new express tunnel
  • Tunnel equipment, vacuums and pay stations
  • Buying an existing car wash
  • Converting a full-service wash to express
  • Working capital through the ramp-up year

Typical loan amounts, down payment and terms

New express tunnels typically cost $3 million to $7 million, which is why many use a 504 paired with a bank loan. Existing wash purchases range from $750,000 to $4 million. Equity is 10% to 15% on a 504 and 10% to 20% on a 7(a). Lenders will cap the loan at what projected car counts support.

Example: a $2,000,000 loan

At today's maximum variable 7(a) rate of 10.00% over 25 years, the payment would be about $18,174 a month. Most well-qualified borrowers get a lower rate. On a 504 project, the SBA-backed 40% would be fixed at 6.57% (November 2026, 25-year). Try your own numbers.

Questions about car wash financings

Can I get an SBA loan to build a car wash?

Yes. A 504 loan is the usual tool for a new build because the land, building and tunnel equipment all qualify as fixed assets. Start-ups need 15% equity instead of 10%, and lenders will want a feasibility study from a car wash consultant.

What down payment does car wash financing need?

10% for a 504 project on an existing wash, 15% for a new build, and 10% to 20% for a 7(a) purchase. Land you already own can count as equity at its appraised value.

What is the term on a car wash SBA loan?

Real estate and tunnel equipment go 25 years on a 504 or on a 7(a) where real estate is the majority of the project. Equipment-only loans run 10 years.

What rate will a car wash SBA loan carry?

The 504 SBA portion priced in November 2026 at 6.57% fixed for 25 years. A 7(a) loan over $350,000 is capped at prime plus 3, or 10.00% today, and the bank sets its own rate below that.

Does the SBA finance unattended self-serve washes?

Yes, as long as you actively manage the business. The SBA excludes passive real estate, but a self-serve wash with an owner handling maintenance, collections and marketing counts as an operating business.
