How roofers measure and price a roof

Roofing is priced by roof area, usually in "squares." One square equals 100 square feet of roof surface. A roof with 2,600 square feet of area is 26 squares. Contractors then add a waste factor, typically 10 to 15 percent, to cover cuts at valleys, hips and edges. Complex rooflines with many angles waste more material than a simple gable.

Roof area is not the same as your home's living area. A one-story brick ranch in Hunters Glen with deep overhangs and a 6/12 pitch can have more roof area than its floor plan. A two-story in Sienna with the same living space has a smaller footprint, but its steeper pitch, dormers and multiple hips add surface and labor. What pushes the price up or down

Pitch and height

Steep roofs require harnesses, roof jacks and slower work. Two-story homes need more setup and more care moving materials. Both raise labor costs compared to a low-slope single-story.

Number of layers

Some older homes in Quail Valley and Fondren Park still have two layers of shingles from a past overlay. Removing both adds labor and disposal fees.

Decking condition

Once the old shingles come off, rotted or delaminated plywood has to be replaced. This is common near valleys, along eaves where gutters overflowed, and under long-running leaks. Most quotes price decking per sheet, so ask what that rate is.

Ventilation and accessories

Ridge vents, new pipe boots, drip edge, starter strips and ice-and-water style membrane in valleys all add cost, and all are worth it. Attic ventilation is especially important here, since a hot, poorly vented attic shortens shingle life in Texas summers.

HOA requirements

Master-planned communities like Sienna and Riverstone often limit roof colors and materials. Approved products may cost a little more, and you may need architectural review approval before work begins.

What a complete quote should include

When you compare estimates, make sure each one spells out:

  • Total roof area in squares and the waste factor used
  • Shingle brand and product line, plus color
  • Type of underlayment (synthetic is standard now)
  • Drip edge, starter strip, ridge cap and valley treatment
  • Replacement of pipe boots, vents and flashing
  • Per-sheet price for decking replacement
  • Tear-off, disposal and magnetic nail sweep cleanup
  • Permit handling, if required
  • Workmanship terms and how the manufacturer warranty is registered

A quote that only says "replace roof, $12,000" leaves too much room for surprises. If one bid is far lower than the others, look for what is missing. Often it is underlayment quality, flashing, or decking allowances.

Watch for deductible offers: Any roofer who offers to "eat" or waive your insurance deductible is breaking Texas law under Business & Commerce Code §27.02. Walk away from that bid.

Insurance, financing and timing

If your roof was damaged by hail or wind, insurance may cover much of the replacement after your deductible. Many Texas policies pay actual cash value first and release the rest of the replacement cost after the work is complete, so read your policy or ask your agent how yours works. Our insurance claim help page explains how we document damage and meet adjusters.

If the roof is simply old, you are paying out of pocket. Plan for the job in late fall or winter when demand is lower and scheduling is easier. Avoid waiting until the roof is actively leaking, since emergency work and interior repairs add to the bill.

  • Single-story brick ranch in Hunters Glen or Fondren Park. About 2,300 square feet of roof area, moderate 6/12 pitch, one layer of old shingles, a few sheets of soft decking near the gutters. With architectural shingles, a budget somewhere in the $11,000 to $17,000 range is a reasonable starting point.
  • Two-story in Quail Valley with a past overlay. About 2,800 square feet of roof area, two layers to tear off, a chimney to re-flash and steeper back slopes. Extra labor and disposal can push this toward $15,000 to $22,000 with architectural shingles.
  • Newer two-story in Sienna or Riverstone. About 3,100 square feet of roof area, multiple hips and valleys, HOA-approved color, and an upgrade to impact-resistant shingles. Expect the upper end of the shingle range or a little above it.

The same homes in standing-seam metal would land roughly two to three times higher. In every case, decking condition is the biggest unknown until the old roof comes off, which is why a good estimate lists a per-sheet price instead of hiding it in the total.

Repair or replace?

Not every worn roof needs replacing. If the shingles are under 12 to 15 years old and the problem is limited to a few areas, a repair may buy several more years. If the roof is over 20 years old, has widespread granule loss, curling shingles or multiple leaks, replacement is usually the better use of money.
