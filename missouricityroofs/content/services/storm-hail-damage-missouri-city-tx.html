What Hail and Wind Actually Do to a Roof

Hail

Hail does not always crack shingles. More often it knocks granules loose and bruises the mat underneath, leaving soft spots that feel like a bruised apple. Over the next few summers those spots lose more granules, the asphalt dries out and leaks follow. That delayed effect is why hail damage is easy to dismiss right after a storm.

Wind

High gusts lift shingle tabs and break the adhesive seal. Even if the shingle settles back down, it is now creased and loose, and the next storm can tear it free. Wind also strips ridge caps, bends drip edge and rips off turbine vents. On homes with mature trees, falling limbs add punctures and gouges to the list.

The First 48 Hours After a Storm

What you do right after a storm affects both your home and any claim you file. Our storm damage checklist covers it in detail, but the basics are simple:

  1. Stay off the roof. Wet shingles and hidden damage make it dangerous.
  2. Photograph anything you can see from the ground: missing shingles, debris, dented gutters, damaged fences and AC units.
  3. Check the attic and ceilings for fresh stains or dripping.
  4. Protect belongings inside and save receipts for anything you spend on temporary protection.
  5. Schedule an inspection before small openings let in more rain.
Watch for storm chasers: After big events, out-of-town crews knock on doors across Missouri City. Be wary of anyone who pressures you to sign on the spot or offers to cover your deductible, which is illegal in Texas.

Repair or Replace After a Storm

Limited wind damage on a younger roof is usually a straightforward repair. Widespread hail bruising across multiple slopes is different, because patching dozens of spots leaves a roof that will keep failing. On older homes in Quail Valley or Hunters Glen, matching discontinued shingles can also be a problem. When damage is extensive, a full roof replacement may be the better outcome, and it may be covered depending on your policy and your adjuster's findings.
