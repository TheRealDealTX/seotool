What We Do, and What We Do Not Do

It is worth being clear about our role up front:

  • We do: inspect the roof, photograph and measure storm damage, write a damage summary, meet the adjuster, review the insurer's scope against what we found, and perform the repair or replacement once you approve it.
  • We do not: act as a public adjuster, negotiate your settlement, sign anything on your behalf, or waive, rebate or cover your deductible.
Texas law on deductibles: Business and Commerce Code section 27.02 prohibits contractors from waiving or absorbing a homeowner's deductible. A roofer who offers to do so is asking you to take part in something illegal.

How a Roof Claim Usually Unfolds

  1. Inspection. We look at the roof and soft metals and tell you honestly whether the damage looks storm related and significant.
  2. Filing. If you decide to file, you contact your insurer and get a claim number. Many policies have deadlines for reporting, so do not wait months.
  3. Adjuster visit. The insurer sends an adjuster. We can be there to walk the roof together.
  4. Scope and estimate. The insurer issues a written estimate of what it will pay for.
  5. Review. We compare that scope to our findings and flag anything that seems missing, such as drip edge, vents or code-required items.
  6. Work and final payment. Once approved, we complete the work and provide the documentation your insurer may need to release any remaining funds.

Policy Terms Worth Understanding

Every policy is different, so read yours or ask your agent. These are the terms that come up most:

Wind and hail deductible

Many Texas policies carry a separate deductible for wind and hail, often a percentage of the dwelling coverage rather than a flat dollar amount. On a typical Missouri City home that can be a meaningful number, so know it before you file.

Replacement cost vs. actual cash value

Replacement cost policies often pay in two parts: an initial check minus depreciation, then the held-back amount after the work is done. Actual cash value policies pay the depreciated value only. The difference can be large on an older roof.

Matching and code upgrades

Some policies address matching materials and code-required upgrades; others limit them. Check the wording, especially on older Quail Valley and Hunters Glen roofs where shingles may be discontinued.

Getting Ready for the Adjuster Visit

A little preparation makes the meeting go smoothly. Have your claim number and policy declarations page handy. Pull together any photos you took right after the storm, plus receipts for tarps or emergency repairs. Note interior stains and when they appeared. Make sure the adjuster can reach the backyard and that pets are inside. If you want us there, share the appointment window as soon as the insurer schedules it, since adjusters often book on short notice after big storms in Fort Bend County.

When a Claim Is Not the Right Move

Sometimes the honest answer is that the roof is simply old, or the damage is minor and below your deductible. In those cases we will say so and quote a straightforward roof repair or replacement instead. Opening a claim that is likely to be denied adds a record to your file without helping you.
