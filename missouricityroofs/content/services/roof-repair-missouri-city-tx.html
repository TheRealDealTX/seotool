Where Missouri City Roofs Usually Fail First

Our homes share a lot of construction details, so the weak spots repeat from house to house. These are the repairs we make most often:

  • Cracked pipe boots. The rubber collars around plumbing vents dry out and split under Texas sun, often years before the shingles wear out.
  • Step flashing at second-story walls. Two-story homes in Sienna, Riverstone and Lake Olympia have long roof-to-wall joints where loose or missing flashing lets water behind the siding or brick.
  • Clogged valleys. Under mature oaks and pines, leaves pile up in valleys, hold moisture and push water sideways under the shingles.
  • Wind-lifted shingles. Strong gusts break the seal strip, and once a tab flaps it creases and eventually tears off.
  • Chimney and skylight flashing. Sealant-only fixes from past repairs crack and need real metal flashing.
  • Ridge and box vents. Cracked plastic vents and missing nails are easy to overlook from the ground.

How We Trace a Leak

We start inside. In the attic we look for water trails, darkened decking, wet insulation and nail tips that are rusted or dripping. Then we go to the roof directly above and work uphill, checking every penetration, seam and flashing detail in that path. When the source is not obvious, we may run a controlled water test on one section at a time. You get photos of what we found and a clear explanation of the fix before we start.

Before we arrive: If water is dripping, move valuables, set out a bucket and poke a small hole in a bulging ceiling spot to let water drain in one place instead of spreading.

Repairs on Older Brick Homes in Quail Valley and Hunters Glen

Many homes around the Quail Valley golf course, Hunters Glen and Fondren Park are on their second or third roof, and some have had repairs from several different contractors. That history matters. We often find mixed shingle brands, sealant smeared over flashing and decking that has been patched more than once. On these roofs, we focus on repairs that hold up rather than cosmetic fixes, and we tell you honestly if the shingles are too brittle to handle more patching.

Shingle matching

Weathered shingles fade, and some older lines are no longer made. We bring samples, match color and profile as closely as current products allow, and place repairs on less visible slopes when there is a choice.

Tree Damage and Emergency Tarping

Big trees are part of what makes Missouri City neighborhoods pleasant, but limbs come down hard during tropical storms and spring squall lines. After Hurricane Beryl in 2024, many local homes had punctured decking from falling branches. When a limb comes through, we tarp the opening to stop water, then return to replace damaged decking, underlayment and shingles. If the damage came from a storm, see our storm and hail damage service, since the repair may be part of an insurance claim.

Repair Now or Replace Soon?

A sound roof with a single failure point is almost always worth repairing. A roof that is losing granules across every slope, curling on the sunny sides and leaking in several places is usually telling you it is done. We give you our honest read, including how many years we think the roof has left.
