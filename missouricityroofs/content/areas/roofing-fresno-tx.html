Fresno's Neighborhoods and Roof Ages

Fresno grew in stages, and the age of your neighborhood is a good guide to what your roof needs:

  • Teal Run: the oldest of the large subdivisions, started in the 1980s and finished after building resumed in the early 1990s. Many homes are on their second roof, and some first replacements are aging out again.
  • Estates of Teal Run and Villages of Teal Run: built around 1999 to the early 2000s. Original builder roofs that have not been replaced are now past 20 years.
  • Winfield Lakes: started in the mid-2000s off Trammel Fresno Road. First roofs are reaching the stage where granule loss and seal failure show up.
  • Cambridge Falls and Andover Farms: other subdivisions in the 77545 ZIP with mostly two-story homes and architectural shingles.

Most homes here are brick or brick-and-siding, with simpler roof shapes than in Sienna or Telfair. That usually keeps replacement straightforward, but large footprints on single-story homes still add up to a lot of roof area.

What Goes Wrong on Fresno Roofs

The open, flat terrain around Fresno leaves homes exposed to wind, and many newer lots have young trees that offer little shelter. Common problems include:

  • Shingles blown off along rakes and ridges after strong storms
  • Cracked plastic vents and pipe boots baked by full sun
  • Attic heat and poor ventilation that age shingles from below
  • Low spots in decking on older Teal Run roofs
Quick check: If your attic feels far hotter than the outdoor air on a summer afternoon, ventilation may be shortening your roof's life. Ask about it during your inspection.

Storms Over the Open Prairie

Fresno sees the same spring hail and summer thunderstorms as the rest of Fort Bend County, often with fewer trees and buildings to break the wind. Hurricane Harvey's rain flooded streets in parts of the area, and Hurricane Beryl in 2024 brought wind damage and long power outages across the county. Unincorporated Rules: HOAs and MUDs

Because Fresno is unincorporated, services like water and drainage come through municipal utility districts, and exterior standards come from HOAs and deed restrictions rather than a city. Some Fresno HOAs are relaxed about roof color, others want a match to the original. We help you confirm the rules before materials are ordered.
