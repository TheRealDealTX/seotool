Wisconsin’s claim calendar

Four seasons, four kinds of damage

Every season here produces its own claims. A Wisconsin Insurance Appraiser has to know how a polar-vortex freeze, a June hailstorm and a July derecho each show up on a roof, and in an estimate.

Winter claims in Wisconsin

  • Ice dams
  • Frozen & burst pipes
  • Snow-load collapse
  • Furnace puffbacks

Long stretches below freezing put more strain on Wisconsin homes than any single storm. Ice dams push meltwater under shingles, polar-vortex cold splits supply lines inside exterior walls, and heavy, wet snow loads test older trusses and farm buildings. These claims often surface weeks after the damage starts.

Spring claims in Wisconsin

  • Hail
  • Tornadoes
  • Severe thunderstorms
  • Basement seepage

Severe-weather season builds through spring. Hail tracks across the southern and western counties, and tornado outbreaks such as the August 2005 storms around Stoughton show how quickly a neighborhood can change. Thawing ground and spring rain add basement water claims to the mix.

Summer claims in Wisconsin

  • Derechos & straight-line wind
  • Flash flooding
  • Sewer backup
  • Lightning & fire

Fast-moving lines of storms can flatten timber and peel roofs across several counties in an afternoon, as the July 2019 derecho did in the north. In cities, a few hours of extreme rain can overwhelm sewers: Milwaukee in 2010 and Dane County in 2018 saw widespread basement backups.

Fall claims in Wisconsin

  • Wind damage
  • Fallen trees
  • Late hail
  • Pre-winter repairs

Autumn storms bring down limbs heavy with leaves, and late-season hail still happens. It’s also the deadline season for many claims: owners racing to finish roof repairs before snow, and depreciation holdback windows that close before the work can be done.

The path to an award

How Wisconsin Insurance Appraiser moves a stalled claim

The appraisal clause swaps an open-ended argument for a defined process with a binding result. Here’s the route from first call to signed award.

1

Free first look

We read the policy, the insurer’s estimate and your photos, then tell you plainly whether appraisal is the right tool for the gap you have.

2

Independent scope

We inspect and measure the property ourselves and build a line-item estimate priced for your part of Wisconsin.

3

Written demand

Appraisal is demanded in writing and Wisconsin Insurance Appraiser is named as your appraiser within the policy’s time limit.

4

Appraiser-to-appraiser work

We go through the disagreement line by line with the insurer’s appraiser. Many disputes narrow sharply here.

5

Umpire and award

Anything still unresolved goes to a neutral umpire. Signatures from two of the three set the amount of loss.

FAQ

Questions for a Wisconsin Insurance Appraiser

Plain answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All FAQs Claims dictionary
What does a Wisconsin Insurance Appraiser do?

We put a dollar value on property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and, when needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate claims outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When is it worth calling a Wisconsin Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage has been accepted, you have the insurer’s estimate, and the gap between that number and a real repair price is meaningful, often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is the appraisal award final?

On the amount of loss, an award signed by two of the three panel members is generally binding. That’s why the documentation you bring matters so much.

How are appraisal fees handled?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee arrangement in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation costs nothing.

Do you cover my part of Wisconsin?

Yes. We work in all 72 counties, from Kenosha and Milwaukee to Green Bay, La Crosse, Eau Claire, Wausau and the North Woods.
