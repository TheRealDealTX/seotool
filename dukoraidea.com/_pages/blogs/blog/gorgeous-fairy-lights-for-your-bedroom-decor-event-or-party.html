Gorgeous Fairy Lights for Your Bedroom Decor, Event or Party

Events & HolidaysPublished Feb 23, 2021Updated Oct 17, 20265 min read

Fairy lights for bedroom decor and fairy lights for a party are the same basic product used in very different ways. In a bedroom you want soft, steady, warm light you can switch off from bed. At an event you want coverage, a little sparkle and lights that will survive a long night and a few bumps.

This guide covers both. Start with the bedroom setups, then jump to party lights, western party lights and event lighting plans for backyards, halls and receptions.

Fairy lights for bedroom decor

A bedroom only needs two or three light sources to feel good at night, and fairy lights are perfect as one of them. Here are the setups that work best.

Behind the bed

A curtain-style strand behind a sheer panel, or a strand outlining the headboard, gives the room a warm glow without any light shining in your eyes. A remote or a smart plug lets you switch it off from bed.

Around the window

Outline the inside of the window frame with small clips. From outside, it looks inviting; from inside, it frames the view.

Under a loft bed or bunk

Attach a strand to the underside of the top bunk or loft frame with clips. It turns the space below into a cozy reading nook.

Desk and dresser

A small jar or bottle of lights on a dresser adds low light for getting ready at night without switching on the main light.

For bedrooms, choose warm white around 2,700 K, steady mode and a dimmer if possible. Plug-in and USB strands are best for nightly use; battery strands are ideal where no outlet reaches. Our indoor fairy lights guide compares the options room by room.

Sleepovers and birthday bedrooms

A bedroom can double as a party space for a sleepover or a small birthday. Swap the everyday warm strand for a multicolor or twinkle mode for the night, add a light curtain behind the bed as a photo backdrop, and switch back to steady warm white afterward. One remote-controlled strand with several modes covers both jobs.

Tip: If you have trouble sleeping with lights on, set a timer for 30 to 60 minutes, or use a strand with a fade mode that dims gradually.

Party lights: planning the basics

Party lights have a different job. They mark out the space, make people look good in photos and set the energy. Before you buy anything, sketch the area and decide which of these you need:

  • Overhead canopy: strands or festoon lights zigzagged above the main area. This is the biggest visual impact.
  • Perimeter: lights along fences, railings or hedges to define the edges.
  • Tables: jars, bottles or garlands on each table.
  • Focal point: a light curtain behind a cake table, bar or photo backdrop.
  • Paths: lanterns or stake lights so guests can find their way safely.

For overhead runs, measure each span and add about 10 to 15 percent for drape. A gentle sag of roughly 1 to 2 ft per 10 ft span looks graceful and takes strain off the anchor points. Our planner in the light tools hub helps work out how many strands and anchors you need for a patio.

Indoor parties and small spaces

Indoors, you rarely need an overhead canopy. Instead, outline a doorway, hang a light curtain behind the food table and fill a few jars for side tables. Dim or switch off the overhead fixtures and let the strands and a couple of lamps carry the room. In an apartment, battery strands with timers save you from running cords across the floor where guests will trip.

Western party lights and themed lighting

For a western or rustic party, warm white is still the base, but the styling changes:

  • Globe or festoon bulbs strung across a barn, a fence or between poles give a saloon-porch look.
  • Mason jar lanterns hung from hay bales, fence posts or shepherd hooks. Our mason jar fairy lights project shows how to make them quickly.
  • Burlap and wood: wrap strands around wooden crates, wagon wheels or old ladders as decor pieces.
  • Amber tones: choose extra-warm strands around 2,200 K for a candlelit, rustic feel.

Other themes work the same way: pick one hero element and support it with simple warm light. For a garden party, wrap tree trunks and hang lanterns. For a winter party, mix lights with garland and pine. For a kids' party, multicolor ball lights are fun and cheerful.

Event lighting for weddings and big gatherings

Event lighting at a larger scale needs a little more planning, but the same principles apply.

  1. Walk the space at night

    Visit at the same time as the event, if you can, and note where it is dark and where the power outlets are.

  2. Plan power first

    Count how many strands can safely run from each outlet and where extension cords will go. Battery strands avoid cords on tables entirely.

  3. Light the overhead space

    String lights across the main area, ideally 8 to 10 ft high, so they clear heads but still feel close.

  4. Add the details

    Finish with table lights, a lit backdrop and path lights.

  5. Test everything

    Turn on every strand the day before and keep spare batteries, fuses and a spare strand on hand.

Our wedding guide, wedding motifs and how to light them, goes deeper on reception lighting.

Safety: Use outdoor-rated strands (IP44 minimum, IP65 for exposed areas) and outdoor-rated extension cords plugged into a GFCI outlet. Tape cords flat or use cord covers across walkways. Never connect more strands end to end than the packaging allows.

After the party

Take lights down carefully so they are ready for next time. Wind each strand around a piece of cardboard or an empty paper towel tube, remove batteries before storage to prevent corrosion, and store everything in a labeled box. A tangle-free strand on event day is worth the five minutes.

Bring the glow home

The lights from a party can easily become the lights for a bedroom, so buy warm white, good quality strands and they will work for both. For more ideas, browse our fairy light decoration ideas or read how to hang outdoor string lights before your next event.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What are the best fairy lights for a bedroom?

Warm white micro LED or curtain strands around 2,700 K, on steady mode with a dimmer or remote. Plug-in or USB power works best for nightly use.

How many string lights do I need for a party?

Measure each overhead span and the perimeter, then add about 10 to 15 percent for drape. Table and path lights are counted separately.

Can I use indoor fairy lights outside for a party?

Only if they are rated for outdoor use. Look for IP44 at minimum and IP65 or higher for exposed spots, and use outdoor-rated cords on a GFCI outlet.

What lights suit a western themed party?

Warm or amber globe and festoon string lights, mason jar lanterns and strands wrapped around wood, burlap or hay bales give a rustic western look.
