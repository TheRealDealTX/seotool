Beautiful DIY Fairy Light Decor Ideas for Every Room

Decor IdeasPublished Feb 2, 2021Updated Oct 17, 20266 min read

The best fairy light decoration ideas are the ones that make a room feel lived in rather than staged. A few strands of warm micro LEDs can soften a dark corner, highlight a favorite object or turn a plain wall into a feature, and they cost less than almost any other decor change.

Below are room-by-room fairy light room ideas, a section on keeping fairy lights up all year round without it feeling like Christmas, and the small styling rules that make the difference between cozy and cluttered.

Three rules for fairy light decor that looks intentional

  • Stick to one color temperature per room. Mixing warm and cool white strands is the fastest way to make a space look messy. Warm white around 2,700 K suits most homes.
  • Hide the hardware. Plan where the battery pack, plug or USB cable goes before you hang anything.
  • Give it a job. Lights should frame, outline, fill or highlight something. A strand draped randomly across a wall rarely looks good.
  • Layer, do not replace. Fairy lights are accent light. Keep a lamp for reading and let the strands add atmosphere around it.

Not sure which warmth fits a room? The color-temperature picker in our light tools shows how different whites change the mood.

Bedroom fairy light decoration ideas

The bedroom is where fairy lights earn their keep, because they give enough light to wind down without the glare of an overhead fixture.

  • Headboard outline: clip a strand around the edge of the headboard or trace a headboard shape on the wall.
  • Canopy: drape sheer fabric from ceiling hooks and thread lights through the folds.
  • Nightstand jar: a mason jar of fairy lights makes a low, warm nightlight.
  • Closet glow: a short battery strand with a timer along a closet shelf helps you see without waking anyone.

Kids' and teen rooms

Kids love a strand of star-shaped lights along a bunk rail or around a reading tent. Choose battery packs with screw-closed compartments, mount the strand out of reach of small hands and use a timer so it switches off after bedtime. Teens tend to prefer a photo wall or a light-up word above the desk.

Our DIY fairy lights guide has step-by-step headboard and canopy methods, and indoor fairy lights covers strand choices for bedrooms.

Living room, kitchen and dining ideas

Bookshelf backlight

Run a strand along the back of each shelf, behind the books. The light spills forward around the spines and gives depth to a flat bookcase. Use one continuous strand and route it down the back edge.

Terrarium and glass displays

Wrap thin copper wire lights around the inside or outside of a glass terrarium, a cloche or a large vase. Copper wire is bendable and almost disappears, leaving just the points of light around the plants or objects inside.

Branch centerpiece

Collect three to five bare branches, set them in a heavy vase and wind a strand around them from the base up. Change the filler in the vase by season.

Picture ledge

Lay a strand along a picture ledge behind framed prints so the frames get a soft halo.

Stairs and hallways

Wind a strand loosely around a stair banister or run it along the top of a hallway baseboard. It works as a gentle night path to the bathroom, and a strand on a dusk sensor or timer means you never fumble for a switch.

Kitchen and dining

  • Glass cabinet glow: lay a short battery strand on each shelf of a glass-front cabinet to light glassware.
  • Open shelving: tuck lights behind jars and plants on floating shelves.
  • Table runner: weave a strand through a garland or runner down the dining table for dinners. Use a timer so they switch off without thought.
  • Bottle lights: empty wine bottles with cork-style light strands make easy table lights.
Safety: Keep strands, battery packs and adapters away from the stove, the sink and anywhere food prep gets wet. Use only LED strands near paper, greenery or fabric.

Fairy lights with greenery and ivy

Pairing lights with greenery is the easiest way to make them look natural. Wind a strand through a faux ivy garland and drape it along a mantel, over a mirror or across a curtain rod. The leaves hide the wire and break up the line of bulbs.

For a living wall effect, attach several lengths of faux ivy to a wall with clear hooks, then weave a strand through the vines. This works especially well on a balcony wall or an accent wall in a bedroom. Our fake ivy leaves guide covers the best way to mount vines without marking the paint.

Real plants work too, with a little care. Drape a strand loosely over a trailing pothos or a potted olive tree, keep the battery pack out of the soil and away from the watering can, and take the lights off every few weeks so the stems do not grow around the wire.

Fairy lights all year round

Warm white lights in a jar, along a shelf or through greenery do not read as holiday decor. To keep them up all year round without looking seasonal, keep to these tweaks:

  • Use warm white, not multicolor. Multicolor reads as Christmas.
  • Swap the companions, not the lights. Pair the strand with pine and berries in winter, fresh or dried flowers in spring, shells in summer and dried leaves in autumn.
  • Use steady mode. Flashing and chasing modes feel festive; steady and slow fade feel calm.
  • Keep it contained. Lights inside jars, behind fabric or along furniture look permanent.
Tip: A built-in 6-hour timer turns lights on at the same time every evening, which makes a strand feel like part of the house rather than something you remember to plug in.

Outdoor and balcony ideas

  • Balcony railing: wrap a strand around the top rail and add a few potted plants.
  • Tree wrap: spiral lights up a tree trunk from the base, spacing the turns about 6 in apart.
  • Lantern path: hang jars of lights from shepherd hooks along a path.
  • Umbrella lights: clip a strand along the ribs of a patio umbrella.

Outdoors, choose strands with at least an IP44 rating, and IP65 or higher for exposed spots. Our outdoor fairy lights guide covers ratings, solar packs and timers.

Quick ideas under 10 minutes

  1. Stuff a strand into a clear glass lamp base or hurricane vase.
  2. Coil a strand inside a wire basket on a side table.
  3. Drape lights over a mirror frame with three clips.
  4. Run a strand under the lip of a floating shelf.
  5. Fill a bowl with lights and pine cones for a coffee table centerpiece.
  6. Clip a few photos to a strand and hang it across a door frame.

Keep the ideas coming

Pick one room and one idea and try it tonight; most of these take less time than reading about them. For more inspiration, see our ways to use fairy lights in DIY projects, or browse the fairy lights guides to find the right strand.

Frequently asked questions

Can you keep fairy lights up all year round?

Yes. Warm white strands on steady mode, used inside jars, behind fabric or with greenery, look like everyday decor rather than holiday lights.

What color fairy lights look best in a bedroom?

Warm white around 2,700 to 3,000 K feels cozy and relaxing. Save cool white and multicolor for workspaces, kids' rooms or parties.

How do I hide fairy light wires?

Use thin copper or silver wire strands, weave them through greenery or fabric, and route the lead along furniture edges with clear clips.

Are fairy lights safe with fake ivy and paper decor?

LED fairy lights run cool and are generally safe with faux greenery and paper. Avoid incandescent mini lights and keep battery packs and adapters uncovered.
