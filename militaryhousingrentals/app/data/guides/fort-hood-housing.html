Fort Hood housing is the most-searched topic on this site, and for good reason: the post outside Killeen, Texas is one of the largest Army installations in the world, home to III Armored Corps and the 1st Cavalry Division, and tens of thousands of Soldiers and families move here every year. This guide walks through your two real options, on-post family housing run by Cavalry Family Housing and off-post rentals in Killeen, Harker Heights and Copperas Cove, with the 2026 BAH rates you will budget against.

A note on the name. The post was renamed Fort Cavazos in 2023 and officially became Fort Hood again in 2025, this time honoring World War I artillery officer Col. Robert B. Hood. You will still see both names on leases, school forms and listings, which is why our installation page is Fort Hood (Cavazos).

Fort Hood housing at a glance

  • Location: Killeen, Texas, in Bell and Coryell counties, about an hour north of Austin. The cantonment and training area covers roughly 340 square miles.
  • On-post family housing: privatized under the Army's Residential Communities Initiative. Cavalry Family Housing, a Lendlease company formerly called Fort Hood Family Housing, manages more than 5,000 homes across a dozen villages. A separate 300-home village of two-bedroom homes, Liberty Village, is run by Universal Services.
  • Rent on post: set to your with-dependents BAH, with electricity, water and gas included and no security deposit for active-duty families.
  • Off-post commute: 10 to 15 minutes from Killeen, Harker Heights and Copperas Cove; about 30 minutes from Temple, Belton, Gatesville and Lampasas.
  • Housing office: Housing Services Office, Copeland Soldier Service Center, Building 18010, T.J. Mills Blvd, Fort Hood, TX 76544. Phone 254-285-2313, Monday to Friday 8:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.

Step one: in-process with the Housing Services Office

Whatever you decide, start at the Housing Services Office (HSO) in the Copeland Soldier Service Center. Per Fort Hood regulation, in-processing through the HSO is mandatory before you rent, lease or buy in the local area, and the office does more than stamp a form. Staff keep a list of registered rental agencies, review leases, help with tenant-landlord disputes and discrimination complaints, and run the waitlist for on-post housing.

Timing matters. If you apply for on-post housing within 30 days of arriving, your eligibility date is backdated to the day you left your last duty station. Apply later and the clock starts on the day you submit the application. On a waitlist that can run from two weeks to 12 months depending on rank, bedroom count and occupancy, those weeks of seniority are worth having. Families enrolled in the Exceptional Family Member Program should bring the EFMP medical memo with the housing requirement form so the HSO can match the right home or help with off-post options.

On-post neighborhoods at Fort Hood

Cavalry Family Housing assigns homes by rank band and bedroom need, so you will not pick an exact address, but it helps to know what the villages look like. Here are the neighborhoods you will hear about most:

  • Heritage Heights is the newest neighborhood on post, replacing the 1960s-era Chaffee Village. Homes are single-family three-bedroom, two-bath designs with two-car garages, built for junior enlisted Soldiers and certified Energy Star. Hundreds were completed in 2024 and construction continues through 2027 as part of a $420 million community development plan.
  • Comanche I, II and III are established villages of two- and three-bedroom single-story and two-story homes near the Exchange, Commissary and Clear Creek golf course. Comanche I has had recent window, door, siding and privacy-fence upgrades.
  • McNair is known for larger homes, some with basements and hardwood floors, close to Carl R. Darnall Army Medical Center.
  • Kouma and Venable offer three- and four-bedroom two-story homes for enlisted families, near basketball courts, a fitness center and a community pool.
  • Patton Park holds ranch-style homes of about 2,000 square feet reserved for senior warrant officers and field-grade officers.
  • Wainwright shares a neighborhood community center with Heritage Heights, Patton and McNair, where residents handle applications, payments and work orders.
  • Liberty Village is the 300-home village of two-bedroom homes operated by Universal Services under a separate ground lease. Apply through the Liberty Village website or ask the HSO.

Across the villages, families get 24-hour maintenance, a two-pet limit with breed restrictions, community centers, pools, playgrounds and dog parks. On-post students attend Killeen ISD schools, including seven elementary schools and two middle schools located on post; high school students are zoned to Killeen High School or Shoemaker High School. Off-post: Killeen, Harker Heights, Copperas Cove and beyond

Most Fort Hood families who live off post choose one of three cities that border the installation. Each has a different feel:

  • Killeen wraps around the main gate and has the deepest rental inventory and the lowest prices. Newer subdivisions on the south side, near Stan Schlueter Loop, are popular with families; the north side offers the cheapest apartments and the shortest drive to the gate.
  • Harker Heights, to the east, is the suburban choice: larger homes, retail along Highway 190, Stillhouse Hollow Lake close by and a short run to the east gates. It is popular with NCOs and junior officers and rents accordingly.
  • Copperas Cove, to the west, is quieter and family-oriented with its own school district, Copperas Cove ISD, and quick access to the west side of post.
  • Nolanville, Belton and Temple add a 20 to 30 minute commute in exchange for newer construction, Belton ISD and Temple ISD schools and the Baylor Scott & White medical campus in Temple.
  • Gatesville, Lampasas and Salado are the small-town options about half an hour out.

The HSO lists registered agencies, but you will also find homes through the base's housing referral tools and ordinary listing sites. On post or off post: how Fort Hood families decide

Living on post trades flexibility for simplicity. Rent, utilities and maintenance are bundled into your BAH, you are minutes from your unit, and neighbors understand deployments. Living off post lets you keep whatever BAH you do not spend, pick your school district and own pets without a breed list, but you take on utilities, yard work and the risk of a lease you cannot easily leave. The renting versus buying guide adds the ownership math, which looks better at Fort Hood than at most posts because home prices in Killeen remain well below the Texas average.

Fort Hood housing FAQ

Who manages Fort Hood family housing?

Cavalry Family Housing, a Lendlease company formerly called Fort Hood Family Housing, manages most on-post family homes under the Army's Residential Communities Initiative. Liberty Village, a 300-home village of two-bedroom homes, is operated by Universal Services. Both are reached through the Housing Services Office at 254-285-2313.

How long is the Fort Hood housing waitlist?

The housing office quotes two weeks to 12 months depending on your rank, the bedroom count you need and current occupancy. Apply within 30 days of arrival so your eligibility date is backdated to when you left your last duty station.

How much is BAH at Fort Hood in 2026?

For the Killeen military housing area, 2026 BAH with dependents is $1,662 for E-1 to E-4, $1,695 for E-5, $1,920 for E-6, $2,070 for E-7, $2,340 for O-3 and $2,577 for O-4. Rates without dependents are lower. Rates took effect January 1, 2026.

Do I have to live on post at Fort Hood?

No. On-post family housing is voluntary unless you hold a key or mission-essential position. Single Soldiers E-5 and below normally live in the barracks, while single Soldiers E-6 and above may apply for on-post family housing or live off post.

Which towns are closest to Fort Hood?

Killeen, Harker Heights and Copperas Cove border the post with a 10 to 15 minute commute. Nolanville, Belton, Temple, Gatesville, Lampasas and Salado are roughly 20 to 30 minutes away.

Is Fort Hood still called Fort Cavazos?

The post was Fort Cavazos from 2023 until 2025, when the Army restored the name Fort Hood in honor of Col. Robert B. Hood. Official signage now reads Fort Hood, but many leases, listings and school documents still use Fort Cavazos.
