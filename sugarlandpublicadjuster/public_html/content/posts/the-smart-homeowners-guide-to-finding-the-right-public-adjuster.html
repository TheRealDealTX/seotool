Understanding What a Public Adjuster Does

Before diving into how to find a public adjuster, it’s important to understand their role.

A public adjuster is a licensed professional who represents policyholders during insurance claims. They inspect damage, calculate repair costs, prepare documentation, and negotiate with the insurance company on your behalf.

Many homeowners don’t realize that there are actually three types of adjusters involved in the insurance world.

Type of AdjusterWho They Work ForPrimary Role
Insurance Company AdjusterInsurance CompanyEvaluates claims for the insurer
Independent AdjusterInsurance CompanyContracted to assess claims
Public AdjusterPolicyholderRepresents the homeowner

Only one of these works for you. That’s the public adjuster.

Knowing how to find a public adjuster who is qualified and trustworthy can make the claims process far less stressful.

Step 2: Verify Licensing and Credentials

In Texas, public adjusters must be licensed by the state. This protects consumers and ensures the adjuster meets professional standards.

One of the most important steps in how to find a public adjuster is verifying that license.

Check for:

  • Active state license

  • Proper business registration

Licensed professionals follow state rules regarding contracts, fees, and claim representation.

Understanding Public Adjuster Fees

Many homeowners researching how to find a public adjuster worry about costs.

The good news is that most public adjusters work on a contingency basis.

This means they are paid a percentage of the insurance settlement.

Typical fee ranges can vary depending on claim complexity, but contingency fees often benefit homeowners because the adjuster only earns compensation if the claim is successfully settled.

What Happens After You Hire a Public Adjuster

Once you’ve completed the process of how to find a public adjuster and selected one, the real work begins.

The claim process typically includes several stages.

Initial Property Inspection

The adjuster evaluates damage, photographs the property, and gathers evidence to support the claim.

Policy Review

Your insurance policy is carefully reviewed to determine coverage limits, exclusions, and applicable protections.

Damage Documentation

A detailed damage estimate is created, often including contractor estimates and repair costs.

Claim Submission

All documentation is submitted to the insurance company to begin negotiations.

Negotiation Process

The public adjuster communicates directly with the insurance company’s adjusters to reach a fair settlement.

This process can take time, but thorough documentation often strengthens the claim significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a public adjuster?

A public adjuster is a licensed professional who represents policyholders during insurance claims, helping evaluate damage and negotiate with insurance companies.

How do I know if I need a public adjuster?

You may benefit from a public adjuster if your property damage is significant, your claim is complex, or your insurance company has denied or underpaid your claim.

How do public adjusters get paid?

Most public adjusters work on a contingency basis, meaning they receive a percentage of the final insurance settlement rather than charging upfront fees.

Is hiring a public adjuster worth it?

For large or complicated claims, a public adjuster can help ensure damages are properly documented and may improve the chances of receiving a fair settlement.

Are public adjusters licensed in Texas?

Yes, public adjusters in Texas must be licensed through the Texas Department of Insurance and follow state regulations.

Can a public adjuster reopen a closed insurance claim?

In some cases, yes. If additional damage is discovered or new evidence is presented, a public adjuster may help request a claim review.

How long does the insurance claims process take with a public adjuster?

The timeline varies depending on the complexity of the claim, but having a public adjuster can help keep documentation organized and negotiations moving forward.

What questions should I ask before hiring a public adjuster?

Ask about their licensing, experience with similar claims, fee structure, communication process, and who will manage your claim.

Can I hire a public adjuster after filing an insurance claim?

Yes, homeowners can hire a public adjuster at any stage of the claims process, even after the claim has already been filed.

What should I avoid when choosing a public adjuster?

Avoid professionals who pressure you to sign contracts quickly, request large upfront payments, or cannot provide proof of licensing.
