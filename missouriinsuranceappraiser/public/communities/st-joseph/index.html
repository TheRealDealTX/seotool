Housing mix

St. Joseph has an unusually large stock of late 1800s brick mansions and Victorian homes on its hills, plus modest frame houses, post-war ranches and newer homes along the Belt Highway corridor.

A historic housing stock that defies software

St. Joseph grew rich in the second half of the 1800s, and the money went into buildings. Museum Hill and the Hall Street Historic District are lined with large brick and stone homes: towers, slate roofs, stained glass, ornate porches and carved woodwork. Even the more modest homes from that era have plaster walls, tall ceilings and trim profiles nobody stocks anymore.

Insurance estimating software is built around average houses. When it meets a three-story Victorian with a slate roof and copper valleys, it tends to underprice. Typical gaps we see:

  • Slate repairs priced as shingle, or slate replacement priced without the extra labor for steep pitches, turrets and hand-formed flashing
  • Plaster damage written as drywall patching
  • Box gutters and built-in drainage replaced with standard gutters
  • Stained or leaded glass written as plain glazing
  • No allowance for scaffolding on tall masonry walls

None of this means the insurer will pay for a museum restoration. It means a fair estimate prices like-kind work on the house that is actually there.

The river, and the line between flood and everything else

The Missouri River runs along St. Joseph’s western edge. In 2011, prolonged high water kept levees under strain for much of the summer. In 2019, flooding breached levees north of town and put the Rosecrans air base area under water.

River flooding is excluded by standard homeowners and commercial property policies; it requires separate flood insurance. Neither appraisal nor anyone else can turn a flood loss into a covered homeowners claim. If you have a flood policy, it has its own procedures. If your damage came from wind-driven rain or a burst pipe instead, that is usually a different coverage path, and a dispute about the amount there may be a candidate for appraisal.

Hail and wind on the hills

Buchanan County gets its share of spring hail and summer wind. On the bluffs and hilltops where many older homes sit, wind exposure is higher, and large old trees add to the risk. After a storm, roofs are the obvious focus, but don’t forget:

  • Ornamental metalwork and finials
  • Wood siding, shingles and porch details
  • Chimneys, which on older homes may need rebuilding with matching brick
  • Interior water staining that appears days later

Our hail and roof damage appraisal page explains how we document those items.

Local considerations and appraisal

Work on historic properties may require approval from the city before exterior changes, and contractors who work on slate, masonry and old woodwork are in short supply in northwest Missouri. Both affect cost and timeline, and both belong in a realistic estimate.

Appraisal is for disputes about the amount of a covered loss. It does not resolve coverage denials, and the policy language controls. If your insurer has accepted the loss but undervalued it, an insurer estimate review is often the first step.

Questions from St. Joseph property owners

My insurer priced my St. Joseph slate roof as asphalt shingles. Is that normal?

It happens, especially with software-generated estimates. If the insurer accepts the damage but uses the wrong material or labor, appraisal can be used to resolve the amount of loss.

Does homeowners insurance cover Missouri River flooding in St. Joseph?

No. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood. You would need a separate NFIP or private flood policy.

Does living in a historic district change my St. Joseph claim?

It can. Required review or material rules may affect what a proper exterior repair includes; whether the extra cost is covered depends on your policy, often its ordinance or law section.
