Living in Cape Girardeau means paying attention to the river. The floodwall along downtown has been tested more than once, including during the Great Flood of 1993 and again when the Mississippi reached a record crest around the start of 2016. Add the New Madrid seismic zone to the south and regular spring storms, and Southeast Missouri owners face a set of risks that do not all sit under one policy.

Know which policy covers what

Before talking about estimates, it helps to be clear on coverage, because it is easy to assume one policy covers everything:

  • Wind, hail, fire and most sudden water damage are generally covered under homeowners or commercial property policies, subject to deductibles and exclusions.
  • Flood is excluded from those policies. It needs a separate NFIP or private flood policy, and properties outside the floodwall or in low-lying areas should take it seriously.
  • Earthquake is usually excluded too, and typically requires a separate endorsement or policy with its own deductible, often a percentage of the dwelling limit.
  • Sewer and drain backup usually requires its own endorsement.

Appraisal decides the amount of a covered loss. It cannot add coverage you do not have. If you are unsure about coverage, check your declarations page, ask your agent, or talk to a Missouri attorney.

Storm claims on older and newer homes

Near the river and downtown

Downtown and the historic areas around Red Star and Haarig have brick commercial blocks, older frame homes and houses with plaster walls and original woodwork. Hail and wind damage to these buildings often involves specialty roofing, masonry repairs, wood windows and trim that standard estimates underprice.

West Cape and Jackson

Newer subdivisions on the west side and toward Jackson mostly have asphalt roofs, vinyl siding and modern finishes. Disputes there center on roof replacement versus repair, siding that no longer matches and whether all affected elevations were included. Our hail and roof damage page covers how we approach those.

Winter weather

Southeast Missouri also gets occasional ice storms, which bring down trees and power lines and can lead to frozen pipes in homes that lose heat. Those claims often combine roof damage, interior water and spoiled food or contents. The winter storm and ice damage page explains how they fit together.

Local factors in your estimate

Cape Girardeau is the regional hub for Southeast Missouri, and contractors here often serve a wide rural area. After a major storm, demand stretches across several counties, and prices and timelines move accordingly. Permits and code requirements come from the city or the county depending on where the property sits.

For buildings near the floodwall or in mapped flood zones, any substantial repair may also bring floodplain rules into play, which can affect what a rebuild must include.

Considering appraisal

If your insurer has accepted a claim but you believe its estimate is too low, appraisal lets each side choose an appraiser, with an umpire to resolve differences. It is generally quicker than litigation, but it has costs, and it only addresses the amount. Many owners start with an estimate review to see how big the gap really is.

Questions from Cape Girardeau property owners

Does my Cape Girardeau homeowners policy cover earthquake damage?

Usually not. Earthquake coverage is typically a separate endorsement or policy with its own deductible. Check your declarations page to see whether you have it.

My property flooded from the Mississippi. Can appraisal help?

Only if you carry flood insurance and disagree on the amount. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood, and NFIP policies have their own dispute procedures.

What if I live outside the Cape Girardeau floodwall?

Property outside the floodwall or in mapped low areas carries more river flood exposure. Flood damage needs a separate flood policy, and any substantial repair may also bring local floodplain rules into play.
