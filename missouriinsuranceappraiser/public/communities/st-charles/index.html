Housing mix

St. Charles pairs brick and stone buildings in the Main Street historic district and Frenchtown with post-war neighborhoods and large newer subdivisions spreading west through the county.

St. Charles is the county seat of one of the fastest-growing counties in Missouri, and it sits between two big rivers. That combination shapes almost every claim we look at here, from a 19th-century brick storefront on Main Street to a five-year-old house in a subdivision off Highway 94.

Rivers on two sides

The Missouri runs along the city’s eastern edge, and the Mississippi meets it a few miles north at the confluence. The Great Flood of 1993 put much of the low ground near that confluence under water for weeks, and high water has returned in other years. The Missouri bottoms on the north and east sides of town remain floodplain.

The point for policyholders is simple, if unwelcome: river flooding is not covered by a standard homeowners or business policy. It needs flood insurance through the NFIP or a private carrier, and those policies have their own claim process and their own limits on things like basements. When a property has both storm damage and flood damage, separating the two is often the hardest part of the claim, and getting it right is a coverage matter before it is a pricing matter.

Tornadoes are not just a spring problem

The December 10, 2021 tornado that hit the Defiance area in western St. Charles County was a reminder that severe weather here does not always follow the calendar. Wind claims after an event like that tend to involve full rebuilds, total loss questions and long fights over the value of what was destroyed. Our page on tornado and straight-line wind appraisal covers how replacement cost, actual cash value and depreciation figure into those disputes.

From Main Street to the subdivisions

Main Street and Frenchtown

The historic district along Main Street and the Frenchtown neighborhood to the north hold some of the oldest buildings in the region: brick and stone storefronts, frame and brick homes, some dating from the early 1800s. Repairs to these buildings involve:

  • Soft historic brick and lime mortar that should not be patched with modern hard mortar
  • Wood windows, doors and trim that may need custom milling
  • Standing-seam metal or other specialty roofing
  • Historic preservation review for exterior changes in designated districts

A standard estimate built on replacement-grade materials usually falls short. Mixed-use buildings with a shop downstairs and apartments upstairs add business income and rental questions on top.

The subdivisions

Most St. Charles County housing is newer: frame construction, asphalt shingles, vinyl siding and finished basements. Hail disputes here look like hail disputes everywhere: partial approvals, discontinued siding and roof slopes written as repairs instead of replacements. Finished basements also turn plumbing failures into bigger claims, since flooring, drywall, built-ins and contents all get wet. Sump failures and drain backups usually need a specific endorsement, so check yours.

Is appraisal right for your claim?

If the insurer agrees there is covered damage and you are stuck on the amount, appraisal may be a quicker path than continued back-and-forth. It will not decide whether something is covered; your policy and, if needed, a Missouri attorney handle that. Our claim toolkit has checklists to get your documentation in order first.

Questions from St. Charles property owners

My St. Charles home flooded from the Missouri River. Can appraisal help?

Only if you have flood insurance and dispute the amount; standard homeowners policies exclude river flooding. NFIP policies have their own dispute procedures, so check your flood policy for what applies.

Do historic district rules on Main Street affect my insurance claim?

They can affect what a proper repair includes, such as required materials or review before exterior work. Whether those added costs are covered depends on your policy, often its ordinance or law section.

Do you work in St. Peters and O’Fallon too?

Yes. We serve St. Charles and the surrounding St. Charles County communities and inspect properties in person.
