Housing mix

Chesterfield is mostly suburban, with large single-family homes from the 1970s onward, upscale subdivisions with cedar, slate or tile roofs, condos and villas, and commercial development in Chesterfield Valley.

Bigger homes, bigger gaps

Chesterfield’s housing is mostly suburban and mostly substantial. Many homes are large, with complex rooflines, multiple gables and dormers, and premium materials: designer shingles, cedar shakes, slate, concrete or clay tile, stone veneer and custom windows. When a hailstorm crosses west St. Louis County, those features drive up the cost of a proper repair, and they are exactly where insurer estimates tend to fall short.

Common problems we see on Chesterfield claims:

  • Premium roofing priced as standard architectural shingles
  • Steep-pitch and high-roof labor charges missing or understated
  • Copper or specialty flashing and gutters swapped for basic aluminum
  • Custom windows and doors priced as stock units
  • Interior finishes such as hardwood, millwork and high-end paint systems underpriced

Higher-value homes often carry policies with extended replacement cost or guaranteed replacement provisions. Those terms do not change how damage is measured, but they can matter a great deal for large losses. Read them before the claim, not after.

The valley and the levee

Chesterfield Valley, the flat land along the Missouri River, was under deep water in 1993 after a levee failed during the Great Flood. Since then the levee system has been improved and the valley has filled with retail, offices and industry. It is still floodplain.

For business owners there, flood insurance is a separate decision from property insurance, and business interruption from a flood is often not covered by a standard commercial policy. For storm claims on commercial buildings, roofs are large and costly, and estimates often miss items such as roof-top equipment, membrane details and insulation. See commercial and HOA appraisal for how those claims run.

Condos, villas and associations

A lot of Chesterfield residents live in condos, villas or HOA communities. After a storm, the first question is who insures what. The association’s master policy may cover roofs and exteriors, while unit owners cover interiors and improvements. Your declarations and the master policy decide where the line falls. Associations facing a large hail claim across many buildings often benefit from an independent estimate before negotiating.

Local notes and the appraisal decision

Chesterfield is a city within St. Louis County, and building permits and code enforcement run through the city and, for some work, the county. Many subdivisions also have trustees or HOAs with architectural rules on roofing and exterior materials, which can shape what a realistic repair looks like.

The western part of the county also has karst ground in places, and sinkholes are not unheard of. As elsewhere, earth movement is generally excluded unless your policy says otherwise.

Appraisal fits when the insurer accepts the loss but you disagree on the amount. It does not decide coverage, and the policy language controls. If you want to know whether your estimate is in the right range before deciding, start with an insurer estimate review.

Questions from Chesterfield property owners

My Chesterfield home has a tile roof. Will the insurer replace it with tile?

Most replacement cost policies pay for like kind and quality, which would generally mean tile. If the insurer accepts the damage but disputes the amount or material, appraisal may resolve the amount of loss.

I own a condo. Is the roof my claim or the association’s?

It depends on your association’s governing documents and the master policy. Often the association insures roofs and exteriors, while unit owners insure interiors.

Is Chesterfield Valley still a flood risk?

It is still floodplain despite levee improvements. Flood damage requires separate flood insurance, which standard property policies do not provide.
