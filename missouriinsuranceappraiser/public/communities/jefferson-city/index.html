2019, and what it taught capital-area owners

Late on the night of May 22, 2019, an EF3 tornado moved through Jefferson City, damaging homes and buildings from the south side into downtown. Within weeks, the Missouri River was flooding North Jefferson and the Callaway County side, as it had in 1993. For many owners, that year meant dealing with two very different kinds of loss at once.

The lessons still apply:

  • Wind and flood are separate coverages. Tornado damage is usually covered under a homeowners or commercial policy. River flooding is not; it needs NFIP or private flood insurance.
  • Documentation needs to be separated. If a property had both wind and water damage, photos, moisture readings and timelines need to show which caused what.
  • Contractor capacity runs out. After a regional disaster, pricing climbs and timelines stretch. An estimate written right after the storm may not reflect what the work ends up costing.

Our tornado and wind appraisal page covers how those claims are measured.

Old brick in the capital

Jefferson City has deep German roots, and you can see it in the buildings. Old Munichburg, south of the Capitol, and the streets around Capitol Avenue have brick homes and commercial buildings from the 1800s and early 1900s. These were built with soft brick and lime mortar, and repairing them correctly means:

  • Matching brick size, color and texture, often from salvage
  • Using compatible mortar rather than modern Portland mixes that can damage old brick
  • Repairing wood windows, cornices and trim instead of swapping in stock replacements
  • Planning for any historic review the city requires on exterior work

An estimate that treats an 1880s brick building as if it were a modern veneer home will come in low. That is a dispute about amount, which appraisal can address.

Two counties and a river

Most of Jefferson City lies in Cole County south of the river. North Jefferson and Holts Summit are across the Missouri in Callaway County, in low ground that has flooded repeatedly. If you own property on that side, find out whether you are in a mapped flood zone and whether you carry flood insurance. Mortgage lenders often require it in high-risk zones, but plenty of owners just outside those zones go without.

Permits come from the city or the county depending on location, and code requirements for re-roofing or structural repairs can add costs. Ordinance or law coverage in your policy may pay some of those.

When appraisal is the right tool

Appraisal settles disagreements about the amount of a covered loss. It does not decide whether a loss is covered, and it cannot convert a flood into a wind claim. If the insurer accepts your claim but the estimate is too low, our appraisal roadmap explains the process from demand to award. For legal questions about coverage, talk to a Missouri attorney.

Questions from Jefferson City property owners

My Jefferson City home had both tornado and flood damage. How is that handled?

Usually as two separate claims: wind damage under your homeowners policy and flood damage under a flood policy if you have one. Clear documentation of which damage came from which cause is essential.

Can appraisal be used if the insurer underpaid my old brick building?

Yes, if the insurer accepts the loss as covered and the disagreement is over the cost of a proper repair, including matching brick and compatible mortar.

I live in North Jefferson. Do I really need flood insurance?

North Jefferson sits in low ground across the river that has flooded in 1993 and 2019, and homeowners policies exclude flood. Whether to buy it is your decision, but without it a river flood is usually uninsured.
