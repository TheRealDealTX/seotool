Appraisal help

Homeowners Insurance Appraisal for Missouri Families

Your house is probably the biggest thing you own, and the adjuster may have spent an hour on it. We take the time to document the full loss and stand in as your appraiser when the numbers don’t add up.

A homeowners claim usually starts well. The adjuster comes out, takes photos, and a few weeks later an estimate arrives. Then your contractor reads it and tells you it won’t cover the job. The roof is priced as a repair, the paint stops at one wall, the drywall line is short, or the pricing looks like it came from a different market. That’s the moment many Missouri homeowners start looking at the appraisal clause in their policy.

Where homeowner claims come up short

From St. Joseph to Cape Girardeau, the same patterns show up in residential estimates:

  • Partial scope. Repairs priced only where damage is obvious, leaving out adjacent materials that have to be removed or replaced to do the job right.
  • Matching. New siding, shingles or flooring that won’t match what’s left. How your policy handles that depends on its wording.
  • Depreciation. On actual cash value policies or before recoverable depreciation is released, heavy depreciation on labor or materials can make the check look small.
  • Overhead and profit. When a job needs a general contractor to coordinate three or more trades, contractor overhead and profit is often a reasonable line item.
  • Code items. Local building departments may require upgrades. Whether they’re paid often turns on an ordinance-or-law coverage limit.

Know your policy form

HO-3 and HO-5

The most common Missouri homeowners forms. The dwelling is usually written on an open-perils basis subject to exclusions, with replacement cost loss settlement. Many pay actual cash value first and the rest once repairs are done.

HO-6 condo unit policies

Condo owners have to sort out what the association’s master policy covers versus their own unit policy. That split comes from the declarations and bylaws, and it matters before anyone argues about price.

DP-3 and rental homes

Landlord policies often settle differently and may include lost rental income. We read the form before we estimate.

Not sure which you have? Our appraisal dictionary explains the terms on your declarations page.

How residential appraisal works with us

  1. Free first look. Send the carrier estimate and photos. We’ll tell you whether the gap looks like an amount-of-loss dispute.
  2. Inspection. We walk the house top to bottom: roof, attic, exterior elevations, every affected room.
  3. Our estimate. Line items, measurements and pricing that reflects your part of Missouri.
  4. Demand and panel. Once appraisal is invoked, we work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, an umpire.
  5. Award. The signed award sets the amount of loss under the policy terms.

For example, an illustrative claim might have a carrier estimate of $14,000 against a contractor bid of $31,000. Appraisal doesn’t guarantee you’ll get the higher figure. It gives both numbers a fair hearing on documented scope and pricing.

Missouri homeowner realities

Missouri sits in the path of spring hail, tornado outbreaks, summer derechos and winter ice. In rural counties a homeowner may wait weeks for a contractor after a big storm, and prices can climb as crews get booked. Older housing stock in places like Kansas City’s historic neighborhoods and St. Louis city brick homes can raise questions about plaster, old-growth trim and materials no longer sold. If your carrier won’t renew you, the Missouri FAIR Plan, formally the Missouri Property Insurance Placement Facility, is a fallback market, and its policies have appraisal provisions too.

If you have a complaint about how a claim is being handled, the Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance accepts consumer complaints. For legal questions, talk to a Missouri attorney.

When to call

The best time is after you have the carrier’s written estimate and before you’ve cashed out and moved on. Use our claim toolkit to organize what you have, then reach out.

Questions about homeowner claim appraisal

Can I use appraisal if my insurer already paid part of the claim?

Usually yes. Accepting a partial payment doesn’t typically waive appraisal, but read any release carefully and ask a Missouri attorney if you’re unsure before signing.

Does appraisal cover my contents too?

It can. If the disagreement includes personal property values, those items can be part of the appraisal as long as coverage for them isn’t in dispute.

How long does a homeowner appraisal take?

Many residential appraisals wrap up in a few months, though it depends on how quickly appraisers are named, how complex the loss is and whether an umpire is needed.
