Appraisal Readiness Score for Your Claim

Demanding appraisal before your file is ready can lock in a weak number. Answer nine questions to get a score and a short list of what to gather first.

Appraisal Readiness Score

Appraisal readiness score

9 questions · nothing stored

  1. Has the insurer accepted that the loss is covered (at least in part)?
  2. Do you have the insurer’s full, itemized estimate?
  3. Do you have an independent, itemized estimate for the same scope?
  4. Is the gap more than about 10% or several thousand dollars?
  5. Do you have dated photos of the damage before repairs?
  6. Have you read the appraisal clause in your own policy?
  7. Are you well inside your policy’s suit-limitation period?
  8. Have you kept receipts for emergency repairs and extra living costs?
  9. Can you describe exactly which items or prices you disagree with?

What to fix first

    For planning only. Results depend on the figures you enter and your policy’s terms. They aren’t a quote, an appraisal or legal advice, and example figures are illustrative.

    Why readiness matters

    An appraisal panel can only work with the evidence in front of it. The questions here are weighted toward what decides most awards: a clear coverage position, two itemized estimates for the same scope, and photos that show the damage before repairs.

    How the score works

    Each question carries a weight from one to three. Your score is the share of weighted points you’ve earned. A score of 75 or more suggests the file is in good shape. Below 45, it usually pays to fill the gaps before invoking the clause.

    Things the score can’t judge

    It can’t judge how strong your estimate is, how your policy is worded, or whether a legal issue such as a denial or a near deadline needs an attorney first. If coverage itself is disputed, appraisal usually isn’t the right tool. See questions answered for more. This tool is for planning only. It doesn’t interpret your policy, and the figures you enter are illustrative.

    Questions about this tool

    What score should I aim for?

    Seventy-five or more is a good sign. A lower score points to specific gaps, listed under the score.

    Does a high score mean I’ll win?

    No. It means your file has the basics an appraisal panel expects. The result depends on the facts and the estimates.

    Are my answers stored?

    No. The score is calculated in your browser and disappears when you leave the page.
