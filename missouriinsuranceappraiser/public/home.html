Why owners call

What a Missouri Insurance Appraiser does that the claim process doesn’t

Most underpaid claims in Missouri aren’t denied. They’re short: a missed slope on a hail-hit roof, gutters left off, siding that can’t be matched, or a code upgrade nobody priced.

The insurer’s adjuster writes one estimate. When yours says something different, you need more than an argument; you need an itemized valuation that stands up when someone checks it. That is the job of a Missouri Insurance Appraiser. We build the number from our own inspection and measurements, then use the appraisal clause to get it in front of a panel that can settle it.

Appraisal fits when

  • The insurer accepts the loss but disputes the cost
  • Repair-versus-replace is the sticking point
  • Matching or discontinued materials are at issue
  • Trades, code items or overhead and profit were left out

It usually doesn’t when

  • The whole claim was denied
  • Bad faith or vexatious refusal is the real issue
  • The gap is too small to cover fees
  • A deadline needs a Missouri attorney first

Not sure which side of the line you’re on? A Missouri Insurance Appraiser will tell you on the first call, including when the honest answer is a supplement request or a lawyer.

The process

How a Missouri Insurance Appraiser moves a stuck claim

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here are the five steps, start to finish.

Read the full roadmap
  1. Step 1

    Free first look

    Send the insurer’s estimate and your photos. A Missouri Insurance Appraiser reviews the policy and tells you honestly whether appraisal makes sense.

  2. Step 2

    Measure and price it ourselves

    We inspect, measure and write an independent line-item estimate priced for your part of Missouri, not a markup of the insurer’s.

  3. Step 3

    Demand appraisal in writing

    If the gap is worth it, appraisal is invoked under your policy’s clause and we’re named as your appraiser within its time limit.

  4. Step 4

    Work the differences

    We go item by item with the insurer’s appraiser. Most disagreements shrink here, once both sides see the same evidence.

  5. Step 5

    Signed award

    Anything left goes to a neutral umpire. Two signatures out of three set the amount of loss, and payment follows the policy’s terms.

Storm history

The storms Missouri still talks about

These events shaped how insurers, contractors and owners in Missouri handle claims today. Every Missouri Insurance Appraiser file is a smaller version of the same story: damage, an estimate, and a question about what it really costs.

  1. 1811–12

    New Madrid earthquakes

    A series of major quakes centered in the Bootheel; the seismic zone is why earthquake coverage comes up in southeast Missouri.

  2. 1993

    The Great Flood

    Missouri and Mississippi river flooding reached homes and businesses from St. Joseph to St. Charles County and Cape Girardeau.

  3. 2001

    I-70 hailstorm

    An April storm along the I-70 corridor in the St. Louis area became one of the costliest hail events of its time.

  4. 2006

    St. Louis derecho

    July windstorms brought down trees and lines across the St. Louis area and left many without power for days.

  5. 2007

    January ice storm

    Freezing rain coated southwest Missouri, including Springfield, snapping limbs onto roofs and cars.

  6. 2011

    Joplin tornado

    On May 22 an EF5 tornado cut across Joplin, one of the most destructive tornadoes in U.S. history.

  7. 2019

    Jefferson City tornado

    An EF3 tornado struck the capital city on May 22, eight years to the day after Joplin, as river flooding built nearby.

  8. 2021

    December tornadoes

    A rare December outbreak included a tornado in the Defiance area of St. Charles County.

What does a Missouri Insurance Appraiser actually do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appraiser we inspect, write an independent line-item estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, present open items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question, the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When does it make sense to call a Missouri Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage has been accepted, you have the insurer’s itemized estimate, and the gap between it and a qualified contractor’s estimate is meaningful, often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Does appraisal decide whether my claim is covered?

Generally no. Appraisal sets the amount of loss. Coverage disputes, denials and questions under Missouri’s vexatious refusal statutes are legal matters for a Missouri attorney.

What does appraisal cost?

Most clauses say each side pays its own appraiser and splits the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my part of Missouri?

Yes. We work in all 114 counties and the City of St. Louis, from the Kansas City metro to the Bootheel.
