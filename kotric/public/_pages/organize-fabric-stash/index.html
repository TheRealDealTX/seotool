How to Organize Your Fabric Stash

OrganizationBy the Kotric TeamUpdated Oct 17, 20268 min read

A fabric stash is wonderful until you can't find anything in it. Then it becomes a pile of guilt you keep adding to. The good news: organizing fabric is a one-afternoon job, and keeping it tidy takes minutes a month once you have a system.

Step 1: Pull everything out

Yes, all of it — the bags in the closet, the box under the bed, the pile on the chair. You can't organize what you can't see, and you'll almost certainly find fabric you forgot you had.

Step 2: Purge honestly

Hold each piece and ask: Would I buy this today? If the answer is no, let it go. Damaged, musty or stained fabric goes in a textile-recycling bag. Good fabric you'll never use is a gift for a school, a charity sewing group or a local animal shelter.

Step 3: Sort in two levels

First sort by type, because that's how you shop your stash when starting a project:

  • Quilting cottons
  • Apparel fabrics (knits, rayon, linen, denim)
  • Home décor and heavy fabrics (canvas, upholstery)
  • Fleece, felt and minky
  • Scraps (see our scrap projects guide for sorting scraps by size)

Then sort each group by color, light to dark. It looks beautiful and makes coordinating fabrics effortless.

Step 4: Fold everything to one size

Uniform folding is the secret behind every photo-worthy stash. Wrap each piece around a comic-book board or a cut piece of foam board (about 7" × 10"), or fold to the width of your shelf:

  1. Fold the fabric selvedge to selvedge.
  2. Fold it again lengthwise to match the width of your board.
  3. Wrap the fabric around the board and pin the end.
  4. Slide the board out if you prefer — the folded bolt holds its shape.
  5. Stand bolts upright like books so you can see every one.

Tap a step to tick it off.

Step 5: Label with yardage

Pin a small tag to each piece with its length and width ("1 ¾ yd × 44""). When a pattern calls for a specific amount, you'll know in seconds whether you have enough. Our yardage calculator helps with that part.

Storage options compared

StorageProsCons
Open bookshelvesSee everything, easy to browseLight fades fabric; dust
Clear lidded binsDust-proof, stackableHarder to browse
Cube storage with fabric drawersTidy lookFabric hidden from view
Hanging file foldersGreat for fat quarters and patternsLimited capacity

Sunlight is fabric's enemy. If you use open shelving, keep it away from windows or hang a curtain over it.

Keep it organized

  • One in, one out: before buying, check your stash first.
  • Put away leftovers immediately, trimmed to standard sizes.
  • Do a 15-minute reset monthly.
  • Keep a running project list with the fabric pinned to each idea.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best way to fold fabric for storage?

Fold it selvedge to selvedge, then fold or wrap it around a board of a consistent size (about 7" × 10") so every piece stands upright like a book.

Should fabric be stored in plastic bins?

Clear, lidded bins keep dust and pests out. Avoid sealing damp fabric, and open bins occasionally so fabric can breathe.

How do I stop my fabric from fading?

Keep it out of direct sunlight. Store fabric in closed bins or cupboards, or cover open shelves with a curtain.
