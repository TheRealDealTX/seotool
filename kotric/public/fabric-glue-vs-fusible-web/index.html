Fabric Glue vs Fusible Web vs Hem Tape

Fabric CraftsBy the Kotric TeamUpdated Sep 7, 20267 min read

"No-sew" covers a lot of different products, and picking the wrong one is why some no-sew projects fall apart in the first wash. Here is how the main options really compare.

Quick comparison

MethodStrengthWashable?FlexibilityBest for
Permanent fabric glueHigh once curedYes, after 24–72 hGoodHems, patches, trims, appliqué edges
Fusible web (paper-backed)Medium–highYesSlightly stiffAppliqué, bonding two fabrics flat
Iron-on hem tapeMediumMostlyGoodTrouser and curtain hems
Hot glueMedium on fabricNoStiffDécor, wreaths, wrapped items
Knots and tiesHighYesExcellentFleece blankets, T-shirt bags, pillows

Permanent fabric glue

Fabric glue soaks into fibers and dries flexible. Apply a thin, continuous bead, press the layers together and weigh them down. The most common mistakes are using too much (it bleeds through and leaves stiff dark patches) and washing too soon — check the bottle, but most need at least 24 hours to cure before laundering.

Fusible web

Fusible web is a sheet of heat-activated adhesive, often with a paper backing. Trace a shape on the paper, iron it to the back of your fabric, cut it out, peel the paper and iron the shape onto the base fabric. It is the go-to for crisp appliqué shapes and for bonding two layers so they behave as one. Use the temperature your fabric can handle, press (lift and lower) rather than slide the iron, and use a pressing cloth.

Iron-on hem tape

Hem tape is fusible web in a narrow ribbon. Fold up your hem, slip the tape inside the fold, and press with steam for 10–15 seconds per section. It's the fastest way to shorten curtains or trousers, though repeated hot washes can loosen it. Pick the "heavy" version for denim and curtain-weight fabrics.

Hot glue

Hot glue sits on top of fabric rather than bonding with it, so it peels off flexible materials and won't survive the washing machine. It's perfect for things that never get washed: wreaths, wrapped jars, frames, headbands and décor.

Knots and ties

The oldest no-sew method of all. Fleece and T-shirt jersey don't fray, so you can cut fringe and knot layers together. Our fleece tie blanket tutorial walks through it.

Which should you choose?

  • Will it be washed often? Choose fabric glue, fusible web or knots.
  • Need a crisp shape or design? Fusible web.
  • Shortening a hem? Hem tape.
  • Making décor? Hot glue is fastest.

Frequently asked questions

Is fabric glue as strong as sewing?

Good permanent fabric glue is strong enough for hems, patches and most crafts once fully cured, but stitching is still stronger for seams that take a lot of stress, like bag handles or trouser crotches.

Can you wash fabric glued with hot glue?

No. Hot glue softens in warm water and peels off flexible fabric. Use permanent fabric glue or fusible web for anything that will be washed.

How long does fabric glue take to dry?

Most fabric glues are touch-dry in an hour or two but need 24–72 hours to cure fully before washing. Always check the label.
