17 Clever Ways to Upcycle Old Jeans

Fabric CraftsBy the Kotric TeamUpdated Oct 2, 20269 min read

Denim is one of the best fabrics a crafter can get for free. It is tough, it doesn't fray much, it softens with every wash, and nearly every household has a pair of jeans that no longer fits. Before you drop them in a donation bag, look at them as roughly a yard of heavy cotton twill, plus pockets, belt loops, rivets and seams you can reuse.

Below are 17 ways to give old jeans a second life, sorted from "scissors only" to "an hour at the machine".

Before you cut: how to take jeans apart

Wash and dry the jeans first so any final shrinkage happens before you measure. Then:

  1. Cut off the waistband just below the stitching. Keep it whole — it makes a great strap or handle.
  2. Cut along both sides of the inseam and outseam, close to the stitching. Felled seams are bulky; it is easier to cut them off than to unpick them.
  3. Save the pockets by cutting around them with a ½" margin of leg fabric.
  4. Keep the hems — the finished edge is handy for coasters and bag tops.

One adult pair of jeans usually yields four flat panels, each about 12–16" wide and 30–40" long.

Use a rotary cutter and a self-healing mat for denim. Scissors work, but long straight cuts through two layers are much faster and neater with a rotary blade.

No-sew denim ideas

1. Denim coasters

Cut 4" squares or circles, stack two with fabric glue between them and press under a heavy book overnight. Fray the edges with a pin for a soft fringe.

2. Braided rag rug

Cut the legs into 2" strips, knot three together and braid. Coil the braid flat and glue or whip-stitch the coils together. A small bath mat takes roughly three pairs of jeans.

3. Pocket organizers

Glue or staple saved back pockets to a board, a canvas or the inside of a cupboard door to hold pens, remotes, sunglasses or craft tools.

4. Denim-wrapped planters and jars

Wrap a strip of leg fabric around a can, jar or plastic pot and secure the overlap with hot glue. Add a strip of hem along the top as a finished edge.

5. Bookmarks and key fobs

Cut a strip of waistband or seam, add a key ring through a punched hole or a grommet, and you have an almost indestructible key fob.

6. Frayed denim garland

Tie short strips of denim (and lighter fabric scraps) onto a length of twine for a rustic garland — a perfect no-sew project to do with kids.

Easy sewing ideas (beginner level)

7. Jean-leg tote bag

Cut one leg 16" long, sew the bottom closed, box the corners, and use the waistband as a handle. It is the quickest bag you will ever make.

8. Pot holders

Layer two denim squares with a piece of cotton batting or an old towel inside and quilt straight lines across. Denim alone is not heat-proof enough, so never skip the insulating middle layer.

9. Patchwork pillow cover

Different washes of denim — dark indigo, faded light blue, black — make a striking patchwork. Sew 5" squares into a grid and finish as an envelope pillow cover.

10. Apron

One leg panel becomes the bib, the back of the jeans becomes the skirt and the original pockets stay in place. Add a cotton tie at the waist and neck.

11. Pencil roll or tool roll

A long rectangle with a deep folded pocket stitched into channels holds pencils, brushes, crochet hooks or small tools. Tie it closed with a strip of waistband.

12. Dog toy

Braid three tight denim strips and knot both ends. Denim is one of the few fabrics that survives a determined chewer for a while — supervise play and toss it when it starts to shred.

Wearable upcycles

13. Jean shorts

The classic. Try them on, mark 1" longer than you want, cut straight across and wash to fray, or fold and stitch a cuff.

14. Denim skirt

Unpick the inseam, open the crotch into a triangle on the front and back, fill the gaps with denim cut from the legs, and topstitch.

15. Visible mending patches

Instead of throwing away jeans with a worn knee, back the hole with a contrasting patch and stitch over it with sashiko-style running stitches.

16. Kids' bucket hat

Denim holds a brim beautifully. Use a free bucket hat pattern and lay pieces out to include a fun seam or pocket detail.

17. Denim scrap quilt

Keep a bin of every leftover piece. Once you have a few pairs' worth, a heavy denim picnic quilt is warm, tough and perfect for the park.

Tips for sewing denim

  • Use a denim/jeans needle, size 90/14 or 100/16. A universal needle will skip stitches or snap at thick seams.
  • Lengthen your stitch to 3–3.5 mm and go slowly over bulky intersections.
  • Hammer thick seams flat before you sew across them — it really helps.
  • Topstitching thread in gold or rust gives you that classic "real jeans" look.

Frequently asked questions

How much fabric is in one pair of jeans?

An adult pair usually gives about ¾ to 1 yard of usable denim once the seams, waistband and pockets are removed — roughly four panels 12–16" wide.

Can you upcycle jeans without a sewing machine?

Yes. Coasters, braided rugs, garlands, wrapped jars, key fobs and pocket organizers only need scissors and fabric glue or a hot glue gun.

What needle do I need to sew denim?

Use a jeans/denim needle in size 90/14 for single layers and 100/16 for thick seams, with a slightly longer stitch length of 3–3.5 mm.
