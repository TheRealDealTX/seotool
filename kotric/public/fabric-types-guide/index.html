Fabric Types Guide: 14 Fabrics and What to Make With Them

SewingBy the Kotric TeamUpdated Aug 17, 20269 min read

Choosing the right fabric is half the battle. The same pattern can look professional in one fabric and sad in another. Here's a plain-English guide to the fabrics you'll meet most often.

Woven vs knit: the first big split

Woven fabrics are made of threads crossing at right angles. They have little stretch, fray when cut and are the easiest to sew. Knit fabrics are made of looped yarn like a sweater. They stretch, mostly don't fray and need a stretch stitch or a ballpoint needle.

Beginners: start with a stable woven cotton.

No-fray favorites for no-sew crafts

  • Felt — pressed fibers, no grain, no fray. Perfect for ornaments, kids' crafts and appliqué. Wool-blend felt is far nicer than acrylic.
  • Polar fleece — warm, stretchy, doesn't fray. The star of tie blankets.
  • Minky — ultra-soft plush; slippery to sew but luxurious for baby blankets.

Fiber content in one minute

  • Natural fibers (cotton, linen, wool, silk) breathe and take dye well but can shrink.
  • Synthetics (polyester, nylon, acrylic) resist wrinkles and shrinkage but melt under a hot iron.
  • Blends try to give you the best of both.

Not sure what a mystery fabric is made of? Snip a tiny piece and (safely, over a sink) hold it to a flame. Natural fibers burn to ash and smell like paper or hair; synthetics melt into hard beads and smell like plastic.

How to choose fabric for a pattern

  1. Read the pattern envelope — it lists suggested fabrics.
  2. Match the weight: light, medium or heavy.
  3. Check the drape by draping the fabric over your hand.
  4. Decide whether you need stretch.
  5. Always prewash natural fabrics before cutting.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest fabric for beginners to sew?

Medium-weight quilting cotton. It's stable, doesn't slip, presses crisply and is forgiving of small mistakes.

What fabric doesn't fray?

Felt, polar fleece, cotton jersey, minky and leather or faux leather don't fray, which makes them ideal for no-sew projects.

What's the difference between woven and knit fabric?

Woven fabric is made from threads crossed at right angles and has little stretch. Knit fabric is made from looped yarn and stretches.
