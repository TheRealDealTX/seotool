The Beginner Sewing Kit: 15 Essentials and What to Skip

SewingBy the Kotric TeamUpdated Sep 21, 20268 min read

You can spend a fortune on sewing gadgets, but a capable beginner kit fits in a shoebox and costs less than a takeaway dinner for two. Here is what genuinely earns its place, in the order you will reach for it.

Cutting tools

  1. Fabric scissors (8–9" dressmaker shears). Buy one good pair and never use it on paper — paper dulls the blades fast. Tie a ribbon on the handle so the household knows they're off-limits.
  2. Small snips or embroidery scissors for trimming threads close to the fabric.
  3. Seam ripper. You will use it more than you'd like. Everyone does.
  4. Rotary cutter and mat (optional at first). Worth it the moment you start quilting or cutting lots of straight strips.

Measuring and marking

  1. Flexible tape measure (60"/150 cm) for bodies and curves.
  2. Clear acrylic ruler, 6" × 24" is the most versatile size.
  3. Sewing gauge — a small ruler with a sliding marker, perfect for hems and seam allowances.
  4. Marking tools: a water-soluble pen or tailor's chalk. Always test on a scrap first.

Needles, pins and thread

  1. Hand-sewing needles — a mixed pack of "sharps" covers almost everything.
  2. Machine needles — universal 80/12 for cottons, plus a few ballpoint/jersey needles for knits and a denim needle for heavy fabrics.
  3. Glass-head pins; they won't melt under the iron. Fabric clips are great for thick layers and fabrics that show pin holes.
  4. A pincushion or magnetic pin dish.
  5. All-purpose polyester thread in white, black, grey and beige. Grey blends into a surprising number of fabrics.

Pressing

  1. An iron and ironing board. Pressing each seam as you go is the single biggest difference between "homemade" and "handmade".
  2. A pressing cloth — a scrap of plain cotton protects delicate or dark fabrics from shine.

Not sure which seam allowance a pattern means, or need to convert centimeters to fractions of an inch? Use the sewing unit converter.

What to skip (for now)

  • Serger/overlocker. Lovely, but a zigzag stitch finishes seams perfectly well while you learn.
  • Specialty presser feet. Your machine's zipper foot and standard foot are enough for the first year.
  • Huge thread collections. Buy thread to match each project instead.
  • Dress form. Useful only once you are fitting garments regularly.

Good first projects for your new kit

Start with straight seams: a pillow cover, a tote bag, a set of napkins or a scrunchie. Our step-by-step hand stitches guide covers the stitches you'll need, and how to sew on a button is a five-minute skill everyone should have.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important tool for a beginner sewer?

A sharp pair of fabric-only scissors. Clean cuts make every other step — pinning, sewing and pressing — easier and more accurate.

What thread should a beginner buy?

All-purpose polyester thread in white, black, grey and beige covers most projects. Buy specialty colors as each project needs them.

Do I need a sewing machine to start sewing?

No. Many small projects can be hand sewn, and no-sew methods like fabric glue and fusible tape work for others. A basic machine speeds things up once you're hooked.
