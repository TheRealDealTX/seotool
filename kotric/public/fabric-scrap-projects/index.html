25 Fabric Scrap Projects That Use Up Every Last Piece

Fabric CraftsBy the Kotric TeamUpdated Sep 28, 20268 min read

Every fabric project leaves something behind: strips, corners, odd triangles and the last six inches of a print you loved. The trick to using scraps is to sort them by size first, because the size of the scrap decides which project it suits.

Sort your scraps first

Scrap sizeBest for
Crumbs (under 2")Stuffing, confetti-style collage, "crumb" quilt blocks
Strips (1–3" wide)Rag garlands, braided rugs, binding, wrapped hoops
Squares (3–6")Coasters, patchwork, hair clips, fabric buttons
Large pieces (fat quarter and up)Bags, pillow covers, scrunchies, pouches

Keep each size in its own clear bin. Our guide to organizing a fabric stash explains a simple system that stops scraps from taking over.

Projects for tiny scraps

  1. Pet bed or floor-cushion stuffing. Fill a sturdy cover with snippets; it is denser than fiberfill and costs nothing.
  2. Fabric confetti collage. Scatter snippets on fusible web, cover with tulle, press and stitch — an instant art piece or card front.
  3. Crumb quilt blocks. Sew the smallest pieces together randomly, then trim to a square.
  4. Pin cushions. Stuff a small fabric pouch with snippets mixed with crushed walnut shells.
  5. Fabric-covered buttons. Cover-button kits need only a 1–2" circle.

Projects for strips

  1. Rag garland. Tie strips onto twine — fast, no sewing, great with kids.
  2. Wrapped embroidery hoop wreath. Wrap a hoop with strips and glue the ends.
  3. Braided coasters and rugs. Braid three strips, coil and glue or hand stitch.
  4. Scrappy binding. Join 2½" strips on the diagonal for a binding that frames a quilt in color.
  5. Bow hair ties. A knotted strip around a hair elastic takes two minutes.
  6. Fabric-wrapped pencils or clothespins. Mod Podge a strip around them for a gift set.

Projects for squares

  1. Coasters. Two 5" squares, a layer of felt, a quick topstitch.
  2. Patchwork pillow. Sixteen 5" squares make an 18" pillow front.
  3. Fabric hair clips. Cover a snap clip with a glued fabric strip.
  4. Mug rugs. A coaster big enough for a mug and a cookie — a perfect beginner quilting project.
  5. Lavender sachets. Two squares sewn on three sides, filled with dried lavender.
  6. Bunting. Cut triangles, glue or sew them over bias tape.
  7. Hand warmers. Fill a 4" pouch with rice; microwave for 20–30 seconds.

Projects for larger leftovers

  1. Scrunchies. A 4" × 20" strip and some elastic.
  2. Zip pouches. Two 8" × 10" rectangles and a zipper.
  3. Reusable produce bags. Lightweight cotton or old curtains work well.
  4. Fabric gift wrap (furoshiki). Any square at least 20" wide wraps a gift with no tape.
  5. Book covers. Protect a journal or school book with a fold-over fabric cover.
  6. Doll and teddy clothes. Kids' toys need tiny amounts of fabric.
  7. A scrappy quilt. Save your favorite leftovers and, eventually, they become a quilt full of memories.

Good scrap habits

  • Trim leftovers into standard sizes (2½" strips, 5" squares) as soon as a project ends — future you will thank you.
  • Be ruthless with pieces smaller than 1" that aren't going to be stuffing.
  • Donate what you won't use: schools, animal shelters and community quilting groups often welcome scraps.

Frequently asked questions

What can I do with very small fabric scraps?

Use them as stuffing for pet beds or floor cushions, for fabric-confetti collage, for crumb quilt blocks, or to cover small buttons.

How do you store fabric scraps?

Sort them by size — crumbs, strips, squares and large pieces — into separate clear bins. Trimming leftovers to standard sizes right after a project keeps them usable.

Can I recycle fabric scraps?

Many textile recycling points accept clean scraps, and schools, animal shelters and quilting guilds often take donations.
