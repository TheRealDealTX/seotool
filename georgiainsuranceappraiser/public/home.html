§ 01

Why property owners call a Georgia Insurance Appraiser

Most underpaid claims aren’t denied — they’re just short. A missing roof slope, a mismatched floor, a code item nobody priced. Georgia Insurance Appraiser exists for that gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

What we do

Value the loss independently

We inspect, measure and price the damage from scratch instead of editing the carrier’s numbers, so every line can be defended in front of an umpire.

When it fits

Coverage accepted, cost disputed

  • Repair vs. replace disagreements
  • Matching and discontinued materials
  • Missing trades, code items and O&P
  • Mitigation invoices cut back
When it doesn’t

We’ll tell you honestly

  • Outright coverage denials
  • Bad-faith or legal claims
  • Gaps too small to justify fees
  • Deadlines that need an attorney first

§ 03

One state, five very different claim climates

Ice in Blue Ridge, hail in Marietta, tornadoes in Macon, hurricanes in Valdosta and Brunswick. A Georgia Insurance Appraiser has to know how each region’s weather shows up on a roof — and in an estimate.

North Georgia Mountains

  • Ice & snow load
  • Frozen pipes
  • Wind on ridgelines
  • Falling hardwoods

Elevation brings winter weather the rest of the state rarely sees. Ice-laden limbs, collapsed gutters and burst pipes in seasonal cabins are the claims we see most in Rabun, Union, Fannin, Gilmer and Whitfield counties.

Communities: Rome, Dalton, Calhoun, Blue Ridge, Ellijay, Dahlonega

Metro Atlanta

  • Hail
  • Severe storms
  • Tornadoes
  • Water losses

The busiest claim market in the Southeast. Spring hail tracks through Cobb, Cherokee, Gwinnett and North Fulton, while in-town neighborhoods add historic homes and high-rise condos to the mix.

Communities: Atlanta, Marietta, Alpharetta, Sandy Springs, Roswell, Lawrenceville, Decatur

Piedmont & Middle Georgia

  • Tornadoes
  • Straight-line winds
  • Tree damage
  • Fire

Spring squall lines strengthen across the Piedmont. Mature oaks over historic homes in Macon and Athens mean tree impacts are as common as roof damage.

Communities: Athens, Macon, Columbus, Warner Robins, Milledgeville, LaGrange

South & Southwest Georgia

  • Hurricanes
  • Fallen pines
  • Farm structures
  • Tornadoes

Gulf hurricanes cross this region with surprising strength. Pine forests, pecan orchards and farm outbuildings create claims that blend residential, agricultural and commercial coverage.

Communities: Albany, Valdosta, Tifton, Thomasville, Moultrie, Americus

Coastal Georgia

  • Named storms
  • Wind-driven rain
  • Mold
  • Condo losses

Named-storm deductibles, wind-versus-flood questions and historic buildings define coastal claims from Chatham County to Camden County.

Communities: Savannah, Brunswick, St. Simons Island, Richmond Hill, Kingsland, St. Marys

§ 04

How appraisal moves a stuck claim

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here is how Georgia Insurance Appraiser takes a file from first call to signed award.

  1. Free claim review

    We read your policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos, then tell you whether appraisal fits.

  2. Independent valuation

    On-site inspection, measurements and a line-item estimate priced for your area.

  3. Written demand

    Appraisal is invoked in writing and we’re named as your appraiser within the policy’s deadline.

  4. Panel negotiation

    We work line by line with the carrier’s appraiser. Most disputes narrow sharply here.

  5. Award

    Unresolved items go to a neutral umpire. Two signatures set the amount of loss.

§ 08

Questions people ask a Georgia Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All FAQsClaim glossary
What does a Georgia Insurance Appraiser actually do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree a loss is covered but disagree on the cost. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

How is this different from a public adjuster?

A public adjuster manages and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate claims outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When should I call a Georgia Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s estimate, and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful — generally more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is the appraisal award binding?

Yes, on the amount of loss, once any two of the three panel members sign. That’s why documentation matters so much before you invoke appraisal.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work starts, and our first conversation is free.

Do you serve my part of Georgia?

Yes. We work statewide across all 159 counties, with regular work in metro Atlanta, Savannah, Augusta, Columbus, Macon, Athens and South Georgia.
