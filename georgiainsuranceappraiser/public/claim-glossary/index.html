Property Insurance Claim Glossary

36 terms you’ll see in policies, estimates and appraisal awards, defined in plain English.

ACDEHLMNOPRSTUX
Actual Cash Value (ACV)
Replacement cost minus depreciation. Many policies pay ACV first and the rest once repairs are complete.
Additional Living Expense (ALE)
Coverage D on a homeowners policy: the increase in living costs while your home is uninhabitable after a covered loss.
Amount of Loss
The dollar value of a covered loss. It’s the only question an appraisal panel normally decides.
Appraisal Award
The written decision of the appraisal panel, signed by at least two of the three members, setting the amount of loss.
Appraisal Clause
The policy provision that lets either party demand appraisal when they disagree on the amount of loss.
Causation
Whether damage was caused by a covered event. Some courts let panels consider causation; others treat it as a coverage question outside appraisal.
Coinsurance
A commercial-policy clause that reduces payment if the property was insured for less than a set percentage of its value.
Collateral Damage
Dents and marks on soft metals, gutters, vents and AC units that confirm hail size and direction.
Coverage A
Dwelling coverage — the house itself and attached structures.
Coverage B
Other structures — detached garages, fences, sheds and similar.
Coverage C
Personal property, or contents.
Debris Removal
Coverage for hauling away damaged property, often with its own sub-limit.
Declarations Page
The summary page of your policy listing coverages, limits and deductibles.
Depreciation
Reduction in value for age, wear and condition. Recoverable depreciation is paid back once repairs are made.
Detach & Reset
Removing an undamaged item to repair something else, then reinstalling it — a satellite dish or gutter guard, for example.
Demand Surge
Temporary spikes in labor and material prices after a major disaster.
Disinterested
Without a financial stake in the outcome. Many appraisal clauses require appraisers to be disinterested or independent.
Estimate Supplement
A request to add items or costs to an existing claim estimate.
Holdback
Recoverable depreciation withheld until repairs are complete.
Line Item
A single entry in an estimate — a quantity, unit, price and description for one task.
Matching
Whether repaired areas can reasonably match undamaged areas in color, texture and appearance.
Mitigation
Steps taken to prevent further damage after a loss — tarping, water extraction, board-up. Policies require reasonable mitigation.
Named-Storm Deductible
A deductible, often a percentage of Coverage A, that applies to damage from a named tropical storm or hurricane.
Ordinance or Law
Coverage for added costs of meeting current building codes when repairing or rebuilding.
Overhead & Profit (O&P)
A general contractor’s charge, commonly 10% and 10%, for coordinating multiple trades.
Percentage Deductible
A deductible calculated as a percentage of dwelling coverage rather than a flat dollar amount.
Proof of Loss
A sworn statement of the amount claimed. Policies often require one within a set time after the insurer asks.
Recoverable Depreciation
Depreciation the insurer will pay once repairs or replacement are completed within the policy’s time limit.
Replacement Cost Value (RCV)
The cost to repair or replace damaged property with materials of like kind and quality, without deducting depreciation.
Scope of Loss
The list of damaged items and the repairs required. Most appraisal disputes are scope disputes.
Square (Roofing)
100 square feet of roof surface. Roofing is measured and priced by the square.
Steep Charge
Additional labor cost for working on roofs above a certain pitch, often 7/12 or 8/12.
Suit Limitation
The policy deadline to file a lawsuit against the insurer, frequently two years from the loss in Georgia homeowner policies.
Test Square
A marked 10 × 10 foot area of roof where hail hits are counted and photographed.
Umpire
The neutral third member of an appraisal panel who decides items the two appraisers can’t agree on.
Xactimate
The estimating software most insurers and contractors use to price property claims.
