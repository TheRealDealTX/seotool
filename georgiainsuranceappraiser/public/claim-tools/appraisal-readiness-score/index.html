Appraisal Readiness Score: Is Your Claim File Ready?

The strongest appraisals are won before they start, with a complete file. Answer twelve quick questions to see where yours stands and what to fix first.

Appraisal readiness score12 questions · about two minutes
  1. Has the insurer accepted coverage for at least part of the loss?
  2. Do you have the carrier’s complete, itemized estimate?
  3. Do you have your own itemized estimate from a contractor or estimator?
  4. Do you have dated photos of the damage taken soon after the loss?
  5. Have you read your policy’s appraisal clause?
  6. Do you know your policy’s suit-limitation date?
  7. Have temporary repairs been made and receipts saved?
  8. Do you have your declarations page?
  9. Is all hidden damage opened up and documented?
  10. Is your correspondence with the carrier organized by date?
  11. Is the gap between estimates more than about 10% or $5,000?
  12. Has a sworn proof of loss been submitted if the carrier asked for one?

    How the score is weighted

    Questions about coverage, estimates and evidence carry the most weight because they decide whether appraisal is even the right tool and how well your number will hold up. Administrative items — correspondence, declarations — matter, but they’re easier to fix.

    What to do with your result

    • 75 and above: your file is in good shape. A professional review of the gap is the logical next step.
    • 45 to 74: close the open items first. Each one strengthens your position.
    • Below 45: invoking appraisal now could lock in a low number. Build the file before you demand.

    Questions about this tool

    Is my information saved or sent anywhere?

    No. The scorecard runs entirely in your browser and nothing is stored.

    Can I share my result with an appraiser?

    Yes — take a screenshot or note your to-do list and bring it to your consultation.
