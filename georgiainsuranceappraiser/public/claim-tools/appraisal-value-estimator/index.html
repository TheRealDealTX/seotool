Plug in the carrier’s estimate and yours, choose where an award might land, and add realistic fees. The estimator shows whether appraisal is likely to put more money in your pocket.

How to use the estimator

  1. Enter the insurer’s RCV total — the replacement cost figure before depreciation and deductible.
  2. Enter your estimate from a contractor or independent estimator, also at RCV.
  3. Move the slider to test outcomes. Try 30%, 50% and 80% to see a range rather than a single answer.
  4. Add fees. Use your appraiser’s quoted arrangement and a realistic umpire fee.

Why the slider matters

Appraisal awards rarely match either side’s opening number exactly. Well-documented line items tend to hold; inflated or unsupported ones tend to fall. If your result only works at 90% or 100%, the case is fragile. If it still makes sense at 40%, appraisal is more likely to be worth pursuing.

What the estimator leaves out

It doesn’t account for depreciation timing, policy limits, sub-limits or deductibles — those affect what you’re paid, not the size of the dispute. For holdback math, use the depreciation & holdback calculator.

Questions about this tool

Is a projected award a guarantee?

No. It’s a planning scenario. Actual awards depend on documentation, the panel and the policy.

What’s a typical umpire fee?

It varies widely with the size and complexity of the claim. Ask the appraisers for a quote once an umpire is proposed; the cost is usually split evenly.
