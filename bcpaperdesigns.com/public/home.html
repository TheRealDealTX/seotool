Anatomy of a party

The eight paper pieces that make a party

Every themed set we designed was built from the same eight pieces. Click through to see what each one does and the sizes that work.

piece 1 of 8

Welcome banner

The piece every photo ends up including. Ours were 5x7 or 8x10 pennants, one letter each, strung on clear line or baker's twine so the banner floats.

  • 5x7 or 8x10 pennants
  • 1 letter per flag
  • Clear line or twine
piece 2 of 8

Cake topper

A cut-out silhouette on two skewers turns a plain bakery cake into a theme cake. Size it to about 75% of the cake's width so it doesn't topple.

  • 5-7 in wide
  • Double skewers
  • Layered cardstock
piece 3 of 8

Cupcake toppers

Two-inch circles on toothpicks, backed with a second circle so the stick is hidden and the topper looks finished from every side.

  • 2 in circles
  • Backed and glued
  • 1 per cupcake + extras
piece 4 of 8

Centerpieces

A double-sided character on a skewer, tucked into a jar of flowers, candy or paper shred. One per table plus one for the food table.

  • 8 in characters
  • Double-sided
  • 1 per table
piece 5 of 8

Door sign

The first thing guests see. A welcome sign with the guest of honor's name tells them they're in the right place and sets the colors.

  • 8x10 sign
  • Ribbon hanger
  • Name personalized
piece 6 of 8

Invitations

Flat 5x7 cards on heavy stock with the date, time, place and RSVP. Mailed 4-6 weeks ahead for showers, 3-4 weeks for birthdays.

  • 5x7 flat cards
  • A7 envelopes
  • Digital option
piece 7 of 8

Favor tags

Small scalloped tags with a thank-you line, tied to favor bags with twine. The cheapest piece that makes favors look intentional.

  • 2 in tags
  • Twine or ribbon
  • Hole-punched
piece 8 of 8

Thank-you cards

Folded cards that match the party, left blank inside for a handwritten note after the gifts are opened.

  • A2 folded cards
  • Blank inside
  • Matching envelopes

Good to know

Party planning questions

What kinds of party themes do you cover?

Baby showers and kids' birthday parties: elephants in pink, blue and yellow, woodland and safari animals, whales and ducks, trucks, dinosaurs, monsters, reptiles, sports and holiday birthdays. Every theme has its own guide with a palette, decor list and checklist.

Are the party tools really free?

Yes. The banner maker, food calculator, planning timeline, theme finder and bingo generator all run in your browser, need no account and print on a home printer.

What paper should I use for printable party decorations?

Use heavy cardstock: 65-80 lb for most home printers, and 100-110 lb if your printer has a straight paper path. Our original sets were printed on 110 lb cardstock so banners and toppers never curled. Our guide to printing party printables at home covers settings and cutting.

How far ahead should I start planning a baby shower?

Six to eight weeks is comfortable. Invitations go out four to six weeks before, and decorations and favors can be made in the final two weeks. Our planning timeline builds a dated checklist from your party date.

Can you make a custom design for my party?

Tell us about your theme, colors and date at info@bcpaperdesigns.com and we will reply with ideas and what we can do.
