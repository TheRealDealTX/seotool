Yellow Elephant Baby Shower Banner

One well-made baby shower banner sets the whole room; here is how to size, word and hang it.

Plan this party Make the banner

A baby shower banner is the first thing guests photograph, so it is worth getting right. Our yellow elephant design was made for gender-neutral parties: grey letters on yellow pennants, with an elephant at each end holding the string in its trunk. It reads as a baby banner from across the room without saying boy or girl.

We printed every pennant on 110 lb cardstock so the letters stayed flat, punched two holes at the top for clean hanging, and could personalize the wording with any message. Below is everything we learned about sizing, wording and hanging an elephant baby shower banner.

What goes in the yellow elephant baby shower banner paper set

Main letter banner

Five-by-seven pennants, one letter each, in yellow with grey lettering.

Elephant end flags

Two shaped pennants with an elephant holding the string, one at each end.

Spacer flags

Small grey chevron or heart flags placed between words.

Mini banner

A 3-inch pennant version for the cake, strung between two skewers.

Name banner

A second line with the baby’s name or the parents’ last name.

Chair banner

Three pennants reading “Mom to Be” for the guest-of-honor chair.

Gift table sign

A 5x7 standing sign with a small yellow elephant.

Hanging kit

Clear string or twine, mini clothespins and removable hooks.

Choosing the right banner size

Measure the wall first. A banner should span about two thirds of the space it hangs over. As a guide, “Baby Shower” in 5x7 pennants with a gap between words runs about 6 feet; in 4x6 pennants it is about 4.5 feet. For a mantel or a dessert table under 5 feet wide, the smaller size looks better.

Count letters plus spacers before you print. “Welcome Baby” is 11 letters, “Oh Baby” is 6, and “Hello Little One” is 14, which is long for a single wall.

Wording ideas for a gender-neutral party

For a neutral party we like “Oh Baby,” “Hello Baby,” “Welcome Little One,” “Baby on Board,” or the family name: “Baby Johnson.” If parents reveal the sex at the shower, add a second short banner that you hang afterward. Yellow and grey baby shower decorations look best with short wording, so the elephant ends get room to breathe.

Make it yourself: printed pennant banner

You can design and print matching letters with our free party banner maker, then assemble them with this method.

  1. Print each letter on yellow 110 lb cardstock or the heaviest stock your printer takes.
  2. Cut pennants with a paper trimmer for straight sides, then trim the point with scissors.
  3. Punch two holes 1 inch from the top corners of every pennant.
  4. Lay all pennants face down in order and thread string through the back, in one hole and out the other.
  5. Leave 2 inches between pennants and a 6-inch gap between words.
  6. Add the elephant end flags last and leave 18 inches of string at each end for tying.

Hanging without damaging walls

Use removable adhesive hooks at each end and one in the middle for banners over 6 feet; a slight swag looks more intentional than a tight line. Hang it at least 12 inches above the top of the cake so it frames photos instead of cutting through them. More banner styles live in our banners collection.

Yellow Elephant Baby Shower Banner FAQ

How long should a baby shower banner be?

About two thirds of the width of the wall or table it hangs over. For most dessert tables that is 4 to 6 feet.

Can I reuse an elephant baby shower banner?

Yes. If you hang it with clothespins rather than glue, you can swap letters and reuse the elephant banner for a first birthday or a sip-and-see.

What paper is best for a homemade baby banner?

Use 80 lb to 110 lb cardstock. Thinner paper curls in humid rooms and shows the string through it.
