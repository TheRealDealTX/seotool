Pink Elephant Baby Shower Decorations

Blush pink, soft grey and a little gold: a gentle elephant shower that feels calm, sweet and grown-up.

Plan this party Make the banner

Pink elephant baby shower decorations were some of the most requested sets we ever made, and it is easy to see why. Elephants stand for memory, family and a mother’s protection, and in blush pink and warm grey they feel tender without tipping into candy-sweet. This page collects the pieces, colors and ideas we used for a pink elephant baby shower, so you can recreate the look at home or mix it with what you already have.

Our originals were printed on heavy 110 lb cardstock, hand-cut and assembled with skewers and hot glue so nothing curled or drooped by the time guests arrived. Every set could carry any message, from the mom-to-be’s name to the baby’s initials. The same approach works for a DIY version: print on the heaviest stock your printer accepts and give yourself an evening for cutting.

What goes in the pink elephant baby shower paper set

Welcome banner

Ten to twelve 5x7 pennants spelling “Baby Shower” or “Oh Baby,” alternating blush and grey, strung on clear line across the gift table.

Door sign

An 8x10 grey elephant holding a pink balloon with the mom’s name underneath, hung with a ribbon loop at eye level.

Elephant cake topper

A 6-inch elephant silhouette on two skewers, sized for an 8-inch round so it does not dwarf the cake.

Cupcake toppers

Two-inch circles with a tiny elephant and heart, backed with a second layer of cardstock so the toothpick stays hidden.

Pink elephant centerpieces

Double-sided 8-inch elephants on wooden skewers tucked into mason jars of pink carnations, one per guest table.

Invitations

Flat 5x7 cards with a mama and baby elephant, printed on 110 lb stock and mailed four to six weeks ahead.

Favor tags

Two-inch scalloped tags reading “Thanks for showering baby with love,” tied to favor bags with grey baker’s twine.

Food labels

Folded 3.5x2 tent cards so every dish has a name and an allergy note.

Thank-you cards

Folded A2 cards with a single small elephant on the front, left blank inside for the mom’s handwriting.

Setting a blush-and-grey elephant scene

Pick one wall as the focal point, usually behind the dessert table, and build everything there first. A grey tablecloth or runner, the banner at roughly 5 feet high, and a cluster of pink and white balloons on one side give you a backdrop that photographs well with the mom-to-be standing in front of it.

Keep gold to small accents: the cake topper edge, napkin rings or confetti sprinkled down the table. Too much gold pulls the look toward a first birthday; a little makes it feel finished. In a bigger room, repeat baby elephant baby shower decorations at the entry, gift table and food table so the theme reads from anywhere.

Pink elephant baby shower cake and dessert table

A pink elephant baby shower cake does not need fondant sculpture. A two-tier naked cake with blush buttercream, a grey ribbon at the base and our skewered elephant topper looks just as special. Surround it with cupcakes at three heights using cake stands or upturned bowls under a cloth. Our party food calculator will tell you how many cupcakes and plates to plan for your guest count.

Make it yourself: layered elephant garland

This garland hangs under the main banner and adds depth. You need grey and blush cardstock, a 4-inch elephant template, a glue stick and clear fishing line.

  1. Print or trace 14 elephants on grey cardstock and 14 small ear shapes on blush.
  2. Cut them out, turning the paper rather than the blade.
  3. Glue a blush ear onto each elephant so the layers give a slight shadow.
  4. Punch one hole at the top of each back and thread onto fishing line, spacing them 4 inches apart.
  5. Tape a tiny dot of glue on the line behind each elephant so they stay evenly spaced.
  6. Hang it 6 inches below the welcome banner and let the ends drape slightly.

Menu and party games

On the menu

  • Pink lemonade with frozen raspberries in a glass dispenser
  • “Peanut” butter and jelly finger sandwiches cut with a heart cutter
  • Strawberry yogurt parfaits in small clear cups
  • Grey-and-pink macarons arranged in an elephant shape
  • Caprese skewers for a savory, fresh option
  • Roasted salted peanuts in mini paper cones

Games and activities

  • Baby shower bingo using cards from our bingo generator, printed on pink paper
  • “Never forget” memory game: guests study a tray of baby items for 30 seconds, then list them
  • Advice for mom written on elephant-shaped cards and clipped to a string
  • Guess the baby food with labels covered by small elephant stickers
  • A peanut-counting jar where the closest guess takes home a centerpiece

Pink Elephant Baby Shower FAQ

What is a pink elephant party?

It is a baby shower or birthday built around elephants in pink, usually paired with grey, white and a touch of gold. For a baby shower it nearly always signals a girl, and the elephant theme nods to family, memory and a mother’s care.

How many pink elephant baby shower centerpieces do I need?

Plan one centerpiece per guest table plus one for the food table. For five tables, make six.

Can I reuse elephant baby shower decorations for a first birthday?

Yes. Swap the banner wording to “Happy Birthday,” keep the centerpieces and add a number cake topper. Many families we worked with turned shower pieces into an elephant birthday party a year later.
