How to Make DIY Cupcake Toppers That Stand Up Straight

The sizes, punches, sticks and glue that make paper cupcake toppers look professional, plus food-safety basics and a quick assembly-line method for two dozen.

Cupcake toppers are the smallest piece of party decor and often the most photographed. A tray of 24 matching toppers turns plain store-bought cupcakes into part of the theme, and DIY cupcake toppers cost very little: a sheet or two of cardstock, a punch and a box of toothpicks. The difference between toppers that look homemade and toppers that look polished comes down to a few details: size, paper weight, how the stick is attached and whether the back is finished.

Supplies

  • Cardstock: 100 to 110 lb cover so toppers do not droop. We used 110 lb for every topper we made.
  • Printer (or plain colored cardstock and stickers, letter stamps or hand lettering).
  • Circle punch: 2 inch is the standard. A 2.5-inch punch works for large or jumbo cupcakes, 1.5 inch for minis.
  • Sticks: round wooden toothpicks, 4-inch lollipop sticks, or 6-inch bamboo skewers trimmed to length.
  • Adhesive: a low-temp glue gun, strong double-sided tape or glue dots.
  • Optional: scalloped punch for a layered backing, glitter cardstock, foam squares for dimension.

Which size to make

A topper should be a little smaller than the top of the cupcake so it sits inside the frosting swirl rather than hanging over it. Here are the sizes we recommend:

MiniAbout 1.75 in1.25 to 1.5 inToothpick30 to 35
StandardAbout 2.5 to 2.75 in2 inToothpick or 4-in stick12 to 20
JumboAbout 3.25 in2.5 to 3 in4-in stick or trimmed skewer6 to 9

For shaped toppers, like an elephant, a dinosaur or a number, aim for about the same height as a 2-inch circle. Tall, skinny shapes look best at 2.5 inches tall on a longer stick.

Step-by-step: 24 two-sided toppers

  1. Print two sheets. One sheet is the fronts, the second is the backs. For a simple back, print a solid color or pattern from the theme palette. Use the heavyweight paper setting and 100% scale, as covered in our guide on printing party printables at home.
  2. Punch or cut. Turn the punch upside down so you can see the design and center it. You should get 24 fronts and 24 backs.
  3. Set up an assembly line. Lay all the backs face down in rows of six, then set a stick on each with about 3/4 inch of the stick overlapping the paper.
  4. Glue the sticks. Add a small dot of hot glue or a strip of double-sided tape over the stick. Keep glue 1/8 inch from the edge so it does not squeeze out.
  5. Press on the fronts. Line up the front with the back, press for a few seconds and check that the stick is centered and straight.
  6. Let set. Leave them flat for at least an hour, or overnight if you used liquid glue.
  7. Add to cupcakes. Insert toppers no more than an hour before serving so frosting does not soften the paper. Push the stick down about 1 inch so it rests on the cake, not just the frosting.
Tip: Use a scrap of cardstock with a pencil line marked 3/4 inch from the edge as a guide, and every stick will sit at the same depth, so all 24 toppers stand at an even height.

Choosing sticks

  • Round toothpicks are the easiest and work for 2-inch toppers on standard cupcakes. Flat toothpicks are less sturdy and can twist.
  • Paper lollipop sticks (4 inch) look clean and are a good choice for white or pastel themes.
  • Bamboo skewers are strongest; cut them with sturdy kitchen shears or garden pruners and sand the cut end if it splinters.
  • Colored or striped sticks add a little extra polish and match the palette.

For cake toppers, which are larger (usually 5 to 7 inches wide), use two skewers spaced about 3 inches apart so the topper does not spin.

Food safety basics

Paper toppers are decorations, not food, so treat them that way:

  • Use only clean, new sticks and wash your hands before assembling.
  • Keep glue, glitter and ink away from the part of the stick that goes into the cupcake. Loose craft glitter is not edible; if you want sparkle, use glitter cardstock where the shine is sealed into the paper.
  • Remove toppers before serving young children, and never leave toothpicks or skewers in treats for toddlers and babies, as sharp sticks are a choking and poking hazard.
  • If you want a topper kids can eat, consider printed edible frosting sheets or fondant cutouts, which are a separate product sold for that purpose.
  • Store finished toppers in a clean, closed box away from dust until the party.

Design ideas

A topper is small, so keep it simple and readable:

  • Numbers: the child's new age in a bold font on a contrasting circle. For a first birthday, a big "1" on a polka-dot circle.
  • Initials or name: one letter reads better than a long name at 2 inches.
  • Theme silhouettes: a whale, owl, lion or truck in one solid color on a white circle.
  • Layered circles: a 2-inch printed circle on a 2.25-inch scalloped backing in an accent color.
  • Mix and match: alternate three designs across a tray, such as an animal, a number and a pattern, for a lively look.

Common problems and quick fixes

  • Toppers lean or flop over. The paper is too light or the stick is too short. Switch to 100 lb or heavier cardstock and push the stick at least 1 inch into the cake.
  • Glue strings show. Use a low-temp glue gun, let it cool for a few seconds after applying and pull the gun away in a quick circle to break the string.
  • Edges look fuzzy. Your punch is dull. Punch through a sheet of aluminum foil a few times to sharpen it, or switch to a fresh craft-knife blade.
  • Frosting soaks in. Whipped cream and very soft frostings soften paper quickly. Add toppers just before guests arrive, or use a firmer buttercream.
  • Colors do not match the cake. Print one topper as a test and hold it next to your napkins or frosting before printing the full sheet.

Storing and transporting toppers

Stick finished toppers upright in a block of floral foam or a shoebox lid with holes poked in it, so the paper never presses against anything. If you are bringing cupcakes to a venue, carry the toppers separately and add them on site; they travel much better than topped cupcakes.

How many to make

Plan one topper per cupcake, plus about 10 percent extra for breakage and a couple for the cake table display. Most hosts serve about 1.5 cupcakes per guest; our party food calculator works out cupcakes, plates and napkins from your guest count and party length.

Frequently asked questions

What size should cupcake toppers be?

For standard cupcakes, 2-inch toppers are the best fit. Use 1.25 to 1.5 inches for mini cupcakes and 2.5 to 3 inches for jumbo cupcakes so the topper sits inside the frosting swirl.

What can I use for cupcake topper sticks?

Round wooden toothpicks work for most 2-inch toppers. Paper lollipop sticks look neat, and trimmed bamboo skewers are strongest for larger toppers. Attach them with a low-temp glue gun or strong double-sided tape.

Are paper cupcake toppers safe on food?

They are safe as decorations when you use clean sticks and keep glue and glitter away from the part that touches the cake. Remove them before serving, especially for young children, and never eat the paper.
