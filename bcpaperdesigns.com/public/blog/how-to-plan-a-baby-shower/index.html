How to Plan a Baby Shower: A Week-by-Week Checklist

A calm, week-by-week baby shower plan: when to set the date, send invitations, order the cake, print decorations and prep the games, from 10 weeks out to party day.

If you have been asked to host, the good news is that learning how to plan a baby shower is mostly a matter of doing the right small task at the right time. A shower is usually a two- to three-hour daytime party with 15 to 35 guests, some light food, a couple of games and a gift opening. None of that is hard on its own. It only gets stressful when everything lands in the final week.

Below is the schedule we have used for years when helping families put showers together, counted backward from party day. If you want the dates filled in for you, enter your shower date in our party planning timeline and it will print a dated countdown checklist.

Before you start: four decisions that shape everything

Sit down with the parent-to-be (unless it is a surprise) and settle these four things first. Every later step depends on them.

  • Date and time. Most showers happen when the parent is 28 to 34 weeks along, comfortable enough to enjoy the party but not so close that baby might arrive first. Weekend afternoons between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m. work for most guests.
  • Guest count. Get a rough number, even a range like 20 to 25. It decides the venue, the food quantities and how many invitations to print.
  • Budget. Agree on a total and who is contributing. Co-hosts often split it evenly; write it down so nobody is surprised later.
  • Venue. A living room comfortably seats about 15 to 20 people; beyond that, look at a backyard, a church hall, a community room or a restaurant private room.

The week-by-week baby shower checklist

This is the core of the plan. If you are starting later than 10 weeks out, combine the first few rows and keep moving.

WhenWhat to do
10 weeks outConfirm date, time, venue and budget. Ask the parent-to-be for the guest list with mailing or email addresses.
8 weeks outPick the theme and color palette. Book the venue if it is not a home. Ask the parents whether they have a registry ready to share.
6 weeks outDesign or order invitations. Decide on the menu style (brunch, dessert table, light lunch). Line up any help: a friend to run games, someone to log gifts.
5 weeks outSend invitations with an RSVP date about 10 days before the party.
4 weeks outPlan games and activities. Order the cake or plan to bake. Make a decor list: banner, centerpieces, signs, cupcake toppers, favor tags.
3 weeks outBuy cardstock and supplies. Print and cut paper decorations. Buy or order favors.
2 weeks outFollow up on missing RSVPs. Finalize the menu and shopping list. Print game sheets and a sign-in sheet or advice cards.
1 week outConfirm final headcount with the venue and bakery. Assemble decorations. Buy non-perishable food and drinks. Plan the room layout.
2 days outGrocery run for fresh items. Prep make-ahead food. Pack a box with tape, scissors, pens, a lighter, trash bags and serving utensils.
Party daySet up 2 to 3 hours before start. Hang the banner first, then tables, then food. Leave the last 30 minutes for getting yourself ready.

Choosing a theme and palette

A theme is not required, but it makes every other choice faster: once you know it is a blue-and-grey elephant shower, you know which napkins, which balloons and which cake design to pick. Choose two main colors and one accent, and write the hex codes down so printed pieces match. A soft example: blush #F4B6C2, warm grey #9AA5B1 and gold #E8C07D.

If the parents are not sharing the baby's sex, or simply do not want pink or blue, a neutral palette like sage, cream and mustard works beautifully. We collected our favorite ideas in 21 baby shower themes that still feel fresh and in our guide to gender neutral shower ideas.

Invitations, RSVPs and registry details

Paper invitations should go out 4 to 6 weeks before the party; digital invitations can go 3 to 4 weeks out. Include:

  1. Who the shower honors (full name, and partner's name if it is a couples shower).
  2. Date, start and end time. Listing an end time helps guests plan and keeps the party from dragging.
  3. Full address, plus parking notes if they matter.
  4. Host name and an RSVP method with a firm date.
  5. Registry information, usually on a small separate insert card or a line at the bottom.
  6. Anything special: book instead of a card, diaper raffle, a dress code or a surprise warning.

A standard invitation is 5x7 inches and fits an A7 envelope. If you print at home, a sheet of 8.5x11 cardstock yields two 5x7 invitations with a little trim.

Food, cake and drinks

A shower is a grazing party, not a sit-down meal. For an early-afternoon party, plan on 4 to 6 savory bites per guest, one slice of cake or 1.5 cupcakes per guest, and about 3 drinks per guest over three hours. If the party lands over lunchtime, increase savory food by roughly half. Our party food quantity guide has a full per-guest table and a worked example you can copy.

Order a custom cake 2 to 3 weeks ahead; bakeries fill up quickly on spring and fall weekends. A non-alcoholic signature drink, such as sparkling lemonade with berries, is a nice touch since the guest of honor cannot share a cocktail.

Decorations you can make yourself

Decor is where a DIY host saves the most money. A shower usually needs only five pieces to look finished:

  • A welcome sign at the door, 8x10 or 11x14 inches.
  • A banner behind the dessert table or gift chair, 10 to 14 pennants long.
  • A cake topper, about 5 to 6 inches wide.
  • Centerpieces, one per table, 8 to 12 inches tall.
  • Cupcake toppers and favor tags, 2 inches across.

Print everything on heavyweight cardstock (we used 110 lb for all our sets) so pieces stand up without curling. Our tutorial on making a DIY party banner walks through paper, sizes, string and hanging.

Tip: Assemble decorations a week early and store them flat in a large pizza box or a shirt box. You will catch any missing piece while there is still time to reprint it.

Games, gifts and the party-day run of show

Plan two or three games at most; a shower that runs four or five games starts to feel like a program. Good picks are bingo during gift opening, a guessing game at the door and one quick group game. See 15 baby shower games guests actually enjoy for printable ideas, or print unique cards with our baby shower bingo generator.

For a three-hour shower, a simple run of show looks like this:

  1. 0:00 to 0:30 Guests arrive, sign in, fill out advice cards, get drinks.
  2. 0:30 to 1:15 Food is open; mingle.
  3. 1:15 to 1:45 One or two games.
  4. 1:45 to 2:30 Gift opening, with someone writing down who gave what.
  5. 2:30 to 3:00 Cake, favors and goodbyes.

After the party, give the parents the gift list, any leftover food and the advice cards. Then put your feet up. If you would like a design matched to a specific nursery or palette, you can always reach us at info@bcpaperdesigns.com.

Frequently asked questions

How far in advance should you plan a baby shower?

Start about 10 weeks before the date if you can. That leaves time to book a venue, send paper invitations 4 to 6 weeks out and make decorations without rushing. A small home shower can be pulled together in 4 weeks if needed.

Who traditionally hosts the baby shower?

Today, anyone close to the parent-to-be can host: a friend, sister, coworker or a parent. Many showers have two or three co-hosts who split the cost and the tasks, which makes the planning much lighter.

How long should a baby shower last?

Two to three hours is the sweet spot. That gives guests time to eat, play one or two games and watch the gift opening without the party dragging, and it is easier on the parent-to-be.
