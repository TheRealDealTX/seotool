How to Make a DIY Party Banner from Cardstock

Step-by-step instructions for a sturdy cardstock pennant banner: sizes, spacing, string length, cutting tools and hanging tricks that keep it from sagging.

A banner is the one decoration that makes a room read as a party the second people walk in. It is also the easiest thing to make well. A DIY party banner needs only cardstock, a printer or a template, scissors and some string, and in an evening you can have a 12-letter banner that looks as crisp as anything from a shop.

When we made banners for our party sets, every pennant was printed on 110 lb cardstock, hand cut and strung on clear or baker's twine. This guide shares the exact sizes and spacing we settled on after making a lot of them.

What you need

  • Cardstock: 8.5x11 sheets, 80 lb cover minimum, ideally 100 to 110 lb so the pennants hang flat and do not curl in humidity. Plan one sheet per two pennants.
  • Printer or letter stencils: an inkjet handles heavy cardstock best. If you do not want to design letters, our party banner maker lets you type any message, pick colors and print ready-to-cut pennants.
  • Cutting tools: sharp scissors work, but a paper trimmer or a craft knife with a metal ruler gives straighter edges.
  • Hole punch: a standard 1/4-inch single-hole punch, or a 1/8-inch punch for thin twine.
  • String: baker's twine, satin ribbon (1/4 inch wide) or clear fishing line for a floating look.
  • Extras (optional): glue dots or a glue stick for layering, mini clothespins, removable hooks or painter's tape.

Choosing pennant size and shape

The size depends on where the banner will hang and how far away people will read it. These are the sizes we use most:

Where it hangsPennant sizeLetter heightPer 8.5x11 sheet
Cake or dessert table backdrop4.5 x 6 in2.5 to 3 in2
Living room wall or fireplace mantel5.5 x 7 in3.5 to 4 in2
Large wall, porch or doorway8 x 10 in5 to 6 in1
High chair or small sign3 x 4 in1.5 to 2 in4 to 6

For shape, the classic triangle flag, the swallowtail (a V notch cut from the bottom) and the rectangle with a point are all easy to cut. Circles and scallops look softer for a baby shower but take longer to cut by hand unless you have a large circle punch.

Step-by-step: making the banner

  1. Write out your message and count the pennants. "HAPPY BIRTHDAY" is 13 letters; add one blank pennant between the words, or a small heart or star, for 14 pieces. A name like "OLIVIA" is 6.
  2. Pick colors. Alternate two background colors with a third color for letters, for example mint #A8DCD1 and coral #F28C7A with white letters. Keep letter contrast high so it reads from across the room.
  3. Print a test sheet on plain paper at 100% scale and check spelling and letter size before you use the good cardstock.
  4. Print on cardstock using your printer's heavy or matte photo paper setting and the rear or manual feed tray if it has one. Let the ink dry for 10 minutes before cutting.
  5. Cut the pennants. Trim the long straight edges with a paper trimmer first, then cut the angled sides with scissors. Cutting a stack of more than two sheets at once usually makes edges shift.
  6. Punch the holes. Place two holes about 1/2 inch from the top corners. Make a cardboard template with the holes marked so every pennant matches.
  7. String it. Thread from the back through the left hole and out the front, then back in through the right hole, so the string runs behind the letter and the pennant hangs straight. Space pennants about 1 inch apart.
  8. Lock the spacing. Add a small dot of glue or a piece of tape on the back of each pennant where the string passes, so letters do not slide together when you hang it.
Tip: Cut the string much longer than you think. Leave at least 24 inches of tail on each end so you can tie it to hooks, wrap it around a curtain rod or adjust the swag on party day.

How much string to buy

Use this rough formula: (number of pennants x pennant width) + (number of gaps x 1 inch) + 48 inches of tails. A 14-pennant birthday banner with 5.5-inch-wide pennants needs about 77 + 13 + 48 = 138 inches, so a little under 4 yards. If you want a deep swag in the middle, add another 12 inches.

Clear fishing line in the 10 to 20 lb range is nearly invisible and holds heavy cardstock with no sag. Baker's twine is the most forgiving to work with and adds color. Ribbon looks the most finished but slides, so the glue-dot step matters more.

Hanging it so it does not sag

  • Use removable adhesive hooks rated for at least 1 lb, placed at the same height. A level or a phone level app helps.
  • For a straight line, pull the string taut and tie off; for a gentle swag, let the middle drop 3 to 5 inches below the ends.
  • Hang the banner at least 12 inches above the top of the cake so candles and hands stay clear.
  • Outdoors, punch a small hole near the bottom point of every third pennant and run a second, thinner thread to stop wind flipping letters over.
  • If your wall is textured, painter's tape plus a hook is gentler than adhesive strips.

Upgrades that make it look store-bought

Layering is the fastest upgrade. Print letters on a white circle, cut it out and glue it centered on a colored pennant with foam dots so it lifts about 1/8 inch. Glitter cardstock for the backing pennant adds sparkle without glitter on the floor. You can also tuck a few tissue tassels or paper hearts between words, like the clothespin garland in the photo above.

For a theme party, swap the plain pennant for a shape: an elephant silhouette for a shower, a dump truck for a construction birthday or scalloped clouds for a hot air balloon party. You can see matching banners in every theme in our banner collection, and our kids birthday party themes post has palette ideas by age.

Message ideas by occasion

  • Birthday: "HAPPY BIRTHDAY," the child's name, "[NAME] IS 5" or a short theme line like "ROAR, I'M THREE."
  • Baby shower: "OH BABY," "WELCOME LITTLE ONE," "BABY [LAST NAME]" or "HELLO BABY."
  • High chair: "ONE" or the baby's first name in 3x4-inch pennants.

Shorter messages read better from a distance. If a message runs past 16 letters, split it into two banners hung one above the other, about 8 inches apart.

Storing and reusing your banner

A "HAPPY BIRTHDAY" banner in neutral colors can come out year after year. Lay it flat between two pieces of cardboard, or wind it loosely around a paper towel tube with tissue between pennants. Name banners can be reused for a sibling's party by adding a new name strand. If your printables are coming out faded or the wrong size, our guide to printing party printables at home covers printer settings and scaling.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best paper for a DIY party banner?

Heavyweight cardstock between 100 and 110 lb is ideal because it hangs flat, resists curling and survives being reused. 65 lb cardstock works for a one-time indoor banner but tends to bend and flutter.

How far apart should banner pennants be?

About 1 inch between pennants reads well for most banners. Leave a blank pennant or a 4 to 5 inch gap between words so the message is easy to read from across the room.

How do I keep banner letters from sliding together?

Thread the string behind each pennant through two holes, then secure it with a glue dot or a small piece of tape on the back. That locks the spacing so the letters stay put once the banner is hung.
