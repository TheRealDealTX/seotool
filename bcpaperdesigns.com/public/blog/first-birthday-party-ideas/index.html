First Birthday Party Ideas for a Happy, Easy Celebration

Themes, smash cake tips, nap-friendly timing and decor that doubles as a photo backdrop: practical first birthday party ideas that keep it fun for everyone.

A first birthday is really a party for the grown-ups who made it through the year, with a very small, very confused guest of honor. The best first birthday party ideas keep that in mind: short, scheduled around naps, with a cake the baby can dig into and a backdrop worth photographing. Your one-year-old will not remember the details, but you will look at the pictures for decades.

Start with timing, not the theme

The single biggest factor in how the party goes is the baby's schedule. A well-rested one-year-old is charming; an overtired one is not.

  • Schedule around the nap. If your baby naps from 12:30 to 2:30, start the party at 3:00 so they wake up, eat and are fresh when guests arrive. Morning naps point to a 10:00 or 10:30 brunch.
  • Keep it to 1.5 to 2 hours. That is long enough to eat, do the cake and open a few gifts.
  • Cake comes about 45 minutes in. Do it before the baby starts to fade, and before guests with little kids of their own need to leave.
  • Keep the guest list realistic. Family plus a few close friends, often 15 to 30 people, is plenty at home. Count babies and toddlers separately so you know how many high chairs and play spaces you need.

The smash cake: what to know

The smash cake is the main event. A few practical notes:

  1. Size: a 4-inch round, two layers, is plenty. A 6-inch cake looks dramatic but is a lot of sugar and frosting for a baby.
  2. Frosting: whipped cream or a lightly sweetened cream cheese frosting is softer and less sticky than buttercream. Dark food coloring can stain skin and clothes for a day or two; pastel shades are gentler.
  3. Allergies: if your baby has not had eggs, dairy or wheat yet, talk to your pediatrician first, and consider having baby try them before party day.
  4. Setup: lay a plastic tablecloth or a splat mat under the high chair, strip baby to a diaper and a cute bib, and have a towel and wipes within reach.
  5. Toppers: remove paper toppers, candles and skewers before the cake goes in front of the baby. Keep the "1" topper for the photo, then pull it.
Tip: Have one person take photos and a second person take short video. Parents are always busy helping, and the first reaction to cake lasts only seconds.

Decorations that double as a photo backdrop

You do not need to decorate the whole house. Focus on three spots: the front door, the cake table and the high chair.

  • High chair banner. A short "ONE" or name banner with 3x4-inch pennants clipped to the tray edge.
  • Cake table backdrop. A "Happy Birthday" or name banner hung 12 to 18 inches above the table, plus a cluster of balloons in 5-inch, 11-inch and 18-inch sizes.
  • Monthly photo display. String twelve 4x6 prints, one per month, on twine with mini clothespins. Guests love it, and it takes 20 minutes.
  • Cake topper. A 5-inch "1" or name topper on 110 lb cardstock stands straight without wobbling.
  • Cupcake toppers. 2-inch circles on toothpicks for the adults' cupcakes; see our DIY cupcake topper guide.

Food that works for every age

Serve food that adults enjoy and that can be torn or cut small for little ones. A grazing table with fruit, cheese cubes, crackers, mini sandwiches and veggies works at any hour. For a brunch party, add a quiche or egg bake and muffins. Plan on about 1.5 cupcakes per adult in addition to the smash cake, and set aside soft finger foods for babies, like banana slices, steamed sweet potato cubes and puffs.

Skip whole grapes, nuts, popcorn and hard candy on any table toddlers can reach, as these are choking hazards for young children. Cut grapes and cherry tomatoes into quarters lengthwise. Simple activities for babies and guests

  • Time capsule. Guests write notes for the baby to open at 18. Seal them in a box with a newspaper from the day.
  • Wishes for the year. Cards that ask "I hope you learn to..." clipped to a string like a garland.
  • Baby play zone. A blanket with soft blocks, board books and a few balls, away from the food.
  • Guest book. A board book or a photo book signed by guests becomes a keepsake.
  • Bubble machine. Outdoors, it entertains babies and toddlers for a surprising amount of time.

Favors and thank-yous

Favors are optional at a first birthday, but a small one is a nice way to send guests off. Ideas that work for both adults and kids: a cookie decorated in the theme colors with a 2-inch favor tag, a mini jar of honey or jam, a packet of wildflower seeds labeled "Watch me grow," or a board book for each child guest. Write thank-you notes within two to three weeks, and include a photo from the cake smash; grandparents especially love it.

A sample two-hour first birthday

  1. 0:00 Guests arrive; drinks and grazing table open.
  2. 0:30 Time capsule notes and play area.
  3. 0:45 Happy birthday song and smash cake.
  4. 1:05 Baby gets cleaned up; adults have cupcakes.
  5. 1:20 A few gifts opened, or save them for later.
  6. 1:45 Favors and goodbyes before the next nap.

Frequently asked questions

What time should a first birthday party start?

Plan the start time around naps. If your baby naps after lunch, a 3:00 p.m. start works well; if they take a morning nap, a 10:00 or 10:30 a.m. brunch is better. Keep the party to about two hours.

How big should a smash cake be?

A 4-inch round cake with two layers is plenty for a one-year-old. Use a soft whipped or light cream cheese frosting and remove any toppers, candles or sticks before setting it in front of the baby.

Do you open gifts at a first birthday party?

It is optional. Many families open a few gifts at the end, or wait until after the party so the baby is not overwhelmed. If you skip it, mention on the invitation that gifts will be opened later.
