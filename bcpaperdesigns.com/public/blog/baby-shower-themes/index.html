21 Baby Shower Themes That Still Feel Fresh

Twenty-one baby shower themes with real color palettes and decor ideas, plus a quick table to match each theme to the parents and the season.

Picking from all the baby shower themes out there can feel like the hardest part of planning, mostly because there are so many. The trick is to choose one that says something about the parents: the nursery they are building, an animal they love, the books they plan to read aloud. A good theme also makes every other decision faster, from the napkins to the cake.

Below are 21 themes we have designed for or styled over the years, grouped loosely by feel. Each one includes a palette you can hand to a baker or match when you print decorations.

Elephant themes: the forever favorite

1. Pink elephant. Blush #F4B6C2, elephant grey #9AA5B1 and a touch of gold. Hang a scalloped banner, set mini elephant figurines on the dessert table and add peanut-shaped cookies. See our pink elephant baby shower decorations.

2. Blue elephant. Powder blue and soft grey with white polka dots. Elephant cupcake toppers and a "Oh Baby" cake topper are all you need. Ideas are on the blue elephant baby shower page.

3. Yellow and grey elephant. Butter yellow #F6D365 with charcoal and white is cheerful and neutral. It pairs well with chevron or polka dot patterns; start with the yellow and grey elephant decorations.

Animal and nature themes

4. Whale. Navy, teal and white with a few wave-pattern napkins. A "Whale hello there, baby" door sign sets the tone. Browse the navy and teal whale shower ideas.

5. Coral owl. Coral and mint feel fresh in fall without leaning on orange. Owl-shaped favor tags and "Look whooo's having a baby" signs are classics. See the coral owl theme.

6. Woodland. Foxes, deer and hedgehogs in rust, sage and kraft brown. Wood slices work as cake stands and candle bases. Ideas live on the woodland baby shower page.

7. Jungle safari. Olive, tan and mustard, with palm leaves and animal cutouts at different heights on the dessert table. See jungle safari baby shower decorations.

8. Pink and gold giraffe. Blush and gold foil feel a bit more glam; giraffe print works on a cake ribbon or table runner. Start with the pink and gold giraffe theme.

9. Lion cub. Navy, gold and tan with a soft lion face on the cake topper. Our lion king baby shower page shows how to style it with generic lion art; it is inspired by the theme and not affiliated with or endorsed by the rights holder.

10. Sea turtle. Aqua, sand and coral; serve "turtle" chocolates and blue punch. See the pink turtle shower ideas.

11. Ladybug. Red and black dots on white, perfect for a garden party in May or June. Ideas are on the ladybug theme page.

Playful themes

12. Hot air balloon. Paper balloons hanging at three or four heights over the dessert table make a striking display. See pink hot air balloon shower decorations.

13. Rubber duck in blue and yellow. Float rubber ducks in a clear drink dispenser or a tub of ice with juice boxes. Ideas: blue and yellow duck shower.

14. Pink rubber duck. The same theme with pink and yellow; see the pink rubber duck version.

15. Dinosaur. Soft greens and orange keep dinosaurs baby-friendly. See dinosaur shower decorations.

16. Little shark. Ocean blues and grey, with fin-shaped cupcake toppers; great when older siblings want to help. See the baby shark shower ideas, designed around generic sharks and not affiliated with or endorsed by the rights holder.

17. Ponies. Lavender, pink and mint with pastel manes and stars.

Story and keepsake themes

18. Storybook. Guests bring a favorite children's book with an inscription instead of a card, building the baby's first library. Pair it with book-page garlands and printable games; see the book themed baby shower and the book shower games.

19. Twinkle twinkle little star. Navy and gold with paper stars on fishing line; lovely for evening or winter showers.

20. Botanical greenery. Eucalyptus, sage #B7C9A8 and cream with very little printed decor; let real greenery and a simple banner carry it.

21. Rainbow. Muted pastels rather than primary colors keep it soft. It is a meaningful choice for a rainbow baby.

Making any theme feel fresh

Even a familiar theme feels new when you change one or two details. A few approaches that work:

  • Shift the palette. An elephant shower in sage and terracotta feels completely different from one in pink and grey.
  • Go smaller on characters. Use the animal as an accent on the cake topper and favor tags, and let pattern and color carry the room.
  • Personalize the wording. A banner with the baby's chosen name or the family's last name makes even a classic theme feel specific to these parents.
  • Use real textures. Wood slices for woodland, sand and shells for turtles, real books for storybook. Mixed with paper decor, they make the table feel layered.

How to pick the right theme

  1. Start with the nursery. If the parents have a nursery palette, match it; the decorations can go up in the baby's room afterward.
  2. Check the season. Owls and woodland suit fall, ladybugs and balloons suit spring, whales and turtles suit summer.
  3. Ask about pink and blue. If the parents are keeping it neutral, see our gender neutral baby shower ideas.
  4. Think about the venue. Bold themes hold up in big halls; soft palettes shine in small homes.
Tip: Give your baker the hex codes for your two main colors. Frosting rarely matches perfectly, but it gets far closer than "light pink."

Frequently asked questions

What is the most popular baby shower theme?

Elephant themes have been a long-running favorite because they work in pink, blue, yellow or grey and feel sweet without being cartoonish. Woodland, safari and hot air balloon themes are also widely used.

Do you need a theme for a baby shower?

No, but a theme makes shopping and decorating much easier. Even a simple color palette of two main colors and one accent will make the room look planned and pulled together.

What baby shower themes work for twins?

Pairs come naturally with animal themes: two elephants, two owls or two ducks. Use one palette with two accent colors, one for each baby, if the parents know the sexes.
