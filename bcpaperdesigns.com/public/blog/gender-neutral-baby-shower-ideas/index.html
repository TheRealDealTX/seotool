Gender Neutral Baby Shower Ideas: Themes, Colors and Decor

Palettes with hex codes, eight themes that skip pink and blue, and decor, menu and game ideas for a warm, gender neutral baby shower.

Plenty of parents choose not to find out the sex before birth, and many more simply do not want a party built around pink or blue. Either way, gender neutral baby shower ideas give you more freedom, not less. Without the default palette, you can match the nursery, the season or the parents' own style, and the decorations can go straight into the baby's room afterward.

Start with a palette

A neutral shower looks intentional when the colors are chosen on purpose. Pick one palette, write down the hex codes and use them for the invitations, banner, cake and balloons. These are six palettes we come back to:

Tip: Add one soft accent color, like the blush of a rose or a touch of gold. A palette that is all beige and white can look washed out in photos; one accent gives the eye somewhere to land.

Eight gender neutral themes

  1. Yellow and grey elephant. The easiest neutral theme there is. Grey elephants on butter yellow read as baby without any pink or blue. See our yellow and grey elephant decorations and the matching gender neutral elephant banner.
  2. Woodland. Foxes, owls and hedgehogs in rust and sage, with wood slices and greenery. Kraft paper and pinecones fill in the gaps for very little money.
  3. Jungle safari. Olive, tan and mustard with palm leaves and animal cutouts. Hang animal cutouts at different heights so the table has depth.
  4. Rubber duck. Yellow ducks with sky blue water feel playful, not gendered. Swap the blue for mint if you want to steer fully clear.
  5. Little lamb or "sweet as can be." Cream, sage and kraft paper with cloud shapes.
  6. Twinkle little star. Navy, gold and white, with paper stars on fishing line hung at different heights.
  7. Botanical greenery. Eucalyptus garlands, potted herbs as centerpieces and a simple calligraphy banner.
  8. Storybook. Guests bring a children's book instead of a card. The cream-and-red palette of old picture books works for any baby.

You will find more options in our list of 21 baby shower themes.

Decor that does not lean pink or blue

  • Welcome sign: "Oh Baby" or "Baby [Last Name]" on an 11x14 sign or a chalkboard easel.
  • Banner: 10 to 14 pennants with a phrase like "Hello Little One" or "Welcome Baby," alternating two palette colors with white letters. Our party banner maker lets you type the message and pick the exact colors.
  • Balloon garland: mix three sizes (5, 11 and 18 inch) in your palette plus white, with eucalyptus tucked in.
  • Centerpieces: small bud vases with white and blush flowers, or a stack of three board books tied with twine and topped with a paper animal on a skewer.
  • Cake topper: a 5 to 6 inch "Oh Baby" or a single animal silhouette on 110 lb cardstock.
  • Diaper cake: wrap it in muslin or a neutral swaddle and you have a centerpiece and a gift.

Make it yourself: a neutral cloud garland

A paper cloud garland is a five-minute-per-cloud project that suits almost any neutral theme, from stars to lambs to hot air balloons.

  1. Draw or print a 4-inch cloud shape on 110 lb white cardstock; you will fit about six per 8.5x11 sheet.
  2. Cut out 20 to 30 clouds, which makes a garland about 8 to 10 feet long.
  3. Add interest by tracing a few clouds onto your accent color, like butter yellow or sage.
  4. Run a line of glue down the center of one cloud, lay clear thread over it and press a second cloud on top so the garland is two-sided.
  5. Space clouds about 3 inches apart and hang the garland in a swag behind the gift table or from the ceiling over the dessert table.

Food and drinks

A neutral palette makes food easier to style because almost everything already fits: cream, gold, green. A few ideas:

  • A grazing board with cheeses, honey, figs, grapes cut in half, crackers and herbs.
  • Lemon or vanilla cupcakes with cream cheese frosting and a sprig of rosemary or a small paper topper.
  • Sage-green macarons or pistachio cookies to pick up a sage palette.
  • A "baby's breath" punch: sparkling lemonade with white grape juice and frozen berries.
  • Mini croissant sandwiches with chicken salad, egg salad and cucumber.

For a two- to three-hour afternoon shower, plan about 4 to 6 savory bites, 1 to 1.5 cupcakes and 3 drinks per guest.

Games and activities

The fun of a surprise is perfect material for games. Have guests vote on whether they think it is a boy or girl with a clothespin on one of two strings, or drop a token in one of two jars. The parents can reveal the result after the birth with a photo to everyone who voted. Baby predictions (weight, date, hair color) and a bingo card during gift opening are easy additions; see 15 baby shower games guests actually enjoy.

Other activities that suit any baby:

  • Onesie decorating with fabric markers on plain white bodysuits in sizes 3 to 6 months and up.
  • Book library: each guest signs the inside cover of the book they bring.
  • Letters to baby for a first-birthday time capsule.

Favors guests will use

Neutral favors are easy because they do not have to match a gendered palette. Good options include small jars of honey labeled "Meant to bee," seed packets labeled "Watch me grow," mini succulents in kraft pots, lavender sachets or sugar cookies iced in the shower colors. Add a 2-inch round favor tag with the parents' names and the date.

Wording for invitations

A few neutral lines that work well on a 5x7 invitation:

  • "A little one is on the way. Please join us for a baby shower honoring..."
  • "Boy or girl, we can't wait to meet you."
  • "Oh baby! Join us to celebrate [parents' names]."
  • "Sweet as can be: a baby shower for..."

On the invitation itself, carry the palette through with a simple border or a small illustration, such as a cloud, a star or a little elephant, rather than a busy pattern. Keep the text dark enough to read; soft sage or butter yellow look lovely as accents but are hard to read as body text, so use charcoal or navy for the details.

Frequently asked questions

What colors are gender neutral for a baby shower?

Sage green, cream, butter yellow, grey, terracotta, mint and navy with gold all work well. Choose two main colors plus one accent, and use the same hex codes for printed decor, balloons and the cake.

What is a good gender neutral baby shower theme?

Yellow and grey elephants, woodland animals, jungle safari, rubber ducks, stars and botanical greenery are all popular choices. They feel festive and baby-like without relying on pink or blue.

Can you have a gender neutral shower if the parents know the sex?

Yes. Many parents simply prefer neutral colors or want the decor to go into a nursery they will reuse later. A neutral theme is a style choice, not only a way to keep a secret.
