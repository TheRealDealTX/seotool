How Much Food to Serve at a Party: Per-Guest Quantities

Per-guest food and drink quantities for kids' birthday parties and baby showers, with a chart, adjustments for time of day and a worked example for 30 guests.

Running out of food is every host's nightmare, but the more common problem is the opposite: a fridge full of leftover sandwiches and three extra trays of fruit. Figuring out how much food for a party you actually need comes down to four questions: how many guests, how many are kids, how long the party lasts and whether it falls over a mealtime. Once you know those, the math is simple.

The numbers below are the rules of thumb we use for kids' birthdays and baby showers. They are starting points, not exact science; adjust for your crowd. To skip the arithmetic, plug your numbers into the party food calculator.

Step 1: Know your party type

  • Snack party (between meals, like 2:00 to 4:00 p.m.): guests expect light bites and cake. Plan the lower end of every range.
  • Meal party (overlapping 11:30 to 1:30 or 5:00 to 7:00): guests expect lunch or dinner. Plan the upper end, or add 50 percent to savory food.
  • Dessert-only party (often evening showers): cake, sweets and drinks only; plan 3 to 4 dessert pieces per adult in addition to cake.

Say which one it is on the invitation ("light refreshments" or "lunch will be served") so guests know whether to eat beforehand.

Tip: Round each quantity up to the nearest package size and then stop. Adding a "just in case" tray on top of rounded numbers is how hosts end up with half the food left over.

Adjusting for kids' birthday parties

Kids eat less than you expect at their own parties. They are excited, they want to play and they will usually eat a few bites of pizza and the frosting off a cupcake. A few practical notes:

  • Count adults separately. At parties for under-fives, nearly every child brings a parent, so the adult count is often equal to or larger than the kid count.
  • Plan one main item kids recognize, like pizza, chicken tenders or mini sandwiches, plus fruit.
  • Serve food before cake and activities. Kids who just had cake will not touch lunch.
  • Juice boxes or small water bottles are easier than cups for kids under 6.
  • Cut grapes and cherry tomatoes into quarters lengthwise for children under 5, and keep whole nuts and popcorn off the kids' table, since they are choking hazards for small children.

Adjusting for baby showers

Showers are mostly adult guests who graze while talking, so quantities run steady through the party. Guests at a 1:00 p.m. shower will expect something closer to lunch. A few notes:

  • Offer at least one substantial item, such as a quiche, a pasta salad or chicken salad croissants, if the shower overlaps lunch.
  • Plan non-alcoholic options that feel special, such as a sparkling punch, so the guest of honor has something festive to drink.
  • Label anything containing common allergens (nuts, shellfish) and keep a clearly marked option for guests who are pregnant, such as pasteurized cheeses and fully cooked meats.

Shopping and prep timeline

  • Two weeks out: order the cake and any catered trays. Buy paper goods and non-perishables like chips, crackers and drinks.
  • Three days out: confirm your final headcount and adjust quantities.
  • Two days out: buy fresh produce, cheese and bread.
  • One day out: wash and cut fruit and vegetables, make dips and assemble anything that holds overnight, like pasta salad.
  • Party day: pick up ice and pizza last, and keep cold food out of the fridge for no more than about two hours, or one hour outdoors in hot weather.

Plates, napkins and cups

Set out food in two or three stations rather than one long table if you have more than 30 guests. It keeps the line moving and stops one end of the buffet from emptying while the other is untouched. Refill trays from the kitchen instead of putting everything out at once; food looks fresher and you can see what is actually being eaten.

Paper goods run out more often than food. Plan per guest:

  1. Plates: 2 per guest (one for food, one for cake), plus 10 percent.
  2. Napkins: 3 per guest; for kids under 6, plan 4 or 5.
  3. Cups: 2 per guest, or 1 if you put out markers for names.
  4. Forks: 1 per guest for cake, more if you serve salads.

Frequently asked questions

How much food do I need for 30 guests?

For a two- to three-hour snack party, plan about 4 to 6 appetizer pieces, 1 cup of fruit and 1 to 1.5 cupcakes per adult, and roughly half the savory amounts per child. If the party falls over a meal, increase savory food by about 50 percent.

How many drinks per person for a party?

Plan about one drink per guest per hour, plus one extra per adult. A three-hour party usually needs 3 to 4 drinks per adult and 2 to 3 per child, with 1 to 1.5 pounds of ice per guest.

Do kids eat a lot at birthday parties?

Usually less than expected. Kids are focused on playing, so plan 1 to 2 slices of pizza and a small portion of fruit per child, and serve the main food before cake so it actually gets eaten.
