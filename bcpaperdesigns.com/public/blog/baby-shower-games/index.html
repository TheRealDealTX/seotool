15 Baby Shower Games Guests Actually Enjoy (Printable)

Fifteen games that are actually fun, sorted by type and time, plus how many to plan, how to run them and what to give as prizes.

Baby shower games have a mixed reputation. Done well, they break the ice between the friends from work and the aunts from out of town. Done badly, they drag on and make people uncomfortable. The difference is choosing games that are quick, easy to explain, and that nobody has to perform in. These 15 are the ones we have seen guests genuinely enjoy, with the printables you need for each.

How many games to plan

For a typical two- to three-hour shower, plan two or three games, total about 30 to 40 minutes. Mix types: one passive game guests can do while mingling, one that runs during gift opening, and one quick group game. Have one backup in case things move faster than expected.

TypeWhen to run itExamples below
Passive (on a table)As guests arriveGuess the jar, baby predictions, advice cards
During giftsWhile the parent opens presentsBingo, gift guessing
Group gameAfter food, before cakePrice is right, word scramble, trivia
Active (optional)Small, playful crowdsDiaper relay, clothespin game

Games that run themselves

1. Guess how many. Fill a jar with candies, mini clothespins or pacifiers, count them as you fill, and set out slips for guesses. Closest guess wins the jar.

2. Baby predictions. A card with blanks for birth date, weight, length, hair color and whose eyes the baby will have. The parents keep the cards and choose a winner after the birth.

3. Advice for the parents. Not a competition, but guests love it. Use 4x6 cards with prompts like "Best thing to do on a hard day" and "A book every child should own."

4. Wishes for baby. Cards that start "I wish for you..." and go into a keepsake box or get clipped to a garland on the gift table.

5. Guess the belly size. Guests cut a length of ribbon or yarn they think fits around the belly. Measure at the end; closest wins. It takes five minutes and is always good for laughs.

Games for gift opening

6. Baby shower bingo. The classic for a reason: it keeps people paying attention while gifts are opened. Guests fill a 5x5 card with gifts they think the parent will receive, then mark them off as gifts are unwrapped. Print unique, pre-filled cards with our baby shower bingo generator so no two match and nobody has to write 24 squares.

7. Gift guessing. Before opening each gift, the parent shakes it and guesses what it is. Keep a tally on a small whiteboard; it adds suspense and moves quickly.

Group games guests enjoy

8. The price is right. Lay out 8 to 10 baby items (diapers, wipes, a bottle, baby shampoo, a pacifier) and have guests guess each price. Closest total without going over wins. Use real receipts so the answers are accurate.

9. Baby word scramble. Fifteen scrambled baby words (BRCI for CRIB) on one printed page. Set a 3-minute timer. Easy to print, easy to grade.

10. Nursery rhyme or storybook quiz. Ten fill-in-the-blank lines from traditional nursery rhymes. This pairs well with a book themed baby shower game set.

11. Who knows mom best? Ten questions about the parent-to-be (favorite cereal, first concert, how she met her partner). Guests answer on a card, then the guest of honor reads the real answers.

12. Baby photo match. Ask a few close friends and family to send their baby pictures ahead of time. Number the photos on a board and have guests match names to faces.

13. Emoji pictionary. Baby-related phrases spelled in emoji, like "rock-a-bye baby" or "diaper change." It is quick and works well with younger crowds.

Active games (for the right crowd)

14. The clothespin game. Each guest gets a mini clothespin at arrival. Pick a forbidden word, like "baby." Whoever hears someone else say it takes their clothespin. Most clothespins at the end wins. It runs quietly in the background all party.

15. Diaper relay. Two teams, two baby dolls, a pile of diapers. Each guest diapers the doll and passes it on. Fastest team wins. Save it for groups who like a little silliness, and skip it at a formal or small shower.

Tip: Assign one friend to be the game host. They explain the rules, hand out pens and keep time, so the guest of honor and the main host can enjoy the party.

Choosing games for your crowd

Think about who is coming before you pick. A shower made up mostly of coworkers will enjoy quiet, pen-and-paper games like the word scramble and the price is right. A big family shower with several generations does well with bingo, since everyone from teens to grandparents can play. A couples or co-ed shower usually prefers trivia and guessing games over anything involving diapers. And if the parent-to-be is shy, skip games that put them in the spotlight, such as anything that measures the belly, and lean on advice cards and predictions instead.

Keep each game to 10 minutes or less. Announce the end of a game with a short timer, collect the sheets and score them while the next activity starts, then announce winners all at once just before cake.

Printing and prep checklist

  1. Choose your two or three games four weeks before the party.
  2. Print game sheets on 65 to 80 lb cardstock so guests can write on their laps without a clipboard. Prediction and advice cards at 4x6 fit two to four per 8.5x11 sheet.
  3. Print about 20 percent more sheets than your expected guest count.
  4. Prepare answer keys and keep them in an envelope.
  5. Buy pens, one per guest. Mini golf pencils are cheap and easy to collect.
  6. Pack prizes, a timer and a small bag for collecting cards.

If you are printing at home, our guide on how to print party printables covers paper and printer settings.

Prize ideas

Plan one prize per game plus a spare. Good prizes are small and useful: a nice hand cream, a candle, a box of gourmet chocolates, a small potted succulent, a coffee shop gift card or a set of themed cookies. Wrap them in the shower colors and attach a 2-inch favor tag.

One last thought: games are optional. If the guest list is small and everyone knows each other, conversation, good food and a relaxed gift opening can be all the entertainment a shower needs. A single passive game, like baby predictions on the table, still gives guests something to do with their hands while they settle in.

Frequently asked questions

How many games should you play at a baby shower?

Two or three games are usually enough for a two- to three-hour shower. Aim for about 30 to 40 minutes of games in total, mixing a passive game, one during gift opening and one quick group game.

What is the most popular baby shower game?

Baby shower bingo is one of the most widely played because it keeps guests engaged during gift opening. Guess how many, the price is right and baby predictions are also long-time favorites.

What are good baby shower games for a small group?

For 8 to 12 guests, try who knows mom best, baby photo match or advice cards. They feel personal and conversational, and they do not require teams or a lot of space.
