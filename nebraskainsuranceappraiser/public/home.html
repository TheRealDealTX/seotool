01 · Start here

Three checks a Nebraska Insurance Appraiser makes before recommending appraisal

Appraisal is powerful, but only for the right kind of dispute. These three questions decide whether it fits your claim.

  1. 1

    Is the loss covered?

    Appraisal values covered damage. If the carrier denied the claim or applied an exclusion, that is a coverage dispute for an attorney, not a panel.

  2. 2

    Do you have the itemized estimate?

    The carrier’s full line-item estimate shows exactly where the disagreement lies. Without it, nobody can measure the gap.

  3. 3

    Is the gap worth the fees?

    A Nebraska Insurance Appraiser costs money, and so does an umpire. On a small gap, a documented supplement may be faster and cheaper.

Appraisal usually fits

  • Coverage accepted, cost disputed
  • Partial roof approved after hail
  • Matching or discontinued materials
  • Missing code items, O&P or outbuildings

Appraisal usually doesn’t

  • Outright denials or exclusions
  • Bad-faith or legal claims
  • Gaps too small to cover fees
  • Suit deadlines that are close

02 · The process

How a Nebraska Insurance Appraiser moves a stalled claim to a signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process. Here is how we take a file from the first call to the award.

  1. 1

    Free claim review

    We read the policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos, then tell you whether appraisal fits.

  2. 2

    Independent inspection

    Our own measurements, photos and a line-item estimate priced for your part of the state.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and Nebraska Insurance Appraiser is named as your appraiser on time.

  4. 4

    Panel work

    We go line by line with the carrier’s appraiser. Most files narrow sharply here.

  5. 5

    Signed award

    Open items go to a neutral umpire. Two of three signatures set the amount of loss.

05 · The record

Storms that shaped Nebraska property claims

Every Nebraska Insurance Appraiser file sits on top of this history. Repeat hail, rare winter derechos and record floods all change how carriers read a claim, and how we answer them.

  1. 1975

    Omaha tornado

    A violent May tornado crossed the city, damaging thousands of homes and businesses.

  2. 1980

    Grand Island “Night of the Twisters”

    Several tornadoes struck Grand Island in one June night, destroying hundreds of homes.

  3. 2004

    Hallam tornado

    A wide, violent tornado devastated the village of Hallam in southern Lancaster County.

  4. 2014

    Pilger twin tornadoes

    Twin tornadoes leveled much of Pilger in northeast Nebraska in June.

  5. 2019

    March bomb cyclone floods

    Rapid snowmelt and rain flooded the Platte, Elkhorn, Loup and Missouri River basins.

  6. 2021

    December derecho

    A rare winter derecho swept the state with hurricane-force gusts.

  7. 2024

    Arbor Day tornadoes

    April tornadoes damaged homes near Elkhorn and Bennington and an industrial area northeast of Lincoln.

09 · Choosing help

Appraiser, public adjuster or attorney?

Each solves a different problem. Nebraska Insurance Appraiser will point you elsewhere when appraisal is not the right tool.

Appraiser

Values the amount of loss

Best once coverage is accepted and the number is disputed. Result: a binding award signed by two of three.

Public adjuster

Presents the whole claim

Best early in a complex claim. Licensed by the state and usually paid a percentage. Result: a negotiated settlement.

Attorney

Handles legal disputes

Best for denials, exclusions, close deadlines or bad-faith concerns. Result: a settlement or judgment.

10 · FAQ

Questions people ask a Nebraska Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it is the wrong move.

All questions Word list
What does a Nebraska Insurance Appraiser do?

A Nebraska Insurance Appraiser values property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on the cost. As your named appraiser we inspect, write an independent estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and present open items to a neutral umpire.

Is this the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster presents and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question, the amount of loss. We do not give legal advice or negotiate outside appraisal.

When should I call a Nebraska Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s itemized estimate and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful, usually more than a few thousand dollars.

Is the award binding?

Yes, on the amount of loss, once two of the three panel members sign. That is why documentation matters before anyone demands appraisal.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee in writing before work begins, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my part of the state?

Yes. We work in all 93 counties, from Omaha and Lincoln to Grand Island, Kearney, North Platte and Scottsbluff.
