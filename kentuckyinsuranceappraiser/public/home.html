Why owners call us

A Kentucky Insurance Appraiser works on one question: the amount of loss

Most underpaid Kentucky claims aren’t denied. They’re just short — a roof slope left off, a steep charge missing, siding that can’t be matched, a code item nobody priced. Kentucky Insurance Appraiser exists for the space between “covered” and “paid in full.”

We inspect and measure the property ourselves, price the repair for the county it sits in, and put every difference with the carrier’s estimate in plain English. When appraisal isn’t the right tool, we say so on the first call.

  • Independent scope. Built from our own inspection, never an edited copy of the carrier’s estimate.
  • Defensible numbers. Priced to hold up in front of an umpire, which is what actually moves an award.
  • Honest triage. If you need an attorney or a simple supplement instead, you’ll hear it from us.

The process

Seven steps from stuck claim to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here is how Kentucky Insurance Appraiser moves a file from first call to award.

  1. 1

    Confirm it is a pricing dispute

    Check that you and the insurer agree a covered loss happened and disagree mainly about how much it costs to repair or replace.

  2. 2

    Send a written appraisal demand

    Invoke the appraisal clause in writing, following the notice requirements in your policy.

  3. 3

    Name your party appraiser

    Each side selects a competent, impartial appraiser within the window most policies set after the demand.

  4. 4

    Select the umpire

    The two appraisers agree on a neutral umpire, or a court of record where the property sits can appoint one.

  5. 5

    Inspect and build estimates

    The appraisers inspect the property, review the documents and each prepare a line-item estimate of the loss.

  6. 6

    Negotiate and narrow the differences

    The appraisers work through the line items together and send only the items they cannot resolve to the umpire.

  7. 7

    Sign the award and collect payment

    A written award signed by any two of the three sets the amount of loss, and the insurer pays under the policy terms.

Straight talk

Myths a Kentucky Insurance Appraiser hears every week

A few common beliefs that cost owners money — and what is usually true instead. Your policy language always controls.

“Appraisal means suing my insurer.”
It’s a contract process inside your policy, not a lawsuit. It decides the amount of loss only.
“The adjuster’s estimate is final.”
It’s the carrier’s opening valuation. Supplements and, when needed, appraisal exist because estimates miss things.
“My contractor can be my appraiser.”
Most policies require an impartial or independent appraiser. A contractor with a stake in the repair can put the award at risk.
“Appraisal always pays off.”
Not when the gap is small. Fees can eat a narrow difference — run the payoff estimator first.

Questions

Asked of every Kentucky Insurance Appraiser

Short answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All appraisal questions Appraisal terms

What does a Kentucky Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, take unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles the whole claim for a fee. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question — the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate the rest of the claim and we don’t give legal advice.

When is it worth calling a Kentucky Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage has been accepted, you have the carrier’s itemized estimate, and the gap to your contractor’s number is meaningful — often more than about 10 percent or several thousand dollars.

Does appraisal decide whether my claim is covered?

No. Appraisal sets the amount of loss. Coverage disputes, denials and bad-faith questions belong with a Kentucky attorney, and the Kentucky Department of Insurance takes consumer complaints.

What does it cost?

Each side usually pays its own appraiser and splits the umpire. Our fee is put in writing before work starts, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my county?

Yes. We take appraisal and review work in all 120 Kentucky counties, from Paducah and Hopkinsville to Lexington, Covington, Pikeville and Ashland.
