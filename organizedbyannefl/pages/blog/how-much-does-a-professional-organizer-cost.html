How Much Does a Professional Organizer Cost? What Affects the Price

Organizing costs vary widely. Here is an honest look at the factors that shape pricing, general market ranges and estimated hours for common projects.

If you are thinking about hiring help, one of your first questions is probably how much does a professional organizer cost. It is a fair question, and the honest answer is that it depends. The size of your home, the scope of the project, how many people work on it and what products you need all play a role.

This guide explains the main factors that shape pricing, shares broad market ranges so you have a starting point, and offers estimated hours for common projects. It is meant to help you plan, not to serve as a quote. Every home is different, and so is every project.

Hourly Rates vs. Packages

Most organizers use one of two pricing models, or a mix of both.

  • Hourly pricing: You pay for time spent working, usually per organizer. This is flexible and works well when the scope is hard to predict.
  • Package or project pricing: You pay a set price for a defined project, such as a pantry or a walk-in closet. This gives you more certainty up front.
  • Session blocks: Some organizers offer a set number of hours at a time, such as half-day or full-day sessions.

As a general reference, many organizers in the US charge somewhere around $60 to $150 or more per hour per organizer. That is a broad market range, not a local or company-specific rate. Prices vary by region, experience, specialty and the type of work involved, so you may see figures above or below that range.

Team Size and Project Scope

A larger team does not always mean a higher total cost. Two or three organizers working together can often finish a project in fewer days, which can be helpful for move-ins or deadlines.

  • Solo organizer: Good for smaller projects like a single closet, pantry or home office.
  • Small team: Helpful for whole-home projects, garages, moves and downsizing, where many hands speed things up.
  • Scope: The number of rooms, the amount of items and how many decisions are involved all affect time. A pantry that only needs a refresh takes less time than one that needs a full edit.
  • Your involvement: Projects where you are available to make quick decisions often move faster.

Product Costs

Organizing products are usually a separate cost from labor. Some clients already own bins and baskets they want to reuse. Others want a complete, cohesive look with new containers, drawer dividers and labels.

  • Shopping your home first can reduce costs. We often find perfectly usable containers in closets and garages.
  • Budget-friendly options work well for many spaces. Not every shelf needs premium acrylic.
  • Specialty items such as custom shelving or closet systems can be a larger investment.
  • Returns: Ask how unused products are handled so you are not paying for items you do not need.
Pro tip

Wait to buy containers until after the edit. Once you know what you are keeping, you can buy exactly what fits, which avoids returns and wasted money.

Travel, Parking and Building Logistics

In Fort Lauderdale, logistics can affect a project more than people expect, especially in high-rise buildings along the beach and in Flagler Village.

  • Parking: Some buildings have limited guest or vendor parking, and paid garages may add a cost.
  • Service elevators: Many condos require reserving a service elevator for deliveries or donations.
  • Certificates of insurance: Some associations require vendors to provide one before working on site.
  • Travel distance: Projects farther from our core service area may include travel time.

Haul-Away and Donation Runs

Decluttering usually creates a pile of items that need to leave the home. Ask how that is handled.

  • Donation drop-offs may be included in a session or billed as additional time.
  • Junk removal for large or non-donatable items is often handled by a separate hauling company with its own fees.
  • Consignment or resale takes extra coordination and time.
  • Hazardous materials like old paint or chemicals need special disposal.

Is Hiring an Organizer Worth It?

When comparing organizers, look beyond the hourly number. Ask what is included, how products are handled, whether there is a minimum session length and how the team approaches decisions about your belongings. A clear, written estimate makes it easier to compare options fairly.

Value looks different for everyone. For some people, it is getting a guest room back. For others, it is a smooth move, a calm kitchen or simply not having to think about it anymore. A good organizer also sets up systems that are easy to maintain, so the results last. Our post on organizing mistakes that backfire explains why systems matter more than products.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a professional organizer charge per hour?

As a broad US market range, many organizers charge somewhere around $60 to $150 or more per hour per organizer, but rates vary by region, experience and project type. Organized by Anne quotes every project individually after a consultation.

Are organizing products included in the price?

Products are usually a separate cost from labor. Shopping your home for containers you already own and waiting until after the edit to buy new products can help control costs.

How many hours does it take to organize a closet?

As a general estimate, a reach-in closet often takes around 3 to 5 hours and a walk-in closet around 5 to 10 hours. The actual time depends on the size of the wardrobe and how much is edited.
