Seasonal Home Organization for Snowbirds in Fort Lauderdale

Arriving for the season or heading north? These open-up and close-down checklists help seasonal residents protect their Florida home and settle in faster.

For seasonal residents, a Fort Lauderdale home is a place to return to every winter, whether it is a condo overlooking the Intracoastal, a townhome in Coral Ridge or a house in Pompano Beach. Each arrival and departure comes with its own to-do list, and the months in between can be hard on a closed-up home in our heat and humidity.

Thoughtful seasonal home organization makes those transitions smoother. With a few repeatable checklists and a well-labeled system, you can arrive to a fresh, ready home and leave knowing everything is protected. Here is how our team approaches it.

The Close-Down Checklist: Before You Head North

A careful close-down protects your home through the hottest, wettest and stormiest months of the year.

Climate and Systems

  • Set the air conditioning to keep the home cool and dry rather than turning it off. Ask your HVAC technician what temperature and humidity settings they recommend for your home while it is unoccupied.
  • Consider a smart thermostat or humidity sensor so you can monitor conditions remotely.
  • Replace the AC filter before you leave.
  • Follow your building's guidance on the water supply, water heater and any shutoff valves.

Kitchen

  • Use up, donate or discard perishable food.
  • Decide whether to leave the refrigerator running or empty it, clean it and prop the doors open, based on your appliance instructions.
  • Move dry goods into airtight containers to discourage pests, or take them with you.
  • Run and empty the dishwasher and leave it slightly open to air out.

Linens, Closets and Living Spaces

  • Wash and fully dry all bedding and towels before storing them.
  • Store linens in breathable cotton bags or on open shelves rather than sealed plastic.
  • Leave closet doors and interior doors open to help air circulate.
  • Add moisture absorbers to closets and cabinets.
  • Bring in patio furniture and anything that could become airborne in a storm.
Close-down happens during hurricane season

Most seasonal residents leave just before or during hurricane season, which runs from June 1 to November 30. Plan your departure with storm prep in mind, and review our hurricane season home organization guide for what to secure before you go.

The Open-Up Checklist: Settling In for the Season

Arriving after months away is exciting, and a little organization helps you settle in quickly.

  • Walk through every room and check for leaks, water stains, musty odors or signs of pests.
  • Return the thermostat to your preferred settings.
  • Run water in every faucet and flush toilets.
  • Restock the refrigerator and pantry with a fresh grocery run.
  • Wash linens that have been stored if they feel or smell stale.
  • Set patio furniture back out and check cushions for mildew.
  • Test smoke detectors and replace batteries if needed.
  • Review mail, building notices and anything your home watch provider has flagged.
Pro tip

Keep both checklists in a sheet protector inside a kitchen drawer, along with any notes from last season. Next year, you will not have to rebuild your list from memory.

Fridge and Pantry Strategy for Two Homes

Managing food between two households is one of the trickiest parts of seasonal living.

  • Plan meals for your last two weeks to use up what you have.
  • Keep a short list of shelf-stable staples that stay in airtight containers year-round, such as coffee, olive oil and salt.
  • Write an arrival grocery list before you leave so your first shopping trip is quick.
  • Freeze or discard, do not guess. If you are unsure whether something will last, it is safer to let it go.

For more ideas, see our pantry organization ideas, including how to keep dry goods safe from South Florida pests.

Linens, Clothing and Labeled Travel Bins

Many snowbirds keep a Florida wardrobe in Florida and travel with the rest. A labeled bin system makes this much easier.

  • Stays in Florida: beachwear, lightweight clothing, sandals and casual outfits.
  • Travels with you: medications, important documents, electronics, valuables and seasonal favorites.
  • Label bins by destination and contents, such as Florida Closet Summer Storage or Travel North Toiletries.
  • Keep a duplicate toiletry kit in each home so you are not packing the same items twice a year.
  • Photograph the contents of each bin and keep the photos in a shared album for quick reference.

Our guide to closet organization in humid climates explains how to store clothing so it stays fresh while you are away.

Paperwork and Important Information

Two homes mean two sets of bills, contacts and deadlines.

  1. Create a home binder with building contacts, HOA rules, appliance manuals, insurance details and your home watch agreement.
  2. Switch to paperless billing where possible so statements reach you in either home.
  3. Set up mail forwarding or a hold before you leave.
  4. Keep digital copies of key documents in a secure cloud folder.
  5. Calendar recurring dates such as insurance renewals, HOA meetings and AC maintenance.

Working With a Home Watch Service

A home watch service checks on your property while you are away. Visits often include walking through the home, checking for leaks or humidity issues, running water and looking for storm damage. If you use one:

  • Share your close-down checklist so they know how you left the home.
  • Give them a list of shutoff locations and important contacts.
  • Ask how they report findings, such as photos after each visit.
  • Confirm the plan for before and after a storm.

Choose a provider you trust, and ask about their insurance and licensing as you would with any vendor. Keep a copy of their contact information in your home binder and with your building management so everyone knows who is authorized to enter while you are away. A short check-in call before each departure helps confirm any changes to your routine.

Make Your Next Season Easier

Seasonal home organization is about building systems you can repeat with less effort every year. Whether you are preparing for your first season in Fort Lauderdale or your tenth, our team can help you set up closets, kitchens and labeled storage that work for both your arrivals and departures. Frequently asked questions

Should I turn off my AC when I leave Florida for the summer?

Turning the AC off completely can allow humidity to build up in a closed home. Most seasonal residents keep it running at a higher setting; ask your HVAC technician what temperature and humidity settings make sense for your home.

How should I store linens while I am away?

Wash and fully dry them first, then store them in breathable cotton bags or on open shelves rather than sealed plastic. Adding moisture absorbers to closets also helps keep linens fresh.

What does a home watch service do?

A home watch service checks on your property while you are away, typically walking through the home, looking for leaks or humidity problems and checking for storm damage. Share your close-down checklist so they know how you left the home.
