Hurricane Season Organization: A Fort Lauderdale Home Guide

When a storm is on the way, you should not be digging for a tarp or your insurance policy. Here is how to organize documents, supplies and outdoor items before hurricane season peaks.

Hurricane season in South Florida runs from June 1 through November 30, and anyone who has lived in Fort Lauderdale for a while knows the routine: watching the forecast cone, checking supplies and wondering where the flashlights went. Preparation is about safety first, and for that the most important sources are official ones. Follow guidance from the National Hurricane Center and Broward County Emergency Management, know your evacuation zone and listen to local officials when a storm approaches.

What we can help with is the organizing side. Good hurricane season organization means that when a storm is on the way, you are not digging through a garage for a tarp or searching drawers for your insurance policy. Everything has a place, and you can act calmly and quickly. Here is how we approach it.

Safety first

This guide covers organizing your home and supplies. It is not a substitute for official emergency guidance. For preparedness checklists, evacuation information and storm updates, rely on the National Hurricane Center, Broward County Emergency Management and your city's official channels.

Build a Grab-and-Go Document Binder

If you need to leave quickly, your most important papers should be in one place you can grab in seconds. A simple binder or a waterproof document pouch works well. Consider including copies of:

  • Identification, such as driver's licenses and passports
  • Homeowners, flood, auto and health insurance policies, including policy numbers and claim contact information
  • Property documents, such as deeds, leases or condo association information
  • Medical information, including a list of prescriptions, allergies and doctors
  • Pet vaccination records
  • A printed list of important contacts, in case your phone battery dies
  • Basic bank and account information

Use clear sheet protectors to keep pages dry, and add tabbed dividers so you can find what you need. Store the binder in a consistent, easy-to-reach spot, such as a top shelf in a hall closet, and make sure everyone in the household knows where it lives.

Back it up digitally

Paper can be lost or damaged, so keep digital copies too. Scan or photograph each document and store the files in a secure cloud account or on an encrypted drive you can take with you. Name files clearly, such as Insurance-Homeowners-2026, so you can find them quickly on a phone.

Organize Supplies Into Zones

Instead of one giant pile of hurricane supplies, group them into labeled, lidded bins by purpose. This makes it easy to see what you have and what you need to replace. A practical setup might include:

  1. Light and power: flashlights, headlamps, batteries, battery banks and a battery-powered or hand-crank radio.
  2. First aid and medications: a stocked first aid kit and a plan for refilling prescriptions ahead of a storm.
  3. Tools and repair: duct tape, a utility knife, work gloves, tarps, zip ties and a basic tool kit.
  4. Hygiene and sanitation: wipes, hand sanitizer, trash bags, toilet paper and personal care items.
  5. Pets: food, bowls, leashes, a carrier and any medications.

Store the bins together in an interior, air-conditioned closet if you can. Heat and humidity in a garage can shorten the life of batteries, medications and some supplies. Label the short end of each bin so you can read it when the bins are stacked.

Pro tip

Tape an inventory list inside the lid of each bin with the date you last checked it. At the start of each season, open every bin, compare it to the list and replace anything expired, used or missing.

Rotate Water and Food

A widely published guideline is to store at least one gallon of water per person per day. Check the National Hurricane Center and Broward County Emergency Management for current recommendations on how many days to plan for, and remember that pets need water too.

The organizing challenge is rotation. Water and nonperishable food that sit untouched for years can expire, and a supply you bought three seasons ago may not be what your household eats today. A few strategies help:

  • First in, first out: put new purchases at the back of the shelf and move older items forward.
  • Date everything: write the purchase or expiration date on cases and cans with a marker.
  • Use it up after the season: as November ends, work the oldest nonperishables into regular meals and restock in the spring.
  • Keep a manual can opener in the food bin so it never goes missing.

Our pantry organization ideas include tips for setting up a dedicated storm shelf that is easy to rotate.

Make a Plan for Garage and Patio Items

Outdoor furniture, potted plants, grills and decor can become hazards in high winds. Many Fort Lauderdale homes, condos and waterfront properties in neighborhoods like Rio Vista and Harbor Beach have patios and balconies filled with items that need to come inside before a storm. Thinking about this before the season saves a lot of scrambling.

  1. List every outdoor item that would need to be secured or brought in.
  2. Decide where each one goes. Chair cushions in a closet, small planters in the garage, a side table in the laundry room.
  3. Clear space in advance. If the garage is too full to fit patio furniture, now is the time to declutter it.
  4. Keep shutter hardware organized. Store panels, bolts, wing nuts and tools together, and label panels by window or door.
  5. Write the plan down and keep it in the document binder so anyone in the household can follow it.

Condo and high-rise residents should also check building rules and association guidance about balcony items and storm procedures.

Record a Home Inventory Video

If your home is damaged, a clear record of your belongings can make the insurance process much easier. Before the peak of the season, walk through your home with your phone and record a video of each room.

  • Open closets, cabinets and drawers as you go.
  • Narrate brand names, model numbers and approximate purchase dates for larger items.
  • Capture serial numbers on electronics and appliances.
  • Include the garage, patio and any storage areas.
  • Photograph receipts for major purchases.

Store the video in the cloud, not just on your phone, and update it each year or after significant purchases. Talk with your insurance agent about what documentation they recommend for your policy.

Keep It Simple Year After Year

The best hurricane setup is one you can maintain. Put a reminder on your calendar for late May to check bins, rotate supplies, update documents and refresh your inventory video. Frequently asked questions

When is hurricane season in Fort Lauderdale?

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30. Late May is a good time to check supplies, update documents and review your plan.

How much water should I store for a hurricane?

A widely published guideline is at least one gallon of water per person per day. Check the National Hurricane Center and Broward County Emergency Management for current recommendations on how many days to plan for.

Where should I store hurricane supplies in a Florida home?

An interior, air-conditioned closet is ideal because garage heat and humidity can shorten the life of batteries, medications and some supplies. Group items into labeled, lidded bins by purpose so they are easy to check and grab.
