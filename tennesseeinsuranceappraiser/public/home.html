The short version

What a Tennessee Insurance Appraiser does — and doesn’t do

Most underpaid claims aren’t denials. They’re estimates that left things out: a roof slope, a matching floor, a code item, a crane for the oak that came through the attic. A Tennessee Insurance Appraiser exists for the gap between “covered” and “paid in full.”

  1. We value the loss from scratch. Our own inspection and measurements, never a marked-up copy of the carrier’s estimate.
  2. We price what we can defend. Numbers built to hold up in front of an umpire move awards; inflated ones don’t.
  3. We tell you when appraisal is wrong. Denials, bad-faith questions and close deadlines belong with a Tennessee attorney, and we’ll say so on the first call.

Three stars, three climates

Claims look different in each Grand Division

The three stars on the state flag stand for East, Middle and West Tennessee. A Tennessee Insurance Appraiser has to know how each one’s weather shows up on a roof and in an estimate.

West Tennessee

  • Derechos
  • Ice storms
  • Tornadoes
  • Earthquake endorsements

Flat, open land between the Mississippi River and the Tennessee River lets storm lines hold their strength. Memphis remembers the 2003 derecho and the ice storms of 2022 and 2023; Jackson has been hit by major tornadoes in 2003 and 2008. Shelby County also sits near the New Madrid Seismic Zone.

MemphisJackson

Middle Tennessee

  • Night tornadoes
  • Spring hail
  • Straight-line wind
  • Subdivision claims

The fastest-growing part of the state is also one of its most tornado-prone. Nashville, Cookeville, Clarksville and Murfreesboro have all taken direct hits since 2009, and spring hail regularly crosses Davidson, Williamson and Rutherford counties.

NashvilleClarksvilleMurfreesboroFranklinCookeville

East Tennessee

  • Mountain wind
  • Ice & snow
  • Wildfire
  • Hillside access costs

Ridges, valleys and the Smokies shape every claim. The 2011 outbreak hammered the Chattanooga area, the 2016 fires swept into Gatlinburg, and Helene’s remnants flooded the northeast in 2024. Steep lots add access and staging costs that estimates often miss.

From first call to signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result. Here is how a Tennessee Insurance Appraiser file usually moves.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing and you name us as your appraiser within the policy’s window.

  4. 4

Choosing help

Appraiser, public adjuster or attorney?

Each solves a different problem. A Tennessee Insurance Appraiser will point you elsewhere when appraisal isn’t the right tool.

Appraiser

Coverage accepted, number disputed

  • Values the amount of loss
  • Works inside the appraisal clause
  • Ends in a binding award

Public adjuster

Early in a large or complex claim

  • Manages the whole claim
  • Documents and negotiates
  • Ends in a negotiated settlement

Attorney

Denials, bad faith, close deadlines

  • Handles legal disputes
  • Can file suit
  • Ends in settlement or judgment

FAQ

Questions people ask a Tennessee Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers about how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All FAQs Glossary
What does a Tennessee Insurance Appraiser do?

A Tennessee Insurance Appraiser values property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and present open items to a neutral umpire.

Is the appraisal award binding?

Yes, on the amount of loss, once any two of the three panel members sign it. That’s why the file should be complete before anyone demands appraisal.

When should I call a Tennessee Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the insurer’s itemized estimate, and the gap between it and a qualified repair estimate is meaningful — generally more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is this the same as hiring a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster manages and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works inside the appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t give legal advice or negotiate outside appraisal.

Does Tennessee have a FAIR plan?

No. Tennessee doesn’t operate a FAIR plan or wind pool. Owners who can’t find standard coverage often use surplus-lines insurers, whose policies may contain different appraisal terms.

What does it cost?

You pay your own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. Our fee is set in writing before work begins, and the first conversation is free.
