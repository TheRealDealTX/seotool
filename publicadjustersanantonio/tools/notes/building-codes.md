# San Antonio building codes — verified facts (checked 2026-10-20)

Note: sanantonio.gov now redirects to sa.gov; DSD documents are still hosted on docsonline.sanantonio.gov.

## Adopted code editions (VERIFIED)
* City Council adopted the **2024 ICC codes** (and the 2024 San Antonio Property Maintenance Code) on **January 30, 2025** (Ord. No. 2025-01-30-0075). Source: DSD Codes & Ordinances page — https://www.sa.gov/Directory/Departments/DSD/Codes-Ordinances
* **Effective May 1, 2025.** Source: cover page of the City's Chapter 10 Building-Related Codes PDF ("EFFECTIVE DATE: MAY 1, 2025") — https://docsonline.sanantonio.gov/DSDUploads/2024Ch10Building-RelatedCodesFinal.pdf
* 2024 editions adopted (Chapter 10 §10 definitions): IBC, IRC, IEBC, IFC, IFGC, IMC, IPC, ISPSC — each "2024 edition, as amended by the city."
* **Energy code excluded from this cycle**: IECC remains the **2021 edition** (Chapter 10 Art. X, Sec. 10-91; DSD page: energy code "was excluded from this cycle and is currently under 2021 codes").
* **Electrical code: conflicting in the City's own document.** The Chapter 10 history note says the ordinance adopted "the 2023 edition of the National Electrical Code" (and the SA Speak Up project page lists "National Electrical Code (2023 NFPA 70)"), but Article VI Sec. 10-51 text in the same PDF reads "Adoption of National Electrical Code (2020)". → On the site, DO NOT name an NEC edition; say "the City's adopted National Electrical Code".
* Previous cycle: 2021 ICC codes, effective Feb 1, 2023 (DSD page; Ord. 2022-11-10-0875).
* Chapter 10 Sec. 10-? "Previous approvals": work under a lawful permit pursued in good faith within 180 days after the chapter's effective date is not required to change.
* Chapter 10 amends IRC R501.6 / IECC C501.5 (historic buildings): code provisions not mandatory for historic buildings where a design professional / SHPO / historic preservation authority demonstrates compliance would threaten historic form/fabric (paraphrase; did not use on page in detail).
* Chapter 10 amends IRC R408.9 Foundation Repair: foundation repair permit plus Engineer of Record letter required; engineer inspects and submits a letter.
* Chapter 10 fee schedule lists "Residential Re-roof Permit $25.00"; Commercial Re-Roof based on building valuation (plus plan review fee when applicable). Fees can change — page says "confirm current fees with DSD".
* Chapter 10 (e) "Work exempt from permit" — finish work (painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, counter tops) exempt; minor fence repairs (≤25% of contiguous length, ≤6 ft) exempt. (g) ordinary repairs exempt, but not cutting walls, structural members, or piping/wiring work. (f) Emergency repairs: permit application must be submitted within the next business day.

## Permits (VERIFIED)
* DSD Getting Started FAQ: "Yes, a permit is required for repairing/replacing a roof." Homeowner can obtain the permit and do own work or hire a registered Home Improvement Contractor (non-structural). If pitch changes, homeowner can still obtain permit (requires plan review) and do the work or hire a Residential Building Contractor (structural). — https://www.sa.gov/Directory/Departments/DSD/Resources/Getting-Started
* DSD Residential Permits page: "Typically a building permit is required if you decide to build, remodel, repair or demolish a structure on your property." Re-roof with no pitch change: issued over the counter (homeowner: proof of ownership; Home Improvement Contractor: site plan). Change in pitch: full plan review. Minor repairs (windows, doors, walls, siding, floors) and foundation repairs issued over the counter. Homeowners must use licensed trade contractors for new electrical, plumbing, HVAC components. — https://www.sa.gov/Directory/Departments/DSD/Constructing/Residential/Permits
* Re-Roof Permit Application PDF: https://docsonline.sanantonio.gov/FileUploads/dsd/form_ReRoofPermitApplication.pdf (link only; contents not reviewed)
* No Permit Required list includes painting/plastering/sheetrock repair/tiling, window glass replacement only, replacement of existing doors, new siding over existing siding; roofs NOT on the list. Caveat: properties in a Historic, Neighborhood Conservation or Corridor District may have design/material restrictions. — https://www.sa.gov/Directory/Departments/DSD/Constructing/Residential/Permits/No-Permit-Required
