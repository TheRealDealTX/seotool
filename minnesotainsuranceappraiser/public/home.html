Covered isn’t the same as paid

Why owners bring in a Minnesota Insurance Appraiser

Most short claims aren’t denied — they’re underscoped. A missed roof slope, steel siding that can’t be matched, ice and water shield left off the eaves, an interior ceiling written as “paint only.” A Minnesota Insurance Appraiser exists for that gap between an accepted claim and a repair that’s actually funded.

  • Scope from scratchWe inspect and measure ourselves rather than editing the insurer’s estimate.
  • Numbers built to hold upEvery line is supported, so it carries weight with the other appraiser and the umpire.
  • Honest triageIf your claim needs an attorney, a supplement or nothing at all, we say so on the first call.
How we work

Appraisal tends to fit

  • Coverage accepted, cost disputed
  • Repair-versus-replace disagreements
  • Matching siding, shingles or flooring
  • Missing trades, code items, overhead & profit

It usually doesn’t

  • Full coverage denials
  • Bad-faith or other legal claims
  • Gaps too small to cover fees
  • Deadlines that need an attorney first

The process

From first call to a signed award

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a defined process with a binding result on the amount. Here is how Minnesota Insurance Appraiser moves a file through it.

Read the full process guide
  1. 1

    Call & claim review

    We read the policy, the insurer’s estimate and your photos, then tell you plainly whether appraisal fits.

  2. 2

    Independent inspection

    Our own measurements and photos, then a line-item estimate priced for your Minnesota ZIP code.

  3. 3

    Written demand

    Appraisal is demanded in writing, and Minnesota Insurance Appraiser is named as your appraiser.

  4. 4

    Appraiser-to-appraiser review

    We go line by line with the insurer’s appraiser. Many differences close at this stage.

  5. 5

    Umpire & award

    Anything left goes to a neutral umpire. Signatures from two of the three set the amount of loss.

Five claim regions

One state, very different claims

Hail in Plymouth, derechos near Mankato, lake-effect snow in Duluth, floods along the Red River. A Minnesota Insurance Appraiser has to know how each region’s weather shows up on a building — and in an estimate.

Twin Cities Metro

  • Hail
  • Ice dams
  • Straight-line wind
  • Condo & HOA losses

Seven counties, three generations of housing. Plaster-walled bungalows in Minneapolis and St. Paul, 1960s ramblers in the first-ring suburbs and townhome associations at the edges — often hit by the same spring hail line.

Communities: Minneapolis, St. Paul, Bloomington, Plymouth, Woodbury, Eagan, Blaine

Southern Minnesota

  • Tornadoes
  • Derecho winds
  • Hail
  • Farm buildings

Open country lets severe storms organize. The March 1998 St. Peter and Comfrey tornadoes and the May 2022 derecho both tracked through this region, where claims mix homes, machine sheds and small-town commercial buildings.

Communities: Rochester, Mankato, North Mankato, Owatonna, Faribault, Winona

Northeast & Arrowhead

  • Heavy snow
  • Frozen pipes
  • Wildfire smoke
  • Flash floods

Lake-effect snow, steep hillside lots and seasonal cabins define claims here. The June 2012 Duluth flood showed how quickly water can overwhelm older storm systems.

Communities: Duluth, Hermantown, Two Harbors, Cloquet, Hibbing, Grand Marais

Central Minnesota & Lakes

  • Snow load
  • Ice dams
  • Fallen trees
  • Cabin losses

Lake country means seasonal properties that sit empty for months — the classic setup for an undiscovered frozen pipe or a snow-loaded porch roof.

Communities: St. Cloud, Sartell, Brainerd, Baxter, Little Falls, Alexandria

Red River Valley

  • Spring flooding
  • Blizzards
  • Wind
  • Hail

Flat terrain and a north-flowing river create a flood season unlike anywhere else in the state. Flood itself usually needs separate coverage, which makes sorting wind, water and flood damage a big part of claims here.

Communities: Moorhead, Dilworth, Fergus Falls, Detroit Lakes, Crookston

A year of claims

Every Minnesota season brings its own losses

We see the same patterns every year. Knowing which season your damage came from helps date it, scope it and defend it.

Winter

Ice dams & snow load

Meltwater backs up under shingles, pipes freeze in cabins and heavy snow tests older roofs.

Spring

Thaw & first hail

Snowmelt finds every weak spot, and the first hail lines of the season roll through the metro and south.

Summer

Derechos & tornadoes

Severe-weather season peaks: straight-line wind, tornadoes, hail and lightning fires.

Fall

Wind & hidden damage

Autumn storms and the last chance to find and document summer damage before snow covers the roof.

Questions

Asked often of a Minnesota Insurance Appraiser

Straight answers on how appraisal works, what it costs and when it’s the wrong move.

All common questions Insurance terms
What does a Minnesota Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent estimate, work with the insurer’s appraiser and, if needed, present the remaining items to an umpire.

Is an appraiser the same as a public adjuster?

No. A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim and is licensed by the Minnesota Department of Commerce. An appraiser works within the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss.

When is it worth calling?

When coverage is accepted, you have the insurer’s itemized estimate, and the gap to your contractor’s number is meaningful — often more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is the appraisal award binding?

An award signed by any two of the three panel members generally sets the amount of loss. It doesn’t decide coverage, which is why the file should be complete before appraisal starts.

Can appraisers decide what caused the damage?

In Minnesota, the state Supreme Court’s 2012 Quade v. Secura decision held that appraisers may decide causation as part of the amount of loss, while coverage questions stay with the courts. How that applies depends on your policy and facts.

Do you work outside the Twin Cities?

Yes. We work statewide in all 87 counties, including Rochester, Duluth, St. Cloud, Mankato and Moorhead.
