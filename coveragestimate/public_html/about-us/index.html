Coverage Estimate · Insurance Appraisal & Valuation

About Coverage Estimate

Coverage Estimate helps condominium associations, HOA boards, property managers, and insurance professionals determine what a property would realistically cost to rebuild after a major loss.

We provide independent insurance appraisals and replacement cost evaluations designed to support accurate master policy limits. Instead of relying on outdated figures, market value, or automatic annual increases, we examine the property itself and calculate a defensible replacement cost based on current construction conditions.

Our goal is simple: help decision-makers identify potential coverage gaps before those gaps become costly problems.

Coverage Estimate at a Glance

A More Reliable Way to Determine Insurance Value

Property insurance limits should reflect the cost of reconstruction—not what the property might sell for.

Market value can be influenced by land prices, neighborhood demand, location, and comparable sales. However, those factors do not determine the cost of replacing roofing, structural components, electrical systems, plumbing, mechanical equipment, common areas, and other insured improvements.

Coverage Estimate focuses on the details that matter after a covered loss, including:

  • Local labor rates
  • Current material pricing
  • Building size and construction type
  • Interior and exterior finishes
  • Roofing and structural systems
  • Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing components
  • Shared amenities and common elements
  • Demolition and debris removal
  • Contractor overhead and professional fees
  • Applicable building code requirements

By evaluating these components carefully, we provide a clearer estimate of what rebuilding may require under current conditions.

Why Our Work Matters

Many associations renew their master insurance policies using values carried forward from previous years. In some cases, those values are increased by a standard percentage without a detailed review of the property.

Although this process may be convenient, construction costs do not always rise evenly. Labor shortages, material price changes, code upgrades, supply disruptions, and local market conditions can cause reconstruction costs to change much faster than expected.

Over time, the difference between the insured amount and the actual replacement cost can become significant.

An independent valuation gives boards and managers a stronger basis for discussing coverage with insurance agents, brokers, and carriers. It also helps demonstrate that policy limits were selected using documented property information rather than assumptions.

Independent, Property-Specific Evaluations

Every property is different.

A small condominium building with basic finishes does not have the same reconstruction requirements as a multi-building community with elevators, clubhouses, pools, parking structures, specialized roofing, or extensive mechanical systems.

For that reason, our evaluations are based on the characteristics of the property being reviewed. We gather relevant records, examine the buildings and common elements, evaluate construction quality, and apply current cost information to the property’s major components.

This property-specific approach produces a more useful result than a generic cost-per-square-foot estimate alone.

Our Process

We follow a structured process so each report is supported by relevant property information. For a step-by-step look at timing and what to prepare, see how the Coverage Estimate process works.

Property Information Review

We begin by gathering available documents, which may include building plans, unit counts, prior appraisals, insurance schedules, property descriptions, renovation records, and other useful information.

Property Inspection

When required by the scope of work, a specialist examines the property’s construction, condition, systems, common elements, amenities, and major improvements.

Cost Analysis

We apply current labor, material, contractor, demolition, and related reconstruction costs to the property’s components. Local conditions and applicable construction requirements are considered as part of the analysis.

Report Preparation

Our findings are organized into a professional report that explains the valuation, summarizes the methodology, and presents the estimated replacement cost.

The completed report can be reviewed by the association, property manager, insurance agent, broker, or carrier.

Reports Built for Real Insurance Decisions

Our reports are designed to be practical.

They are not simply informal estimates or market-value opinions. Each evaluation is prepared to help clients understand how the final replacement cost was developed.

Depending on the engagement, the report may include:

  • Property and building descriptions
  • Construction classifications
  • Component-level cost summaries
  • Replacement cost calculations
  • Local cost considerations
  • Demolition and debris removal allowances
  • Contractor overhead and related soft costs
  • Photographic documentation
  • Insurance-to-value comparisons
  • A summary of assumptions and methodology

This structure gives boards and insurance professionals a clear document they can use during coverage reviews and policy renewals.

Our Commitment to Independence

Coverage Estimate provides independent valuation services.

Our role is to evaluate the property and develop a supportable replacement cost estimate. We do not base our conclusions on a desired policy limit, a previous appraisal amount, or a predetermined outcome.

This independence allows us to focus on the property, the construction data, and the cost factors relevant to rebuilding.

Accuracy Without Unnecessary Complexity

Insurance valuation can involve detailed construction information, but the final report should still be understandable.

We aim to explain our findings in clear language so board members, property managers, and insurance professionals can see how the estimate was developed and why the recommended value may differ from the existing policy limit.

When questions arise, we help clients understand the major cost drivers and the assumptions behind the valuation.

Supporting Responsible Coverage Decisions

Association boards and property managers carry an important responsibility. They must protect shared property, manage community resources, and make insurance decisions that may affect every owner.

A documented replacement cost evaluation cannot prevent a fire, hurricane, hailstorm, or other major loss. However, it can help an association make more informed decisions before a loss occurs.

That preparation matters.

Adequate insurance limits may help reduce uncertainty, support smoother claim handling, and lower the risk that an association will face a substantial rebuilding shortfall.

When to Update an Insurance Appraisal

Replacement costs change over time. Associations should consider obtaining a new valuation when:

  • The current appraisal is several years old
  • Major renovations or additions have been completed
  • Construction costs have increased significantly
  • New amenities or structures have been added
  • The insurance carrier requests updated documentation
  • The property has changed management or insurance representation
  • The association is approaching a policy renewal
  • Existing coverage appears inconsistent with similar properties
  • The board is concerned about coinsurance or underinsurance

Regular reviews help ensure that insurance values remain connected to current reconstruction conditions. Many carriers and reserve professionals recommend a full appraisal every 3–5 years, with lighter updates in the years between.

Frequently Asked Questions About Coverage Estimate

Is Coverage Estimate an insurance company or broker?

No. Coverage Estimate is an independent appraisal and valuation firm. We do not sell insurance, place coverage, or set premiums. Our role is to establish a documented replacement cost that the association and its insurance professionals can rely on.

Why does market value not determine insurance value?

Market value includes land and reflects buyer demand. Insurance value should reflect what it costs to rebuild the insured structures, which depends on construction type, labor, materials, and building codes.

How long does a Coverage Estimate appraisal take?

Most engagements are completed in 10–15 business days from site inspection to final report delivery.

Where does Coverage Estimate work?

Our licensed appraisal specialists serve associations nationwide, across all 50 states.
