Why owners call

When the check doesn’t match the damage, call a South Carolina Insurance Appraiser

Most underpaid claims aren’t denied — they’re simply short. A slope left off the roof diagram, flooring that can’t be matched, a code upgrade nobody priced, a tree-removal line capped too low. South Carolina Insurance Appraiser exists for that space between “covered” and “paid in full.”

We don’t edit the carrier’s estimate. We inspect, measure and write our own, then compare the two line by line so you can see exactly where the money went missing.

How the firm works

✓Appraisal usually fits

  • Coverage accepted, price disputed
  • Repair-or-replace and matching arguments
  • Missing trades, code items or overhead and profit
  • Roof measurements and steep or high charges

✕Usually not the right tool

  • A full denial of coverage
  • Bad-faith or other legal claims
  • A gap too small to justify the fees
  • A deadline that needs an attorney first

FAQ

Questions people ask a South Carolina Insurance Appraiser

Short, straight answers. The full FAQ covers costs, timing and South Carolina specifics.

All FAQs Glossary
What does a South Carolina Insurance Appraiser do?

A South Carolina Insurance Appraiser values the damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. As your appointed appraiser we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, when needed, present open items to a neutral umpire.

How is an appraiser different from a public adjuster?

A public adjuster handles and negotiates the whole claim. An appraiser works within the policy’s appraisal clause on one question: the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

Can appraisal settle a wind-versus-flood argument?

It can value the wind damage under the wind policy, but whether an item was caused by wind or water may be treated as a coverage question. Flood policies have their own rules. Good photos and water-line evidence matter most.

Is the award binding?

On the amount of loss, generally yes, once any two of the three panel members sign. Coverage questions and legal claims sit outside appraisal.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half of the umpire. We put our fee in writing before any work begins, and the first conversation is free.

Where does a South Carolina Insurance Appraiser work?

Statewide — all 46 counties, with regular work in Charleston, Columbia, Greenville, Myrtle Beach, Hilton Head Island, Beaufort, Florence, Rock Hill and Aiken.
