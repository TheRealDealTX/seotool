How to Plan a Vancouver Island Elopement

August 30, 20265 min readBy Evergrey Photography

A Vancouver Island elopement gives you old-growth forest, wild Pacific beaches and mountain viewpoints, often within an hour of each other. You also skip the guest list, the seating chart and the months of logistics. Eloping still takes some planning, though. This guide covers what we walk couples through before every elopement we photograph: the legal paperwork, how to pick a place, permits, weather, and how the day itself tends to unfold.

What counts as an elopement today?

An elopement used to mean running off in secret. Now it usually means a small, intentional wedding built around the two of you. Some couples bring nobody but their two witnesses. Others bring parents, a handful of friends and a picnic. What they share is that the day is shaped by the experience you want, not by what a venue package includes.

On Vancouver Island that often means a ceremony on a driftwood-strewn beach at golden hour, a hike to a viewpoint, or vows under the cedars followed by dinner somewhere cozy in Victoria or Sooke. If you want to see how one of these days really unfolds, read the story of Kasha and Justin's rainy, handmade elopement.

Step 1: Get your BC marriage licence

To be legally married in British Columbia you need a marriage licence, issued by a marriage licence issuer. Many Service BC offices and private issuers (often insurance or registry offices) can issue one. As of our most recent check:

  • The licence fee is about $100.
  • The licence is valid for three months from the date it is issued, so do not get it too early.
  • You need valid government ID. There is no residency requirement, so couples visiting from outside Canada can marry here too.
  • You need two witnesses at the ceremony. Your photographer can sometimes act as one, but ask ahead.
  • The ceremony must be performed by a licensed marriage commissioner or an authorized religious representative.

Requirements and fees can change. Please confirm the current rules with BC Vital Statistics before you book anything that depends on them.

Marriage commissioners are assigned by geographic area, and many will travel to a beach or trailhead for a small added fee. Book yours early in summer, when calendars fill up.

Step 2: Choose the landscape that feels like you

The island has three broad flavours of elopement backdrop. Most couples know straight away which one they want.

Forest

Mossy cedars, ferns and soft, even light, even at midday. Goldstream Provincial Park and the trails of the Sooke Hills give you that deep-green, cathedral feeling without a long drive from Victoria. Forests are also forgiving in drizzle, because the canopy keeps the worst of the rain off.

Beach and coastline

Pebble coves, tide pools and long horizons. East Sooke Regional Park, including the meadow at Aylard Farm, French Beach and Botanical Beach near Port Renfrew are favourites. Check the tide tables, since some beaches nearly disappear at high tide.

Mountains and viewpoints

Panoramic views over the Salish Sea and the Olympic Mountains. Mount Douglas (PKOLS) and Mount Work are close to the city, and the drive up-island opens up much bigger terrain.

Step 3: Check permits and park rules

Many of the most photogenic places on the island are in parks, and each park system has its own rules for ceremonies and commercial photography.

  • CRD Regional Parks (East Sooke, Witty's Lagoon, Mount Work and others) may need a permit for ceremonies or organized gatherings, depending on group size and setup.
  • BC Parks (Goldstream, French Beach, Juan de Fuca and others) often require a park use permit for weddings and for commercial photography.
  • Parks Canada manages Pacific Rim National Park Reserve near Tofino and Ucluelet, which has its own permit process for ceremonies and professional shoots.

Permit requirements, fees and processing times change, so please confirm directly with the managing authority well before your date. We are happy to help you work out who manages your chosen spot.

Step 4: Plan to leave no trace

These places stay beautiful because people look after them. Skip confetti, petals and anything that can blow away. Stay on established trails, especially through fragile meadows and moss. Pack out everything you bring. Bubbles, a sparkler exit in a safe spot (where fire rules allow), or simply a long walk along the shoreline make lovely, low-impact alternatives.

A sample elopement timeline

Here is how a relaxed, half-day summer elopement near Sooke might flow:

  1. 4:00 pm: Getting ready at your rental or cabin. Details, letters, and a first look on the deck.
  2. 5:15 pm: Drive to the trailhead and take a short walk in.
  3. 6:00 pm: Ceremony with your commissioner and witnesses, followed by signing.
  4. 6:30 pm: Toasts, hugs and a few family photos if guests have joined.
  5. 7:00 pm: Portraits as the light softens, exploring the shoreline and forest edge.
  6. 8:30 pm: Golden hour and sunset portraits.
  7. 9:15 pm: A picnic, a bonfire where permitted, or dinner reservations in town.

Including the people you love

Eloping does not have to mean leaving everyone out. Some couples video-call family during the ceremony. Others host a backyard party a few weeks later and share a slideshow of their photos. Many simply write letters to be read during getting-ready. Do whatever feels honest to you.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need witnesses to elope in BC?

Yes. British Columbia requires two witnesses at the ceremony, in addition to the marriage commissioner or religious representative. Confirm current requirements with BC Vital Statistics.

How long is a BC marriage licence valid?

A BC marriage licence is valid for three months from the date it is issued, so plan to pick it up within that window before your elopement date.

Do we need a permit to elope in a park on Vancouver Island?

Often, yes. CRD Regional Parks, BC Parks and Parks Canada each have their own rules for ceremonies and commercial photography. Contact the managing authority for your location to confirm.

When is the best time of year for a Vancouver Island elopement?

Summer offers the driest weather and longest evenings. Late spring and early autumn offer softer light and fewer crowds. Every season works with good planning and a rain plan.
