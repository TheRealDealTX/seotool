How to Build a Wedding Day Timeline in Victoria, BC

August 16, 20265 min readBy Evergrey Photography

A good wedding day timeline is the quiet hero of every relaxed wedding. It decides whether you get twenty unhurried minutes with your partner at golden hour or five frantic ones in a parking lot. After photographing weddings and elopements across Victoria and Vancouver Island, we have learned that the best timelines are built backwards from two fixed points: your ceremony time and the sunset. This guide walks you through both, with a sample schedule you can adapt.

Start with the light, not the clock

Victoria sits far enough north that sunset time swings dramatically through the year. That swing changes everything about when portraits look their best. As a rough guide:

Time of yearApproximate sunsetWhat it means for your day
Late June (summer solstice)Around 9:20 pm PDTGolden hour falls during or after dinner. Plan a short portrait break between courses.
Late March / late September (equinoxes)Around 7:15 pm (March) to 7:00 pm (September)Golden hour lands near cocktail hour. Easy to fit portraits in before dinner.
Early December (winter)Around 4:20 pm PSTLight fades early. Consider an earlier ceremony and a first look so portraits happen in daylight.

These are approximate. For your exact date and location, use our golden hour calculator, which gives sunset, golden hour and blue hour times for spots around the island. Remember that hills, forests and tall buildings can hide the sun earlier than the official sunset, so a forest ceremony at Goldstream loses direct light sooner than an open beach on Dallas Road.

A sample 8-hour wedding day timeline

Here is a typical eight-hour schedule for a 4:00 pm ceremony in early September, with a first look. Shift every row earlier or later to suit your season.

TimeWhat happens
12:00 pmPhotographer arrives. Details: rings, invitation suite, florals, shoes.
12:30 pmGetting ready candids, final touches on hair and makeup.
1:15 pmGetting dressed. Letters or gifts exchanged.
1:45 pmTravel to first look location.
2:00 pmFirst look and couple portraits.
2:45 pmWedding party photos.
3:20 pmBuffer, touch-ups, tuck away before guests arrive.
4:00 pmCeremony (allow 20 to 40 minutes).
4:40 pmFamily formals, then cocktail hour.
5:45 pmReception room details before guests enter.
6:00 pmGrand entrance, dinner and speeches.
7:15 pmGolden hour portraits (15 to 20 minutes).
7:45 pmCake cutting and first dance.
8:00 pmOpen dance floor, coverage ends.

First look or no first look?

There is no wrong answer here, only trade-offs.

With a first look

  • Most portraits and wedding party photos are done before the ceremony, while hair and makeup are fresh.
  • You get to actually attend your own cocktail hour.
  • It works especially well in winter, when daylight runs out early.

Without a first look

  • The moment you see each other happens at the end of the aisle, in front of everyone you love.
  • Portraits and wedding party photos move to after the ceremony, which usually means most of cocktail hour.
  • You need a little more time between ceremony and dinner. Plan for at least 75 to 90 minutes.

Some couples split the difference with a private first touch, holding hands around a corner without seeing each other. It is a lovely way to share a quiet moment without giving up the aisle reveal.

Build in buffer time (more than you think)

Every timeline we have seen run late ran late for the same reasons: hair and makeup, travel and family photos. A few rules of thumb we use:

  • Add 15 minutes to hair and makeup. Ask your artists when they will actually finish, not when they plan to.
  • Double the travel time on Google Maps if you are crossing town on a summer Saturday. Downtown Victoria, the Malahat and ferry traffic can all add surprises.
  • Schedule a 15 to 20 minute buffer just before the ceremony. If everything runs on time, you get to breathe.
  • Keep the ceremony start realistic. Guests often trickle in, so a 4:00 pm ceremony frequently starts at 4:05 or later.

Family formals without the chaos

Family photos are where timelines most often slip. A little preparation keeps them to 20 or 30 minutes:

  1. Write a numbered list of groupings and share it with us before the day. Around 8 to 12 groupings is a comfortable number.
  2. Start with the largest group and work down, so people can leave as they finish.
  3. Ask your officiant to announce that family should stay at the front after the ceremony.
  4. Choose a family member who knows everyone to help gather people. That person is worth their weight in gold.
  5. Pick a shaded or evenly lit spot near the ceremony site so nobody has to walk far.

Tip: If grandparents or guests with mobility concerns are part of the formals, put their groupings first and have chairs nearby.

Placing golden hour portraits

Even if you do most of your portraits early, we always suggest sneaking away for 15 to 20 minutes during golden hour. In summer that usually means a break between dinner courses or right after speeches. In autumn it often lines up with cocktail hour. Let your planner and caterer know in advance so dinner service can work around it. Those few minutes are often where your favourite images come from.

Putting it all together

Start with the ceremony time and sunset, block in getting ready, decide on a first look, add generous buffers and keep family formals organized. Then share the draft with your planner, venue and photographer so everyone is working from the same page. Frequently asked questions

How many hours of wedding photography do I need?

Most full weddings fit comfortably into 7 to 9 hours of coverage, from getting ready through the first dance and some open dancing. Smaller weddings and elopements often need 2 to 5 hours.

When is golden hour in Victoria, BC?

Golden hour is roughly the hour before sunset. In Victoria that ranges from about 8:20 pm in late June to about 3:20 pm in early December. Use our golden hour calculator for your exact date.

How long do family photos take?

With an organized list of 8 to 12 groupings and a helper to gather people, family formals usually take 20 to 30 minutes.

Should we do a first look?

A first look frees up your cocktail hour and is especially helpful in winter when daylight is short. Skipping it keeps the aisle reveal, but you will need more time between the ceremony and dinner.
