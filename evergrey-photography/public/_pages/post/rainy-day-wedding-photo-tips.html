Rainy Day Wedding Photos: How to Love the West Coast Weather

October 4, 20264 min readBy Evergrey Photography

If you are getting married on Vancouver Island, it is worth planning for rainy day wedding photos no matter which month you choose. Victoria is one of the drier corners of the coast, but showers can roll in any time of year, especially from October through April. The good news? Some of our favourite images were made in the rain. Soft, overcast light flatters everyone, the forests turn a deep, glowing green and the mood becomes wonderfully cinematic.

Change the mindset first

Rain only ruins photos when it ruins the mood. Couples who laugh it off, grab an umbrella and lean in to each other end up with images that feel more alive than any blue-sky afternoon. One of our favourite examples is Kasha and Justin's elopement. The rain stopped just as their ceremony began, and the damp forest and sand added to the story rather than taking away from it.

Gear that makes a real difference

Clear umbrellas

Clear bubble umbrellas let light reach your faces and keep the background visible. Coloured or black umbrellas cast shadows and can tint your skin. Buy two or three; they are inexpensive and photograph beautifully. We often bring one as well.

Footwear and a towel

Pack a second pair of shoes for walking to portrait spots: waterproof boots, clean sneakers or rain boots that suit your style. A small towel for drying hands, faces and the hem of a dress is a lifesaver.

Layers

A cozy knit, a wool coat or a jacket over a suit can look intentional and romantic in photos. Choose layers in neutral or earthy tones so they feel like part of your look.

Dress care

Many gowns survive rain, mud and sand far better than you might expect. Bustles and a helper to lift the train between locations help. Ask your bridal shop about cleaning before the day so you are not worrying about it during.

Covered and sheltered spots around Victoria

You do not have to cancel portraits when it pours. Some ideas:

  • Your venue's covered spaces. Porches, verandas, covered patios, arbours, barns and large doorways all create lovely, directional light. Ask your venue coordinator where they are before the day.
  • Old growth and second growth forest. A dense canopy catches a surprising amount of rain. Parks like Goldstream and the trails around East Sooke can feel almost dry under the trees during a light shower.
  • Heritage buildings and arcades. Downtown Victoria has plenty of character, from brick laneways to covered entrances, that work well for a short portrait session.
  • Your car or a doorway. Some of the most intimate images happen in small, sheltered spaces with a little window light.

Build a rain plan into your timeline

A rain plan is not just an indoor ceremony site. It is also a timeline that bends. A few habits that help:

  1. Add buffer around portraits. If a shower passes through, we can wait ten minutes for it to clear. Our wedding timeline planner makes it easy to slot in an extra cushion.
  2. Watch the radar on the day. West Coast showers often move quickly. We keep an eye on the forecast and grab breaks in the weather for outdoor portraits.
  3. Consider a first look. Doing portraits earlier gives you more chances to catch a dry window.
  4. Have the indoor option ready. Ask your venue for the latest time they need to know whether you are flipping to the indoor setup.
  5. Tell guests. A note on your website suggesting layers and sensible shoes keeps everyone comfortable.

One more thing: give yourselves permission to step out into the rain on purpose. A two-minute dash under a clear umbrella, or a kiss with drops in the air, often becomes the image couples frame first.

Keeping guests and details comfortable

Rain affects more than portraits. A few small touches keep your guests happy and your details looking their best:

  • Set out a basket of umbrellas or blankets near the entrance. They also make sweet details in photos.
  • Move paper goods such as seating charts and signage under cover, or have them printed on acrylic or wood.
  • Ask your florist about arrangements that can handle wind and moisture if they will sit outside.
  • Lay down mats or runners at entrances so floors stay safe and dry.
  • Have a dry spot ready for your ceremony musicians and their equipment.

When guests are warm and dry, they relax, and that relaxed energy shows in candid photos throughout the day.

How we photograph in the rain

We carry weather-sealed gear, rain covers and lens cloths, so a downpour does not stop us. We look for backlight to make raindrops sparkle, use puddles for reflections and lean into the moody tones that make West Coast weddings feel so distinct. When rain is heavy, we work under cover and move out in short bursts. When it is light, we usually stay out and enjoy it. And after dark, rain plus string lights or a single off-camera flash can create some of the most dramatic images of the night.

Remember: overcast skies are actually ideal for portraits. No squinting, no harsh shadows, just soft light that makes skin look its best.

Embrace it

The couples who get the most beautiful rainy day wedding photos are the ones who decide early that weather will not define their day. Plan for it, pack for it and then let it go. If you are planning a Victoria wedding or a moody coastal elopement, we would love to hear about it. Write to us at info@evergrey-photography.com.

Frequently asked questions

Do rainy day wedding photos look good?

Yes. Overcast skies create soft, flattering light, and rain adds atmosphere. With clear umbrellas, sheltered spots and a flexible timeline, rainy days often produce some of the most memorable images.

What umbrellas are best for wedding photos?

Clear umbrellas are best because they let light through to your faces and keep the background visible. Dark or coloured umbrellas can cast shadows or tint skin tones.

When does it rain most in Victoria, BC?

Victoria is relatively dry for the West Coast, with most rain falling between about October and April. Summers are usually dry, but showers are possible at any time of year.

Will you still photograph outside if it rains?

Usually, yes. We carry weather-sealed gear and rain covers and look for breaks in the weather, sheltered spots and forest canopy so you still get outdoor portraits.
