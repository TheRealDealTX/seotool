Sarina + Riley: An Intimate Summer Wedding on Vancouver Island

August 22, 20203 min readBy Evergrey Photography

Sarina and Riley's wedding was small in the best way: a summer day on the island, the people who mattered most, and plenty of room to simply be together. It is the kind of day that reminds us why we love Vancouver Island wedding photography. When there is less to manage, there is more space for real moments to happen, and more time to notice them.

Why intimate weddings photograph so well

With a smaller guest list, everyone in the frame matters. There are no crowds to work around and no rushing from one obligation to the next. The conversations are longer, the hugs last a little more, and people stop performing for the camera far sooner than they would at a big event.

For Sarina and Riley, we kept our presence quiet and our direction gentle. Most of the day we photographed from the edges, letting it unfold. When we did step in, it was with simple prompts, such as walk together, lean in, tell each other something only the two of you know, rather than stiff poses.

Working with long summer light

Summer on Vancouver Island gives you a gift: very long evenings. Around the solstice the sun sets well after 9 p.m., so golden hour arrives late and lingers. Midday sun, on the other hand, is high and harsh. We planned the timeline so the brightest part of the afternoon was spent in open shade, and saved couple portraits for the evening when the light turns warm and low.

That kind of planning is easy to do ahead of time. Our golden hour calculator shows exactly when the light gets good for any date, and the wedding timeline planner helps you build a day around it.

The timeline choices that made the difference

  • Breathing room between moments. We built in short buffers so nobody felt hurried, which means fewer tense faces and more laughter.
  • Family photos kept short. A clear, pre-written list meant groupings were done in minutes and everyone got back to celebrating.
  • A quiet sunset walk. Ten or fifteen minutes away from the party, just the two of them, almost always produces the photos couples love most.

Details that tell the story

Intimate weddings tend to be full of personal touches, and we make time for them early in the day, before guests arrive and the schedule speeds up. Rings, florals, stationery, an heirloom, the shoes that will be kicked off by the second dance: we photograph them in soft window light or open shade so they sit naturally alongside the rest of the gallery. They are small things, but years later they are often what brings the whole day rushing back.

We also photograph the room before anyone walks in. Tables set, candles not yet lit, chairs waiting. It is a quiet record of all the planning that went into the celebration.

Moments over perfection

Our favourite frames from the day are not the most polished ones. They are the in-between moments: a hand squeezed during the vows, a parent wiping away a tear, friends doubled over laughing during a toast. We look for those moments all day and keep our editing natural and true to colour, so the photos feel like the day felt.

That is what we try to bring to every wedding, whether it is ten guests or a hundred and fifty. You can see more of our approach on our wedding photography page, or browse the portfolio.
