20 Questions to Ask Your Wedding Photographer Before You Book

June 21, 20264 min readBy Evergrey Photography

Your photos are one of the few things from your wedding that last for decades, so it pays to ask the right questions to ask your wedding photographer before you sign anything. We have grouped twenty questions into five categories, with a short note on why each one matters. Bring this list to your consultation calls, whether you are booking us or anyone else.

Style and approach

  1. How would you describe your photography style? Documentary, editorial, fine art, moody, bright and airy? You want a photographer whose natural style matches what you love, not one who has to imitate it.
  2. Can we see two or three full wedding galleries? Highlights show the best moments. Full galleries show consistency through tricky light, family formals and dark receptions.
  3. How much do you direct versus observe? Some couples want gentle prompts; others want to be left alone. Knowing the balance helps you picture the day.
  4. How do you handle harsh midday sun or dim venues? Victoria weddings range from bright beaches to candlelit heritage halls. Ask how they adapt.
  5. Have you photographed at our venue, or somewhere similar? Familiarity is helpful, though not essential. A good photographer will scout or research a new location.

Logistics on the day

  1. Will you be the photographer at our wedding? Some studios send associate photographers. Make sure you know exactly who will be there.
  2. Do you work with a second photographer? Ask whether it is included, optional, and who that person is.
  3. How do you help plan the timeline? An experienced photographer should help you build a realistic schedule. Our wedding timeline planner is a good starting point.
  4. Do you need a meal and breaks? Most photographers working long days need a vendor meal. Plan for it in your catering numbers.
  5. What happens if it rains? On the West Coast, this one matters. Listen for a calm, practical answer. See our rainy day wedding photo tips.

Contract, insurance and backups

  1. Do you provide a written contract? Always get one. It should cover coverage hours, deliverables, delivery timelines, payment schedule and cancellation terms.
  2. Do you carry liability insurance? Many venues in Victoria and across Vancouver Island require proof of insurance from vendors.
  3. How do you back up images on the day? Look for cameras that record to two memory cards at once, plus multiple backups once they are home.
  4. What happens if you are sick or have an emergency? A professional will have a plan, such as a network of trusted photographers who can step in.
  5. What is your cancellation and rescheduling policy? Life happens. Understand what you would owe and what is refundable.

Why backups matter: memory cards can fail. Dual card recording means every image exists in two places from the moment it is taken.

Editing and delivery

  1. How many images will we receive? Ask for a typical range rather than a guarantee. Quality matters more than quantity.
  2. How long until we see our photos? Many photographers share a preview within days and the full gallery within a set number of weeks. Make sure it is in the contract.
  3. How are photos delivered, and for how long are they hosted? Online galleries are standard. Ask about download quality and how long the gallery stays live, then download and back up your images yourself.
  4. Do we get print rights? You should be able to print your photos for personal use without restrictions.

Pricing and value

  1. What is included, and what costs extra? Ask about travel, overtime, second photographer, engagement sessions, albums and prints, and whether tax is included. Our guide to wedding photographer costs in Victoria explains typical ranges, and our pricing estimator shows how our collections add up.

Do not be shy about asking follow-up questions, either. A good photographer will happily explain how they work, and clear, specific answers are a strong sign of a professional who has done this many times before. Vague answers, on the other hand, are worth a second look.

Questions to ask yourselves first

Before you contact anyone, it helps to answer a few questions as a couple. Your answers make consultations far more productive:

  • Which three or four photographers' galleries made you stop scrolling, and what did they have in common?
  • Do you prefer candid, documentary moments or more posed, polished portraits?
  • How many hours of coverage do you think you need, and which parts of the day matter most?
  • Would you like an engagement session, an album or prints?
  • What is your realistic budget range for photography?

Knowing your priorities lets you compare answers quickly and keeps the conversation focused on what matters to you.

Beyond the questions

Answers matter, but so does how a conversation feels. Your photographer will be beside you for most of your wedding day, from the quiet moments getting ready to the last dance. Notice whether they listen, whether they are curious about your plans and whether you feel at ease. That comfort shows up in your photos.

A quick checklist before you book

  • You have seen at least two full galleries you love.
  • You know exactly who will photograph your day.
  • Coverage, deliverables and timelines are written in a contract.
  • Backups, insurance and an emergency plan are in place.
  • You understand the total cost, including tax and extras.

Frequently asked questions

What should I ask a wedding photographer before booking?

Ask about their style, whether you can see full galleries, who will photograph your day, how they back up images, their insurance, delivery timelines, print rights and exactly what is included in the price.

Why should I ask to see full wedding galleries?

Full galleries show how consistently a photographer works through the whole day, including difficult lighting, family formals and receptions, not just their best highlight images.

Do wedding photographers need insurance?

Many venues require vendors to carry liability insurance, and it is a sign of a professional business. Ask your photographer to confirm their coverage.

How long does it take to receive wedding photos?

It varies by photographer. Many share a small preview within days and deliver the full gallery within several weeks. The timeline should be written into your contract.
