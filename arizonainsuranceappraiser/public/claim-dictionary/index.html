Arizona Property Claim Dictionary

42 terms from policies, estimates and appraisal awards, from actual cash value to wildland-urban interface, defined in plain English.

42 terms

ABCDEHILMOPRSUWX
Actual cash value (ACV)
Replacement cost minus depreciation. Often the first payment on a replacement cost claim.
Additional living expense (ALE)
Coverage for extra costs of living elsewhere while your home is being repaired after a covered loss.
Amount of loss
The dollar value of covered damage. The only thing an appraisal panel decides.
Appraisal award
The written decision on the amount of loss, binding once signed by two of the three panel members.
Appraisal clause
The policy condition that lets either party demand appraisal when they disagree on the amount of loss.
Battens
Wood or plastic strips fastened to the roof deck that hold tile in place on some roof systems.
Business income
Commercial coverage for lost income while a business is closed or limited because of covered property damage.
Coinsurance
A commercial policy condition that can reduce payment if the building is insured for less than a required percentage of its value.
Coverage A
Dwelling coverage on a homeowners policy, the limit for the house itself.
Coverage B
Other structures coverage for detached garages, walls, fences, ramadas and similar items.
Declarations page
The summary page of your policy listing limits, deductibles, endorsements and the policy period.
Depreciation
The reduction in value from age, wear and condition, deducted from replacement cost to reach ACV.
DIFI
The Arizona Department of Insurance and Financial Institutions, which regulates insurers and handles consumer complaints.
Elastomeric coating
A flexible reflective coating applied over spray foam roofs to protect the foam from sun and weather.
Endorsement
A written change to a policy that adds, removes or modifies coverage.
Haboob
A wall of dust pushed ahead of a collapsing thunderstorm, common during the Arizona monsoon.
Holdback
Recoverable depreciation the insurer withholds until repairs are completed.
Ice dam
A ridge of ice at the eave that traps meltwater, which can back up under roofing.
Line item
A single priced entry in an estimate, such as a quantity of drywall or a roof component.
Matching
Whether repairs must reasonably match undamaged areas in color, texture and profile.
Microburst
A sudden, concentrated downdraft from a thunderstorm that spreads damaging straight-line wind.
Mitigation
Reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a loss, such as tarping or drying.
Monsoon
Arizona’s summer storm season, officially June 15 to September 30.
Ordinance or law coverage
Coverage for added costs of meeting current building codes during repair.
Overhead and profit (O&P)
A general contractor’s charge for coordinating trades and running the job, often 10% plus 10%.
Price list
The regional cost database an estimating program uses to price line items.
Proof of loss
A sworn statement of the amount claimed, which some policies require within a set time after the insurer asks.
Public adjuster
A licensed professional who represents policyholders in preparing and negotiating claims for a fee.
Recoverable depreciation
Depreciation you can collect after repairs under a replacement cost policy.
Replacement cost value (RCV)
The cost to repair or replace damaged property with like kind and quality, without deducting depreciation.
Scope of loss
The list of everything that must be repaired or replaced, before pricing.
Slab leak
A leak in a water line running beneath a concrete slab foundation.
Spray polyurethane foam (SPF)
A sprayed roofing material common on Arizona flat roofs, protected by an elastomeric coating.
Square
100 square feet of roof surface, the standard unit for roofing.
Steep charge
An added labor charge for roofs too steep to walk safely, often starting at 7/12 or 8/12.
Suit limitation
The policy time limit for filing a lawsuit over the claim.
Supplement
A request to add missing items or costs to the insurer’s estimate.
Surplus lines insurer
A non-admitted insurer that covers risks the standard market will not. Policies can differ from standard forms.
Umpire
The neutral third member of an appraisal panel who decides items the two appraisers cannot agree on.
Underlayment
The waterproof layer beneath roof tile or shingles; on tile roofs it does most of the waterproofing.
Wildland-urban interface (WUI)
Areas where homes meet forest, brush or grassland, with higher wildfire exposure.
Xactimate
Widely used estimating software for property insurance claims.
