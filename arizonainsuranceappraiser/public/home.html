The short-pay problem

Covered is not the same as paid in full

Most disputed Arizona claims are not denials. The carrier pays something, just not enough: a tile roof priced as a few spot repairs, a block wall left off a monsoon claim, a slab leak scoped as one wet room. An Arizona Insurance Appraiser works in exactly that gap between “covered” and “paid.”

We are independent estimators who serve as the appraiser you name under your policy. Arizona Insurance Appraiser never edits the carrier’s estimate; we measure, count and price the loss ourselves, so every line can be defended in front of an umpire.

  • Our own scope. Measured on site, never copied.
  • Defensible numbers. Built to persuade an umpire, not to impress you.
  • Honest triage. If you need an attorney or just a supplement, we say so.

How an appraisal moves with us

The appraisal clause turns an open-ended argument into a process with a binding result. Here is how Arizona Insurance Appraiser takes a file from first call to signed award.

Read the full appraisal roadmap
  1. 01

    Free review

    We read your policy, the carrier’s estimate and your photos, then tell you plainly whether appraisal is the right tool.

  2. 02

    Independent inspection

    Measurements, tile counts, moisture readings and photographs, building our own scope from the ground up.

  3. 03

    Line-item valuation

    An estimate priced for your Arizona ZIP code, with code items, permits and overhead and profit handled correctly.

  4. 04

    Panel work

    The written demand goes out, appraisers are named, and we work line by line with the carrier’s appraiser.

  5. 05

    Signed award

    Items still in dispute go to a neutral umpire. Two signatures set the amount of loss, and payment follows under the policy.

Clearing things up

Four myths an Arizona Insurance Appraiser hears every week

Appraisal is only for huge commercial claims.

Most of our files are homes. If the gap is meaningful after fees, appraisal can fit a residential claim.

The appraisal panel decides whether I’m covered.

No. Appraisal decides the amount of a covered loss. Coverage disputes belong with an attorney.

My contractor can be my appraiser.

Policies usually require an independent appraiser. A contractor who will do the work invites a challenge.

Asking for appraisal stops every deadline.

Not automatically. Suit limitation and other deadlines may keep running, so calendar them.

All questions Claim dictionary
What does an Arizona Insurance Appraiser do?

We value property damage when you and your insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on what it costs. Named as your appraiser, we inspect, build an independent line-item estimate, work with the carrier’s appraiser and, if needed, present unresolved items to a neutral umpire.

How is an appraiser different from a public adjuster?

A public adjuster manages and negotiates your entire claim. An appraiser works inside the policy’s appraisal clause on one question, the amount of loss. We don’t negotiate outside appraisal and we don’t give legal advice.

When should I call an Arizona Insurance Appraiser?

When coverage is accepted, you have the carrier’s final estimate, and the gap between it and your contractor’s number is meaningful, usually more than about 10% or several thousand dollars.

Is the appraisal award binding?

On the amount of loss, generally yes, once two of the three panel members sign. That is why documentation matters before anyone invokes appraisal.

What does it cost?

Each side pays its own appraiser and usually half the umpire’s fee. We put our fee in writing before any work begins, and the first conversation is free.

Do you cover my part of Arizona?

Yes. We work in all 15 counties, including metro Phoenix, Tucson, Flagstaff, Prescott, Yuma, Lake Havasu City and Sierra Vista.
