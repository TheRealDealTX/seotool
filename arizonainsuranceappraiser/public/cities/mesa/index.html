Mesa’s housing mix

Mesa grew in waves. Dobson Ranch and much of west Mesa filled in during the 1970s and 1980s with stucco and block homes, many of which are now on their second or third roof. East Mesa added large active-adult communities and manufactured-home and RV resorts that fill with winter visitors. Red Mountain Ranch, Las Sendas and the Usery Pass area added custom homes on desert lots, and Eastmark brought a new master-planned community on the former General Motors proving grounds.

Storms and losses we see

East Valley monsoon storms often build over the Superstition Mountains and race west. Microbursts topple palms and eucalyptus, peel tile and rip manufactured-home roofing and skirting. Dust storms follow the same path. Hail is less frequent than wind but heavy when it comes, and it can crack tile and dent cooler housings across whole subdivisions.

Inside the home, water heaters, supply lines and slab leaks are frequent, especially in houses with original plumbing. Seasonal residents may not discover a leak for weeks, which brings the question of long-term seepage into play.

Manufactured homes and 55+ communities

Manufactured homes are repaired with different materials and methods than site-built homes, and pricing must reflect that. In age-restricted communities, the HOA may insure some components while the owner insures others. We read both documents before valuing the loss.

Areas we cover

Dobson Ranch, downtown Mesa, Lehi, Alta Mesa, Red Mountain Ranch, Las Sendas, Eastmark, Superstition Springs, Leisure World and the communities along the US-60 corridor.

Questions from Mesa property owners

Do you handle manufactured-home claims in Mesa?

Yes. We price manufactured-home repairs with the methods and materials those homes actually use.

I was away for the summer and found water damage. Can I still claim?

Report it promptly. Expect questions about when the leak began and about vacancy terms. Evidence such as utility usage can help establish timing.

Does Mesa require permits for roof replacement?

Many roofing projects require permits in Mesa. Permit fees and code items are legitimate parts of the repair cost when required.
