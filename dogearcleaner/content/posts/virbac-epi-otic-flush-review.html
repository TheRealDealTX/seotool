Epi-Otic review at a glance: who should buy it

Epi-Otic Advanced is a maintenance cleaner. It suits dogs that build up brown wax, dogs with floppy ears that trap moisture, swimmers, and allergy-prone dogs whose vets want the ear canal kept clean between flare-ups.

It is not a cure. If your dog's ear smells yeasty, looks swollen or hurts to touch, start with a vet visit and our guide to dog ear infection signs.

What is Virbac Epi-Otic Advanced Ear Cleaner?

Virbac is a veterinary pharmaceutical company, and Epi-Otic has been a fixture in vet clinics for years. The product most US owners buy today is Epi-Otic Advanced. Virbac describes it as a cleanser for "routine cleansing of ears and maintenance of healthy ear canals."

Virbac specifically recommends regular at-home cleaning for three groups:

  • Allergic or atopic dogs
  • Dogs with floppy or droopy ears (see our floppy-ear breeds guide)
  • Dogs that swim or get wet often

You'll see it listed as "Epi-Otic Advanced Ear Cleanser," "Epi-Otic Advanced Ear Cleaner" and simply "Epi-Otic Ear Cleaner." In the US these are the same product.

Epi-Otic Advanced vs the original Epi-Otic formula

A lot of confusion in Epi-Otic reviews comes from the fact that there have been two formulas.

  • Original Epi-Otic was built around lactic acid and salicylic acid. Labels from Australia and New Zealand, where it is still sold as "Epi-Otic Ear and Skin Cleanser," list roughly 25 mg/mL lactic acid and 1.1 mg/mL salicylic acid. It was an acidifying cleanser.
  • Epi-Otic Advanced was introduced after veterinarians asked for something for dogs with more stubborn, chronic ear problems. It switched to a neutral pH and added Virbac's glycotechnology sugars and an odor-neutralizing complex.

So if an older article, including the previous version of this page, tells you Epi-Otic works mainly through lactic acid, that describes the original. On the current US Advanced label, lactic acid is listed only as "may contain."

Is Epi-Otic alcohol-free and pH balanced?

There is no SD alcohol or ethanol on the label, which is the drying, stinging type of alcohol many owners want to avoid. (Phenoxyethanol is a preservative, not a drying alcohol.) Virbac states the pH is neutral, pH 7, "unlike acidic cleansers that can sting."

Virbac also notes that because the pH is neutral, it should not interfere with other ear preparations. That matters if your vet has prescribed drops.

Epiotic around the world: Ohrreiniger, nettoyant auriculaire, oorreiniger

A good share of the traffic to this Epi-Otic review comes from the Netherlands, the UK, Germany and the UAE. Here is what to know if you're buying outside the US.

  • Germany: sold as Virbac Epiotic Ohrreiniger ("ear cleaner").
  • France and Belgium: Epiotic nettoyant auriculaire.
  • Netherlands and Flanders: Epiotic oorreiniger.
  • UK: usually just "Virbac Epiotic Ear Cleaner," often in 125 ml bottles.
  • UAE and other import markets: you may find US "Epi-Otic Advanced" stock or European "Epiotic," depending on the seller.

The European Epiotic label we checked lists water, disodium EDTA, PCMX, diethylhexyl sodium sulphosuccinate, salicylic acid, the same three glycotechnology sugars, plus "Defensin technology" (Peumus boldus leaf extract and Spiraea ulmaria extract). It is also described as neutral pH.

"Diethylhexyl sodium sulphosuccinate" is the chemical name for docusate sodium, so the core cleaning chemistry matches. The plant extracts are the main difference. Virbac formulas and names can vary by country and change over time, so the label on your bottle is the final word.

How to use Epi-Otic as an ear flush, step by step

People search "epi otic ear flush" and "epiotic flush" because the technique matters as much as the product. This method follows Virbac's label directions. For more detail, see our full how to clean dog ears guide.

  1. Check the ear first

    Look and sniff. Mild brown wax is fine to clean. Redness, swelling, pus, blood, a strong yeasty smell or obvious pain means stop and call your vet.

  2. Shake the bottle and open the nozzle

    Shake well, then twist the tip to "open." Have [[amazon-search:cotton-rounds|large cotton rounds]] and a few treats ready.

  3. Fill the ear canal liberally

    Lift the ear flap and squeeze in enough solution to fill the canal. The label says to apply "liberally," and under-filling is the most common mistake. Don't jam the tip deep into the ear.

  4. Massage the base of the ear

    Massage the cartilage just below the ear opening for 20 to 30 seconds. A squishing sound means the liquid is working through the canal.

  5. Let your dog shake

    Step back. Shaking brings loosened wax and liquid up to where you can reach it.

  6. Wipe the inner flap and opening

    Use a cotton ball or soft cloth to wipe the inner ear flap and the visible part of the canal. Virbac's label warns against cotton-tipped applicators, which can push debris deeper.

  7. Reward and repeat on the other side

    Finish with a treat. A [[amazon-search:lick-mat|lick mat]] smeared with something tasty keeps wriggly dogs busy.

Tip: If your vet has prescribed ear drops, use Epi-Otic about 30 minutes first. If you can't wait, gently dry the canal with a cotton ball before applying the medication, per Virbac's directions.

How often should you use Epi-Otic Advanced?

The label gives a range: two or three times a week up to daily, or as your veterinarian directs. In practice:

  • Healthy ears, low wax: weekly or every other week is plenty for most dogs.
  • Swimmers and bath days: flush after the water exposure to help dry the canal (see after-bath ear care).
  • Allergy-prone or chronic cases: follow your vet's schedule. Some prescribe more frequent cleaning during flare-ups.

More is not always better. Over-cleaning a healthy ear can irritate it. Our ear cleaning routines guide covers schedules by breed and lifestyle.

When not to use Epi-Otic

See a vet first if: the ear is painful, swollen, bleeding or has thick discharge, your dog tilts its head or loses balance, or you suspect a ruptured eardrum. Virbac's label warns against using it in an ear with a ruptured eardrum, and no cleaner treats bacterial or yeast infections or ear mites on its own.

Also stop using it and call your vet if irritation develops or gets worse after cleaning. Not sure whether a symptom is a home-care issue? Try our ear symptom checker.

Using Epi-Otic alongside vet treatment

One reason vets like Epi-Otic is that it fits into a treatment plan instead of competing with it. Virbac positions the Advanced formula for use with ear medications for chronic otitis externa, and its neutral pH is designed not to interfere with other ear preparations.

A typical vet-directed routine looks like this:

  • Exam first. Your vet checks the eardrum and may take an ear cytology sample to see whether bacteria or yeast are involved.
  • Clean, then medicate. Flush with Epi-Otic, wait about 30 minutes, then apply the prescribed drops so they reach the canal instead of sitting on wax.
  • Maintenance afterward. Once the infection clears, many vets keep allergy-prone dogs on a regular cleaning schedule to help reduce relapses.

Always follow your vet's specific instructions. Some treatments, such as the enzymatic Zymox Otic, tell you not to clean the ear before applying them.

Tips to make an Epi-Otic ear flush less stressful

A good cleaner only helps if you can actually use it. These habits come up again and again in owner feedback and veterinary handling advice:

  • Warm the bottle in your hands or a cup of warm (not hot) water. Cold liquid in the ear is startling.
  • Pick your spot. The bathtub, a tiled floor or the yard makes the post-flush shake easy to clean up. Keep a [[amazon-search:dog-towel|microfiber dog towel]] handy.
  • Go slow with nervous dogs. Start by just handling the ears and rewarding, then add the bottle, then a small squirt, over several sessions.
  • Get a helper for large or wriggly dogs: one person steadies and feeds treats while the other flushes.
  • Keep the nozzle clean. Wipe it after each use and avoid touching it to a dirty ear, so you don't contaminate the bottle.

Which dogs benefit most from Epi-Otic?

  • Floppy-eared breeds such as Cocker Spaniels, Basset Hounds, Golden Retrievers and many Doodles, where poor airflow traps moisture.
  • Swimmers and water dogs that need help drying the canal after every dip.
  • Allergic dogs, whose vets often recommend a routine cleaner between flare-ups.
  • Heavy wax producers without infection, where regular flushing keeps buildup under control.
  • Cats, too: the product is labeled for dogs and cats.
