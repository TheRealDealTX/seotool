Vetericyn Plus is the dog ear rinse we would reach for when comfort matters most. It is gentle enough for frequent use, it does not sting by design, and it is safe if a dog licks it.

What Is Vetericyn Plus Ear Rinse?

Vetericyn is best known for its hypochlorous wound and skin sprays. The ear rinse applies the same technology to ears. Vetericyn sells it as the Plus Antimicrobial Ear Rinse, a 3 oz dropper bottle labeled for dogs, cats and other animals, and it is also listed as a dog ear rinse on Amazon.

Vetericyn says it is intended for routine ear care: flushing out debris, cleaning after swimming or outdoor play, and supporting ears that are irritated. The company also makes a higher-strength "Plus VF" line sold only to licensed veterinarians. The bottle you buy online is the consumer version.

Vetericyn Ear Rinse Ingredients: How Hypochlorous Acid Works

The ingredient list is unusually short. According to Vetericyn's label and product pages:

  • Active ingredient: hypochlorous acid, listed as 0.009% on some labels and 0.010% on Vetericyn's website.
  • Other ingredients: electrolyzed water, sodium chloride, and phosphates (pH buffering agents). Some labels also list a trace of sodium hypochlorite.
  • Not included: alcohol, steroids, antibiotics.

What hypochlorous acid is

Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) is the same compound white blood cells produce to fight microbes. In commercial products it is made by passing an electric current through salt water. At very low concentrations it is gentle on healthy tissue, which is why it shows up in eye, skin and wound care products for both people and pets.

Vetericyn says its rinse will not sting, harm healthy tissue or stain fabric. Its FAQ also notes a slight chlorine smell, which comes from the small amount of free active chlorine in the formula.

What the formula does not do

Notice what is missing: there are no surfactants, no salicylic or lactic acid, and no enzymes. Those are the ingredients that soften and lift wax in other cleaners.

So treat Vetericyn as a gentle rinse. It moves loose debris and water out of the ear, but thick, waxy buildup will need a dedicated cleaner. Vetericyn also says the product will not work on ear mites, and it advises vet supervision if the eardrum may be damaged.

How to Use Vetericyn Dog Ear Rinse

The label directions are simple, and there is no rinsing step. Here is the routine, combined with standard technique from our guide on how to clean dog ears.

  1. Look before you rinse

    Lift the ear flap and check for redness, swelling, discharge or a strong smell. If you see any of these, or your dog pulls away in pain, call your vet first.

  2. Open the nozzle

    Twist the dropper nozzle to open it. Hold the ear flap up so the canal is straight.

  3. Saturate the ear

    Apply enough rinse to saturate the ear opening and canal. The liquid is water-thin, so it flows down easily.

  4. Massage gently

    Massage the base of the ear for 20 to 30 seconds to work the rinse through the canal.

  5. Let your dog shake

    Head shaking afterward is normal and expected. It brings loosened debris up and out.

  6. Wipe the outer ear

    Wipe away moisture and debris from the opening with [[amazon-search:cotton-rounds|cotton rounds]] or gauze. No rinsing needed.

Vetericyn's directions say to use the rinse one to two times daily as needed. If you are layering other products, Vetericyn advises applying the rinse first and waiting at least 60 seconds before anything else, and telling your vet about any other treatments.

Tip: Because the rinse is unscented and does not sting, it is a good product for retraining a dog that has learned to hate ear cleaning. Pair each session with a treat and keep it short.

Pairing Vetericyn Plus with Triple-Action Ear Treatment

Vetericyn sells a second ear product that is often bought alongside the rinse: the Triple-Action Ear Treatment. They are not the same kind of product, and the order matters.

What Triple-Action contains

The Triple-Action treatment's active ingredient is 1% hydrocortisone, a mild corticosteroid that relieves itch and inflammation. It also contains an enzyme blend Vetericyn calls Otizyme (including lysozyme) and botanical extracts such as Phellodendron bark and chrysanthemum flower.

How to use them together

Vetericyn's own kit instructions lay out a two-step plan:

  1. During a flare-up: fill the ear canal with Triple-Action once daily for up to seven days. Leave it in; do not rinse it out.
  2. After the issue resolves: switch to the Plus Ear Rinse for routine care, one to two times daily or after swimming and outdoor play.

The Triple-Action label says not to use it together with other ear solutions or in an ear with a ruptured eardrum. It also says to stop and contact your vet if things do not improve after seven days.

Hydrocortisone caution: A steroid can calm itch while an underlying infection keeps going. The Triple-Action label notes it has not been found by the FDA to be safe and effective. If your dog needs it more than once in a while, ask your vet what is driving the itch.

Reading Pet Ear Rinse Reviews: What to Expect From Vetericyn

When you scan pet ear rinse reviews, it helps to know what this product is built to do. Based on the formula and label, these are fair expectations:

  • Comfort: the main selling point. No alcohol and no fragrance mean less for a sensitive dog to react to.
  • Light cleaning: good for dust, loose debris, and water after swimming. Less effective on caked-on wax.
  • Mess: the liquid is as thin as water, so expect it to run. Clean outdoors or in a tub.
  • Smell: a faint pool-like chlorine note that fades quickly, rather than a perfume.
  • Bottle size: 3 oz goes faster than a 12 oz budget flush, especially on a large dog.

Negative reviews of any dog ear rinse often come from using it on an ear that needed a vet. No rinse fixes a deep infection, mites or a foreign body.

Vetericyn Plus Ear Rinse Review: Value and Bottle Life

Vetericyn Plus costs more per ounce than budget flushes, and the bottle is small. Whether that matters depends on how you use it.

For a small dog rinsed once or twice a week, 3 oz lasts a while. For a large retriever rinsed after every swim, you will go through bottles quickly. Vetericyn also says the rinse keeps its strength through freezing and thawing, though a leaking or bulging bottle is a sign it has frozen. Use it before the printed expiration date and keep it in the original bottle, as the label directs.

Can You Use Vetericyn Ear Rinse on Puppies and Cats?

Vetericyn describes the rinse as safe for all animals of all ages, and the product page names cats specifically. That makes it a convenient single bottle for homes with both dogs and cats.

With puppies, look closely before you rinse. Dark, crumbly debris that looks like coffee grounds often points to ear mites, and Vetericyn says the rinse will not treat mites. A vet visit and a proper parasite product come first.
