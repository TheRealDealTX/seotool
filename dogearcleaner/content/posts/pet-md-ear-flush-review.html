Pet MD Veterinary Tris Flush: Ingredients and How It Works

The Tris Flush is the product most people mean when they search for a Pet MD ear flush review. It comes in a 12 oz squeeze bottle and is labeled for dogs and cats.

What's in Pet MD Tris Flush

Published ingredient lists from major pet retailers show the same formula:

  • Active ingredient: ketoconazole 0.1%.
  • Inactive ingredients: purified water, SDA-40 (denatured alcohol), nonoxynol-9, benzyl alcohol, disodium EDTA, tromethamine, and fragrance.

Tromethamine is the "Tris" and disodium EDTA is the "EDTA." Together they form the Tris-EDTA buffer that veterinary dermatologists have used in ear flushes for years.

Why Tris-EDTA matters

EDTA binds calcium and magnesium in bacterial cell walls. That makes gram-negative bacteria such as Pseudomonas more vulnerable, which is why veterinary teaching hospitals list Tris-EDTA among useful ear cleaner ingredients for those rod-shaped infections.

In practice, vets often use Tris-EDTA as a pre-treatment flush before prescription ear drops. In an over-the-counter bottle, it mainly works as a gentle, non-acidic cleaning base.

Why ketoconazole matters

Ketoconazole is an antifungal. The yeast Malassezia is a common reason dog ears turn greasy, brown and sour-smelling. A cleaner with an antifungal can help keep that overgrowth in check between vet visits.

At 0.1%, this is a low, cleaner-level concentration. It is not a replacement for a prescribed antifungal ointment when a vet has diagnosed a yeast infection.

The alcohol question

Here is the part many reviews skip. Retailer listings describe the Tris Flush as gentle and non-stinging, yet the ingredient list includes SDA-40 and benzyl alcohol. Alcohol helps the ear dry after flushing, which is useful for swimmers.

On intact skin, most dogs tolerate small amounts of alcohol well. On raw, scratched or ulcerated skin, it can sting. If your dog's ears are already sore, a no-sting rinse such as the one in our Vetericyn Plus ear rinse review is a kinder choice.

Pet MD Otic Clean Ear Solution: The Everyday Ear Cleaner

Otic Clean is Pet MD's routine cleaner. It comes in an 8 oz bottle, and the version in our catalog has a cucumber melon scent.

What's in Pet MD Otic Clean

The retailer ingredient list includes deionized water, propylene glycol, aloe vera gel, SD alcohol 40, lactic acid, glycerin, dioctyl sodium sulfosuccinate, salicylic acid, and vitamins.

  • Salicylic acid and lactic acid help loosen wax and dead skin cells.
  • Dioctyl sodium sulfosuccinate (also called docusate) is a wax-softening surfactant also found in vet-brand cleaners.
  • Aloe and glycerin add some soothing and moisturizing feel.
  • SD alcohol 40 aids drying, with the same sore-ear caveat as the Tris Flush.

Who Otic Clean suits

Otic Clean makes sense for dogs whose ears are basically healthy but get waxy or dirty. Think weekly maintenance for a Labrador, a Cocker Spaniel, or a dog that rolls in everything outdoors.

It does not contain an antifungal, so if your dog's ears keep turning yeasty, the Tris Flush is the better Pet MD fit. The scent is a plus for owners, though some dogs and people prefer unscented products.

Pet MD Ear Wipes: Quick Cleanups, Not a Flush

Pet MD Dog Ear Cleaning Wipes come in a 100-count tub. Product listings describe them as alcohol-free wipes made with aloe vera and eucalyptus oil.

A full ingredient list published for the wipes also includes glycerin, propylene glycol, mild surfactants, lactic acid, salicylic acid, tetrasodium EDTA, preservatives, fragrance and dyes. Other listings show a shorter version, so check your package if your dog has skin sensitivities.

How to use ear wipes correctly

The directions are simple: wipe the visible part of the ear and the ear opening, then repeat with a fresh wipe until it comes away clean. The label lists them for dogs over 12 weeks old.

Wipes cannot reach down a dog's L-shaped ear canal. That is fine for healthy ears, but swimmers, floppy-eared breeds and dogs with heavy wax still need a liquid flush. Never push a wipe or cotton swab into the canal.

How to Use Pet MD Ear Flush the Right Way

Pet MD's own directions for the Tris Flush are brief (shake well before use). The steps below follow standard veterinary ear cleaning technique, which also applies to Otic Clean. For a fuller walkthrough, see our guide on how to clean dog ears.

  1. Check the ear first

    Lift the flap and look. If you see redness, swelling, blood, or thick discharge, or your dog yelps when touched, stop and call your vet.

  2. Fill the canal

    Hold the flap up and squeeze enough solution to fill the ear canal. Keep the nozzle at the opening; do not jam it deep inside.

  3. Massage the base

    Gently massage the base of the ear for about 30 to 60 seconds. A squishing sound means the liquid is moving through the canal.

  4. Let your dog shake

    Step back and let your dog shake out the liquid and loosened debris. Doing this outdoors or in a bathroom saves your walls.

  5. Wipe the outer ear

    Use [[amazon-search:cotton-rounds|large cotton rounds]] or gauze to wipe the ear opening and inner flap. Skip cotton swabs in the canal.

  6. Reward and repeat on the other side

    A treat after each ear builds a calmer routine over time.

How Often to Use Pet MD Ear Flush

There is no single schedule printed on the Tris Flush, so frequency comes down to your dog's ears. As a starting point:

  • Healthy ears with little wax: every two to four weeks, or only when you see debris.
  • Waxy or floppy ears: about once a week.
  • Swimmers: after swim days, once the outer ear is towel-dried. Our after-bath ear care guide covers the drying step.
  • Allergy-prone dogs: as your vet advises, since cleaning helps but does not fix the allergy.

More is not better. Flushing healthy ears every day can irritate the lining of the canal and upset its normal balance.

Who Should Skip Pet MD Ear Cleaners

Pet MD's liquids are good value, but they are not right for every dog. Skip them, or talk to your vet first, if:

  • Your dog's ears are red, raw or scratched open. The alcohol content can sting.
  • Your vet suspects a ruptured eardrum. No over-the-counter flush should go into an ear with a damaged eardrum.
  • Your dog is already on prescription ear medication. Some drops should not be mixed with cleaners, so ask about timing.
  • You suspect ear mites. Coffee-ground debris, especially in puppies, needs a vet-prescribed parasite treatment.

See a vet: Head tilt, loss of balance, a strong foul odor, pus, bleeding, or ears that are hot and painful are not cleaning problems. Read our guide to dog ear infection signs before buying anything.
