Floppy ear breeds are some of the most loved dogs in the world, but those soft, hanging ears come with a built-in problem: they trap heat and moisture right over the ear canal. If you share your home with a Cocker Spaniel, Basset Hound, Labrador, Poodle or any other floppy-eared dog, ear care isn't optional grooming. It's preventive health care. This guide covers why pendant ears are prone to trouble, a 20-breed ear care table, the plucking debate, snoods, and breed-by-breed cleaning tips.

Key takeaways

  • Hanging ear flaps cover the canal opening, cut airflow and hold in moisture, so yeast and bacteria can overgrow more easily.
  • Floppy ears are a risk factor, not the only cause. Allergies are among the most common underlying triggers of recurring ear infections.
  • Most floppy eared dogs do well with a weekly ear check, a cleaning every 1-2 weeks, and a cleaning after every bath or swim. Your vet may adjust that.
  • Hairy-canal breeds such as Poodles and Shih Tzus need a vet's call on plucking. Many vets no longer pluck healthy ears routinely.
  • Very long ears (Bassets, Cockers, Bloodhounds) drag through food and water. A snood or a narrow bowl keeps them cleaner.

Why Floppy Ear Breeds Are Prone to Ear Problems

Every dog, not just floppy ear breeds, has an L-shaped ear canal. It runs down vertically, then turns toward the eardrum. That bend already makes it harder for debris and moisture to escape, whatever the ear shape.

In dog breeds with floppy ears, the ear flap (pinna) folds over the opening as well. The canal ends up covered, warm and humid, which is ideal for the Malassezia yeast and Staphylococcus bacteria that normally live on the skin in small numbers.

How pendant ears trap moisture

  • Less airflow: a hanging flap acts like a lid, so bath water, swim water and sweat take longer to evaporate.
  • More heat: a covered canal stays warmer than an open one, and warm, damp skin is easier for microbes to colonize.
  • Heavy flaps: long, thick ears such as a Basset's or Bloodhound's press tightly against the head and seal the opening further.
  • Hair and feathering: coated ears hold water like a sponge, and hair inside the canal catches wax.

The ear shape is only part of the picture. Veterinary dermatologists describe floppy ears as a predisposing factor. The primary cause behind many recurring infections is allergy, either environmental or food-related, which inflames the skin that lines the canal. If your floppy-eared dog gets infection after infection, talk to your vet about allergies as well as cleaning.

Ear Types Explained: Pendant, Drop, Folded and Rose

Breed standards describe ears in specific terms, and the type gives a rough idea of how much airflow the canal gets.

  • Pendant: long, heavy ears that hang well below the jaw line (Basset Hound, Cocker Spaniel, Beagle). Lowest airflow.
  • Drop: ears that hang down close to the cheek but are shorter (Labrador, Golden Retriever, Dachshund). Moderate airflow.
  • Folded: ears that hang in distinct folds or curls (the Bloodhound's long folded ears, the Shar-Pei's small, tight triangles).
  • Rose: small ears that fold back and expose part of the inner ear (Bulldog, Pug, Greyhound, Whippet). Better airflow, though some rose-eared breeds have narrow canals.

Hairy Ear Canals: Poodles, Shih Tzus and the Plucking Debate

Some floppy ear breeds have hair growing inside the ear canal, not just on the flap. Poodles, Shih Tzus, Maltese, Bichons, Lhasa Apsos, Schnauzers and most doodles fall into this group.

That canal hair catches wax and debris and blocks airflow even more. For decades groomers routinely plucked it out.

Why many vets have moved away from routine plucking

Current veterinary opinion is split, and many vets now advise against plucking healthy ears. Pulling hair out of the follicle can leave tiny injuries and inflammation in the canal lining, and that irritation can set the stage for the infection plucking was supposed to prevent.

Plucking is still commonly recommended when hair is visibly matted with wax, when it stops medication from reaching the canal, or when a dog has a history of recurring infections. A reasonable middle ground for hairy-eared dogs:

  • Ask your vet to look in the canal and decide whether the hair is actually causing a problem.
  • If plucking is advised, remove only the loose hair near the opening, a little at a time, ideally with ear powder for grip.
  • If you are told not to pluck, have your groomer trim the hair around the canal opening and keep up regular cleaning.

If your vet does recommend plucking, a light dusting of dog ear powder makes the hair easier to grip with fingers and less likely to snap.

Long Ears in Food and Water: Snoods and Smarter Bowls

The longest-eared floppy ear breeds have an extra problem: their ear tips end up in the dinner bowl. A Basset Hound, Cocker Spaniel, Bloodhound or Afghan Hound can come away from a meal with ears soaked in water or crusted with food.

Wet, food-coated ear leather stays damp, gets matted and attracts dirt. Some owners also see a sore, thickened edge on the ear tips.

Simple fixes:

  • Snood at mealtimes: a stretchy fabric tube holds the ears back against the neck while the dog eats. A dog ear snood is common kit for show spaniels and Cavaliers. Only use one while you are supervising.
  • Narrow, deep bowls: "spaniel bowls" have a tapered top so the ears fall outside the rim.
  • Wipe the tips after meals: a damp cloth followed by a dry one stops food and water from soaking into the feathering.
  • Trim feathering: on pet (non-show) dogs a groomer can shorten the hair on the ear tips so less of it drags.

Ear Care for Floppy Eared Dogs: A Simple Weekly Routine

Good ear care for floppy eared dogs is mostly about consistency, and it matters more for floppy ear breeds than for prick-eared dogs. Here is the routine we suggest at Dog Ear Cleaner, built from veterinary cleaning guidance and manufacturer directions. For the full technique, see our step-by-step guide to how to clean dog ears.

  1. Lift and look

    Flip each ear flap up in good light. A healthy ear is pale pink, with little or no wax and no strong smell.

  2. Sniff test

    A yeasty, sour or musty smell is often the first sign of trouble, before your dog starts scratching.

  3. Fill and massage

    If cleaning is due, fill the canal with a dog ear cleaner, then massage the base of the ear for 20-30 seconds until you hear a squishing sound.

  4. Let them shake, then wipe

    Step back and let your dog shake, then wipe the loosened wax from the opening and the folds with large cotton rounds or gauze. Never push anything into the canal.

  5. Dry and air

    Pat the flap dry. On humid days, folding the ears back for a few minutes lets air reach the canal.

Bath days and swim days matter most. Water that sits under a pendant ear is a common trigger, so read our after-bath ear care guide for how to keep water out and dry the canal afterward.

Breed-Specific Tips for Dog Breeds With Floppy Ears

Cocker spaniel ear cleaning

Cocker Spaniels are among the breeds veterinarians most often see for chronic ear disease. Heavy, feathered pendant ears, hairy canals and a tendency toward allergic skin all play a part. Some long-term cases develop thickened, narrowed canals that may eventually need surgery.

  • Check the ears every week and clean weekly, or whenever you see waxy buildup.
  • Keep the hair on the underside of the flap and around the opening trimmed short for airflow.
  • Clean after every bath, because Cocker coats hold water.
  • Get recurring infections treated early. Cocker ears that are left inflamed are more likely to undergo permanent changes.

Basset hound ear care

Basset Hound ears are long enough to drag on the ground, so they collect dirt, dew and food. Their weight also seals the canal shut.

  • Wipe the ear leather daily or after walks, front and back.
  • Clean the canals weekly with a gentle cleaner, and dry the deep fold at the base of the ear.
  • Use a snood or a narrow bowl at meals.
  • Check the ear tips for scabs or thickened edges where they rub or get stepped on.

Labrador and Golden Retrievers

Retrievers are milder floppy ear breeds with shorter drop ears, but they swim and many have allergies. "Swimmer's ear" problems are common in water-loving Labs and Goldens. Use a drying ear cleaner after lake, pool or ocean swims, and dry the feathering on a Golden's flaps thoroughly.

Cavalier King Charles Spaniels

Cavaliers have the usual feathered-ear risks, plus a breed tendency toward primary secretory otitis media (PSOM), a buildup of thick mucus in the middle ear. Signs can include head scratching, head shaking or neck sensitivity even when the outer canal looks clean. If your Cavalier shows these signs, see a vet rather than just cleaning more often.

Poodles, Shih Tzus and other hairy-eared breeds

Clean every 1-2 weeks, keep the coat around the ear trimmed, and get your vet's verdict on canal hair. Between groomer visits, check that hair at the canal opening isn't matted with dark wax.

Beagles, Bloodhounds and scent hounds

Nose-down dogs pick up grass seeds, burrs and dirt. After hikes, look under every flap for plant material. A grass awn in the canal often causes sudden, intense head shaking on one side and needs a vet.

Shar-Peis, Bulldogs and Pugs

These breeds don't have long ears, but their canals can be very narrow and their skin heavily folded. Narrow canals are hard to flush and easy to irritate, so follow your vet's advice on product and frequency rather than cleaning aggressively.

When Floppy Ears Need a Vet, Not a Cleaner

See a vet if you notice: redness or swelling, dark or yellow discharge, a strong odor, pain when the ear is touched, constant head shaking, a head tilt, loss of balance, or a swollen, pillow-like ear flap (a possible aural hematoma). Ear cleaners are not medication and can't treat an established infection. Cleaning a painful ear can also be harmful if the eardrum is damaged.

Floppy ear breeds can go from mild to painful quickly, so learn how to spot the early stages in our guide to dog ear infection signs. If you want a printable routine that also covers teeth, nails and coat, see our dog cleaning routines.

The Bottom Line on Floppy Ear Breeds

Floppy ear breeds aren't doomed to ear infections. They just need more attention than prick-eared dogs. A weekly look-and-sniff, regular cleaning with a gentle cleaner, thorough drying after water, and early vet visits when something seems off will prevent most problems.

Frequently asked questions

Why do floppy ear breeds get more ear infections?

A hanging ear flap covers the ear canal opening, which cuts airflow and holds in warmth and moisture. Yeast and bacteria that normally live in the ear can overgrow in that environment. Floppy ears are a contributing factor, though, not the whole story: allergies are one of the most common underlying causes of recurring ear infections in dogs.

How often should I clean my floppy-eared dog's ears?

Many floppy-eared dogs do well with a weekly ear check and a cleaning every one to two weeks, plus a cleaning after every bath or swim. Dogs with hairy canals, heavy wax, or a history of infections may need weekly cleaning. Over-cleaning can irritate the ear, so ask your vet to set the right interval for your dog.

Which dog breeds are most prone to ear infections?

Breeds frequently named by veterinarians include Cocker Spaniels, Springer Spaniels, Basset Hounds, Labrador and Golden Retrievers, Poodles and Poodle crosses, Shar-Peis, and West Highland White Terriers. The common threads are pendant ears, hairy or narrow ear canals, a love of water, and a tendency toward allergic skin disease.

Should I pluck the hair inside my Poodle's or Shih Tzu's ears?

Routine plucking is debated. Many veterinarians now advise against plucking healthy ears because pulling hair can cause small injuries and inflammation in the canal. Plucking is more often recommended when hair is clearly trapping wax and debris or when a dog has recurring infections, so let your vet decide for your individual dog.

What is a snood for dogs?

A snood is a soft fabric tube that slides over a dog's head and holds long ears back against the neck. Owners of Cocker Spaniels, Basset Hounds, Cavaliers, and Afghan Hounds use them at mealtimes to keep ear tips out of food and water bowls. They should only be worn under supervision.

Can I use cotton swabs to clean my dog's floppy ears?

Use swabs only on the outer folds of the ear flap where you can see the tip at all times. Pushing a swab into the canal can pack debris deeper and risks injuring the canal or eardrum. A liquid ear cleaner followed by wiping with a cotton round is the safer approach.
