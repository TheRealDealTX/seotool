This Zymox Ear Cleanser review clears up the single biggest source of confusion around the brand. The Zymox Ear Cleanser is a gentle, non-medicated enzymatic cleaner for routine care. It is not the same product as Zymox Otic, the small bottle of enzymatic ear solution sold with hydrocortisone. We break down the LP3 enzyme formula, label directions, owner feedback, and how it compares to Virbac Epi-Otic.

Key takeaways

  • What it is: an enzymatic ear cleaner for dogs and cats, built on Zymox's patented LP3 enzyme system (lactoperoxidase, lactoferrin, lysozyme), plus glucose oxidase.
  • What it's for: long-term maintenance of non-infected ears, one to two times a week or after swimming.
  • Don't confuse it with Zymox Otic. Otic is a 7 to 14 day course applied to an uncleaned ear, with or without hydrocortisone.
  • Label caution: avoid it in irritated ears or with a ruptured eardrum. Red, painful or smelly ears need a vet.
  • Vs Epi-Otic: Zymox is the milder enzyme option. Epi-Otic is the stronger wax-and-drying cleaner that can also be used alongside vet treatment.

What is Zymox Ear Cleanser?

Zymox Ear Cleanser (sometimes listed as "Zymox Ear Cleanser with Bio-Active Enzymes" or "Zymox Enzymatic Ear Cleanser") is a non-medicated cleaner. The manufacturer says it "gently cleans and soothes dirty ears" and is ideal for "long-term maintenance of non-infected ears."

It's marketed as antibiotic-free, free of harsh chemicals and detergents, safe for all ages, and suitable for dogs that swim. Zymox also suggests it as the follow-up cleaner after finishing a course of Zymox Otic.

Zymox Ear Cleanser ingredients: the LP3 enzyme system

A current label lists:

Purified water, glycerin, coco-glucoside, propylene glycol, benzyl alcohol, lactoperoxidase, lactoferrin, lysozyme, glucose oxidase, zinc gluconate.

Some retailer listings show sodium lauryl sarcosinate as the cleansing agent in place of coco-glucoside and benzyl alcohol, which suggests the formula has been updated at some point. Your bottle's label is the one to trust.

IngredientWhat it does
LactoperoxidaseThe "LP" in LP3. A naturally occurring enzyme (found in milk and saliva) that forms part of a natural antimicrobial system.
Glucose oxidaseAn enzyme that produces the small amounts of hydrogen peroxide the lactoperoxidase system needs to work.
LactoferrinAn iron-binding protein. Zymox includes it for what it calls natural bio-active properties.
LysozymeAn enzyme found in tears and saliva that can break down bacterial cell walls.
Coco-glucosideA mild, plant-derived cleansing agent that lifts dirt and wax.
Glycerin, propylene glycolHumectants and solvents that keep the solution smooth and help it spread through the canal.
Zinc gluconateA zinc salt, a common supporting ingredient in Zymox formulas.

Zymox markets the enzymes as offering "mild microbe control." That's a manufacturer claim about a cleaner. It isn't a substitute for diagnosis or treatment when an ear is already infected.

Why choose an enzymatic ear cleaner?

Most ear cleaners rely on surfactants, acids or alcohol to break down wax and dry the canal. Zymox takes a different route, using proteins that occur naturally in milk, tears and saliva, where they form part of the body's own defenses.

The appeal for many owners is philosophical as much as practical. They want a cleaner with no antibiotics, no steroids and no harsh detergents for an ear that's basically healthy. If that's you, an enzymatic cleanser is a reasonable fit.

What enzymes don't do is replace a diagnosis. Ear infections in dogs are usually driven by something underneath, most often allergies, ear shape, moisture or a foreign body. That underlying cause still needs finding and managing.

Is Zymox Ear Cleanser alcohol-free?

There's no SD alcohol or ethanol, the drying and stinging kind, on the label. The version above does list benzyl alcohol, which is used as a preservative. If you're checking ingredients because your dog reacts to a specific substance, read the label on your exact bottle.

How to use Zymox Ear Cleanser

These steps follow the label directions. Our how to clean dog ears guide has more on technique and handling nervous dogs.

  1. Check the ear

    The label says to avoid using it in irritated ears. If you see redness, swelling or discharge, or your dog flinches, skip cleaning and check our ear infection signs guide.

  2. Shake well

    Shake the bottle so the solution is evenly mixed.

  3. Fill the ear canal

    Lift the ear flap and apply liberally to fill the canal. Keep the tip at the opening; don't push it deep.

  4. Massage the base of the ear

    Massage gently for 20 to 30 seconds to work the cleaner through the canal and loosen wax.

  5. Let your dog shake, then wipe

    Let your dog shake, then wipe away loosened debris with a cotton ball or soft cloth. Large cotton rounds are easier to handle than balls. Never push cotton swabs into the canal.

Tips for better results with Zymox Ear Cleanser

  • Warm the bottle in your hands first. Cold liquid poured into an ear is what makes many dogs bolt.
  • Be generous. The label says to fill the canal. A few drops won't reach the wax deeper in a dog's L-shaped ear canal.
  • Clean after swimming, not days later. The label specifically suggests using it after swimming, while the canal is still damp.
  • Keep the tip clean. Wipe the nozzle after each use, and don't let it touch a dirty ear, so the bottle stays clean.
  • Make it rewarding. Pair every session with soft training treats so your dog learns ear cleaning predicts something good.

Going from Zymox Otic to Zymox Ear Cleanser

Because the two products are designed to be used one after the other, a sensible sequence looks like this:

  1. Vet check if the ear is red, sore or smelly, so you know what you're dealing with and that the eardrum is intact.
  2. Zymox Otic course, if your vet agrees: once daily for 7 days (14 for chronic cases), applied to an uncleaned ear, with no other ear cleaners and no water in the ear during treatment.
  3. Recheck if the ear hasn't clearly improved. Persistent or worsening ears need veterinary treatment.
  4. Switch to Zymox Ear Cleanser once or twice a week for maintenance after the course is finished.

Mixing these steps up, for example cleaning the ear with the cleanser before each Otic dose, works against the Otic directions.

Which dogs is Zymox Ear Cleanser best for?

  • Dogs with healthy ears that just need regular cleaning to stay that way.
  • Swimmers: the label specifically allows use after swimming. Pair it with our after-bath ear care tips.
  • Dogs coming off a Zymox Otic course, once your vet confirms the ear has cleared.
  • Floppy-eared breeds with mild buildup (see our floppy-ear breeds guide).
  • Cats: the product is labeled for dogs and cats.

Who might want a different cleaner

  • Heavy wax producers: dogs that build up thick, dark wax every few days usually do better with a dedicated wax-dissolving formula such as Epi-Otic.
  • Dogs under active vet treatment: if your vet wants the ear cleaned before prescription drops, ask which cleaner they prefer. Many reach for Epi-Otic for this job.
  • Very sensitive or sore ears: once a vet has ruled out a ruptured eardrum, a no-sting rinse like Vetericyn Plus is the gentlest option.

When to skip it and see a vet

Skip the cleanser if your dog has red, hot or swollen ears, dark or yellow discharge, a strong yeasty smell, bleeding, head tilting or loss of balance, or if a ruptured eardrum is possible. Those signs need a veterinary exam, not a routine cleaner. Not sure? Run through our symptom checker.

Is Zymox Ear Cleanser worth buying?

For the right dog, yes. If your dog's ears are healthy and you want a simple, gentle routine with no antibiotics, steroids or SD alcohol, Zymox Ear Cleanser is one of the most established options available.

It's also a practical bridge for owners who already use Zymox Otic. Using the same brand for treatment and then maintenance keeps things simple, as long as you use each product according to its own label.

Where it's less worth it is for dogs whose main problem is heavy wax, or dogs in ongoing vet care. There, Epi-Otic's stronger cleaning and drying formula and its track record alongside prescription drops make it the better buy. Multi-pet households should note that Zymox also sells a separate enzymatic ear cleanser marketed for cats and kittens, though the standard cleanser is labeled for both species.

Zymox Ear Cleanser does what it says: a gentle, enzyme-based cleaner for keeping healthy ears clean, used once or twice a week. Dog Ear Cleaner rates it a solid maintenance choice, especially for swimmers, cats, and dogs finishing a Zymox Otic course.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Zymox Ear Cleanser and Zymox Otic?

Zymox Ear Cleanser is a non-medicated enzymatic cleaner for routine care of non-infected ears, used once or twice a week. Zymox Otic is a small-bottle enzymatic ear solution, sold with hydrocortisone or hydrocortisone-free, that is applied once daily for 7 to 14 days to an uncleaned ear. They are used very differently, so check which one you have.

What is in Zymox Ear Cleanser?

A common current label lists purified water, glycerin, coco-glucoside, propylene glycol, benzyl alcohol, lactoperoxidase, lactoferrin, lysozyme, glucose oxidase and zinc gluconate. Some listings name sodium lauryl sarcosinate as the cleansing agent instead, so read the label on your bottle.

How often should I use Zymox Ear Cleanser?

The label directions are one to two times a week, or after swimming, or as directed by your veterinarian. Most healthy dogs don't need it more often than that.

Can Zymox Ear Cleanser treat an ear infection?

No. It is designed for long-term maintenance of non-infected ears, and the label says to avoid use in irritated ears. An ear that is red, painful, smelly or has discharge should be seen by a vet, who may prescribe medication or recommend a product such as Zymox Otic.

Can I use Zymox Ear Cleanser before Zymox Otic?

No. Zymox Otic's directions say not to clean the ear before or during treatment because it disrupts the enzyme system, and not to combine it with other ear cleaners. Zymox suggests the cleanser for maintenance after the Otic course is finished.

Is Zymox Ear Cleanser safe for puppies and cats?

The cleanser is labeled for dogs and cats, and retailers describe it as safe for all ages. As with any ear product, check with your vet first for a very young puppy or kitten, or any pet with a history of ear problems.

Is Zymox Ear Cleanser better than Epi-Otic?

Neither is better for every dog. Epi-Otic Advanced has a stronger wax-cleaning and drying formula and can be used alongside vet-prescribed medication. Zymox Ear Cleanser suits owners who want a mild enzyme-based cleaner for healthy ears once or twice a week.
