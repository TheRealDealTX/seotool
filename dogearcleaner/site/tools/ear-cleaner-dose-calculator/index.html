How much ear cleaner should you use?

Most dog ear cleaner labels tell you to fill the ear canal until the solution is visible at the opening, then massage the base of the ear. In practice that is roughly 1 to 2 mL per ear for a toy breed and 5 to 10 mL per ear for a giant breed. Using too little is the most common mistake: a few drops just sits on top of the wax instead of loosening it.

These are estimates. Always follow your product's label or your vet's directions, which take priority over any calculator.

Which bottle size makes sense?

Small dogs on a monthly routine can make a 4 oz bottle last a year or more, but ear cleaner doesn't last forever once opened, so check the expiry date. Large dogs, weekly cleaners and multi-dog homes usually save money with the bigger sizes, such as Epi-Otic Advanced 8 oz or Pet MD Tris Flush 12 oz.

Frequently asked questions

How much ear cleaner do I put in my dog's ear?

Fill the canal until you can see the liquid at the opening. That is roughly 1 to 2 mL per ear for toy breeds, 2 to 4 mL for small dogs, 4 to 6 mL for medium dogs and 6 to 10 mL for large and giant breeds. Follow your product label first.

Can you use too much ear cleaner on a dog?

The extra simply runs out when your dog shakes, so overfilling one session isn't dangerous for a healthy ear. Cleaning too often is a bigger problem than using too much at once.

Should the bottle tip touch my dog's ear?

No. Keep the nozzle just above the canal opening so it doesn't contaminate the bottle or poke the ear. Wipe the tip if it touches anything.

How long does a bottle of dog ear cleaner last?

It depends on your dog's size and how often you clean. A 4 oz bottle used weekly on a 40 lb dog lasts roughly 3 months; the calculator gives an estimate for your dog.

Does ear cleaner expire?

Yes. Check the expiry date printed on the bottle and replace it if the liquid changes color, smell or texture.
