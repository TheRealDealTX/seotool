After Bath Care for Dogs: How to Protect and Dry Your Dog's Ears (and Everything Else)

Good after bath care for dogs starts with the ears. The shampoo is rinsed out and your dog smells clean, but water trapped in the ear canal can quietly turn into an itchy, smelly ear infection within days. This guide shows you how to keep water out of your dog's ears during the bath, how to dry dog ears after a bath or swim, when to use a drying ear cleaner, and how to finish the coat, paws and skin so bath day doesn't become a vet visit.

Key takeaways

  • Protect the ears before you start: a loose cotton ball in each ear opening and a washcloth for the face keep most water out.
  • After the bath, remove the cotton, let your dog shake, then wipe the flap and ear opening dry. Never push anything into the canal.
  • For floppy-eared dogs, swimmers and dogs with a history of ear trouble, a drying ear cleaner after baths and swims helps clear trapped water.
  • Dry the coat with a towel and a low-heat dryer, brush while slightly damp, then check paws and skin.
  • Head shaking, odor, redness or discharge after a bath means a vet visit, not more cleaning.

Why After Bath Care for Dogs Starts With the Ears

A dog's ear canal is L-shaped. It runs down vertically, then turns toward the eardrum, so water that gets in doesn't simply drain out.

That leftover moisture softens the skin lining the canal. It also creates the warm, damp conditions that let Malassezia yeast and bacteria, which normally live in the ear in small numbers, overgrow. Veterinarians commonly list excess moisture from bathing and swimming as a trigger for otitis externa (outer ear infection).

Dogs with pendant ears have it worst, because the flap covers the opening and holds the moisture in. If you own a Cocker Spaniel, Basset Hound, retriever or Poodle, see our guide to floppy ear breeds for breed-specific risks.

Before the Water Runs: How to Protect Your Dog's Ears During a Bath

The easiest ear to dry is one that never got wet inside, so after bath care for dogs really begins before the bath. A few small changes to how you bathe go a long way.

Use cotton balls in the ear openings

Place a loose cotton ball at the entrance of each ear canal before you start. It should sit in the opening like a soft plug, not be pushed into the canal. Many groomers do this as routine.

Tip: Count the cotton balls in and count them out. A forgotten cotton ball in the canal can cause irritation and odor, so take them out right after the bath.

Wash the head last, with a washcloth

  • Start at the neck and work back, so your dog is less tempted to shake the whole time.
  • Don't pour or spray water over the top of the head. Clean the face and ears with a damp washcloth instead.
  • Hold the ear flap down over the opening while you rinse around the head and neck.
  • Rinse thoroughly. Shampoo residue left on the ear flap and around the opening can irritate the skin.

Lay out your supplies first

Have everything within reach before your dog gets wet: towels, cotton rounds, ear cleaner, a brush and treats. A wet dog waiting while you hunt for a towel is how water ends up all over the house.

  • An absorbent microfiber dog drying towel
  • Large cotton rounds or gauze for the ears
  • A drying, alcohol-free ear cleaner (see below)
  • A brush suited to your dog's coat, and treats

How to Dry Dog Ears After a Bath

Here's the core of dog ear care after bath time, and the step most owners skip. It takes about two minutes.

  1. Remove the cotton balls as soon as the rinse is done.
  2. Let your dog shake. Shaking is the most effective way a dog clears water from its own ears, so encourage it, ideally outdoors or in the tub.
  3. Squeeze the flap gently in a towel to soak up water from the hair on both sides.
  4. Wipe the ear opening and inner folds with a cotton round or gauze wrapped around your finger. Go only as deep as your finger comfortably reaches.
  5. Air the ears. For floppy-eared dogs, fold the flaps back over the head for a few minutes so air can reach the canal.

Skip cotton swabs inside the canal. They tend to push wax and water deeper and can injure the canal lining. And don't point a blow dryer into the ear. Hot or forceful air on the sensitive canal skin does more harm than good.

Cleaning Dog Ears After a Bath: Why a Drying Cleaner Helps

Wiping the outer ear removes visible water, but it can't reach the bend in the canal. That's why many vets suggest cleaning dog ears after a bath for at-risk dogs. A cleaner flushes out water, shampoo and loosened wax, and formulas with drying properties leave the canal less damp than water alone.

After a bath is also a convenient time: the wax is already soft, the dog is already wet, and you're already holding a towel.

Which dogs benefit most

  • Floppy-eared breeds and dogs with hairy ear canals
  • Dogs that swim or are bathed more than every few weeks
  • Dogs with allergies or a history of ear infections
  • Any dog that got water in its ears despite the cotton balls

Short-coated dogs with upright ears and clean canals often don't need a cleaner after every bath. For them, after bath care for dogs can be as simple as a wipe and a dry.

Dog Ears After Swimming

Swimming puts far more water in the ears than a bath does, and lakes, ponds and the ocean can add bacteria, algae, sand and salt. Chlorinated pool water can also dry and irritate the skin. "Swimmer's ear" is a common problem for Labs, Goldens, spaniels and doodles.

The routine for dog ears after swimming is the same as after a bath: shake, towel the flaps, wipe the openings, and use a drying ear cleaner once your dog is out of the water for the day. Rinse salt or chlorine from the coat with fresh water. On beach and lake trips, look under the flaps for sand and plant material.

A Complete Post-Bath Routine for Dogs

Ears come first, but complete after bath care for dogs covers the coat, paws and skin too. Here's the full routine in order.

  1. Remove cotton balls and let your dog shake

    Pull the cotton out of both ears and let your dog shake in the tub or outside before you start toweling.

  2. Dry the ears

    Squeeze the flaps in a towel and wipe the ear openings and folds with a cotton round. Air floppy ears for a few minutes.

  3. Use a drying ear cleaner if needed

    For floppy-eared, swimming or ear-prone dogs, fill, massage, let them shake and wipe.

  4. Towel the coat

    Press and squeeze rather than scrubbing. Rubbing in circles tangles long coats. Work on the armpits, belly, groin, under the collar line and between the toes.

  5. Blow-dry on low heat

    Use a dog dryer or a human dryer on its coolest setting, keep it moving and hold it several inches from the skin. Keep airflow away from the face and ear canals.

  6. Brush while slightly damp

    Brush in sections from the ends of the hair toward the skin to keep mats from tightening as the coat dries.

  7. Check and dry the paws

    Dry between the toes and pads, and look for cracks, redness, debris or overgrown nails.

  8. Do a quick skin check

    While the coat is parted, look for red patches, flakes, bumps, scabs, hot spots, fleas or ticks.

  9. Reward and settle

    Finish with a treat and a warm, dry spot to rest, and keep your dog out of drafts until fully dry.

Drying the Coat: Towel, Dryer or Air Dry?

Towel drying

Microfiber towels absorb much more water than cotton bath towels, so the dryer has less to do. Blot and squeeze, and use a second towel once the first is soaked.

Using a dryer safely

Dogs can't easily tell you when air is too hot, and some human dryers run very hot. A low-heat dog grooming dryer is the safer choice for thick coats. Keep the nozzle moving, check skin temperature with your hand, and never leave a dog in a crate with a heated dryer running. Introduce the noise gradually with treats if your dog is nervous.

Is air drying okay?

For short, single-coated dogs in a warm house, air drying after a good towel-off is usually fine. Thick double coats (Huskies, Shepherds, Goldens) and long, dense coats can stay damp at the skin for hours, which can lead to musty odor, matting and hot spots. Even air-dried dogs still need their ears dried by hand.

Brushing After a Bath

Freshly washed hair tangles easily, and knots tighten as they dry. Brush while the coat is still slightly damp, or right after blow-drying.

  • Long or curly coats: a slicker brush followed by a wide-tooth comb.
  • Double coats: an undercoat rake or deshedding tool. A bath loosens a lot of undercoat.
  • Short coats: a rubber curry brush to lift loose hair and spread natural oils.

Pay special attention to the feathering behind and under the ears, a classic spot for mats that trap moisture against the ear.

Paws and Skin: The Two-Minute Post-Bath Check

A skin check is the most overlooked part of after bath care for dogs. Bath day is the best chance you get to look closely at your dog's skin. Wet or freshly dried fur parts easily.

Look for redness, flaking, lumps, scabs, hot spots and parasites, and note anything new so you can mention it to your vet. Dry carefully between the toes, where moisture and soap residue can linger and lead to irritation and licking.

Frequent bathing with harsh shampoos can strip skin oils. If your dog gets itchy after baths, ask your vet about bathing frequency and a gentler or medicated shampoo.

Post-Bath Zoomies: Normal and Mostly Harmless

That burst of sprinting, rolling and couch-diving after a bath has a name: frenetic random activity periods, or FRAPs. Dogs may be letting off stress, celebrating the end of the bath, or trying to rub off the wet feeling and the shampoo scent.

It's normal. Clear away slippery rugs and breakables, keep your dog indoors until dry (so they don't roll in the dirt), and let them run it out. A predictable routine with treats at the end tends to make the whole experience calmer over time.

Breed Notes for Dog Ear Care After a Bath

  • Cocker Spaniels, Springers, Bassets, Cavaliers: long pendant ears hold water. Use cotton balls every bath and a drying cleaner afterward.
  • Poodles, Shih Tzus, Bichons, doodles: hair in the canal soaks up water. Dry thoroughly and ask your vet about managing canal hair.
  • Labs and Goldens: swimmers and allergy-prone. Make post-swim ear cleaning a habit.
  • Huskies, Shepherds and other double coats: the ears are usually upright and lower risk, but the undercoat needs a full blow-dry.
  • Bulldogs, Pugs, Shar-Peis: dry the facial and body skin folds as carefully as the ears. Narrow canals call for your vet's guidance on cleaning.

Common After Bath Care for Dogs Mistakes to Avoid

  • Pouring water over the head or spraying directly at the ears
  • Forgetting to remove cotton balls
  • Using cotton swabs deep in the canal
  • Blowing hot air into the ears or holding a dryer in one spot
  • Leaving shampoo residue on the ears and skin
  • Using alcohol-heavy ear products on irritated ears
  • Letting thick coats air-dry overnight

Frequently asked questions

Should I clean my dog's ears before or after a bath?

After is usually better. Water that gets past the ear flap during a bath stays in the canal, and a cleaner with drying properties helps flush it out and leaves the canal less damp. Cleaning after the bath also means shampoo residue doesn't sit in the ear.

How do I dry my dog's ears after a bath?

Remove any cotton balls you placed in the ears, let your dog shake, then gently squeeze the flap with a towel and wipe the opening and inner folds with a cotton round or gauze. Never push swabs or towel corners into the canal. For floppy-eared dogs or dogs that got water in their ears, follow with a drying ear cleaner.

Is it safe to put cotton balls in my dog's ears during a bath?

Yes, many groomers and vets suggest loosely placing a cotton ball in the ear opening to block splashes. It should sit at the entrance, not be pushed into the canal, and it must be removed straight after the bath. Count them in and count them out so none get left behind.

Can I use a hair dryer to dry my dog's ears?

Avoid pointing any dryer directly into the ear canal, because hot or forceful air can irritate the skin and frighten your dog. A low, cool setting kept moving over the outside of the ear flap is fine for drying hair. Inside the ear, a cotton round and a drying ear cleaner work better.

Do dogs need their ears cleaned after swimming?

Dogs that swim often, especially floppy-eared and allergy-prone dogs, benefit from a drying ear cleaner after swims in pools, lakes or the ocean. Water left in the canal is a common trigger for ear infections. If your dog swims every day, ask your vet how often to clean so you don't over-clean.

Why does my dog get the zoomies after a bath?

Post-bath zoomies are a form of frenetic random activity periods, a normal burst of energy. Dogs may be releasing pent-up stress, celebrating the end of the bath, or trying to rub off the wet feeling and shampoo smell. Keep the area safe and let your dog run it out.

What are the signs of an ear infection after a bath?

Watch for head shaking, scratching or rubbing the ears, a yeasty or sour smell, redness, swelling, discharge, or pain when the ears are touched. These signs can show up a day or two after water exposure. Ear cleaners aren't medication, so see a vet if you notice them.
