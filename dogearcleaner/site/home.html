Dog ear cleaning in 6 steps

How to clean your dog's ears (the quick version)

Here's the routine Dog Ear Cleaner recommends for healthy ears. For photos-free detail, mistakes to avoid and how to handle a dog that hates it, read the full guide to how to clean dog ears.

  1. Check first. Pale pink skin and a little light wax is normal. Redness, pain, discharge or a bad smell means vet first, cleaner later.
  2. Get set up. Ear cleaner, cotton rounds or gauze, a towel and treats. No cotton swabs inside the canal.
  3. Fill the canal. Lift the ear flap and squeeze in enough dog ear cleaner to fill the canal. Most people use too little.
  4. Massage 20-30 seconds. Rub the base of the ear until you hear a squish. That's the cleaner breaking up wax.
  5. Let them shake. Step back. The head shake brings loosened debris up and out.
  6. Wipe and reward. Wipe the flap and the opening, never deeper than your finger reaches, then praise and treat.

Dog ear cleaner vs. infection

Clean at home, or call the vet?

A dog ear cleaner is for maintenance: it lifts wax, rinses away debris and dries out moisture so yeast and bacteria have less to work with. It isn't a treatment for an infection that's already there. If your dog's ear smells bad, looks red or swollen, has yellow, green or dark discharge, or your dog cries when you touch it, use the symptom checker or book a vet visit before you clean.

Red flags that need a vet today: head tilt, loss of balance, flicking eyes, a drooping face, bleeding, or a puffy, fluid-filled ear flap. More in our guide to dog ear infection signs.

Dog ear cleaner FAQs

What is the best dog ear cleaner?

For most dogs, Dog Ear Cleaner recommends starting with a gentle, alcohol-free cleaner such as Virbac Epi-Otic Advanced, the cleaner many vet clinics hand out. Dogs with sensitive or sore ears often do better with a no-sting rinse like Vetericyn Plus, and smelly, waxy ears with an enzymatic cleaner like Zymox.

How often should I clean my dog's ears?

Most healthy dogs need their ears cleaned every 2 to 4 weeks, or when you see wax. Floppy-eared dogs, swimmers and dogs with allergies often need weekly cleaning. Our free schedule calculator gives a personal answer in 30 seconds.

Can a dog ear cleaner treat an ear infection?

No. A dog ear cleaner removes wax, debris and moisture, which helps prevent infections, but it does not cure an active bacterial or yeast infection. Those need a vet's diagnosis and usually medicated drops.

Can I use homemade ear cleaner on my dog?

It's not a good idea. Vinegar, hydrogen peroxide and rubbing alcohol can sting, dry out and irritate the ear canal, and can be harmful if the eardrum is damaged. A purpose-made, pH-balanced ear cleanser for dogs is safer.

Should I use cotton swabs to clean my dog's ears?

Not inside the canal. Cotton swabs push wax deeper and can injure the ear. Use cotton balls, cotton rounds or gauze on the ear flap and the opening of the canal only.
